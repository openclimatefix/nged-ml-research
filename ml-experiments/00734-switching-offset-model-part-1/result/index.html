    Does an unsupervised Bayesian model of the switched power find more of the long, shallow injected load transfers than the event table and a plain changepoint search?

    We ask whether a probabilistic model that reads a Primary substation's whole history can find more of the long, shallow load transfers than the best of the event tables that experiments 653 to 682 built. A Primary substation steps electricity down to a local distribution network, and the project forecasts the demand at each one. Operators sometimes switch part of a circuit from one Primary to a neighbour for days or months, which moves demand between the two. This page calls that switching event a load transfer. The switching events page says what a transfer looks like in the data and why the project wants to find transfers.

    The probabilistic model needs no labels and no training. For each Primary the probabilistic model takes the residual, which is the observed power minus a demand forecast that uses no lagged power, and asks, block by block, how much of the residual is switched power. The answer is a probability distribution over the switched megawatts in every 6-hour block, plus a table of events with a grade that says how sure the probabilistic model is that each event is a switch. To test the probabilistic model, we add synthetic transfers of known size, start, and end to the real history of 15 Primaries, and measure how many of the added megawatts each method finds. This page holds the plan, which was written before any world was decoded, and the result.

    The headroom is larger than the earlier pages suggest. The figure of 0.67 of long, deep megawatts found, quoted on the event-table pages, comes from the six worlds of experiment 653's own injector. On the 40 default worlds of the shared generator of experiment 660, which this experiment uses, the event table E1-J-sym finds 0.515 of the long and deep megawatts and 0.393 of the long and shallow megawatts (stored rows of experiment 680, recomputed for this plan).

    Result

    Every number below comes from the first and only scoring of the scored worlds, and is interim until the reviews are complete. The numbers come from the console output of the report stage, stored as report_output.txt, and from the tables it reads: scored/<set>/<arm>_<seed>.parquet and scored/<set>/<arm>_<seed>_calls.parquet (each world), real/summary.json and real/events_<arm>.parquet (the real record), params.json, edge_check.json, and g3b.json, all under data/ml-experiments/00734-switching-offset-model-part-1/. No figure on this page is a chart of real substation demand. The event figures made from the real record and from the synthetic worlds show demand of D8-region Primaries and stay in the private folder.

    The result in plain terms

    The offset model did not find more of the long, shallow transfers than the event table. The question was whether a hidden Markov model that reads a Primary's whole record (H-nbr: the model with the shared term and the neighbour mirror evidence) finds more of the megawatts of injected transfers that last more than 7 days (or never end) and are 2 to under 8 noise units deep. The score is the MW-weighted share found: the injected megawatts of the transfers a method found, over all the injected megawatts in the cell (higher is better; 0.355 means that 35.5% of the injected megawatts were found). On the 40 scored default worlds H-nbr found 0.355, E1-J-sym (the event table of experiment 680, a detector that segments each Primary's record with a fixed penalty per block) found 0.393, and PELT (a plain per-Primary penalised changepoint search) found 0.384.

    C1, the counted contrast, is -0.039, and its interval includes zero. C1 is H-nbr minus E1-J-sym in the long and shallow cell. The 97.5% interval resampling whole worlds is -0.090 to +0.014, and the interval resampling the calendar months of the events is -0.094 to +0.019. The bar was +0.10, so the verdict is not supported. Both vetoes passed, so they do not change the verdict. H-nbr's called rates on the real record, as multiples of E1-J-sym's, are 0.34 for all called events and 1.09 for events of 2 noise units or more (V1, limit 1.10). In event-free time they are 0.26 and 0.97 (V2, limit 1.10).

    C2 also fails: H-nbr minus PELT is -0.029 (world interval -0.078 to +0.016). The two probabilistic models, H-own (the model without neighbour evidence) and H-nbr, are no better than a plain penalised search in the cell H-own and H-nbr were built for. H-nbr minus H-own (P4) is +0.003 (-0.031 to +0.036), so the neighbour evidence added nothing in the long and shallow cell.

    All of this is on synthetic transfers injected into the real history of 15 Primaries. The real record has no labels that could confirm the result. The 77 logged shifts in the real record are matched by H-nbr 13 times under the strict rule and 19 under the loose rule, and by H-own 14 and 22 times.

    Other reads

    • The real record, scored years: over 36.3 Primary-years H-nbr calls 84 events (2.32 per Primary-year, 1.93 for events of 2 noise units or more), H-own 99, PELT 89, and E1-J-sym 6.87 and 1.76 per Primary-year. Of H-nbr's 84 events, 10 are mirrored and returned, 64 returned without a visible partner, and 10 neither.
    • Hard negatives (steps, ramps, pulses, and placebo pairs that are not switching events): H-nbr makes 0.86 calls per hard negative, H-own 1.15, PELT 0.97, and E1-J-sym 1.90.
    • Placebo worlds (the same events moved to random times): C1 on the placebo worlds is -0.100 (world interval -0.164 to -0.038), so H-nbr loses to E1-J-sym there too.
    • Un-injected worlds: H-own calls 3 false events, H-nbr 2, and PELT 73.
    • Solar growth (24 worlds, 72 grown Primaries) and seasonal bias (2 worlds, 10 grown Primaries): H-nbr makes 0.48 false events per grown Primary-year under solar growth and 0.96 under seasonal bias; H-own makes 0.60 and 1.04.
    • Tuning: H-own's best value of the return-to-zero rate (rho_off) sat on the edge of its extended search grid. The other tuned values were interior or limited by a called rate (see Amendment 1).

    What the result means

    In the long and shallow cell, H-nbr finds fewer injected megawatts than E1-J-sym, so the result gives no reason to replace E1-J-sym there. H-nbr does find more megawatts of events that last 1 to 7 days, and it calls fewer events in total on the real record (0.34 times E1-J-sym's rate), though about as many events of 2 noise units or more (1.09 times). The claim the plan set out to test, a gain of +0.10 in the long and shallow cell, did not hold. The plan's later step, a restricted version of the offset model (part 2), is for the maintainer to decide; this page makes no commitment.

    Terms

    • Residual: observed cleaned power minus the forecast of the lag-free convex demand model of experiment 130, as stored by experiment 327 (arm C, refitted monthly, driven by ERA5 and CAMS estimates of past weather). Every output here is therefore a hindsight product.
    • Block: a 6-hour mean of the residual, on four fixed UTC blocks a day (00:00 to 06:00, 06:00 to 12:00, 12:00 to 18:00, and 18:00 to 24:00). The record from 2022-01-01 to 2026-06-30 has 6,568 blocks per Primary.
    • Noise unit: 1.4826 times the median absolute deviation of a Primary's block means about its slow level, as experiment 653 defines and stores it (0.16 to 1.02 MW across the 15 Primaries, median 0.22 MW). The 6-hour noise unit is a different unit from the 2-hour step unit of experiment 732.
    • Cell: one of the six groups of injected events of experiment 665. Long is more than 7 days or no end, medium more than 1 day up to 7 days, short 1 day or less; deep is 8 noise units or more, shallow 2 to under 8. The size and the duration are the injected ones.
    • Found: an injected event is found when a method has an event at the same Primary with the same sign, a start within 24 half-hours (12 hours), and an intersection over union of the two intervals of at least 0.5 (the match function of experiment 653), among the method's called events. The MW-weighted share found is the injected megawatts of found events over all injected megawatts in the cell.
    • World: one copy of the real power panel with synthetic events added by the generator of experiment 660. Every world sits on the same real history, so worlds share all real-record noise and real unlabelled events.
    • Called event: an event in a method's output table at the method's tuned threshold. The two called rates are the number of called events per Primary-year, and the number of called events of 2 noise units or more per Primary-year. The 2-unit threshold is set in megawatts from experiment 653's stored noise unit of each Primary, for every method, so that the methods' different internal units cannot move it.
    • Event-free time: the 27.6 Primary-years from 2024-01-01 to 2026-06-30 that lie more than 14 days from every logged or verified event of experiment 580. NGED's logs miss events, so the called rate in event-free time is an upper bound on the false-call rate.
    • Primary-year: one Primary observed for one year. D3, D5, D16, and the like: anonymised Primary names. MVA Primary: one of the 8 of the 15 Primaries whose meter reports apparent power, which has no sign.

    The arms

    Four arms are counted or benchmarked, and two ablations and one reference row are read without being counted.

    Arm What it is Input
    E1-J-sym Experiment 680's event table: experiment 653's Viterbi segmentation with the two-way neighbour search residual minus experiment 653's common mode
    PELT a plain penalised changepoint search per Primary (Killick, Fearnhead, and Eckley, 2012), with a Gaussian cost on block means as E1-J-sym
    H-own the offset model, own series only, no shared term and no neighbour evidence as E1-J-sym
    H-nbr the offset model with the fitted shared term and one round of neighbour evidence residual, with the shared term in place of experiment 653's common mode
    H-nbr-noramp H-nbr with a second step in the same direction given the probability of a fresh step (ablation) as H-nbr
    H-nbr-noshared H-nbr with experiment 653's common mode in place of the fitted shared term (ablation) as E1-J-sym
    E1 experiment 653's table without any search (reference row) as E1-J-sym

    The arms are ordered so that each step changes one part. H-own against E1-J-sym changes only the inference, because the two read the same input. H-nbr against H-own adds the shared term and the neighbour evidence, and the two ablations split that difference.

    E1-J-sym is the benchmark rather than experiment 653's original table, because E1-J-sym is the current event table. Experiments 685 and 692 ran E1-J-sym, and experiment 680's advice is to compare any new method with E1-J-sym. E1-J-sym's stored rows exist for the 40 scored worlds, and its real-record table (249 events in 2024 to mid-2026) is stored. E1-J-sym finds slightly more than E1 in every cell (0.393 against 0.365 in the long and shallow cell), so it is also the harder benchmark.

    PELT is written by hand in this folder, because ruptures is not a dependency in pyproject.toml. PELT is an exact optimal partitioning with pruning; on 6,568 blocks per Primary it runs in about a second.

    The offset model

    The offset model writes each Primary's block residual as a slow level, plus a shared term, plus three hidden components, plus noise. For Primary s and block t:

    y(s, t) = slow(s, t) + β(s) · σ(s) · c(s, t) + o(t) + ℓ(t) + g(t) · F(s, t) + noise,

    where the components are these:

    • slow(s, t) is experiment 653's seasonal slow level (3 day-of-year harmonics plus a linear spline with knots every 365 days, fitted with a Huber loss with candidate event windows given zero weight, then frozen). The slow level is refitted in every world, as experiment 653 does.
    • c(s, t) is the shared term: the median, over the other Primaries with a reading, of each Primary's block residual minus its slow level, in its own noise units. Leaving Primary s out of its own median stops a large event at s from entering its own shared term. A transfer moves two Primaries in opposite directions, so the median of 14 barely moves with it. The loading β(s) is a Huber fit on 2022 and 2023 with candidate event windows masked.
    • o(t) is the total switched offset, on 41 levels from -20 to +20 noise units. The forecast of the normal running arrangement removes o(t).
    • ℓ(t) is a permanent level, on 21 levels from -20 to +20 noise units in steps of 2. The forecast of the normal running arrangement follows ℓ(t).
    • g(t) is a slow gain on the demand forecast F(s, t), on 5 levels from -0.06 to +0.06 (a share of the forecast demand). The forecast of the normal running arrangement follows g(t).
    • The noise is a Student-t density with 4 degrees of freedom, as in experiment 653, with one scale per Primary and per block of the day, fitted on 2022 and 2023.

    The transitions encode the cue that a switch is temporary, and allow a switch to arrive in two or three steps.

    • Temporary. From o = 0, the offset jumps with probability ρ_on per block, to a level drawn with weight 1/|k| (log-uniform, the same law as the generator's sizes, which flatters the offset model on injected events). From a nonzero level the offset returns to zero with probability ρ_off, which is far larger than ρ_on, and moves to another nonzero level j with probability ρ_move = ρ_on, split with weight 1/|j − i|. The permanent level ℓ jumps with probability ρ_ℓ = ρ_on to another level j, split with the same weight 1/|j − i|, with no preference for returning. A step that later returns therefore has a higher prior probability as a switch than as two permanent jumps. A step that never returns lowers the probability of the switch path by a factor of about 1 − ρ_off for every block spent away from zero, so a step that never returns is more probable as a permanent jump.
    • Steps in a row. A flag records whether the offset moved in the previous block and in which direction. A further move in the same direction in the next block has probability ρ_cont, which is far larger than ρ_on but well below 1. The rule makes two cases nearly as probable as a single step: a cascade of two or three switches within a day, and a step that starts part-way through a 6-hour block, whose first block holds only part of the step. The rule does not separate a switch from a slow ramp: a ramp over several days moves less than one grid level per block, so the offset model explains it with the gain, the shared term, or the noise whatever ρ_cont is, and a ramp that the grid does resolve is treated by ρ_cont like a cascade. The ramp check under "Gates" is therefore a printed diagnostic, not a gate.
    • Slow gain. The gain moves by at most one level per block, with probability ρ_g = 0.02. A sudden step of any shape is therefore more probable as o or ℓ than as g, and g follows slow, demand-shaped drift.

    The four blocks of the day separate a flat shift from a demand-shaped change and from embedded solar. A switched offset moves all four blocks by the same amount. A gain change moves them in proportion to the forecast demand, which differs between night and day. A change in embedded solar moves only the two daylight blocks, which carry a larger noise scale, so no component of the offset model explains a daylight-only change well and the offset model leaves the change in the noise. The night block (00:00 to 06:00 UTC) holds a little solar output on summer mornings after about 04:00 UTC.

    Inference is exact. The joint state of the offset, its move flag, the permanent level, and the gain has 41 × 3 × 21 × 5 = 12,915 values. The transition matrix factorises over the three components, so one forward-backward pass (Rabiner, 1989) over a Primary's 6,568 blocks takes about 7 seconds on one core (a timing of the forward step alone gave 0.21 milliseconds per block; the backward pass and the jump probabilities have not been timed). The pass gives the posterior distribution of o(t) in every block. A Viterbi pass (Viterbi, 1967), in log space, gives the single most likely path, from which the event table is read; the Viterbi pass is timed first in stage 1, because its run time is not yet measured.

    Neighbour evidence is one round of message passing, without feedback. Pass 1 runs the offset model on each Primary alone and records the probability of an upward and of a downward jump in o at every block. Pass 2 runs each Primary again with the probability of a fresh jump in each direction (a jump from a settled block, not a continuation of a move) multiplied by 1 + κ · m(t), where m(t) is the largest probability, over the Primary's neighbours and over the blocks within 3 of t (18 hours either side), of a jump in the opposite direction at the neighbour in pass 1. The window of 3 blocks covers the generator's recipient start offset of up to 36 half-hours. A Primary's pass 2 never reads its own pass 1 through a neighbour, so no evidence is counted twice. Each Primary keeps an unseen-partner sink: the unboosted jump probability, so a transfer whose partner is unmetered keeps its own-series odds. 21 of the 66 hand-filtered real transfers have no metered receiver. The graph is experiment 383's list of 25 links between the 15 Primaries, first-order neighbours only.

    An MVA reading near zero is reported as unknown. At an MVA Primary the reading is the magnitude of the power, so a step near zero can fold. Blocks where the observed MVA block mean is below 3 noise units get no emission term (the offset model treats them as missing), and every output there carries the flag unknown. A block flagged unknown gives no neighbour evidence.

    The output has three parts. Per Primary and block: the posterior quantiles q05, q10, q25, q50, q75, q90, and q95 of the switched megawatts, read off the 41-level grid without interpolation and converted to megawatts with the Primary's noise unit, the probability that the switched megawatts are zero, and the unknown flag. Per event: start and end (refined to the half-hour by experiment 653's step search within 12 hours of the block edge), amplitude, a confidence, and one of four grades:

    • mirrored and returned: a neighbour has an opposite-sign called event whose start lies within 3 blocks, and the event ends inside the record;
    • mirrored, not yet returned: the same, with no end inside the record;
    • returned, no visible partner: the event ends inside the record, with no mirror;
    • neither: no end and no mirror, which includes every jump of the permanent level. Here the posterior of the switched megawatts is two-peaked (zero, or the full step), and the forecast follows the change by default, because one wrongly masked 60-day interval raised the tail error by 57% in experiment 38.

    The event table is read from the Viterbi path. An o-event is a maximal run of blocks with a nonzero offset of one sign on that path, split where the level changes by 2 noise units or more after at least one settled block, so a run of consecutive moves in one direction (a cascade, or the partial first block of a step that starts mid-block) stays one event. The o-event's confidence is the mean posterior probability that o is nonzero over the run. An ℓ-event starts at each jump of ℓ on the path and ends at the next jump that brings ℓ back within one level of its earlier value, or stays open; its confidence is the posterior probability of an ℓ jump within one block of the start. Both kinds enter the found share, because the cells of experiment 665 include permanent shifts. An event is called when its confidence reaches the threshold τ.

    The scored default worlds stay exactly as experiment 680 scored them, so E1-J-sym's stored rows remain the benchmark and gate G1 can check them. The cascade and cold-spell layers go on new worlds, and E1-J-sym is re-run on those worlds with experiment 680's code.

    The cascade layer tests the rule for steps in a row. In each cascade world, 15% of the positive events (edge transfers and solo shifts, chosen at random) have their start replaced by 2 or 3 steps (equal chance) within 24 hours, at random times on the half-hour, with the event's change split into random shares that sum to the whole. In half of the chosen events the return is cascaded the same way. A donor and its recipients are cascaded at the same times. A cascaded event is one truth event for scoring, with the first step as its start and the whole change as its size.

    The cold-spell layer tests the shared term. Each cold-spell world has 3 cold spells in each November to February, each lasting 4 to 14 days with a raised-cosine shape. At each Primary the spell adds a share a · λ(s) of the forecast demand, with a drawn from 0.03 to 0.10 for each spell and λ(s) drawn from 0.5 to 1.5 for each Primary. Two Primaries per world, drawn at random, get λ = 0, which mimics an industrial Primary that does not follow the weather. Cold spells are hard negatives: a call that overlaps a spell by half its length is a false call.

    The intervals resample whole worlds and are therefore optimistic. The 40 worlds share one real history, so the world-resampled interval ignores the variation that a second real history would add. Experiment 653 had only 6 worlds. The month-resampled interval resamples the 30 calendar months in which events start, pooled over worlds, which keeps the real history's month-to-month variation. A contrast counts only when both intervals exclude zero.

    The cells hold enough events to read. The long and shallow cell holds 840 injected events over the 40 default worlds (11 to 45 per world), and every cell holds at least 127 events. A cell with fewer than 20 events or 10 edge-transfer ends is too small to read, as in experiments 665 to 682.

    Tuning, once, before any scored world is decoded

    Tuning chooses five numbers for the offset model and one for PELT, and fixes everything else in advance.

    • Fixed: the grids above, ν = 4, ρ_move = ρ_ℓ = ρ_on, ρ_g = 0.02, the 3-block mirror window, and the MVA fold rule (3 noise units).
    • Tuned for H-own on the tuning worlds: ρ_on in {1e-4, 3e-4, 1e-3}, ρ_off in {1/60, 1/240} (a mean switched duration of 15 or 60 days; the second extension adds 1/20 and 1/8, about 5 and 2 days), and ρ_cont in {0.05, 0.2}, 12 settings.
    • Tuned for H-nbr: κ in {0, 5, 20}, with H-own's ρ values. H-nbr-noramp and H-nbr-noshared use H-nbr's values.
    • Objective: the MW-weighted share found of the tuning worlds' events in the four long and medium cells (not the counted cell alone), at equal called rates.
    • Equal called rates: for each setting, τ is the lowest value in {0.5, 0.6, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9, 0.95, 0.99} at which both called rates on the real record of 2022 and 2023 (all calls, and calls of 2 noise units or more) are no higher than E1-J-sym's rates there (E1-J-sym re-run on those years). Holding the rate of all calls stops a method from passing by calling many events just under 2 noise units. PELT's penalty, from a grid of 12 values spaced geometrically from 2 to 200 times the noise variance, follows the same rule.
    • When no threshold meets the rates: a setting for which no τ in the grid (no penalty, for PELT) meets both rates is ineligible. If every setting is ineligible, the setting with the lowest rate of all calls at τ = 0.99 is used (the highest penalty, for PELT), the page says so, and vetoes V1 and V2 are read as they fall.
    • Shared term on or off, per Primary: on when the shared term lowers the Primary's noise unit on the real record of 2022 and 2023 by 5% or more, and off otherwise. The pre-check under "Stage 0" prints the share of each Primary's block variance that the shared term explains.

    The tuned values are written to params.json with a hash at the end of stage 2, before the scored worlds are generated, and the stage 3 script refuses to run without that file and the review marker review_stage3.ok, which is written once the review of params.json has passed.

    Reading the result

    A null result says that whole-record inference does not find more long, shallow megawatts than a segmentation with a fixed penalty per block on this bank. If C1's intervals include zero while P7 (calibration) and P9 (cold spells) hold, the offset model's value lies in its calibrated distribution and its grades, which serve use-cases 2 and 3, and not in events found; the next experiment would then test the grades on NGED's answers rather than chase recall. If H-own already matches H-nbr, the neighbour evidence adds nothing at 15 Primaries, which agrees with experiment 682 (an all-pairs graph paired 0.32 against 0.33 for the real links).

    A gain that holds only in the world-resampled interval is reported as not counted. The 40 worlds share one real history, so a gain that the month-resampled interval does not support may come from a few real months.

    What would change our mind. The design would be wrong, rather than unlucky, in four cases:

    • the known-answer test G3 passes but H-own finds less than E1-J-sym in the long and deep cell, which would mean the return-to-zero prior hands long events to the permanent level;
    • H-nbr-noramp finds as much of the cascaded events' megawatts as H-nbr on the cascade worlds, which would mean the higher probability of a second step in the same direction does no work for cascades or for steps that start part-way through a block;
    • H-nbr-noshared matches H-nbr on the cold-spell worlds, which would mean experiment 653's fixed common mode is already enough;
    • the share of events graded "neither" exceeds half of all found injected temporary events, which would mean the offset model cannot tell a temporary switch from a permanent change at this noise level.

    Risks and failure modes

    The main risk is a baseline error that looks like an unpaired temporary transfer. A holiday fortnight, a cold spell at an industrial Primary that does not follow the shared term, or one of D8's multi-day drops shifts one Primary's residual for days to weeks and then returns. The offset model grades that shift "returned, no visible partner", and with 21 of 66 real transfers lacking a metered receiver that grade cannot be dropped. The Christmas, cold-spell, and D8 rows above measure how often the risk occurs, and only NGED's answers can measure the grade's real precision.

    The second risk is headroom. On this bank E1-J-sym already finds 0.515 of the long and deep megawatts and 0.393 of the long and shallow megawatts, and experiment 653's 25-state decoder already has a forward-backward pass, so the gain may be smaller than the +0.10 bar.

    • Proportional shapes. The generator's events follow the donor's daily profile for a share of 0.3 to 1.0, and the two verified transfers measured 0.31 to 0.96, so the "flat" cue is weak in the bank and in reality. The gain moves at most one level per block, so a sudden proportional step should still go to o or ℓ; the found share by the generator's prop_share (below 0.5 against 0.5 or more) checks this.
    • Permanent shifts in the counted cell. 130 of the 840 long and shallow events are permanent shifts, of which E1-J-sym finds 0.09 of the megawatts. ℓ-events enter the found share, so the model is not penalised for calling a permanent shift permanent.
    • The size prior matches the generator. The log-uniform jump prior is the generator's own size law, which flatters every arm of the offset model on injected events.
    • The geometric duration. A constant ρ_off gives a geometric duration, while the generator's durations are log-uniform from 3 hours to 112 days, so the prior probability of a very long switch is lower than the generator's, and the tuning of ρ_off can only partly correct that.
    • The shared term at few Primaries. Where fewer than 8 other Primaries have a reading (before D15 and D18 start, or in gaps), the shared term is set to zero.
    • Monthly refits. The residual's demand model refits monthly, which can step the residual at a month boundary; the refit-boundary row measures this.
    • Unlabelled real events. The worlds sit on the real record, so a real unlogged transfer can coincide with an injected event. Following the data-processing page, a called event that recurs at the same Primary and day in 10 or more of the 40 worlds is treated as a real-record event, not a false call, wherever false calls on injected worlds are counted.

    Audit triggers

    The audit triggers of the ml-experiment skill apply, adapted to a detector. Trigger 1 fires if a gate or the hard-negative placebo fails; trigger 2 if a counted prediction fails without a tested explanation; trigger 4 if leaving out one Primary or one world flips C1's verdict (the report prints the leave-one-out table); trigger 5 if H-own beats H-nbr in the counted cell by more than C1's half-width; trigger 6 if C1's world-resampled interval is more than twice or less than half as wide as experiment 680's interval on the same worlds; trigger 7 if a later experiment, such as a long-window neural network taught by this model, is planned on this result before it is replicated. Trigger 3 does not apply, because no forecast quantile score is contrasted. An audit runs on a fresh agent that re-derives C1 from the stored event tables with its own code.

    Standing practices and earlier advice

    • Followed: cleaned data with the substation_zero half-hours dropped (the count is reported); D15 and D18 rows before 2025-03-01 dropped; stored rows reused as the benchmark; a synthetic world gallery and a smoke check; a per-phase time log; no stage past its review gate; the hash of every edited reviewed script recorded; mid-day onsets in the known-answer tests; the four scenarios.
    • Not applicable: monthly refits, model saving, training-loss plots, and screening folds, because nothing is trained; tail coverage of a forecast, because no forecast is scored; weather at the forecast origin, because the residual uses estimates of past weather and the output is a hindsight product, which the page states.
    • Advice followed: compare with E1-J-sym (experiment 680); report shares found and shares paired separately (experiment 653); report short events apart (experiment 665); read a cell only with 20 events or more (experiment 665); import generator 660 and name its configuration, report a shuffled_placebo beside every contrast, and treat un-injected time as unlabelled (experiment 660); allow a start tolerance of at least 4 half-hours between the two ends of a transfer (experiment 686); drop candidates that recur in many worlds (experiment 692); first-order neighbours only, because an all-pairs graph paired as well as the real one (experiment 682).
    • Advice departed from: experiment 653's advice to build an event table without partners next. This experiment keeps partners as evidence, never as a requirement, because the unseen-partner sink gives an unpaired event the same prior probability as in H-own.

    Changes from the design memo

    Six changes to the design that the maintainer approved, each made for a reason found while planning.

    • Benchmark. The memo names experiment 653's table; this plan uses E1-J-sym, the current event table, with E1 as a reference row.
    • Headroom. The memo quotes 0.67 of long, deep megawatts from experiment 653's own worlds. On generator 660's worlds the figures are 0.515 and, in the counted cell, 0.393.
    • Observation. A 6-hour block cannot hold both a night mean and a day mean, and a daily step would blur the edges to a day. The offset model keeps 6-hour blocks and lets each block of the day carry its own demand loading and noise scale.
    • Permanent level. The memo's two-peaked "neither" distribution needs an explanation that competes with a switch, so the offset model adds the permanent level ℓ beside the gain.
    • Lag-free demand estimate. The stored residual already subtracts a lag-free demand forecast, so every arm keeps that baseline, and stage 0 compares it with a weekly profile and a neighbourhood share for part 2 only, so that stage 1's arms differ only in their inference.
    • Mirror window. The memo says "within one step"; the plan uses 3 blocks, because the generator starts a recipient up to 36 half-hours after its donor.

    Method

    Architecture.

    • Input: the residual of the Terms section on the 15 Primaries, 2022-01-01 to 2026-06-30, as 6-hour block means; experiment 653's common mode removed for every arm except H-nbr, which removes the fitted shared term instead.
    • Model: the hidden Markov model of "The offset model", with 12,915 joint states, exact forward-backward and Viterbi passes, and one round of neighbour evidence for H-nbr.
    • Output: block quantiles of the switched megawatts, the probability of zero, the unknown flag, and a graded event table refined to the half-hour.
    • Baselines: E1-J-sym and E1 from experiments 680 and 670 (stored rows, re-run where needed); PELT written here.

    Training regime.

    • No training: priors, κ, τ, and the PELT penalty are tuned once on 30 tuning worlds with the scored years hidden and on the real record of 2022 and 2023; NGED's labels enter nothing.
    • Compute: CPU only, about 68 CPU-hours at 1 thread per worker (stop at 90 after two extensions), 0 GPU-process-hours.

    Measurement.

    • Rows: injected events of 2 noise units or more, of kind edge, solo, permanent transfer, or permanent shift, starting 2024-01-01 to 2026-06-30, weighted by absolute megawatts, in the cells of experiment 665.
    • Matching: experiment 653's match function for every arm.
    • Intervals: paired world-resampled and month-resampled bootstraps, 10,000 draws, 97.5%.
    • Real record: called rates in 2024 to mid-2026 and in event-free time, logged shifts matched, and the verified transfers.
