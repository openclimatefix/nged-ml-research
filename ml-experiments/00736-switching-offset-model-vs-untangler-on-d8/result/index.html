    Does a probabilistic model of switched load find more injected load transfers at D8 than a neural network's step detector?

    We asked whether two detectors of load transfers, built in two earlier experiments of this repository, find the same synthetic transfers at one substation. The substation, D8, is a Primary substation, the kind that steps voltage down towards customers. A transfer is the switching of part of a circuit between two Primaries, which moves demand for hours to days. The first detector is the untangling network of experiment 732, a neural network that reads two days of D8's electricity readings at a time and gives, for each half-hour, the probability of a step in demand (its step head). The second is the offset model of experiment 734, a probabilistic model of D8's whole history that infers, for every 6-hour block, how much of the demand is switched power, which is power moved by a transfer. We added the same synthetic transfers of known start, end, and size to D8's record and asked each detector which starts it finds. The measure is the share of the 21 injected transfers, lasting 24 to 96 hours and moving at least 1.42 MW, whose start each detector finds. The plan registered that cell as the headline because it matches the part of the medium cell of experiment 665 (transfers of more than 1 day and up to 7 days) that lasts 4 days or less. A detector finds a start when it calls a step of the same sign within 6 hours of the injected start.

    The offset model and the step head found about the same number of long transfers, so the verdict is inconclusive. The offset model found 13 of the 21 and the step head found 12, a difference of 1 transfer that the experiment cannot tell from chance. The step head found far more of the shorter transfers: 17 of 29 transfers of 6 to 24 hours and 29 of 54 transfers under 6 hours, against 2 and 0 for the offset model.

    Result

    The verdict is inconclusive: on the 21 counted transfers the offset model and the step head find about the same number, and 21 transfers are too few to say which is better. The counted cell holds the 21 injected transfers that last 24 to 96 hours and move at least 4 step noise units, all in the 6 held-out months. A step noise unit is D8's noise scale for steps in experiment 732 (0.355 MW), so 4 units are 1.42 MW. C1 is the share found by the offset model H-nbr minus the share found by the step head U-noBSP (the step head without the input from the bulk supply point), paired on the same transfers. C1 is +0.048, which is 1 transfer in 21. The exact two-sided sign test on the 7 transfers that only one detector found gives p = 1.0, so C1 does not count (C1 counts when the sign test gives p below 0.05). The 95% interval is -0.25 to +0.25 when the 8 draws are resampled and -0.21 to +0.29 when the 6 months are resampled. A draw is one random placement of the injected transfers, and a world is one draw of all 6 months merged into one record, so there are 8 worlds. The pre-registered rule gives inconclusive because C1 does not count and the upper end of the wider interval, the one resampled by draw (+0.25), is above the porting threshold of +0.15, the gain in share that would justify adopting the offset model.

    The step head found more of the 11 mirrored transfers, and the offset model found more of the 10 unmirrored ones. A mirrored transfer is one that experiment 732 mirrored at its partner Primary (D3, D5, or D14), by adding the equal and opposite change there. Of the 11 counted mirrored transfers, both detectors found 5, only the step head 3, only the offset model 1, and neither 2. Of the 10 counted transfers with no mirror, both found 4, only the step head 0, only the offset model 3, and neither 3. Each split holds too few transfers for a test. Of the 21 counted transfers, 2 sit in segments with neither a simulated engine array nor a simulated solar array added to D8's record, where both detectors read the same signal. Each detector found both of those 2 transfers.

    Post hoc, a tolerance of 12 hours moves the offset model ahead by 3 transfers, which is still inside the noise. This row was not pre-registered, because the plan fixed the tolerance at 6 hours. At 12 hours both detectors find 10 transfers, only the step head finds 2, only the offset model finds 5, and neither finds 4, so the step head finds 12 and the offset model finds 15. C1 is then +0.143 (3 of 21), which is below the +0.15 threshold, and the sign test on 2 against 5 discordant transfers gives p = 0.45. The pre-registered verdict is unchanged.

    Checks

    • The checks that compare a median forecast and a mean forecast with the metered values do not apply, because no forecast is scored.
    • One audit trigger fires, for P1, and no audit has been run. The trigger "a result contradicts its prediction" fires for P1, because the offset model found more transfers than the plan expected. The small cell and the tuning years listed below are the explanations on offer. The trigger "a failed control" does not fire, because the placebo is near zero. No other trigger applies, because no quantile score, leave-one-out, nested arm, or sister interval is involved.
    • All four gates passed. G1 reproduced the head's 304 calls and its recall of 0.490 for transfers of at least 4 step noise units, and the 12 of 21. G2 found the injected megawatts equal to the plan's values (worst relative error 5.8e-8), found no injected half-hour with a zero addition in 9,063 half-hours, and asserted that the offset model's panel index is the bank's half-hour plus 1, so transfers are not placed 30 minutes early. G3 reproduced the 131 stored events of experiment 734's un-injected decode exactly. G4 reproduced experiment 732's overlay checksum, its 384 transfers, and its 768 edge rows.
    • The world design that ran was Design A with the simulated engine and solar arrays added, so both detectors read the same signal.

    Limits that apply to this result

    Five handicaps of the plan apply to the result, and each is listed here so that a reader can discount the number.

    • The head's recall was computed before the plan was frozen. The step head's 12 of 21 came from stored files before the offset model was decoded. Only the offset model's side was predicted blind.
    • Experiment 734's tuning years overlap the test months and favour the offset model. All 8 test months fall before the end of the years that experiment 734 tuned on. The tuning touched the priors, the time constant, the noise units, the block scales, and the shared-term loadings. No injected transfer was seen in tuning, and the step heads never trained on those months. A win by the offset model would have carried this caveat, and the offset model's 13 against 12 carries it too.
    • Veto V1 excuses a call near an injected edge of any size. A call within 6 hours of an injected edge of the right sign counts as not false even when that transfer moves 0.3 MW, a transfer that neither detector can see. Both detectors get the same excuse. Each rate is therefore lower than its unexcused rate, and the veto tests the ratio.
    • The small gain verdict row cannot be reached with 21 transfers. Any gain that passes the sign test is at least 6 transfers (0.29), so a gain of 1 to 3 transfers can only land in inconclusive or not supported.
    • The placebo and the real rows do not cover exactly the same starts. Each world merges one draw of each of the 6 months into one record. The placebo drops every moved start that crosses a month end, lands inside a transfer, or meets another drop rule, so only 177 of the 288 starts remain in the placebo. Transfers near a month's end are scored in the real rows.

    Six further limits apply, and the last one concerns the registered text itself.

    • The counted cell holds only 21 transfers, so the experiment resolves a difference of shares only to about 0.25.
    • The transfers last 96 hours or less, so the experiment says nothing about the long, deep transfers on which experiment 734 scored the offset model.
    • The size band is below the offset model's design range, so the weak showing on short transfers may reflect D8's large block noise unit as well as the offset model. At D8, 4 step noise units are 1.39 block noise units (1.42 MW against 1.02 MW), below the 2 block noise units where experiment 734 scored its shallow cells.
    • Both detectors read the future of the event (see "Look-ahead"), so the result bears on labelling a historical record and not on real-time use.
    • The experiment tests one substation.
    • The registered text has three inconsistencies, and the Result follows the decision rule's own table. First, the prediction says that a transfer of 1.4 to 4.2 MW is 1.4 to 4 block noise units, "below the 2 block noise units" of experiment 734's shallow cells, but that range straddles 2 block noise units. Second, the prediction says that refuted needs both intervals below zero, while the decision rule defines refuted by the sign test (b exceeds c and C1 counts). Third, the prediction calls small gain nearly impossible, while the decision rule says that small gain cannot occur.

    Compute

    The experiment used 0.91 CPU-hours, against the plan's estimate of 2.4 and its stop at 8 CPU-hours. The time log sums the start-to-end time of every job on one core. The 16 decodes (8 worlds, 2 arms) took 0.82 CPU-hours, with H-own taking about 2.1 minutes and H-nbr about 4.0 minutes per decode (3.1 minutes on average), and G3's un-injected decode took 4.1 minutes. The plan's estimate of 15 CPU-minutes per world, and its worst case of 27.6 minutes per decode, were far too high. The plan had taken experiment 734's median of 27.6 wall-minutes per scoring job as a guide to the time of one decode. Peak memory was 0.57 GB, against the plan's 1.5 GB.

    What the result means for choosing a detector

    The offset model cannot replace the step head on D8, because the step head sees the transfers under 24 hours that the offset model does not. On the transfers of 24 to 96 hours the two detectors are tied, with the offset model giving fewer false calls. Of the 21 counted transfers, 16 were found by at least one detector (9 by both, 3 by only the step head, and 4 by only the offset model), against 13 for the offset model, the better single detector. A union of the two detectors would also carry both detectors' false calls. The result leaves open whether a rule that uses both detectors, where the step head gives short calls and the offset model long ones, finds more transfers than either alone.

    The plan as registered

    The two earlier experiments cannot be compared as they stand, because they measured different rows. Experiment 732 scored 8 held-out months of D8's real days with simulated transfers of 1 to 96 hours, one 48-hour window at a time. Experiment 734 scored the 15 Primaries over a continuous record from 2022 to mid-2026, with transfers of hours to months. The offset model cannot be decoded on one 48-hour window, and the step head cannot see more than 48 hours. The experiment put both detectors on the same transfers.

    Everything from here to "Method" is the plan as it was frozen before the offset model was decoded, and is kept unchanged. The plan's "Open questions before the first run" were settled by the code review, and the world design that ran is stated above.

    Design

    The offset model is decoded on experiment 732's transfers, added to D8's record. Three designs could put both detectors on identical transfers.

    • Design A (chosen). Take the 288 transfers of experiment 732's test bank that fall in the 6 held-out months inside experiment 653's record. Build each of the 8 worlds from D8's power plus the segment's synthetic engine and solar output plus the transfers, so that the offset model reads the same signal as the step head (experiment 732's test segments add simulated engine or solar output in most of the 64 segments, and the head reads that sum together with the synthetic sky). Add the mirror to the partner Primary when experiment 732 mirrored the transfer (D3, D5, or D14), where the mirror is the equal and opposite change added at the partner. Decode the 15-Primary panel with H-own and H-nbr, with the tuned values of experiment 734. Read the step heads' outputs on the same transfers from experiment 732's stored test predictions. The code review checked that experiment 734's apply_additions and WorldDecoder(power=...) accept the engine and solar arrays of experiment 732's overlay segments (engine and solar), and they do. Had they not, the worlds would have held transfers alone, the offset model would have read a cleaner series than the head, and the "Every handicap" list would have said so.
    • Design B (rejected). Run U-noBSP and U* on experiment 734's D8 worlds. Experiment 734 injected events into D8's record in its scored years, which lie later in the record than the heads' training days, and the heads were trained only on pre-engine days, with a synthetic sky and a BSP meter that experiment 732's banks supply and experiment 734's worlds do not. The sizes also differ: experiment 732's transfers are 0.31 to 4.2 MW, and experiment 734's shallow cell starts at 2.05 MW at D8. The test banks of experiment 732 are the rows that the heads' stored predictions cover, and no other rows have heads' outputs.
    • Design C (rejected). Chain experiment 732's daily windows into months and decode only the heads' output. Experiment 732's stored predictions are already read this way, and chaining windows gives the offset model no continuous record.

    The 6 held-out months inside experiment 653's record hold 288 injected transfers, and all of them last 96 hours or less. The two oldest of the 8 held-out months fall before experiment 653's record begins (2022-01-01), so their 96 transfers have no residual to decode. The durations are log-uniform from 1 to 96 hours, so the long cell of experiment 665 (more than 7 days) is empty, and the question is about the part of experiment 665's medium cell (transfers of more than 1 day and up to 7 days) that lasts 4 days or less.     Every handicap is stated before the run.

    • The step head sees 48 hours and calls edges, not events. The head has no memory beyond its window, so a transfer of 24 hours or more reaches the head as two separate windows. The plan scores the start edge alone for both detectors (the generous reading for the head), and scores the rule that needs both ends as an uncounted secondary row.
    • The offset model works on 6-hour blocks. The offset model cannot place a start more finely than 6 hours, which is why the one start tolerance is 6 hours (12 half-hours, one block) for both detectors. The tolerance is wider than the 1 half-hour that the recalibration report used for the head, so the head's 6-hour number is higher than the report's.
    • The size band sits below the offset model's design range. 4 step noise units of experiment 732 is 1.39 block noise units at D8 (1.42 MW against 1.02 MW), and experiment 734 scored nothing under 2 block noise units (2.05 MW). A weak result for the offset model here reflects D8's large noise unit and small transfers, and does not bear on the long, deep transfers of experiment 734.
    • The offset model was tuned on these years, in six ways. Experiment 734 tunes on the years before 2024-01-01 (TUNE_BLOCKS is the number of blocks up to its scored years), and all 8 test months precede 2024-01-01, with 6 of them inside experiment 734's record from 2022. The months touch the priors, the time constant tau, the noise units, the block scales, and the shared-term loadings and switches, though none of the injected transfers has been seen. The step heads never trained on those months. This tuning favours the offset model, and a win by the offset model carries that caveat.
    • The step head's validation overlay was also the networks' early-stopping set. The recalibration report states this caveat, and the head's frozen threshold is therefore tuned on rows that the networks also stopped on.
    • The two detectors read the same signal, because Design A could add the engine and solar arrays. Experiment 732's test segments add simulated engine or solar output in most of the 64 segments, which the head reads. Had Design A been unable to add the arrays, only the transfers would have distinguished the worlds. A secondary row scores the counted transfers that sit in segments with neither engine nor solar output, where both detectors read the same signal, and reports how many such transfers there are (the reviewer's count is 2 of the 21).
    • The counted contrast has a half-hour offset to get right. On the valid half-hours of segments with neither engine nor solar output, D8's value in the offset model's panel equals the reading in points times 0.10379 MW only when the panel index is the bank half-hour plus 1, so a builder that writes the additions at the bank's half-hour puts every transfer 30 minutes early. The world builder uses the +1 index, G2 asserts it, and the known-answer test of the match runs through it.
    • Both detectors read the future. See "Look-ahead".
    • Twenty-one transfers are few. The counted contrast resolves a difference of shares only to about 0.25, so the plan expects a verdict of not supported or inconclusive. A cell with fewer than 20 transfers after the drops for gaps gives inconclusive.
    • The head's side was not blind. The recalibration test rows (recall 0.490 and F1 0.531 for U-noBSP, at a tolerance of 1 half-hour on all 8 months) were seen before this plan, and the head's number below was computed before the plan was frozen. Only the offset model's side is predicted.

    A step head's calls become event calls by the start edge alone, and found is the same for both detectors. A head call is a half-hour at which the mean of the three seeds' step probability, after the frozen isotonic map, reaches the frozen F1-rule threshold. Calls of the same sign less than 2 hours apart merge into one call, and the sign of the call is the sign of the step head's size output (positive when D8 gains load).

    An offset-model call is the start of an event in its event table. The event table is the H-nbr (or H-own) table with its sign, which is the sign of the event's amp_mw read as positive when D8 gains load (the code review checked the convention against experiment 734's table). A transfer is found when a call of the same sign lies within 6 hours of its injected start, and one call matches at most one transfer, with the larger transfer assigned first. The step head's predicted size and the offset model's amp_mw do not enter the match. A match to an injected end edge counts as a match for V1's false-call count, so a call near either injected edge is not false.

    The secondary rule also needs an end call. The head needs an opposite-sign call within 6 hours of the injected end. The offset model needs an event whose end lies within 6 hours of the injected end. With about 0.155 calls a day, a chance match to a given start is about 0.04.

    Both detectors are restricted to D8 and to scored base days for the false-call count. The day count for V1 is the same for both detectors: 185 base days per draw times 8 draws, which is 1,480 draw-days in the 6 months that lie inside experiment 653's record, for the head's calls and for the offset model's D8 events alike. Real D8 events in the base days count as false calls for both detectors. The false-call count compares each call with the injected edges of all 288 transfers, including the transfers that the drops for gaps remove from the share found.

    Veto V1 excuses a call near an injected edge of any size, and both detectors are treated alike. A call within 6 hours of an injected edge of the right sign is not false, even when that transfer moves only 0.3 MW, which neither detector can see. Both detectors gain the same excuse, so each detector's false-call rate is lower than its rate on unexcused calls, and V1 tests the ratio of the two rates.

    Predictions, gates, and the placebo

    The numbered predictions are in the front matter, and four gates must pass before any verdict counts.

    • G1, the head's rows reproduce. Re-scoring the stored test predictions with the frozen thresholds, on all 8 months and with the recalibration report's one-half-hour match, gives U-noBSP's 304 calls and a recall of 0.490 on 4 step noise units and more. The same script then reproduces the 12 of 21 above under this plan's match rule.
    • G2, the injection is exact. In every injected half-hour with a reading, the added megawatts equal the transfer's mean level, from Transfer.mean_levels() in 64-bit floats, times 0.10379 MW per point, to a relative tolerance of 1e-6 (experiment 732's overlay arrays t and mirror are 32-bit floats, so their relative error is about 6e-8). D8 is an MVA Primary in experiment 734 (is_mva is true), so the offset model's panel gets the addition |p + add| of apply_additions, and the head's reading gets experiment 732's refold with the reactive floor, which moves the reading by about the transfer times x/r rather than the transfer itself. G2 checks each of the two against its own rule, and checks that every mirrored partner moves by minus the jitter factor times the transfer. In the transfers-only worlds, G2 also checks that D8 has no addition outside the injected half-hours. G2 also asserts the +1 index of the half-hour offset above.
    • G3, the decode reproduces. The decode of the 15-Primary panel with no addition gives experiment 734's stored un-injected H-nbr event table (uninjected/H-nbr_events.parquet, 131 events at 12 Primaries, 28 of them at D8), exactly on the primary, start, and end of every event, and to a relative tolerance of 1e-6 on amp_mw and confidence, with an equal grade, compared over every Primary in the table. The decode also stops when the compiled Viterbi kernel of experiment 734 was not the one used.
    • G4, the overlays reproduce. The regenerated test overlays reproduce experiment 732's digest 4c57546a9278dc0d0a6e3b2803d880b3205ae441dea7725681ab168000c30c6f and its 384 transfers and 768 edge rows over the 8 months.

    A placebo moves every injected start 7 days later, onto time where no transfer was injected, and scores each detector's calls against the moved starts. The placebo gives the share of starts that each detector matches by chance, and P4 predicts it. Starts whose moved time lands inside a transfer, within 6 hours of any real start or end edge of the segment (the edges of transfers that the drops for gaps remove count too), off a scored half-hour, across a month end, or in a gap are dropped from the placebo, and the page reports how many starts remain. The one-to-one match of the placebo still draws on the moved starts of every scored transfer, dropped starts included, so a dropped start can still take a call away from a kept start of the same sign.

    Synthetic world gallery

    The gallery shows only synthetic quantities. D8's readings in MW are private, and no figure shows a metered series, a date, or a real residual. The plan has 19 figures before the first decode, which is 1 histogram figure, 9 injected additions, and 9 detector rows.

    • The injected transfers. Histograms of the 288 durations and sizes, with the 24-hour and 4-step-noise-unit lines, and the counts of the table above (1 figure).
    • Nine injected additions in MW, one per duration and shape cell. Constant, demand-shaped, and solar-shaped additions (3 shapes) at about 6 hours, 30 hours, and 90 hours (3 durations), each drawn alone with its mirror at the partner, on an axis of hours from the start with no date (9 figures).
    • Nine detector rows. For the same nine transfers, the head's step probability and the offset model's posterior of the switched power, in noise units, each against the injected truth. The decoded offset is shown on an axis shifted to zero, so no D8 level is visible (9 figures).

    The smoke check on the data files asserts that the injected addition is nonzero in every half-hour of every transfer (the failure that silenced one earlier run's solar output in 37.5% of its test rows, in experiment 729), that every read partner carries a nonzero mirror, and that all 288 transfers are present in the 8 worlds, and that in every segment with an engine or solar array the array is nonzero at the half-hours where it is written into D8's addition (the check reports a segment's index and never a value). The write-up repeats the gallery with the results added.

    Look-ahead

    Neither detector forecasts, and both read the future of the event, so the comparison is a hindsight comparison. The step head reads a 48-hour window that holds the 24 hours before and the 24 hours after the half-hour it scores: D8's folded reading, the sky inputs of the simulated segment, the BSP meter, and the three neighbours' readings. The offset model reads the whole record from 2022 to mid-2026. Its forward-backward decode uses blocks before and after every block, and its residual uses ERA5 (the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts' reanalysis of past weather) and CAMS (the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service's estimates of past solar radiation) and a demand forecast that uses no lagged power, as experiment 734 defines. No input of either detector is a weather forecast at a forecast origin. The result bears on whether the offset model can label transfers in a historical record, and not on whether either detector could run in real time.

    Stages and compute

    The compute slot is 1 core and about 2.4 CPU-hours, and the estimate is uncertain. Experiment 734's time log gives a median of 27.6 wall-minutes for each of its 40 score-default jobs and 4.1 wall-minutes for an uninjected full-record decode of one arm. The plan's 15 CPU-minutes per world with H-own and H-nbr is an estimate from those figures and from about 12 CPU-minutes for each world's H arms, and is not a measurement. If one decode of one arm takes 27.6 minutes, the 16 decodes cost about 7.4 CPU-hours (16 decodes of 27.6 minutes), which is the worst case. The manager set the stop at 8 CPU-hours, so the worst case stays below it. The first world's measured time replaces the estimate, and the plan asks the manager before passing the 8 CPU-hour stop. The runner also stops when the finished decode tasks project more than 8 CPU-hours.

    Run order and reviews

    The experiment runs in this order, and no stage runs before its review marker exists.

    1. A fresh Sonnet agent that did not write the plan reviews the plan, and a fresh Sonnet agent that did not write the code reviews the code (leakage, the match rule, the +1 index, the sign convention, and a match with the recipe the code copies). A second fresh Sonnet agent reviews the fixed code if the first review flags many problems.
    2. After the code review, a person or the manager runs review-mark, which writes the hash of the reviewed code (the scripts, run.sh, and run_workers.sh) into review_stage1.ok. Every stage from world onward refuses to start unless the marker holds the current code hash, and freeze-code refuses unless the code hash equals the marker's content. Stage 0 needs no marker. A done marker of a decode task holds the code hash too, and a re-run ignores a marker of other code.
    3. A real-environment smoke test runs stage 0 and one world's decode into a scratch folder (.claude/scratch/) that E00736_OUT names, with nothing written to the experiment's data folder (the runner refuses E00736_OUT unless E00736_SCRATCH=1 is set or the path has a scratch component, prints the output folder at the start of every stage, and stores it in scores.json). The smoke test has no validation mode, because this design has test rows only, and none of its numbers is used to tune anything: every parameter is frozen before it runs. The test reads the real input files and takes the world's call tables through the scoring code.
    4. The runs happen inside systemd-run --user --scope with a memory cap of 4 GB and 1 thread for each numerical library, after the ML experiment manager approves the workstation run.
    5. A Sonnet agent checks the write-up against the printed report, and a second Sonnet agent runs the prose review.

    Standing practices, advice, and scenarios

    • Followed: cleaned data with the substation_zero half-hours dropped (the count is reported); stored rows reused as benchmark and as the head's outputs; a synthetic world gallery and a smoke check; a per-phase time log; no stage past its review gate; the hash of every edited reviewed script recorded; mid-day onsets in the known-answer test of the match (a transfer starting at 13:20 and one at 00:10, built through the +1 index); a placebo beside the contrast.
    • Not applicable: monthly refits, model saving, training-loss plots, and screening folds, because nothing is trained; tail coverage of a forecast, because no forecast is scored; the D15 and D18 cut, because D8 is not one of them.
    • The four load-shift scenarios do not apply, because no forecast is scored. The experiment scores detection of injected transfers, and the share found is the headline.
    • Advice followed: use U-noBSP, the head without the BSP input, and ask for neighbour mirrors only where they can be read (experiment 732's advice items on the BSP input and the neighbour mirror); compare with a stored reference that the same rows produced and read a cell only with 20 events or more (experiment 734's rule, from experiment 665; the counted cell holds 21, and fewer than 20 after drops gives inconclusive); report shares found and shares paired separately (experiment 653, hence the secondary rule); expect a test of one extra input at one Primary to resolve only a coarse gain, and plan the threshold above that (experiment 733's advice on the half-width of its confidence interval for a change at one Primary, which this page's C1 is not).
    • Advice departed from: experiment 734's tolerance of 12 hours and its intersection-over-union rule, which become 6 hours and a start-only match here, because the head has no intervals. This experiment's reading at a tolerance of 12 hours is reported as a post hoc row, and labelled so.

    Open questions before the first run

    • D8's residual series. Whether the real D8 series of experiment 653 on the 6 months equals the folded reading that experiment 732 used (the code review checked that both are MVA and equal to rounding).
    • Whether the world builder can add the engine and solar arrays. The code review settled it before any decode, and the "Every handicap" list was updated to match.
    • Gaps in the residual. Transfers whose start block has no residual at D8 are dropped from both detectors, and the count is reported, together with the discordant counts per month.

    Method

    Architecture

    • The step head is the U-net of experiment 732 with three seeds, read from stored predictions (predictions/<arm>_s<k>/test_transfer.npz), with no network run and none retrained.
    • The offset model is experiment 734's hidden Markov model on a grid of 41 offset levels, with slow states, a shared term across Primaries, and (H-nbr) neighbour evidence. Tuned values come from experiment 734's params.json, read unchanged.
    • The residual is observed cleaned power minus the forecast of the lag-free convex demand model of experiment 130, as experiment 734 builds it; blocks are 6-hour means.

    Training regime

    • Nothing is trained or tuned. The injected transfers are those of experiment 732's test bank, re-drawn by its own overlay code (the same seed and segments), so the sizes, starts, shapes, and partners are experiment 732's.
    • Each of the 8 worlds merges one draw (draw 0 to draw 7) of every one of the 6 months. A world is D8's power plus the segment's synthetic engine and solar output plus the draw's additions, with the partners' power plus the mirrors, all written at the +1 half-hour index.

    Measurement

    • The scored rows are the 288 transfers of the 6 months, and the counted cell is the 21 of 24 to 96 hours and 4 step noise units or more.
    • The metric is the share of injected starts matched, by the rule above. The secondary rows are the other duration bands, the both-ends rule, the counted transfers in segments with neither engine nor solar output, the shifted-start placebo, and false edge calls per 100 scored base days.
    • The page reports the table of paired counts (found by both, only by U-noBSP, only by H-nbr, by neither), the exact sign test on the discordant transfers, and the discordant counts per month. Intervals resample the 8 draws and the 6 months, 10,000 draws each. Every number before the final scoring is interim.
    • One step noise unit is 3.42 points (0.355 MW), so 4 step noise units are 13.67 points, or 1.42 MW. One point is one unit of the folded reading (0.10379 MW). One block noise unit at D8 is 1.02 MW. The jitter factor is the factor by which a partner's mirror differs from the transfer (1.0 for an exact mirror). The page writes "step noise unit" and "block noise unit" in full, because the two units differ.
