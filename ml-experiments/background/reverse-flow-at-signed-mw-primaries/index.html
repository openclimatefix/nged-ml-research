    Reverse flow at the signed-MW Primaries

    Only D15 has reverse flow as its usual state, and D10 has small midday reverse flow on below 1% of half-hours. Reverse flow means a half-hour in which the signed active-power reading, in megawatts (MW), is below zero, so that the Primary substation exports power to the grid above it. This page records, for the eight Primaries that National Grid Electricity Distribution (NGED) meters in signed MW, how often and how deeply reverse flow occurs, and what that means for training a detector that recovers the sign of a reading that has been folded into a magnitude. The Primaries metered only in megavolt-amperes (MVA), the unit of apparent power, are covered by experiment 79 and experiment 110, and the units are explained on the NGED power telemetry page.

    What was counted

    • The eight signed-MW Primaries are D5, D9, D10, D11, D14, D15, D16, and D18. The count uses the cleaned power store, every row, with hours in local time on the start of each half-hour (exploration on 2026-11-25; 8 Primaries; provisional).
    • The numbers come from a one-off, read-only exploration of the cleaned power store, with each Primary's capacity taken from the series table of experiment 103. No script or table in the repository reproduces them.
    • Depth is in points of capacity. A depth of 10 points is 10% of the Primary's effective capacity, which is the 99th percentile of the series' absolute power (what is on disk).
    • A folded reading is the magnitude of the apparent power, in MVA, and is never negative. Signed power is in MW. A reverse-flow half-hour is ambiguous after folding when its absolute signed power is below 1 MVA, or below 0.3 MVA, because a small folded reading looks the same as a small forward flow.
    • The drivers named below, such as solar photovoltaic (PV) generation, are inferences from the time of day and the season. No generator is identified.

    Primaries with no reverse flow

    • D9, D11, D14, and D16 never read below zero. Their lowest readings are above zero, so a folded reading and a signed reading are the same number at these Primaries (exploration on 2026-11-25; 4 Primaries; provisional).
    • D11 and D16 show a midday dip that does not reach zero, consistent with solar PV. At D16, the median midday reading in 2026 is below the median night reading. The solar PV cause is an inference from the daily profile alone.

    D15: reverse flow as the usual state

    • D15 is in reverse flow on 76% of half-hours, in a profile that is flat through the day. The share is 69% to 83% in every hour except 16:00 to 21:00, where it falls to 58% to 74%. A flat all-day profile is consistent with base-load generation and not with solar PV, which is an inference.
    • Reverse flow at D15 is deep and persistent. It is deeper than at every other Primary, and it continues for long runs of consecutive half-hours.
    • The share is 87% in 2025 and 69% in 2026. The series starts in 2025, so both years are short, and the 2025 figure covers only part of a year.
    • Folding makes D15 badly ambiguous. More than half of its reverse-flow half-hours, 55%, read below 1 MVA, and 12% read below 0.3 MVA.

    D10: small midday reverse flow

    • D10's reverse flow is 0.26% of half-hours and sits between 10:00 and 15:00, peaking at 13:00, from March to August. The pattern is consistent with solar PV.
    • Reverse flow at D10 is shallow, with one outlier. The median depth is 3.2 points and the 99th percentile is 14.3 points. One isolated reading reaches 94 points, which we infer is probably a switching event.
    • Folding hides nearly all of it. Of D10's 317 reverse-flow half-hours, 315 read below 1 MVA.

    D5 and D18: a handful of half-hours

    • D5 has 19 reverse-flow half-hours since the series began, all between 10:00 and 15:00. They fall in spring and late summer, with a depth of at most 8.6 points, so they are consistent with solar PV on days of low demand.
    • D18 has 48 reverse-flow half-hours, all in a short episode in 2025, and none in 2026. The episode spans every hour of the day, so the readings do not match a solar profile, which is an inference. The cause is not identified.

    What this means for training a sign detector

    • Few real reverse-flow examples exist outside D15. D10, D5, and D18 together hold 384 reverse-flow half-hours, of which 98% (375) sit under 1 MVA, where the folded reading carries almost no signal about the sign.
    • D15 is the one Primary with enough reverse-flow half-hours to train or test on, and it supplies one regime. That regime is deep, all-day reverse flow, consistent with base-load generation. D15 has a short history, so any split by time leaves few weeks on each side.
    • The reverse-flow regimes are separate: D15 shows all-day reverse flow, and D10 and D5 show only midday reverse flow. A detector validated on D15 or D10 says little about a Primary with both patterns.
    • Real examples are too few to score a detector, so evidence must come from synthetic reverse flow or from other sources. Experiment 79 found no reverse flow at 7 of the 8 MVA-only Primaries, and experiment 110 fitted a power factor only where reverse flow reached at least 0.5% of training rows. The signed-MW Primaries add D15 and, weakly, D10 to the Primaries with usable reverse flow.
