    Can the axial-attention demand network learn the sign of a Primary's power flow from a magnitude reading, when the sign of two signed Primaries is hidden and a real solar farm's output is added to them?

    We ask whether a forecasting neural network can learn the direction of power flow through a Primary substation whose meter reports only a magnitude. A Primary substation steps the voltage down to 11 kV. Eight of the 16 Primaries report apparent power in megavolt-amperes (MVA), a magnitude with no sign, so a Primary that exports solar output shows a rise in its reading and not a negative value. We test the idea where the answer is known. We hide the sign of two Primaries that do report a signed value in megawatts (MW), add the recorded output of a real solar farm to each so that they export on sunny middays, and compare a network that has a sign-inference component with the same network without the component. The score is the signed forecast error on the half-hours when the true flow is an export. Every score is a ceiling, because the history rows read recorded weather that a live forecast would not have. The page was a plan before any fit. The section "Result of the first wave" records the first wave, which stopped at the pre-registered harm stop.

    Result of the first wave

    Verdict row 0 applies: the first wave stopped at the pre-registered harm stop, so the experiment has no verdict. Every number in this section is interim and post hoc, because each comes from one fold (2026-05), one world (W2), and seed 0. No counted reading (G1, G2, or G3) was read. The front-matter verdict of inconclusive stands in for row 0 only because the page schema has no value for "stopped, no verdict". The harm stop is a pre-registered limit of 0.20 points of capacity on the import rows, read at the quick look. This page does not revisit that limit.

    On import rows, the point estimates put both signed arms worse than P0 at both hidden Primaries. Import rows are the scored half-hours whose signed truth is at or above zero. The quick look found SG-A minus P0 of +0.426 points at D5 (8,072 rows) and +1.245 points at D10 (8,818 rows), Both point estimates exceed the stop of 0.20. A positive difference is harm. Pooled means the import rows of D5 and D10 scored together. A re-score read the same stored forecasts a second time, on the CPU, with the unchanged scoring code, and reproduced the quick look's numbers before adding intervals.

    The control without a sign head is also worse than P0, which points to the shared signed read-out more than to the sign head. SG-0 minus P0 is +0.492 at D5 and +0.952 at D10, and SG-A minus SG-0 is +0.121 points pooled (raw interval [−0.020, +0.243]). Both signed arms read the sign from the network's output alone (the recipe of experiment 420). On this fold and seed, SG-0 carries 0.732 of the pooled 0.854 points, so the shared read-out is the likelier cause. No arm varies the read-out alone, so the cause is untested.

    The harm sits in the daylight hours of the target time and grows with lead at both Primaries. Pooled SG-A minus P0 on import rows is +4.5 to +6.8 points at 09 to 13 UTC and within ±0.8 points at 18 to 07 UTC. The hour split carries a point estimate and a row count and no interval. By lead band from the 12:00 UTC origin, the difference at D10 is +0.84 (24 to 48 h), +1.10 (48 to 120 h), and +1.42 points (120 to 240 h). At D5 the difference is +0.27, +0.34, and +0.51 points.

    Both signed arms gained 18.5 to 19.7 points on reverse-flow rows. SG-A minus P0 is −19.73 points and SG-0 minus P0 is −18.53 points on 5,345 rows. SG-A minus SG-0 is −1.19 points. Over all 22,235 rows the differences are −4.09 for SG-A and −3.90 for SG-0. Experiment 420 found that its ungated reference scored as well as its gated arm on reverse rows (6.99 against 6.98). An arm that can go below zero therefore beats P0 on reverse-flow rows. This gain does not count towards G1.

    Stage 0 passed, the synthetic world was fitted and not scored, and no later wave ran. The flag test passed, the noise check required 0.0970 against a margin of 0.10, and reachability gave an area of 0.526 against a bound of 0.776. Wave 1 had no scoring step for the synthetic world, and no counted reading was computed. The five wave 1 fits took about 1.2 GPU-process-hours of fit time (1.3 hours of wall time), plus 0.2 for the code-works check.

    The splits by lead band and valid hour were chosen after the harm stop and feed no counted reading. The splits are descriptive. Any later plan would need to pre-register what it reads from the splits.

    Rung 1 is the second rung of a ladder that starts with experiment 720. Rung 0 (experiment 720) tests, on the CPU and without a neural network, whether the sign can be recovered in hindsight. Rung 1 (this experiment) asks the same question inside the forecasting network that the experiments 713 to 719 build. Rung 2 would repeat the test on more Primaries and score the eight MVA-only Primaries against the stored network. This page covers rung 1 only.

    The terms below are used on the whole page.

    • MSA network and M*: the axial-attention demand network of experiment 713, in M1, the arm of experiment 713 that was selected as the reference network. The page uses P0, S, SG-0, SG-A, and the other arm names as the arm table below defines them.
    • F4, F7, and F9: the follow-up experiments on the MSA network that share its GPU queue. F4 adds neighbouring substations' rows to the network (experiment 715). F7 adds an estimate of the unmetered photovoltaic (PV) power in each history row (experiments 716 and 717). F9 adds similar days from the past (experiment 718 and its second part).
    • HT: F7's arm that gives the network the PV estimate p of the unmetered solar power in the history rows and the target-row PV forecast p_0.
    • The Candidate: the class 2 Candidate demand network of experiment 667, whose weather columns and B1 columns (14 trailing-temperature and solar-gain columns) the MSA network reuses.
    • MDE: the minimum detectable effect, the smallest gain that a reading can tell from noise.
    • q01 coverage: the share of true values that fall below the forecast's 1% quantile. A reliable forecast scores 0.01.
    • The six signed-MW Primaries: D5, D9, D10, D11, D14, and D16.
    • The eight MVA-only Primaries: D2, D3, D4, D8, D12, D19, D21, and D22.
    • The hidden pair: D5 and D10, the two signed-MW Primaries whose sign is hidden in rung 1 (rotation r1 of experiment 420). D10 holds real reverse flow on 0.76% of its half-hours, and D5 is the Primary at which three earlier fits invented reverse flow. The other four signed-MW Primaries (D9, D11, D14, and D16) are the visible Primaries.
    • Folded reading: the magnitude sqrt(X² + Q²) of the signed power X, with a reactive floor Q of 2 points. A point is 1% of the Primary's capacity. "Hidden" means that the network sees only the folded reading.
    • Reverse-flow rows: the scored half-hours of the hidden pair on which the signed truth is below zero, injected or real, as in experiment 420.
    • Injection level 2.0: a real solar farm's recorded output, added at twice the host's median absolute reading on sunny summer middays before 2025-07-01. The farm's series is a private input and appears on no page.
    • Worlds W2 and W0: W2 folds the hidden pair after the injection. W0 folds the same pair with no injection, and is the placebo that must show no invented reverse flow. Both worlds carry the same injection of the two other farms' output at the four visible Primaries, in the readings of every arm. The visible Primaries D11 and D16 carry the same farms as the hidden pair D5 and D10 (by experiment 506's farm map), so the sign head learns the sign of farms whose output it later reads at the hidden pair. A hidden-sign result is therefore a ceiling on what a sign head learns where no labelled Primary shares a farm with the hidden pair.
    • Export gate: the per-Primary switch of experiment 420, set once per refit from the readings, that allows a Primary to export at all.
    • Sign posterior ρ: the probability that a history token exported, from the gross-demand read-out or from the learned head.
    • Signed CRPS: the continuous ranked probability score of the 11 signed quantiles against the signed truth, in points of capacity. A score below another score is better.

    The training budget, checked against the F4 audit's finding C1

    The synthetic worlds train at up to 400 passes with a patience of 30, and not at the 150 passes and patience 8 that failed the F4 gate. The F4 audit's finding C1 is that the known-answer harness of experiment 715 used experiment 713's budget from before its amendment 3. The W3 fits then stopped at passes 42 to 54 while the validation loss was still falling by about 0.05 every 10 passes. On experiment 713's synthetic world W0, 713's arm M needed 350 passes, and under 715's cap the same pair of fits stopped at pass 143 and at the cap of 150. Experiment 713's amendment 3 raised the synthetic budget to 400 and 30 for this reason, and experiment 716's arm M of its known-answer worlds uses the same pair.

    Fits Passes Patience Why
    Synthetic world of rung 1 (stage 1 only) At most 400 30 A hard stop gate must not fail for a harness reason, as the F4 gate did
    W2 and W0 real folds Experiment 713's real-fold budget: at most 60 passes per stage, raised to 120 passes for a stage that hits the cap of 60 8 Experiment 713's budget is the recipe that decision 2 keeps; the fit prints its stopping pass

    The real folds keep experiment 713's budget for the scored checkpoint, and a measurement of the learning that the budget cut off guards G1. Experiment 716's known-answer gate (F7's KF9, in synthetic worlds) shows why. Its arm H trained 60 passes with no early stopping, and patience 8, applied afterwards, picked passes 4, 23, and 5 of the three seeds, where the best of the 60 passes were 43, 55, and 32. The audit of that gate found the PV history path still learning at pass 60. Every SG arm uses the same path, so G1 is read under a budget that is known to under-train the history path. No counted reading compares a rung 1 arm with M* or with F7, so the budget does not need to match theirs for G1 to G3. A rule that reads the shape of the patience-8 curve alone cannot tell a plateau from a slow fall, because the truncated curve does not hold the information. The guard therefore continues training and measures the lost learning directly.

    Stage 0 states which stage trains the sign head, before any flag is computed. The design note puts the hidden pair's folding in stage 1 and fixes no stage for the sign head, and experiment 716 trains F7's gains a and c at the stage 2 rate. The page pre-registers the default that the sign head trains in stage 1, with the trunk, because the head reads trunk tokens and its cross-entropy loss is added to the stage 1 loss. The first Opus code review reads the code and records the stage, and one of two guards then applies.

    • Branch A, the sign head trains in stage 1: the combined flag on stage 1. The scored checkpoint of every score and contrast is the one at the patience-8 stop, as in experiment 713 (decision 2). In the same run, stage 1 of an SG-A fit in W2 keeps training after the patience-8 stop until patience 30 ends it, with a cap of 150 passes in total, and the run logs the patience-8 best and the patience-30 best validation loss. The gap is the patience-8 best minus the patience-30 best. The noise is the sample standard deviation of the validation loss over the last 10 passes of the patience-8 run (all the passes it ran, when it ran fewer than 10). The noise measure is the page's own. The stage's descent is its first-pass validation loss minus the patience-30 best. Stage 1 is flagged stopped while falling when the gap exceeds 0.5 times the noise and is at least 5% of the descent. Experiment 713's stored stage-1 curves, continued as flat plateaus with their own post-best noise, give a modelled false-flag rate of about 0.7% per stage-1 stage, so about 5.5% across the eight stage-1 tests of SG-A in W2 (four folds, two seeds). An SG-A fit is flagged when its stage 1 is flagged, and either seed's flag sets the flag. Stages 2 and 3 are not read by any verdict row. The gains a and c, which experiment 716 trains at the stage 2 rate, are therefore unguarded, and their learned gains are reported and carry no bar. SG-0 carries the same HT inputs, a, and c at the same budget, so any under-training of them is shared by both arms of G1 and does not favour SG-A.
    • Branch B, the sign head trains in stage 2 or 3: a direct budget check in the quick look. No loss rule has a tolerable false-flag rate on stages 2 and 3, because those stages start near their optimum and their descents are tiny. The quick look instead fits SG-A, and SG-0, a second time on fold 2026-05 at seed 0, with patience 30 and a cap of 150 passes in the stage that trains the sign head. Every later stage is refitted from that stage's patience-30 state, so each of the two extra fits has a G1 point estimate of its own. The quick look compares the two budgets on the held-out folded loss of the hidden pair and on the two interim G1 point estimates. If those two estimates differ by more than 0.20 points, decision 2 returns to the maintainer before the rest of W2 runs, and the result is row 0 if the maintainer stops the line. The 0.20 is a constant fixed here before any data, and it is not derived from the MDE, which is written later. This reading is interim and carries no bar, so it adds no look at the verdict. The two extra fits cost about 0.5 to 0.8 GPU-process-hours. Rows 7 to 9 then read no flag, and the verdict rows treat every SG-A fit as unflagged.

    Both branches keep the scored chain untouched. Experiment 713's train_stage draws each pass's order from the seed, the stage, and the epoch, and holds a constant rate after warm-up, so passes after the patience-8 stop do not change the passes before it. The patience-8 best state is the state that is saved, scored, and passed to the next stage, and the continuation's better state is saved apart and never passed on. SG-A and SG-0 then start stage 2 from a stage 1 of the same budget, and G1 compares heads and not budgets. The code-works check asserts that the scored SG-A state equals the state of a run with no continuation. The rerun at 120 passes and the continuation are one run, so a stage that hit the first cap is not trained twice.

    • The validation loss. The validation loss that early stopping and the flag read is the folded loss at the hidden pair of every SG arm and of P0, computed against the folded reading and never against the signed truth, so that no checkpoint is selected on the hidden sign. Experiment 713's validation split (lead days 1, 2, 4, 7, 10, and 14 before the cutoff, with the stage embargo) is inherited unchanged.
    • The test in Stage 0. The flag is run on every stored learning curve of experiments 713, 715, and 716 that runs past its patience-8 stop, before any fit. The test requires the combined flag to fire on experiment 716's arm H in world WH at seeds 0, 1, and 2 and on its arm HT in world WT at seed 0, and not to fire on its arms in world WN at seeds 0 and 2. A second test continues each of 713's stored real-fold stage-1 curves as a flat plateau at its own post-best mean, with Gaussian noise of its own post-best spread, and requires a false-flag rate of 2% or less per stage. Both tests can fail, and a failure stops the line before any fit with no verdict (row 0). A code fix that changes no constant may rerun the test once, and both outputs are kept. Every other stored curve is printed with its flag for the write-up.
    • Where the constants came from. The constants 0.5, 0.05, and the 2% ceiling were chosen on the same stored curves that the test above then checks, so the test checks the code and is not independent evidence for the rule. Its margins are thin. Arm HT in world WT at seed 0 fires at a share of descent of 0.061 against 0.05. Arm H in world WN at seed 1 sits at 0.048 and 0.66 noise units, just under both thresholds. The plan leaves it out of the quiet set and records no reason for doing so, so the quiet set is chosen after the fact, and the test does not cover that curve. The known-answer curves are single-stage fits that train the HT gains with the trunk, so they stand in for stage 1 only if a and c train there. Every fit's stopping pass per stage, its flag, and the learned gain of each new path as a share of its reachable range are printed and appear in the write-up.

    The continuation adds GPU time, which the projection counts. In the 160 stored real-fold fits of experiment 713, a stage ran 12.1 s per pass in stage 1, 4.0 s in stage 2, and 3.9 s in stage 3, and a whole fit took 641 s on average (0.178 GPU-process-hours; the median is 567 s). A stage-1 continuation from a converged stop needs 22 more passes (30 minus 8), which is 266 s, or 0.07 GPU-process-hours, for each of the 8 SG-A fits in W2: 0.6 in all. A stage that is still learning keeps resetting patience and runs to the cap of 150 passes. For a stage-1 stop at the mean of 38 passes, that is 112 extra passes, or 0.38 GPU-process-hours per fit and 3.0 for the eight. A continuation of all three stages would cost 0.68 per fit and 5.4 for the eight, which is why the continuation runs only in the stage that Stage 0 fixes.

    The floor of 0.6 is enforced between fits, because the session cannot pause a running fit to ask the manager. A started continuation runs until patience 30 or the cap of 150 passes ends it. Before each SG-A fit in W2 starts, the session adds up the continuation time of the fits that have finished. When that sum has reached 0.6 GPU-process-hours, the line stops at that fit, and the session asks the manager before the next fit starts. A fit whose continuation is running when the sum passes the floor finishes, and the manager is told afterwards. The worst case before the manager is asked is about 0.98 GPU-process-hours of continuation (the floor plus one running continuation), a total of about 14.0. If the manager allows further SG-A fits without the continuation, each of those fits counts as flagged in rows 7 to 9, fixed here before data, because its stage cannot be shown to have converged.     Counted readings and verdict rows

    The three counted readings are G1, G2, and G3, and G4 is a report. The decision rule in the front matter states each bar. Each counted reading is read at 98.33% from a block bootstrap and reads seeds 0 and 1 together. In words:

    • The bootstrap. The two-stage resampler is written in e00721_score.py. studies.bootstrap.bootstrap_difference_at_level draws its units uniformly from one flat list keyed by its month column, so relabelling month with a fold-and-block identifier would give a flat draw over fold-blocks, which is about 0.77 times as wide as the two-stage draw on the seed-pair null (decision 8). No counted reading uses the function, and no file under src/studies/ changes. Each resample draws one seed, then draws folds with replacement, then draws blocks of 10 consecutive valid dates with replacement within each drawn fold, across both hidden Primaries. The folds are separate fits whose blocks share an offset, and a block of origins overlaps the next block in valid time, so blocks on the valid date are the unit on which the pairing already runs. The interval describes a one-seed contrast and is conservative for the point estimate, which is the mean over the two seeds. Before the reverse-flow restriction, the three sunny folds of W2 fall in about 11 blocks, and W0 falls in 15 (4 per fold, and 3 in fold 2026-06 because valid times stop before 2026-07-01), with a Kish effective number of 13.8. Both counts are under the minimum of 20 resampling units that the page requires, so each interval is widened about the point estimate. The first factor is the ratio of the Student t quantile to the normal quantile at 98.33%, with B - 1 degrees of freedom and B the Kish effective number of blocks, because the reverse-flow rows gather in the sunny blocks: about 1.22 at a Kish B of 10.1 (the 11 blocks of W2) and 1.15 at a Kish B of 13.8 (the 15 blocks of W0). The second factor is the square root of B / (B - 1), about 1.05 at 10.1 and 1.04 at 13.8. The two factors together are about 1.29 in W2 and 1.19 in W0, and both are planning figures, because the counted readings use the Kish number of their own reverse-flow rows. The folds are not counted again as degrees of freedom, because the two-stage resampler already carries the fold-level clustering, and the page applies no fold-count correction on top of the two-stage draw. On the seed-pair null such a correction would change the interval by about 3%. Each reading uses 20,000 resamples and the generator seed 721, and the power check of G3 runs once with them, because the 98.33% tails of the resample move by up to 0.1 points at D5 between runs of 4,000 resamples. The write-up reports the Kish number of the reverse-flow rows, and states beside every interval that the June valid dates of folds 2026-05 and 2026-06 share weather outcomes across two fits.     The target row reads only the ensemble forecast issued at or before the forecast origin, and a test asserts the rule row by row.     The margin of the synthetic world is rung 0b's own bar in its own unit, with four parts. Rung 0b's pre-registered margin is 0.10 in area under the receiver operating characteristic curve, with the real sky above the shifted sky (experiment 720's decision rule, row V1 of addendum 1, and V1_AUC_GAP in its code). Rung 1 reuses that bar for the sign posterior ρ: the area for SG-A minus the area for SG-A-S is at least 0.10, on distinct history tokens of the two exporting synthetic Primaries whose real shortwave likelihood u is at least 0.3 and which lie outside the 2-point dead band, in each of the two seeds. Area does not depend on the share of exporting tokens, and the restriction to u of 0.3 or more removes the information that the placebo keeps through the likelihood gate. A margin in sign-accuracy units is not pre-registered, because the token count is unknown, each history token is scored several times, and the accuracy of the shifted-sky arm is expected to sit below 0.95. Two checks, a rule for a failed check, and the maintainer's confirmation are written before any synthetic fit.

    1. A reachability check. The synthetic gross demand is a noise-free function of calendar and temperature, and the SG arms keep the calendar and the folded reading in the tokens that feed the head. Given the demand exactly, the two candidate signs of a reading are separated wherever only one of them fits, so SG-A-S could read the sign with no sky at all. The check computes the area of a sky-blind rule that knows the demand exactly, on the margin's token set, and requires it to leave at least 0.10 plus 3 standard errors below 1. If the check fails, demand noise (a 5-point day offset, as experiment 720's harder worlds used) is added to the world before any fit and the check is run once more. Only when the noisy world also fails does the line stop (row 0).
    2. A correlated-difference standard error. The two areas are computed on the same tokens, so the standard error of their difference uses the correlated form of Hanley and McNeil (1983) or, once scores exist, of DeLong et al. (1988), and not the single-area formula of Hanley and McNeil (1982). Both correlated forms need a correlation between the two areas that no data give before a fit, so the check before any fit assumes a correlation of 0 (the independent form, which is conservative for positively correlated areas). It is computed with the class counts set to the numbers of distinct days that hold an export token and an import token, because tokens within a day share that day's sky, and at assumed areas of 0.95 and 0.85. The margin of 0.10 must be at least 3 standard errors.
    3. The outcome when a check fails. No synthetic fit runs until the maintainer amends the margin or the world, and the result is "stopped before any fit, no verdict" (row 0). A rerun of the noise check after the reachability check has added demand noise is not an amendment. An area computed on fewer than 30 tokens of either class (experiment 720's minimum) is not evaluable, and the result is also row 0, because the margin cannot be read.
    4. The maintainer's confirmation. The margin sets a threshold that a verdict row reads, so Gate 3 keeps it closed until the maintainer confirms the margin.

    Method

    Architecture

    • The network is the axial-attention demand network of experiment 713, with the 14-row by 48-column token grid of history rows and the target row, the three-component Gaussian mixture head, and the read-out of a weighted mean of history power added to the mixture means.
    • The gate-open Primaries read F7's HT inputs: p in each history token, p_0 in the target row, and per-Primary gains a and c that start at zero. The SG arms zero the shortwave column, the B1 solar-gain columns, and the solar-geometry columns of the tokens that feed the head, so the sky enters only through p, p_0, and ρ. Calendar and the time-of-day embedding stay.
    • The loss at a gate-open Primary is the folded likelihood, a log-sum-exp over 3 components and 2 signs, with the censored branch below Q. It folds about zero megawatts and not about zero in standardised units. The mixture means are G_out + head - c_i p_0, and the 11 folded quantiles come from the bisection of experiment 397. Each folded quantile goes through sqrt(q² + Q²) before the scorer.
    • SG-A adds the learned sign head of the section "Worlds, arms, and folds", and SG-0 fixes ρ at 0. P0 and S use the signed loss and no gate. S adds HT's inputs to P0.
    • No forecast uses information after its origin: the target-row weather is the ensemble run issued at or before the origin, and the check above asserts it.

    Training regime

    • Training data: the 16 Primaries, with the hidden pair folded in stage 1 too (stage 1 covers 2019-09 onwards, and the farm frame of experiment 39 runs from 2019-09-17 to 2026-09-10, with 87% to 96% of half-hours present per whole year). A half-hour is dropped when any farm has a gap, and the write-up reports the dropped share per fold. Cleaned data are used everywhere, and the noisy start of the short-history Primaries D15 and D18 before 2025-03-01 is cut.
    • Injection: the injected reading is built from a within-half-hour interpolation of the signed power (6 sub-steps, averaging sqrt(P'² + Q²)), so crossings read as D12's do. The injected capacity in p is the oracle capacity, as experiment 720's code does, with 0.7 and 1.3 times that capacity as the one sensitivity. The capacity of the hidden pair's own host PV in p comes from experiment 662's probe on the folded readings (section "Leakage and origin-time weather"). The injection code is experiment 720's, imported and not edited.
    • Fits: each arm fits from scratch on each world and fold, in three stages, with experiment 713's optimiser, schedule, and real-fold budget (patience 8, with a rerun at the larger cap). The synthetic fits run stage 1 only, at up to 400 passes with patience 30. Seeds 0 and 1 give the verdict (and seeds 2 to 4 run only after the futility test, for a secondary read). Folds are 2025-12, 2026-04, 2026-05, and 2026-06, refitted monthly.
    • GPU hours: about 13.6 GPU-process-hours for the plan under Branch A (64 fits at 0.2, the code-works check, and the continuation floor) and 13.5 to 13.8 under Branch B, against a cap of about 14, and about 12.2 at experiment 713's measured per-fit mean. The first wave is about 1.2 to 1.3 under Branch A and about 1.7 to 2.0 under Branch B.
    • Saved outputs: every fitted network's state file under the experiment's data folder, never in git, and per-row forecasts through experiment_store.write_predictions.

    Choices fixed in the code

    • Day offset: the synthetic world's 5-point day offset is the standard deviation of a per-day offset of the gross demand.
    • Censor rule: a reading within 1e-4 of Q takes the probability mass inside |X| <= Q, and every other reading takes the density of both signs with the Jacobian r / |X|.
    • Block counting: blocks of 10 consecutive valid dates are numbered from all of a fold's rows before the reverse-flow restriction, so a block with no reverse-flow row stays a resampling unit with a zero count.
    • Sign head training: the sign head trains in stage 1 only, with the trunk (Branch A). Its cross-entropy enters the stage 1 loss with the weight SIGN_CE_WEIGHT of 1.0, and the head's parameters sit in experiment 716's new-parameter group for stage 1 only. In stages 2 and 3 the head is frozen: it takes no sign step, no cross-entropy, and no gradient, and none of its parameters is in an optimiser group. The signed-expected history still reads the frozen head's ρ values in stages 2 and 3, but ρ is detached there, so the frozen head passes no gradient to the trunk through ρ.
    • Probe jobs: the probe's pretraining job for each world and fold runs on the GPU (about 30 seconds each, about 4 minutes for the 8 jobs, in a 6 GB process) and the regression that reads the capacity runs on the CPU. The probe runner sequences before the W2 and W0 fits.
    • Synthetic world on the real grid: the pipeline of fold 2026-03 supplies the real weather, the Primaries D9 and D11 export, and D2 and D3 do not. All four train on the folded reading and the true sign of all four trains the head. Each process serves one fold month, because experiment 713's pipeline reads its fold month once.
    • Folded loss at D15 and D18: the page names D15 and D18 in neither list, so they train on their stored readings with the plain likelihood, with no gate and no sign labels.
    • Sky blinding: besides the shortwave, the solar-gain columns, and the solar-geometry columns, the longwave and precipitation columns and their ensemble spreads are blanked at a gate-open Primary, because both carry cloud.
    • Sign read-outs: the sign accuracy uses every distinct token outside the dead band, with no restriction on u, because the page states none, and the bar stays as stated. A report-only read-out gives the same accuracy at u of at least 0.3, because night tokens are trivially right and inflate the pre-registered figure. The area uses tokens with u of at least 0.3. The false-call rate counts sunny midday half-hours (10:00 to 14:00 UTC with a clear-sky index of at least 0.7) of the two non-exporting Primaries, with the gate forced open.
    • Widening: a reading widens its interval only when it has fewer than 20 resampling units.
    • Sign head read-out base: the history power that the read-out averages is the signed expectation (1 - 2ρ) v, and its variance includes the sign variance 4ρ(1 - ρ) v².
    • Primary gate: the per-Primary export gate is computed in NumPy from the training window's rows alone (the rule of experiment 417, design A).
    • Gap rows: every signed Primary drops the half-hours at which any farm has a gap, in both worlds.
    • Synthetic fold and scored span: the synthetic fit trains on data before 2026-03-01 (after the purge) and scores origins from 2026-03-01 to 2026-06-20 12:00 UTC, the last origin whose 10 days of targets end before the 2026-07-01 holdout, whose guard is kept. The scored sign tokens are the never-trained history tokens from 2026-03-01 00:00 to 2026-06-19 09:00 UTC, so they end in mid-June. A one-month span cannot pass the noise check, because the check needs about 92 distinct days per class and a month holds at most 30. The distinct days per class with u of at least 0.3 outside the dead band, as export days and import days, are 96 and 101 at day offset 0 (seeds 0 and 1), 95 and 101 at offset 5 for seed 0, and 94 and 101 at offset 5 for seed 1, which give 3 standard errors of 0.0970, 0.0974, and 0.0979 against the margin of 0.10. Stage 0 runs the reachability check and the noise check on exactly that scored set, with each exporter's own capacity, and the margin and the noise-check rule are unchanged. The synthetic fit replaces the power with synthetic power, so the real folds 2026-04 to 2026-06 and their training windows are untouched. The extra cost is about 3 more months of scoring (prediction) in each of the 4 synthetic fits and about 1% less training data, an estimate of about 0.1 GPU-process-hours in all that the code-works check re-measures; the projection stays at about 13.6 until then.
    • Synthetic Primaries: D9 and D11 export and D2 and D3 never export. The world uses the real grid of the fold 2026-03 pipeline, the real calendar with England's bank holidays, the real 2 m temperature, and the real shortwave likelihood u, in experiment 713's fixed demand table w. The page does not say which Primaries or which fold, so these are choices.
    • Synthetic sign labels: the true sign of all four synthetic Primaries trains the sign head, and all four train on the folded reading. The page does not state this, so it is a choice. In W2 and W0 only the four visible signed Primaries carry a sign label, and the sign cross-entropy has weight 1.0, a value that the page does not state either. The two non-exporting synthetic Primaries carry labels too, but their export gate is 0 in training, so their labels give the head no gradient. They read the sky in training, and the check of the false-call rate blinds them to the sky when it forces their gate open at prediction.
    • Process split: e00721_cli.py runs the fit, the predict, and the score phases as separate processes. The score phase writes the MDE file from the seed difference of SG-0 in W2 on fold 2026-05, then runs the power check of G3 over experiment 713's stored M1 seeds 0 to 4.
