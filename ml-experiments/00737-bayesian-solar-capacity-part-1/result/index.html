    Can a Bayesian fit read the capacity of unmetered solar panels, quarter by quarter, from the power of the six Primary substations that NGED meters in signed megawatts, when we add a fleet of known size to the real record?

    The Bayesian fit followed a growing synthetic solar fleet, but its 80% interval was too narrow, and a cloud-following load and a 30-day load transfer each moved its answer by about a whole fleet, so it cannot yet be trusted to read real solar capacity. We asked whether a fit that knows the physics of solar panels can read, from a Primary substation's power meter alone, how many megawatts of unmetered solar panels sit behind the substation in each quarter. A Primary is a substation that steps voltage down towards customers, and NGED (National Grid Electricity Distribution) meters six of them in signed megawatts (positive when the Primary imports power from the grid, negative when it exports power). We added the synthetic output of a fleet of known capacity, growing over the 18 quarters from 2022 to mid-2026, to each Primary's real power, and compared the capacity the fit read with the capacity we had added. One Primary's real record with one fleet added is a world. The fit is a Bayesian regression (a regression that returns a range as well as a value) of the power on a calendar-and-temperature baseline and a solar term, with one capacity per quarter and a prior (an assumption stated before the data are read) that favours growth from one quarter to the next. The fit reads weather and power from after each half-hour it labels, so it can label a historical record and cannot estimate a live one. The verdict is not supported (row 6 of the verdict mapping). An arm is one fitting method compared in the experiment, and an arm fit is one arm fitted to one world or to one real record. The 80% interval uses the sandwich variance of Amendment 3 (a change to the plan, described below), and 95 of the 1,560 arm fits (6.1%) fell back to the stock variance (the simpler formula that the sandwich variance replaced).

    The fit recovered the fleet's path, and the growth prior did not help. The path error is the mean absolute gap between the fitted and the injected capacity over the 18 quarters, as a share of the mean injected capacity. On the 162 main worlds (the worlds whose fleet has an orientation mix, tilt, and DC:AC ratio that differ from the fit's), its median is 0.110 for the Bayesian fit (arm A), against 0.771 for a fit with one flat capacity (arm F). The increment is the capacity fitted to a world minus the capacity fitted to the same Primary's real record alone. The median recovered share (the sum of the fitted increments over the sum of the injected capacities, over the 18 quarters) is 1.08. A fit that gives each quarter its own capacity with a flat prior (arm Qt) has the same median path error as arm A (0.110), so the growth prior adds nothing.

    Arm A failed three of the seven robustness bars (B1 to B7), which the section "Bars" defines. The 80% interval covered the injected increment 0.497 of the time (B2, bar 0.65 to 0.92). A load that follows the day's cloud moved the fitted increment by a median of 1.02 of the injected fleet (B6, bar at most 0.35). A 30-day load transfer moved the fitted capacity by a median of 0.50 MW (B7, bar at most 0.25 MW). Every result is for a synthetic fleet, and none supports a claim about the real capacity.

    The 80% interval uses the sandwich variance of Amendment 3 in place of the stock variance. The first run of gate G1 (a gate is a check that must pass before a verdict counts, and G1 fits a synthetic series with Gaussian noise) found that the stock posterior variance (X' W X)^-1 of the Student-t fit (a regression with a heavy-tailed noise density) covered only 0.718 of the true capacities, against a bar of 0.75 to 0.85, because the Student-t noise scale is smaller than the Gaussian noise's standard deviation. Before any scored fit, Amendment 3 replaced the stock variance by the sandwich variance (a variance that stays valid when the noise density is wrong), and G1 then covered 0.806.

    Of the 1,560 arm fits, 95 (6.1%) fell back to the stock variance. When the observed information is not positive definite, the fit keeps the stock variance and records that it did. The section "Fits that fell back to the stock variance" gives the count by arm and the coverage of the two groups.

    The power loader reads grid index i from store index i + 1. The power store's index i is the half-hour that ends at the origin (the start of the grid, 2022-01-01) plus 30 minutes times i. Amendment 1 gives the history of the correction, and the audit in it found that no earlier published result depends on the same off-by-one.

    The timing gate G4 passes only in a relaxed form. Gate G4 compares each Primary's timing with the timing of NGED's metered solar farms. A best shift is the delay, in half-hours, at which a series' daily pattern best matches the modelled solar curve. The registered gate required a best shift of 0 at each Primary and pooled. The relaxed gate passes when the pooled best shift lies within 0.5 half-hours of the farms' and at least 4 of the 6 Primaries lie within 0.75 half-hours of the metered-farm reference for each Primary. The Primaries D9 and D14 lie outside the bound, so both are excluded from the real-record results and from bar B5, and they stay in every synthetic-world result.

    Verdict and what the result means for the history-detector pipeline

    The verdict is not supported, which is row 6 of the verdict mapping. Contrast C1 (the flat fit F minus A, on the path error) passes its sanity gate. Contrast C2 (the independent-quarter fit Qt minus A) is in state N (the sign test does not pass, and Qt does not beat A at all six Primaries). A beats Qt at only 1 of the 6 Primaries (D11, by +0.010), and the upper end of the world-resampled 95% interval of the pooled median, -0.0002, lies below the threshold of 0.05. The three failed bars do not change the row. They show that the interval of A is too narrow and that A's answer moves with a load that is not solar.

    For stage 2 of the history-detector pipeline (the solar-capacity fit, described in How the history detectors fit together), the fit tracks a growing synthetic fleet and also reads two kinds of change that are not solar as solar capacity. The fit recovers about the right total for a synthetic fleet (a median recovered share of 1.03 to 1.25 at the six Primaries). A load that follows the cloud moved the fitted increment by 0.99 to 1.06 of the injected fleet. A 30-day transfer of load from a neighbouring substation moved the fitted capacity by 0.10 to 1.01 MW.

    The switching offset model of stage 3 subtracts the solar estimate from the power before it looks for transfers, and the pipeline page lists a transfer absorbed by the solar fit as a risk. The transfer world of this experiment measured how far one 30-day transfer moves the fitted capacity at each Primary, and the move exceeded 0.25 MW at 5 of the 6. The section "Questions the result leaves open" lists what a later experiment could answer.

    Gate results

    All six gates passed, and G4 passed in the relaxed form.

    • G1, recovery with Gaussian noise. On synthetic series of a baseline, the prior's own fleet, and Gaussian noise, arm A recovered the capacity to within 1.1% of the mean capacity at most (bar 3%), recovered 0.994 of the fleet, and covered 0.806 of the truth with its 80% interval (bar 0.75 to 0.85), over 100 draws.
    • G1b, the heavy-tailed recovery. With Student-t noise, the recovered share was 0.990 over 100 draws (bar at least 0.95), so the Student-t weights do not bias the capacity down.
    • G2 and G3, the injection and the look-ahead. The injected output equals the fleet formula and is nonzero in at least 99% of the sunny half-hours at each Primary (the worst share was 0.995 to 0.997), and the injected output and the design matrix are identical when the power is shuffled.
    • G4, the timing convention. The pooled best shift of the Primaries is 0.25 half-hours later than the farms' (bound 0.5), and 4 of the 6 Primaries lie within 0.75 half-hours of their farm reference (D5, D10, D11, and D16).
    • G5, convergence. 99.9% of the 1,104 cold fits (fits that start from the initial weights, for arms A, F, Qt, O, and R) stopped on the tolerance, and no fit ended with every capacity at its bound.

    The tuning chose a ridge scale of 0.1 and a growth standard deviation of 0.15 MW a quarter, and the growth value barely mattered. The ridge scale multiplies the penalty that keeps the baseline coefficients small, and the growth standard deviation is the standard deviation of the prior's growth step from one quarter to the next. The lowest error was at a ridge scale of 0.1: the median largest capacity error on 30 synthetic series was 0.161 MW at ridge scale 1, 0.061 MW at 0.1, and 0.067 MW at 0.01. The median path error on the 120 tuning worlds was 0.1126, 0.1142, and 0.1128 at growth standard deviations of 0.15, 0.30, and 0.60 MW a quarter.

    Why B2, B6, and B7 failed

    No explanation below was tested, and each rests on the numbers named. The code is frozen, no fit was rerun, and a later experiment would have to test them.

    • B2: the interval may hold no allowance for the mismatch between the injected fleet and the fit's forward model (the model that turns sky, temperature, and wind into panel output). The coverage is 1.00 on the 24 matched worlds (ramp fleets of 2.5 MW and 5 MW whose mix, tilt, and ratio equal the prior's), 0.972 for arm O, and 0.497 on the 162 main worlds. On the main worlds, the coverage falls with the fleet's size, at 0.74 for 1 MW, 0.46 for 2.5 MW, and 0.29 for 5 MW, while the mean standard deviation of the capacity stays at 0.063 to 0.064 MW and the mean signed error (the fitted increment minus the injected capacity, pooled over the scored quarters) grows (+0.040 MW, +0.102 MW, and +0.208 MW). The fallback to the stock variance does not explain the failure: the 151 main worlds in which arm A used the sandwich variance cover 0.513, which is also below the bar.
    • B6: the cloud-following load may be large against the fleet's output. The load is 25% of the fleet's capacity on every daylight half-hour, on average, and the fleet's output averages well under its capacity, so the load added 0.59 to 0.75 of the injected fleet's energy at the six Primaries (the energy ratio of the stored cloud worlds). The prediction of +10% to +30% rested on experiment 40's error-bar finding of about +20%. A fitted shift of 1.02 is larger than the load's energy, and the page cannot say why. The coverage of arm A on the cloud worlds is 0.00.
    • B7: the transfer's flat 1.5 MW over 30 days may leak into the quarter's capacity. The fit has a flat level per quarter that absorbs a change that is flat across the day, but a 30-day window covers about a third of a quarter, so the level cannot absorb the transfer fully. This explanation comes from reading the fit's flat quarterly level and was not tested. The largest move of the capacity was 0.87 MW at D5, 0.68 at D9, 0.26 at D10, 1.01 at D11, 0.10 at D14, and 0.33 at D16.

    Real-record fit

    At the four Primaries that pass G4, the fitted capacity of the real record rises, and the result is descriptive only. The four are D5, D10, D11, and D16, and the page publishes no per-Primary capacity in megawatts. The median over the four of the ratio of the last year's mean capacity to the first year's is 1.73 (the four values lie between 1.26 and 2.03). The capacity rises between the first and last year with posterior probability 0.9 or more at 4 of 4. The 80% interval is wider than half the median capacity in none of the scored quarters. The capacity of arm R is 0.067 of arm A's at the median (0.03 to 0.09). The capacity table of experiment 649 (capacity from stored probes) overlaps the fit's April-to-September capacity at 2 of the 4 Primaries. Because B6 and B7 failed, the rise cannot be read as solar growth, and the fit's interval is too narrow.

    Every Primary has at least 11 scored quarters, and the counts of dropped half-hours are small except for D9's missing power. A quarter is scored when at least 60% of its daylight half-hours are usable. D5, D10, D11, D14, and D16 have 18 scored quarters. D9 has 11, because its first 7 quarters are unscored: the first six have no power (28,791 missing half-hours, about 6.6 quarters of 4,380), and the seventh has only about a third of its daylight half-hours usable. The substation_zero rule and missing readings drop 202 half-hours at D5, 2 at D10, 2 at D11, 2 at D14, 4 at D16, and 28,791 at D9. CAMS (the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service's satellite sunshine record) reliability under 0.9 drops 7,590 to 7,744 half-hours at each Primary.

    Fit using only past data

    All 24 past-only fits returned NaN capacities, so the loss from cutting the record is unmeasured. A past-only fit reads the real record cut at a quarter end and sees nothing after the cut. The sample fits arm A to the real record of each Primary cut at 4 quarter ends (6 Primaries, 24 fits). Every file has NaN in every capacity and in every standard deviation, an iteration count of 30, and the not-converged flag, so the past-only gap reads NaN with 16 fits counted (4 Primaries). The code sets the power after the cut to NaN before the fit, and the cause of the NaN was not investigated. The G5 count of 1,104 fits does not include these 24 files.

    Compute

    The run took 5.85 CPU-hours at one thread, against 17.5 estimated and a stop at 26, and used no GPU time. The sum of the job durations in time_log.csv is 0.01 for the gates, 2.15 for the tuning, 0.70 for G1 and G1b, 0.05 for the first unit (the first world, run before the bulk fits as a timing check), and 2.93 for the 264 fit jobs (the controls, worlds, placebos, and past-only fits). The estimate assumed 30 expectation-maximisation iterations and 24 seconds per fit. The cold fits of the first unit (fits that start from the initial weights) stopped after 6 to 8 iterations, and one fit of arm A took 5.4 seconds. The log covers the final run only. The archived earlier runs (before Amendment 3, and a failed run caused by a name clash) have no time log.

    Checks

    • Median minus mean forecast per site, and the error of the mean or median forecast beside each quantile-score contrast. Not applicable, because the fit scores no forecast, so there is no forecast whose median and mean can differ.
    • Audit triggers. Trigger 1 (a control, known-answer test, or placebo fails) fired, because the confound tests B6 and B7 failed. Trigger 2 (a result contradicts its own prediction, and no tested explanation is recorded) fired, because the results contradict P1 and P5 and no explanation was tested. No audit has run, which means that no separate agent has re-derived the headline contrasts from the stored fits.
    • Figures. No figure is published. The data folder holds only the three figures of the forward model (the model that turns sky, temperature, and wind into panel output) from the planned gallery of 11, and the other eight were not drawn. The real-record figure would show real-record curves and is therefore not published.
    • Anonymisation. The page names substations only. It gives no metered generator's name, identifier, size, or date, and the real-farm worlds report shares over six farms with none labelled.

    Questions the result leaves open

    The result leaves three questions that a later experiment could answer. The first is whether the interval's failure comes from the mismatch between the injected fleet and the fit's forward model, as the matched worlds and arm O suggest. The second is whether the cloud-following load and the transfer worlds were set at sizes that the plan intended, and how much of the confound is the fit's and how much is the test's. The third is why all 24 past-only fits returned NaN, because the loss from using only past data is the number that a live estimate would need.

    The fit labels history after the fact, and cannot estimate a live record. The experiment asks whether a fit that knows the physics of solar panels can read, from a Primary substation's power meter alone, how many megawatts of unmetered solar panels sit behind the substation in each quarter of a year. A Primary is the kind of substation that steps voltage down towards customers. NGED (National Grid Electricity Distribution) meters the power at a Primary as demand minus the output of every solar panel connected below, and does not meter the panels. NGED meters six of the Primaries in signed megawatts, which means the power is negative when the substation exports power to the grid, and the six are the subject of the page.

    The fit is a Bayesian regression, and the test adds a fleet of known size to the real record. The regression turns satellite sunshine (the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service, CAMS), air temperature, and wind speed (the ERA5 reanalysis, which is a record of past weather) into the alternating-current (AC) output of one megawatt of panels. It then fits one capacity per quarter, with a prior (an assumption stated before the data are read) that makes growth from one quarter to the next more likely than a fall. The test adds the synthetic output of a fleet of known capacity, growing over the 18 quarters from 2022 to mid-2026, to the real signed power of each of the six Primaries, and asks how closely the fit recovers each quarter's capacity. The fit reads weather and power from after each half-hour it labels, so it can label a historical record and cannot estimate a live one.

    The timing gate G4 passes in a relaxed form. G4 compares each Primary's timing with the timing of the metered solar farms, in half-hours. The relaxed gate passes when the pooled best shift of the Primaries lies within 0.5 half-hours of the farms' pooled best shift, and at least 4 of the 6 Primaries lie within 0.75 half-hours of their own farm reference. In the final run of the gates stage, the pooled difference is +0.25 half-hours, and 4 of the 6 Primaries pass (D5, D10, D11, and D16). D9 (+0.92) and D14 (+1.08) lie outside the bound, so both are excluded from the real-record results only: bar B5, the real-record aggregates, the real-record figure, and the past-only gap. The synthetic-world bars keep all six Primaries. The audit that preceded the gate found that the power store's index i is the half-hour that ends at the origin plus 30 minutes times i, and that the loader had read the power one half-hour late, so the loader now reads grid index i from store index i + 1. The audit found that no earlier published result depends on the same off-by-one. Experiments 653 (edge-state event table), 660 (injected load-transfer events), 672 to 692 (the graph model of load transfers and the event tables), and 734 (switching offset model) use index arithmetic only. Experiment 736 places its additions at panel index equal to the bank's half-hour plus 1, and checks the placement with its gate G2. The padded store of experiment 685 (event table on the final test window) matches the store of experiment 327 exactly at end stamps. The second decision of Amendment 1 gives the per-Primary numbers.

    The page belongs to the second of four detectors that read a Primary's history. The detector for unmetered solar capacity is stage 2 of the pipeline in How the history detectors fit together. Part 1 covers the six Primaries metered in signed megawatts, and the Primaries metered in MVA (apparent power, which has no sign) wait for a later part. The background page on the signed-MW Primaries lists eight: D5, D9, D10, D11, D14, D15, D16, and D18. Part 1 uses six of the eight, for two reasons.     The earlier experiments leave a gap that this experiment fills, because none of them combines a quarterly capacity path, an interval on every quarter, and a test with a known growing fleet. Experiment 39 added the recorded output of a real solar farm to a Primary's power (CAMS injection). Its fit recovered 0.77 of the added farm's capacity half-hourly and 0.85 on daily sums, where a recovered share is the fitted capacity divided by the capacity added, and it fitted one capacity per year. Experiment 45 (weekly capacity) found that a weekly path was too noisy. The probes of experiment 656 and experiment 662, a redesign of experiment 656, read a capacity through a neural network and give no interval. A probe is a linear read-out of capacity from a trained neural network, and the experiments ran it at Primaries whose meters report MVA. A quarter is the middle ground between a year and a week: 90 days is long enough to average the cloud, and short enough to follow a farm that is connected part-way through the record.

    Design

    The fit reads the signed power itself, with its own demand baseline, and does not read the residual of experiment 734. Three designs could supply the observation.

    • Design A (chosen): the cleaned signed power, with a calendar-and-temperature baseline fitted together with the solar term. Every Primary's power is baseline + slow level - solar, as in experiment 39 and studies.pv_separation. The fit reads no lagged power, no demand forecast, and no weather forecast.
    • Design B (rejected): power minus the stored forecast of experiment 130, as in experiment 734 (switching offset model). The stored forecast comes from a convex regression model of demand (experiment 130: demand fitted to the calendar and the weather, refitted monthly on ERA5 and CAMS) that has already seen the sunshine. Experiment 657 (explicit solar term in the event table) fitted a gain of 0.003 for a solar term on that residual, which means that almost no solar output is left in the residual. A fit of capacity to the residual would read near zero on the real record, and an injected fleet would still look fully recoverable, because the stored forecast never saw the injection. The reference arm R runs design B to show both effects.
    • Design C (rejected): daily sums. Experiment 39 found that daily sums recovered 0.08 more (+0.04 to +0.10) than half-hours. The reference arm Ad runs the same Bayesian fit on daily sums, and the main arm keeps half-hours because stage 3 needs the half-hourly solar estimate.

    The inference method is a linear-Gaussian regression with a Student-t likelihood, solved by expectation-maximisation, with a conditional Gaussian posterior at the converged weights. Three facts support the choice. First, the solar term is linear in the capacity once the sky, the orientation mix, the tilt, and the DC:AC ratio are fixed, so the posterior of the capacities, given the noise weights, is Gaussian. Second, experiment 734 and experiment 653 use a Student-t density with 4 degrees of freedom for the residual, because switching steps and spikes make the tails heavy. The fit uses the same density as a scale mixture, and expectation-maximisation updates the mixture's weights in at most 30 iterations. Third, a CPU solve of about 420 columns on 78,816 rows takes about 0.8 second for one weighted Gram matrix, so no sampler is needed. Markov chain Monte Carlo would add run time and a convergence question, and no information about a Gaussian posterior. The posterior at the converged weights is a conditional posterior, because it holds the weights and the noise scales fixed, and the page never calls it a Laplace approximation.

    The likelihood and the priors are written out in full.

    • Likelihood. The power of half-hour i is baseline_i + level_q(i) - capacity_q(i) x s_i + e_i, where s_i is the unit solar term (below) and e_i follows a Student-t density with 4 degrees of freedom. The scale of e_i is sigma_day on daylight half-hours (CAMS irradiance above 5 W/m2) and sigma_night on the others, because the variance of solar power is much larger by day. Each expectation-maximisation iteration sets the weight of row i to (4 + 1) / (4 + (r_i / sigma)^2), where r_i is the residual and sigma is the scale of the row's class, and then sets each class's sigma to the weighted root mean square of the residuals in the class.
    • Why the weights are tested. Down-weighting large residuals could remove the cloudy half-hours that identify the capacity, and bias the capacity down. Gate G1b tests the bias: with heavy-tailed noise at the daytime scale, A's recovered share is at least 0.95.
    • Baseline prior. Every baseline coefficient has a zero-mean Gaussian prior whose precision is the ridge penalty of studies.pv_separation (RIDGE_PER_COLUMN = 1e-3, relative to the mean squared column norm) times a ridge scale that the tuning chooses from 1, 0.1, and 0.01 (Tuning). Every arm that carries the baseline ridge uses the same chosen scale. The 18 quarterly levels and the 144 day-type indicators both sum to 1 in every row, so the design would be rank deficient by 1. The first quarter has no level, which leaves 17 levels, and the ridge penalty keeps the remaining solve well conditioned.

    • Capacity prior. The AC capacity of quarter q is the capacity of quarter q-1 plus a growth step that is Gaussian with a positive mean (0.10 MW a quarter) and a standard deviation tuned once on tuning worlds from the grid 0.15, 0.30, and 0.60 MW. The first quarter has a Gaussian prior with mean 2 MW and standard deviation 2 MW. Quarters in which the Primary has no usable daylight rows (D9's first six) take their capacity from the prior alone and are not scored.

    • Baseline. One indicator per local half-hour of the day and day type (weekday, Saturday, and Sunday), as studies.pv_separation.baseline_design builds them; two annual harmonics (a cosine and a sine each) for each half-hour; the ERA5 2 m temperature as heating and cooling degree-hours around 15.5 degrees Celsius plus the trailing 24-hour and 72-hour means of temperature; 48 indicators for bank holidays, one per half-hour of the day, read from the is_holiday column of experiment 6's features.parquet (baseline_design has no bank-holiday day type); and one flat level per quarter. The design has 423 columns: 144 day-type indicators, 192 harmonic columns, 4 temperature columns, 48 holiday columns, 17 quarterly levels, and 18 solar columns. The level absorbs a demand change that is flat across the day, which is the shape of a switching step. A daytime-only change in demand reads as a change in solar output. The write-up reports this limitation. Two annual harmonics per half-hour may also absorb part of the solar seasonal profile, and a cooling-degree term correlates with sunny days. A reviewer estimated that the harmonics could cost 25% to 30% of the recovered share. Bar B1 measures the loss on the real records, and arm O shows the ceiling.

    • Solar term. The AC output of one megawatt of panels of a fixed fleet, built by e00737_forward.py from studies.pv_physics: CAMS hourly irradiance split into half-hours, a plane-of-array model for east-, south-, and west-facing panels at 25 degrees from horizontal with the fixed shares 0.2, 0.6, and 0.2 (the fleet mix that experiment 73 (orientation mix and DC:AC ratio) used as its guess), the cell-temperature derate, and clipping at the AC capacity with the fixed DC:AC ratio of 1.15. The page does not publish the shares. Experiment 73 found that the shares are identified only with an error of 0.20, and that the DC:AC ratio could not be recovered once a farm was added to a Primary's power.
    • Cell-temperature derate (new code). studies.pv_physics and studies.pv_separation hold no cell-temperature model, and physics_model.py and dp_gam.py use a fixed 25 K rise at 1,000 W/m2. e00737_forward.py adds the derate in this order: plane-of-array irradiance G; cell temperature from the Faiman model, T_cell = T_air + G / (u0 + u1 x wind), with the coefficients u0 = 25 W/m2/K and u1 = 6.84 W s/m3/K (the defaults of the pvlib library); DC power equal to the DC rating times G / 1000 times 1 - 0.004 (T_cell - 25); and then clipping at the AC capacity. The DC rating is the AC capacity times the DC:AC ratio. ERA5 is hourly and the power is half-hourly, so the temperature and the wind speed at a half-hour's end time are linear interpolations between the two neighbouring hourly values. Three unit tests cover the new code. The Faiman test checks that G = 800 W/m2, 20 degrees Celsius, and 2 m/s give a cell temperature of 40.7 degrees. The order test checks that a DC:AC ratio of 1.5 at a cell temperature of 45 degrees gives exactly the AC capacity, where clipping before the derate would give 0.92 of it. The interpolation test checks a half-hour at the half-hour mark against the mean of its two neighbours.
    • Interval. The 80% interval of each quarter's capacity is the central 80% of a Gaussian around the posterior mean, whose variance is the sandwich variance of the Student-t fit (Amendment 3) multiplied by (1 + r) / (1 - r), where r is the median lag-1 autocorrelation of the whitened daylight residuals within each day. Bar B2 tests the coverage.
    • Switching events. The fit treats a switching event as step noise. The Student-t weights and the flat quarterly level absorb part of a step, and the page reports how far a 30-day transfer moves the capacity (P5, bar B7). The fit masks no event. Masking the called events is stage 4 of the pipeline, and the plan does not include it.

    What the fit reads, and what it must not read. The inputs are listed here so that the review can check each one.

    • Read: the cleaned signed power of experiment 327's store (power/power_<id>.npy, in which the substation_zero rule has flagged half-hours that are dropped, and the page reports the count); CAMS global horizontal irradiance at the Primary's own coordinates for the hours whose reliability is 0.9 or more (other hours are left out of the fit, and are not set to zero); ERA5 2 m temperature and 10 m wind speed (experiment 6's features.parquet, a recorded analysis with no forecast-origin column, read from the temperature_2m and wind_speed_10m columns); the solar position, which is a function of time and place; and the calendar.
    • Not read: any past power as an input to the baseline; any demand forecast; any weather forecast, ensemble member, or lead time; the temperature_mean_24h and temperature_mean_72h columns of features.parquet (they are not a trailing, centred, or leading rolling mean of temperature_2m, because the mean gap is 0.9 K, so e00737_forward.py builds the means from temperature_2m); any switching label from NGED's logs (the logs score nothing here); and any row from 2026-07-01 on. The weather files run past that date (ERA5 to 2026-07-31, CAMS to 2026-09-10, and the farm file runs past the cut date), so every loader cuts at 2026-07-01 and a test asserts the cut.
    • Look-ahead. The fit is a whole-record smoother, so the capacity of quarter q uses power and weather from later quarters. ERA5 and CAMS are published about 5 days late, so the result bears on labelling a historical record and not on a live estimate. A past-only sample (6 Primaries, the real record cut at 4 quarter ends) measures the loss from cutting the record, and the page reports the sample beside the whole-record fit and never in place of it. Gate G3 tests the design and the injection.

    The worlds and the arms

    A world is the real record of one Primary with one synthetic fleet added, and the unit of evidence is the Primary. The injected output is subtracted from the signed power, because generation lowers the reading. The fleet's capacity follows one of three growth shapes from zero in the first quarter to its final AC capacity: a ramp (linear), a step (zero for the first eight quarters, then the final capacity), and an accelerating path (quadratic). The three final capacities are 1 MW, 2.5 MW, and 5 MW. Experiment 649's probe reads 2.4 MW to 3.9 MW of unmetered solar across the six Primaries in April to September (whole-summer values, the range over the six as that experiment published it, and not a per-Primary figure of this fit), so the three sizes span from well under that fleet to above it. At the two Primaries with the lowest midday power readings, D5 and D10, the midday reading is about 1 MW to 1.5 MW, so a 5 MW fleet is several times the reading, and the page says so. A power reading is not a capacity estimate. A fleet smaller than 1 MW sits below the noise of a quarter at the smallest Primary and is not planned. The injected fleet's orientation mix, tilt (15 to 40 degrees from horizontal), and DC:AC ratio (1.0 to 1.5) are drawn per world from a seed, so the fleet differs from the prior in the way a real fleet will.

    The three special worlds are built as follows.

    • Real-farm worlds. The half-real-farm file is cleaned_solar_farms.parquet of experiment 179 (six farms; columns time_series_id, time, power as float32, clear_sky_ghi_w_m2, and drop_reason; 579,023 rows). The file has no capacity column, so each farm's power is divided by studies.pv_dataset.pv_sites().effective_capacity_mw to give a per-unit output, and the loader cuts it at 2026-07-01. With the six farms sorted by their series identifier as farm 0 to farm 5, and the six Primaries in ascending D-number order as Primary 0 to Primary 5, Primary k takes farms k and (k + 1) modulo 6, one farm for each of the two seeds. In each quarter the world's output is half the capacity from the farm's per-unit output and half from the physics mixture. A farm half-hour with no finite reading takes the physics mixture instead, and a quarter in which the farm has finite readings in under 60% of its daylight half-hours (for example before a farm with a late first reading) takes the physics mixture alone.
    • Cloud-following load. Experiment 40's e00040_arms.confound_shapes(window=...) returns eight shapes, and the world uses the one named flat, the day's cloud fraction scaled to a mean of 1, at 25% of the fleet's capacity, added as demand (a positive load that rises with cloud). The code review fixes the window before any world is built.
    • Transfer. Three 30-day windows, placed by the seed without overlap, each add a flat 1.5 MW to the Primary's signed power, as a direct array addition. A test compares the array addition with apply_events of experiment 660 on one row, and the code review checks that the two agree.

    The fitted increment of a world is its fit minus the same arm's fit of the uninjected record, and the increment is the uncut posterior mean. The real record already holds unmetered solar, so the fit of the injected world holds the real capacity plus the injected capacity, and the control fit holds the real capacity alone. With a linear fit and fixed weights the two would differ by the injection exactly, which experiment 39 showed makes a known-answer test blind to a demand confound. The heavy-tailed weights make the difference inexact, and the real noise enters the difference. The cloud-following load and the transfer worlds are the tests of the confound. The path error E, the recovered share, and the coverage all use the uncut Gaussian posterior mean, so a fit can report a negative increment. Arm Q is non-negative by construction and cannot report one, so the page notes Q's one-sided errors beside its row. The cut at zero applies only to the capacities reported for the real record.

    The 80% interval of the increment has a defined variance. The increment is the injected world's posterior mean minus the control's posterior mean, and the two fits' errors are strongly correlated. The interval takes its standard deviation from the injected fit alone, with the (1 + r) / (1 - r) inflation of the interval's definition applied, which is conservative because the difference of two positively correlated errors has a smaller variance than either. It is the central 80% of a Gaussian around the uncut increment, with no truncation before the subtraction.

    The sign test is deliberately strict, and its power is low. A true gain that is positive at each Primary with probability 0.9 passes the test with probability 0.53 (0.9 to the power 6), and a gain that is positive with probability 0.8 passes with probability 0.26. The worlds of one Primary share that Primary's real noise, so the worlds do not add independent evidence. The six Primaries also share their weather, so the observations are not independent either, and the effective number of observations is below 6. The world-resampled 95% intervals that the page reports ignore the Primary-to-Primary spread, and decide only between not supported and inconclusive. The recovered-share bar B1 uses six Primaries too, so B1 resolves a median share only to about 0.10.     Tuning

    The ridge scale and the growth standard deviation are tuned, and the tuning shapes are named. The tuning has two steps, and arm A is the only arm fitted in either.

    1. The ridge scale comes first, on synthetic series whose absolute capacity is known. The ridge penalty shrinks the fitted capacity of the real record and of a world alike, so the shrinkage cancels in the increment that the 120 tuning worlds score. The ridge scale is therefore chosen on the series that gate G1 builds: a baseline, the prior's own fleet with a known capacity in each quarter, and Gaussian noise at the Primary's own scale. Thirty draws use seeds 737200 to 737229, which differ from every scored seed (737101 to 737103), from the tuning seeds (737000 to 737009), and from the G1 seeds (737900 upwards). Arm A is fitted to each draw at each of the three ridge scales (1, 0.1, and 0.01, multiplying RIDGE_PER_COLUMN) with the growth standard deviation fixed at 0.30 MW a quarter. The error of a draw is the largest absolute gap between the fitted and the injected capacity over the 18 quarters. The scale with the lowest median error over the 30 draws is kept, and a tie goes to the larger scale. The baseline and noise scales of the series come from a fit of the first Primary's real record at the unscaled ridge.
    2. The growth standard deviation comes second, at the chosen ridge scale. The 120 tuning worlds use fleets of 2 MW and 4 MW with the shape assigned by the seed, and they use seeds 737000 to 737009 only, never a scored seed. Each grid value (0.15, 0.30, and 0.60 MW a quarter) is fitted, and the value with the lowest median path error over the tuning worlds is kept.

    The chosen ridge scale and the chosen growth standard deviation are stored together in tuned.json, beside tuned.sha256, which holds the SHA-256 of that file and the code hash. Every scored fit of every arm, every G1 and G1b draw, and the fleet and posterior figures read both values through a check of that digest, and refuse to run when it does not match. The reference fit of gates G1 and G1b and the record of each of their draws store the tuned values and the code hash, and the collection of G1 and G1b stops when any of them differs from the current tuned.json. Gates G1 and G1b read the tuned values, so they run after the tuning, and they gate the bulk fits and not the tuning. Each of the two collect stages (tune-ridge-collect and tune-collect) stops unless every task it collects has a done marker of the current code and no failure record exists. A growth standard deviation of 0.60 MW a quarter cannot follow a 5 MW step in one quarter, which is about 8 standard deviations. The prior spreads the step over several quarters, so the step shape loses to Q and Qt, and the prediction P3 already allows for that (C2 of -0.08 to 0.00 on the step shape). The page reports C2 for each shape beside the pooled C2 that the decision rule counts, and tuning on a mix of shapes picks a value that suits the ramp and the accelerating path.

    Gates

    Six gates must pass before any verdict counts, and a failed gate gives invalid.

    • G1, the noise-free recovery. On a synthetic series that holds only a baseline, an injected fleet of the prior's own mix and ratio, and Gaussian noise at the Primary's own scale, A recovers each quarter's capacity to within 3% in the mean over 100 draws, and its 80% interval covers the truth between 0.75 and 0.85 of the cells.
    • G1b, the heavy-tailed recovery. On the same series with heavy-tailed noise (a Student-t density with 4 degrees of freedom) at the daytime scale, A's recovered share is at least 0.95 in the mean over 100 draws. The gate tests whether the Student-t weights bias the capacity down.
    • G2, the injection is exact and nonzero. The added output equals the fleet formula to a relative tolerance of 1e-9. In every quarter, the injected output is nonzero in at least 99% of the half-hours whose CAMS irradiance exceeds 50 W/m2 and exactly zero when the sun is below 5 degrees of elevation. The check would have caught the silent solar output of experiment 729 (invalid), where half the simulated solar farms produced nothing.
    • G3, no look-ahead of the forbidden kind. The injected output is a function of CAMS, ERA5, the sun's position, and the seed only, and the test builds a copy of the same Primary with shuffled power and checks that the injected output and the design matrix are identical. The temperature means are trailing means built from temperature_2m, the test checks each mean against a direct calculation from values at or before the half-hour's end, and the inputs hold no forecast column.
    • G4, the timing convention. Three conventions meet, and a one-slot error is exactly 30 minutes, which a test of the peak against solar noon cannot see (CAMS is hourly). CAMS labels the end of the hour. half_hour_sun_and_sky returns half-hour end stamps. Index i of experiment 327's store holds the half-hour that ends at the origin plus 30 minutes times i (the power_mw column of experiment 6's features.parquet, whose valid_time is a half-hour end stamp, matches the store at that index in every row), whereas the grid's index i ends 30 minutes times i + 1 after the origin and baseline_design takes end times. The loader therefore reads grid index i from store index i + 1 (Amendment 1 records the correction). The gate runs on the real half-hourly series: for each Primary, it compares the mean daytime dip of real summer power minus real winter power (April to September against October to March, by half-hour of the day) with the model's unit solar curve for the same two seasons, shifted by -1, 0, and +1 half-hour, and requires the best shift (the one with the highest correlation over the 48 half-hours) to be 0 at each Primary and in the pooled mean. Amendment 1 replaces the summer-minus-winter comparison with a same-month comparison of sunny and dull days, scans the shift in steps of 0.25 half-hour, and requires the Primaries to match the metered farms' best shift within stated bounds.
    • G5, convergence. The expectation-maximisation fit converges in 30 iterations or fewer in 99% of the fits, and no fit ends with every quarter's capacity at its bound.

    Method

    Architecture

    • The fit is a Bayesian linear regression with a Student-t likelihood (4 degrees of freedom), whose weights and daylight and night scales come from at most 30 expectation-maximisation iterations, and the posterior mean is the converged coefficients. Their variance is the sandwich variance of the penalised Student-t likelihood (Amendment 3).
    • The inputs are the cleaned signed power, the CAMS irradiance (reliability 0.9 or more), the ERA5 2 m temperature and 10 m wind speed, the solar position, and the calendar. Nothing else enters.
    • The solar term is studies.pv_physics' plane-of-array model for three orientations at the fixed shares and 25 degrees from horizontal, with the Faiman cell-temperature derate (new code in e00737_forward.py, applied before the clipping) and clipping at the DC:AC ratio 1.15.
    • The capacity of each of the 18 quarters follows a random walk with a positive drift (0.10 MW a quarter) and a tuned standard deviation, with a Gaussian prior on the first quarter. Each quarter after the first also has a flat level, and the baseline has a ridge prior whose scale is tuned.
    • Arm F sets the growth step to zero, Qt gives each quarter an independent flat prior, Q fits each quarter alone with the bounded least squares of studies.pv_separation, Ad uses daily sums, Af selects the DC:AC ratio by marginal likelihood, O reads the injected fleet's parameters, and R replaces the power by experiment 734's residual.

    Training regime

    • Nothing is trained. Two values are chosen, each from three. The ridge scale is chosen first, on 30 synthetic series with a known capacity (seeds 737200 to 737229). The growth standard deviation is then chosen at the chosen ridge scale, on 120 tuning worlds (seeds 737000 to 737009, with fleets of 2 MW and 4 MW on all six Primaries, the shape assigned by the seed). The tuning and the scored worlds share the real history, so the reported recovery is optimistic by at most the effect of two tuned values.
    • The injected fleets are drawn by the experiment's own code from the seed of each world: the growth shape and the final capacity, the orientation mix (a Dirichlet draw around the fleet mix, or the prior's mix for the matched worlds), the tilt, and the DC:AC ratio.
    • Compute: CPU only, about 17.5 CPU-hours at one thread estimated (30 iterations assumed for each cold fit, 10 for each warm fit of Af), a stop at 26, 5.85 CPU-hours measured, and 0 GPU-process-hours.

    Measurement

    • The scored rows are the quarters from 2022 to mid-2026 for each Primary with at least 60% of the quarter's daylight half-hours usable (the table above gives each Primary's count), with the control fit subtracted from each injected fit.
    • The metrics are the path error E, the recovered share, and the coverage of the 80% interval, each per world, then summarised per Primary by the median over its worlds. All three use the uncut posterior mean.
    • The contrasts C1 and C2 use the exact one-sided sign test over the six Primaries at 0.025, with world-resampled 95% intervals (10,000 draws) reported beside them, and the verdict is read as descriptive evidence because the Primaries share their weather. Every number before the final scoring is interim.
    • The path error divides by the mean injected capacity of the world, so the path error is a share, and the recovered share divides by the injected capacities' sum. A capacity is an AC capacity in megawatts (the fleet's 99th-percentile half-hourly AC output equals the AC capacity at a DC:AC ratio of 1.15).

    Limits

    The posterior covariance (X' W X)^-1 understates the variance when the noise is Gaussian, and the sandwich variance corrects that. The Student-t density's daylight scale (0.287) is smaller than the Gaussian standard deviation of the noise in gate G1 (0.349), so the covariance at the converged weights is too small by about 1.5 times in variance (the ratio 0.349 squared over 0.287 squared). On 42 Gaussian draws of the G1 series, the reported standard deviation was 0.87 of the empirical standard deviation of the capacity estimates, and the 80% interval covered 0.71 of the truth. The sandwich variance of Amendment 3 removes the mismatch without a tuned constant. It does not remove the ridge and prior shrinkage, which pulls each estimate about 0.3 standard deviations below the truth and keeps the coverage slightly under the nominal 0.80. It also ignores the uncertainty in the estimated noise scales. The sandwich variance is derived for independent rows, and the lag-1 inflation is the only correction for autocorrelation. Bar B2 on the injected worlds, whose noise is the real record's, is the test of both.

    The baseline's ridge penalty shrinks the fitted capacity downwards, and the tuning chooses the ridge scale to limit that. The 405 baseline columns explain 79% of the variance of the solar column, so a ridge penalty on their coefficients leaves part of the solar signal in the residual, and the capacity absorbs that part with a negative sign. On one tuning world, the mean error of the fitted capacity is -0.09 MW at a ridge scale of 1 (a recovered share of 0.93), -0.02 MW at 0.1, and -0.012 MW with almost no ridge. The Student-t density's daylight scale (0.287 against the true 0.349) makes the effective ridge about 1.5 times stronger, because the penalty is divided by the squared daylight scale. This is shrinkage and not a code error. At the smallest ridge scale, 0.01, a known-answer test recovers a noise-free fleet's capacity to within 1%, and the tuning picks the scale that gives the lowest median error on synthetic series, not the smallest scale. Some shrinkage can remain at the chosen scale, and the recovered share on the injected worlds shows it.

    The ridge scale is not chosen without bias. The ridge selection runs on synthetic series whose baseline is the fit of one Primary's real record at ridge scale 1, with independent Gaussian noise. That construction favours scale 1, because the baseline already carries the shrinkage of that scale, and it can under-select a weaker ridge. The real record has autocorrelated residuals and structure that no fit captures, and the synthetic series have neither.

    The G4 timing gate is relaxed, and two Primaries are excluded from the real-record results. The gate passes when the pooled difference from the metered farms lies within 0.5 half-hours and at least 4 of the 6 Primaries lie within 0.75 half-hours of their farm reference. In the final run of the gates stage, the pooled best shift is 0.75 half-hours against 0.5 for the farms (a difference of +0.25), and 4 of the 6 Primaries lie within the bound. D9 (+0.92 half-hours) and D14 (+1.08) lie outside it, so bar B5, the real-record aggregates, the real-record figure, and the past-only gap are computed on four Primaries. The synthetic-world bars B1 to B4, B6, and B7, the contrasts C1 and C2, and the placebo ratio of B3 keep all six. A Primary's power is net load, so the fleet's west lean and the demand's response to the sun can explain a remaining delay of up to 1 half-hour without a timing error, and the gate cannot show that none remains.

    The loader read the power one half-hour late, and no earlier published result shares the off-by-one. The power store's index i is the half-hour that ends at the origin plus 30 minutes times i, and the loader now reads grid index i from store index i + 1. The audit found that experiments 653, 660, 672 to 692, and 734 use index arithmetic only. Experiment 736 places its additions at panel index equal to the bank's half-hour plus 1 and checks the placement with its gate G2. The padded store of experiment 685 matches the store of experiment 327 exactly at end stamps.

    Amendment 1 to the plan of experiment 737 (2026-11-28)

    Date: 2026-11-28. The amendment applies before any scored fit and before any fit of any stage.

    Reason

    The registered G4 compares two seasons, and the winter demand confounds the comparison. The gate correlates the real power of April to September minus October to March, by half-hour of the day, with the model's solar curve for the same two seasons. Winter evening demand is much higher than summer evening demand, so the real difference is deepest at the winter evening peak and not at solar noon. With the corrected loader, the registered comparison gives a best shift of +1 at each of the six Primaries and in the pooled mean (diagnostic_summer_minus_winter in gates.json), and the minimum of the full-day difference sits in the evening (half-hour slot 34 of 48) at five of the six Primaries. A shift of +1 therefore does not show a timing error, because the demand moves the real curve for a reason that has nothing to do with the sun.

    What changes

    1. G4 is a same-month comparison. Within each calendar month of April to September of each year, the usable weekdays are ranked by their daily clear-sky index (the day's summed CAMS irradiance over its summed CAMS clear-sky irradiance, over the reliable CAMS hours). For each half-hour of the day, the test takes the mean real power on the days of the top tercile minus its mean on the days of the bottom tercile, and the same difference of the model's unit solar output on the same days, and averages each difference over the months. Both terciles come from one month, so the seasonal swing of the demand cancels.
    2. The correlation runs over the daylight half-hours, and the shift is scanned. These are the half-hours at which the sun is up on more than half of the days of April to September. The real difference is correlated with minus the model's difference, delayed by each shift from -2 to +2 half-hours in steps of 0.25 (the model's curve is interpolated linearly between slots). The fleet mix is the prior mix of 20%, 60%, and 20%, and the mix is not tuned. The pass rule is relative to the metered farms and is given in item 3. A Primary with no readable difference fails the gate. The gate reports each Primary's and the pooled best shift, the reference shifts as numbers, and the correlation at each shift.
    3. The pass rule is relative to the metered farms (version 3). The six metered solar farms give the reference for the same method. Their file labels each half-hour with its end, and its timing was checked against a clear-sky calculation, so the farm series are correctly indexed. For each Primary, the gate runs the same contrast, with the same code, scan, and prior mix, on each farm's output per unit of capacity against that Primary's own sky and days (a farm needs 5 readable months to count), and takes the mean of the farms' best shifts as that Primary's reference. The gate's rule has three parts: each Primary's best shift minus its reference lies from -0.75 to +0.75 half-hours inclusive (the second decision below relaxes this part), the pooled best shift of the Primaries minus the pooled best shift of the farms (the average of every Primary-and-farm pair's contrast curves) lies from -0.5 to +0.5 half-hours inclusive, and the nights carry no CAMS irradiance. A Primary or a reference that cannot be read counts as outside the bound. The second decision below replaces "each Primary" in that rule. The gate record holds the shifts as numbers and no farm identifier, capacity, or date. The page publishes the per-Primary best shifts, the pooled best shift, and the per-Primary and pooled differences from the metered-farm reference. Those are half-hours of timing, and they are neither a capacity nor an output. The page publishes no farm name, series ID, size, date, or series.
    4. Why the rule is relative to the farms. The registered rule (a best shift of 0) cannot hold for a Primary, because a Primary's power is net load and sits later than a farm's output by the fleet's lean (west-facing panels peak later than the prior mix) and by the demand's response to the sun. A version 2 rule (a pooled range of -0.5 to +1.5 half-hours and a per-Primary range of -1.0 to +2.0, exclusive) is not the rule, because a loader one half-hour late also passes it. Against the farms as the reference, a Primary that is one half-hour later than a farm-like series differs from its reference by 1.0 half-hour and fails. Two tests check this: a Primary series delayed by one half-hour relative to a farm-like series fails, and an on-time series passes.
    5. The usable days and half-hours are fixed in advance. A weekday is a Monday to Friday that is not a bank holiday, a day is readable when its reliable CAMS hours hold at least 90% of its clear-sky irradiance, and a half-hour of one month is used when at least 3 days of each tercile have a power reading and a reliable CAMS hour there.
    6. The registered comparison and version 2 stay as diagnostics. gates.json keeps the summer-minus-winter correlations and best shifts under diagnostic_summer_minus_winter, and the best shift of the same-month comparison at the integer shifts -1, 0, and +1 under diagnostic_integer_shift. Version 2's absolute pass ranges are kept the same way, under diagnostic_v2_absolute_ranges. All three are marked as not deciding the gate.     Cause: the store's index counts half-hour ends, and the loader read it one half-hour late

    The power store's index i holds the half-hour that ends at the origin plus 30 minutes times i. The power_mw column of experiment 6's features.parquet, whose valid_time is a half-hour end stamp, matches the store at index (valid_time - 2022-01-01) / 30 minutes in every row. A loader that treats index i as the half-hour that starts there delays the power by one half-hour relative to the end stamps of the sky, the weather, and the calendar, and the same-month comparison then gives a pooled best shift of about +2 half-hours, which is too large for the fleet's lean alone.

    No earlier published result depends on the same off-by-one.     Amendment 2 to the plan of experiment 737 (2026-11-28)

    Date: 2026-11-28. The amendment applies before any scored fit and before any fit of the tuning stages.

    Reason

    The baseline's ridge penalty biases the recovered capacity downwards, and its effect cancels in the increment that the tuning worlds score, so the ridge scale is chosen where the absolute level is known. The plan borrowed the ridge value RIDGE_PER_COLUMN = 1e-3 from studies.pv_separation, and Amendment 1 does not change it. On one tuning world (never a scored world), the 405 baseline columns explain 79% of the variance of the solar column, so the penalty leaks into the capacity. The mean error of the fitted capacity is -0.09 MW at the borrowed value (a recovered share of 0.93), -0.02 MW at a tenth of it, and -0.012 MW with almost no ridge. The Student-t density's daylight scale (0.287 against the true 0.349) makes the effective penalty about 1.5 times stronger. The shrinkage is not a code error. On the same tuning world, the ridge scale barely moves the increment (the fitted capacity of the world minus the fitted capacity of the real record): the recovered share is 0.9869 at all three scales, because the shrinkage appears in both fits and cancels. The mean fitted capacity of the real record itself moves by about 0.09 MW between the scales. A ridge scale chosen on the tuning worlds' path error would therefore be an arbitrary choice.

    What changes

    1. The ridge scale is tuned and not set by hand. The baseline's ridge precision is RIDGE_PER_COLUMN times a ridge scale chosen once from 1, 0.1, and 0.01. Arm A is fitted to each series at each ridge scale with the growth standard deviation fixed at 0.30 MW a quarter. The scale with the lowest median, over the 30 series, of the largest absolute capacity error over the 18 quarters is kept, and a tie goes to the larger scale. The growth standard deviation is then chosen from 0.15, 0.30, and 0.60 MW a quarter at the chosen ridge scale, on the 120 tuning worlds.
    2. Every arm that carries the baseline ridge uses the chosen scale. Arms A, F, Qt, Ad, Af, O, and R read it from the tuned record. Arm Q keeps the ridge of studies.pv_separation, because it calls that function.
    3. G1 and G1b run after the tuning, with the tuned values. Both gates gate the bulk fits, and they do not gate the tuning, which needs only G2, G3, and G4. The stage order is the tuning (ridge, then growth standard deviation), G1 and G1b, the first unit, and the bulk fits.
    4. The compute estimate is 17.5 CPU-hours. The tuning stage takes 3.0 CPU-hours, of which the ridge step is 0.6 (90 fits of 24 seconds: 30 series at 3 ridge scales). The total stays under the 26 CPU-hour stop.
    5. The past-only gap keeps its exclusions. The Primaries that gate G4 excludes from the real-record results stay excluded from the past-only gap, as Amendment 1 sets.

    Amendment 3 to the plan of experiment 737 (2026-11-28)

    Date: 2026-11-28. The amendment applies before any scored fit. It follows the first run of gate G1 at the tuned values (ridge scale 0.1, growth standard deviation 0.15 MW a quarter), which recovered the capacity but failed the coverage bar.

    Reason

    Gate G1 recovered the capacity and failed its coverage bar, because the posterior variance of the Student-t fit is too small when the noise is Gaussian. The recovery passed: the largest gap between the mean fitted and the injected capacity was 1.14% of the mean capacity against the bar of 3%, and the recovered share was 0.994 (G1b passed at 0.990). The 80% interval covered 0.718 of the truth against the bar of 0.75 to 0.85. The fit's noise density is Student-t with 4 degrees of freedom, and its daylight scale on G1's series is 0.287 against the true Gaussian standard deviation of 0.349. The covariance (X' W X)^-1 of the converged weights assumes the Student-t noise, so it understates the variance by about (0.349 / 0.287)^2 = 1.48 when the noise is Gaussian.

    The check on the G1 series confirms the diagnosis. On the 100 stored G1 draws, the reported standard deviation (the mean over quarters) was 0.0235 MW against the empirical standard deviation of the fitted capacities of 0.0271 MW. On 42 fresh Gaussian draws of the G1 construction (seeds 737900 to 737941, with the stored reference's noise scales), the reported-over-empirical ratio and the coverage of the 80% interval were these.

    Variance Reported sd over empirical sd Coverage of the 80% interval
    (X' W X)^-1 (before the amendment) 0.87 0.705
    Scaled by the Gaussian-consistent ratio of the mean squared daylight residual to the squared Student-t scale (about 1.45) 1.05 0.787
    Sandwich with the weighted least-squares information X' W X 0.75 0.634
    Sandwich with the observed information of the Student-t likelihood 1.06 0.796

    On 12 Student-t draws (the G1b construction), the same four variances gave ratios of 1.02, 1.44, 0.82, and 1.12, and coverages of 0.741, 0.931, 0.648, and 0.810. The scalar scaling therefore inflates the interval by 44% when the noise is Student-t, and the sandwich with the weighted least-squares information understates the variance in both settings, because it treats the weights as fixed when they depend on the residuals.

    What changes

    1. The reported variance is the sandwich variance of the penalised Student-t likelihood. The score of row i is psi_i x_i, with psi_i = 5 r_i / (4 sigma_i^2 + r_i^2), where r_i is the residual and sigma_i the noise scale of the row's class. The variance of the capacities is the capacity block of A^-1 B A^-1, with A the observed information sum_i psi'_i x_i x_i' + P (P holds the ridge precision of the baseline and the precision of the capacity prior) and B = sum_i psi_i^2 x_i x_i'. The matrix B has no prior term, because the prior is not random. When the noise is Student-t, A and B agree and the result is the usual posterior variance. When the noise is Gaussian, they differ, and the result is the variance that the estimator has.
    2. The correction has no constant. It comes from the theory of M-estimators (the Huber and White sandwich), and no value in it was chosen from the coverage of any world. The scalar scaling and the weighted least-squares sandwich of the table above were rejected on their behaviour in the two noise settings, not on their coverage in the G1 bar alone.
    3. Every arm that reports an interval uses it. Arms A, F, Qt, Ad, Af, O, and R read their standard deviations from the same fit function, so all of them change. Arm Q reports none.
    4. The lag-1 autocorrelation inflation stays as it is. The variance is multiplied by (1 + r) / (1 - r) after the sandwich is computed. When the observed information is not positive definite, the fit falls back to the covariance (X' W X)^-1 and records that it did.
    5. The tuned values, the code hash, and the stored results of the real folder are stale. The code hash changes, so the review marker, the frozen code, the gates, the tuning, and G1 and G1b run again before any scored fit.

    What does not change

    The amendment changes no bar, no arm, no contrast, and no verdict rule. The G1 coverage bar stays 0.75 to 0.85, and the recovery bar stays 3%. The bars B1 to B7, the contrasts C1 and C2, the arms, the worlds, the seeds, and the verdict table are as registered. The correction is derived from theory and not from any scored world, so bar B2, the pooled coverage on the injected worlds with the real record's noise, remains the honest test of it. If B2 fails, the page reports the failure.
