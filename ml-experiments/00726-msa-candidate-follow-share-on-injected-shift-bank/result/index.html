    Does the axial-attention network carry a shift in a Primary's recent power into its forecast more than the Candidate does, on the same injected shifts?

    We asked whether the axial-attention demand network of experiment 713 carries a shift in a Primary substation's recent power into its forecast by a larger share of the shift than the Candidate network does. Both neural networks forecast a Primary's demand from its recent power and from weather. A real load transfer shifts a Primary's recent power for days or weeks. A forecast that carries the shift forward is right while a permanent change lasts and wrong after a temporary transfer ends. We added a constant shift to the recent power that each network reads, ran the stored weights of each network on the shifted power, and measured the share of the shift by which each median forecast moves. The axial-attention network moved its forecast by a larger share of the shift than the Candidate in three of the four counted comparisons, and the fourth is inconclusive. The verdict is partly supported.

    The experiment fitted nothing. It ran stored weights on shifted inputs and compared each forecast with the same weights' forecast on unshifted inputs. The final cost was 3.62 GPU-process-hours, under the reporting ceiling of 5. The figure counts the cand and msa jobs and excludes 0.207 h of CPU-only check jobs, so phase_times.tsv sums to 3.824 h in all. Everything in the sections from "Why this runs" to "Compute, order, and machine rules" is the design as it was registered before any forecast existed, written in the future tense and left unchanged except where a section says otherwise. The sections "Results", "Readings that were not run", "Disclosures from the run", and "Method" describe what happened.

    Results

    Both ways of reading "follows more" must be stated before any good or bad wording. Experiment 581's bank shifts the inputs only, never the targets, and every shift ends 14 days after the month starts. Against the bank's own truth, every shift is therefore temporary, the ideal follow share is 0 in every slice, and any following raises the error. The table gives the right response for each kind of shift in this experiment.

All five seeds of M1 come from experiment 713's run, so the design's explanation by two weight roots does not apply. All four M1 split differences are negative (-0.012 to -0.023): seeds 2 to 4 of M1 follow more than seeds 0 and 1. The page does not use the split to argue the cell back to "follows more".

The share above capacity or below zero at the raising passes is 44% (the six signed-MW Primaries) and 31% (the eight MVA-only Primaries). The result review checked the two stored Candidate files for 2025-11 and 2025-12 against the MSA command's files, and the two agree on every one of the 36 rows in n_half_hours, beyond_clip, and out_of_range.

    Readings that were not run

    Four registered readings did not run, because each needs new GPU work or a new script, and none was approved. The registered design promised them, and the verdict does not depend on them.

    • Reading 7, the safe-side share and the coverage of the central 90% interval, did not run. It would answer whether the extra following makes the forecast bands unsafe after a shift ends. Reading 7's follow ratio at q05 and q95 did run, and is in the stored report.
    • Reading 8, CRPS against the unshifted power, did not run. It would answer whether the extra following raises the error in points of capacity, which the follow share alone does not say.
    • Reading 10, the follow share on experiment 709's true level steps, did not run. It is the one reading where following is right without qualification, so it would answer whether the extra following of M1 is a strength for a permanent step. Until it runs, L1 is not called a help.
    • The rows for K and E in reading 1 did not run. Reading 1 prints the Candidate and M1 only. The rows would answer how large the absolute follow share of K and E is. Reading 2 already gives K and E against M1 and the Candidate.

    Phase 5, the attention extraction, was cut, so the page has no attention maps. It was the first cut in the registered order of cuts.

    Disclosures from the run

    Experiment 713's known-answer gate failed KA3 and KA12 and was waived, so no reading shows that the network can learn to follow a lasting step. KA3 gave a follow share of 0.1418 on a synthetic world against a threshold of 0.75, and KA12 failed too, at code hash e85b84e2ea04b3cb. The maintainer waived both checks, as experiment 715's section "Experiment 713's known-answer gate was waived" (in pull request 406) records. The follow share here is measured on fitted states and needs no such learning, and both facts apply to every reading. Every verdict of this experiment states the waiver, and the line "Experiment 713's known-answer gate was waived" in the scorer's output is that registered disclosure, not a new waiver. Experiment 726's own checks all passed, and none was waived.

    Reading S6 carries a second waiver: experiment 715's own gate failed KN6 and KN7. KN6 gave an F4 gap of 0.0288 between the follow share of a permanent step and of a temporary step, against a requirement of at least 0.60, and KN7 was not evaluated. The maintainer waived both, and experiment 715's section "Experiment 715's own known-answer gate failed KN6 and KN7" records the decision. KN6 and KN7 reach this experiment only through reading S6, which scores F4's stored forecasts.

The Opus review and its re-check passed the driver afterwards, and no phase 1 job started before then.

The Opus review found that the check KM3 could not stop the line when its input half failed, and that KC4 and KW had been listed for two fold months only. The driver was stopped during or just after one Candidate zero-world job (root seeds713, 2025-11, seed 2), whose status was not recorded, and before any MSA job started. With the manager's agreement, compare km3 now also requires the stored result of the input half to say passed, phases.sh runs KC4 and KW on all 8 fold months, bounds its retry loop at 720 one-minute waits, checks that the data folder exists, and refuses unknown phase names. An Opus re-check runs before the driver resumes.

    Disclosure: the Candidate's lag encoder clips its input, and check KC5 first ignored the clip. The design said that the Candidate's lag columns are not clipped. That sentence was wrong. The encoder standardises the day lag and the week lag with one mean and one standard deviation over the training rows of all 16 Primaries, then clips the result at 5 standard deviations. The clip is the deployed behaviour, so the Candidate's files hold its true response to the shifted panel, and no forecast file or stored hash changes. Check KC5 expected one constant move per Primary, which holds only where no row is clipped.

    Order of events. Phase 1 jobs 1 to 11 ran under the earlier KC5, at commit 129fca96a, and finished with exit 0. Job 12, the all-shifted world of fold 2026-01 with seeds 0 and 1, stopped on a KC5 failure before it wrote any file: the shift divided by the move of the lag column was 3.8638795 at Primary 11 against 3.8755734 at the other rows. Nothing was waived. The fix tightens the gate: KC5 now expects, for each row, the move clip((shifted - m) / s) - clip((clean - m) / s) with the stored lag mean m and standard deviation s, asserts it per row to float32 precision, keeps the count of rows moved outside a window at 0, tests the constant per Primary only on rows where neither value is clipped, treats a row clipped at the same bound before and after as expected not to move, and prints the clipped-row count per Primary. No tolerance or threshold was loosened. The fix is commit 9fd66fd48, where e00726_candidate_panel.py, e00726_common.py, and e00726_score.py have the SHA-256 hashes d90e4e79937e3f6332c75926ec8b094ac9ace102cbfd3a1c3b0bc6e412fdee2f, ba3995c9a71ec917d674335658350a054e79d2d8695f95384e3d59d92acc8dbc, and 36c7f6bbdde31aca425d4c9af6c54485e0befbb4802a13ce87f47da01a6ff73f. Later commits only rewrap docstrings, with identical syntax trees, and add the clip script. The job records of jobs 1 to 11 name 129fca96a, and the records of every job from job 12 onward name the head that the driver runs from, a descendant of 9fd66fd48, so the mixed commits are disclosed here. Job 11 is the only finished job in fold 2026-01. It is the host-only world, whose KC5 ran on a set that does not hold Primary 11, and its files are valid.

    Caveat for reading 3 and the sensitivity rule, stated before any score was read: the Candidate's clip binds at some Primaries and passes in fold 2026-01. These Primary and pass figures are the KC5 audit's approximations of the 2026-01 lag statistics, and no stored file holds them. The 2025-11 and 2025-12 files were not computed pass by pass. Both files match the MSA command's own files on every one of the 36 rows in n_half_hours, beyond_clip, and out_of_range, so a clip share from them may be quoted, with the caveat of section "Results" that it counts every shifted half-hour of the window.

    Why this runs

    Experiment 713 reported a follow share for M1 alone, and no follow share for the Candidate. Experiment 713 planned the comparison and could not run it, for two reasons.

    • The Candidate's stored injected forecasts hold other events. The files under experiment 667's P7_D50_<month>/work/inject/ were written with experiment 650's event generator: random depths from 0.03 to 0.50 of capacity, random signs, free onsets, ramps, and mirrored receivers. Experiment 581's event definition has no meaning on those files.
    • The two neural networks were scored on different origins. The 30-day shifts of experiment 581's bank start 16 days before the fold month. Experiment 713's shifts command forecast M1 at origins before the month start wherever the ensemble table held a row. The Candidate's test rows hold origins inside the fold month only.

    The CRPS contrasts of experiment 713 on experiment 709's bank leave the question open. On that bank M1 minus the Candidate is +1.28 [+1.08, +1.49] points of capacity from the onset of a temporary switch to 7 days after the switch ends, and -3.23 [-3.74, -2.72] at origins 7 to 28 days after a lasting step. The audit of experiment 713 attributes the lasting-step gain to any network that reads its last 14 days, because K minus the Candidate is -2.75 and E (M1 with a convolutional stem) minus the Candidate is -3.25. A CRPS contrast mixes how far a forecast moves with how wide its bands are. The follow share measures the movement alone, on a bank whose depths and lengths are balanced across Primaries.

    The result changes what the write-ups of the MSA line may say and ports no change. Against the bank's own truth every shift is temporary and the ideal follow share is 0, so following more raises the error in every slice. The lasting-step slice counts as help only if a shift of 7 to 28 days' standing stands in for a permanent step, which the inputs at those origins cannot tell apart from a temporary shift. Experiment 709's level steps (reading 10) are the one reading where following is right without qualification. production_status is not-for-production under every verdict. That is a stated departure from the ml-experiment skill, which flags a small gain experiment as candidate: this experiment is a diagnostic of how the forecast behaves, not a change to the forecast, and a "M1 follows slightly more" verdict carries no claim of a better forecast.

    Honest expectation: M1 follows more than the Candidate in both slices, by 0.03 to 0.15 of the depth. Experiment 713 stores a follow share of 0.181 for M1 on a nearby set of origins. The arms of experiment 581 that were trained with synthetic shifts, as the Candidate is, followed 0.038 to 0.104.

    What is compared

    Terms. M1 is experiment 713's selected arm. K is M1 without attention, E is M1 with a convolutional stem, and N is M1 without history rows. C is the Candidate, the demand network of experiment 667 (arm P7+D50). The six signed-MW Primaries are D5, D9, D10, D11, D14, and D16. The eight MVA-only Primaries are D2, D3, D4, D8, D12, D19, D21, and D22. D15 and D18 are forecast and reported apart. The native origin of a forecast is 09:00 UTC on the origin day. The newest power reading that either neural network reads is stamped 24 hours before the native origin. CRPS is the continuous ranked probability score, in points of capacity.

    Network Stored weights Seeds Fold months Role

    M1 Experiment 713, stage 3 0 to 4 8 Treatment of both counted contrasts

    The bank is experiment 581's. e00581_bank.contaminate(clean, month_start_idx, r) adds a constant shift to the power of every Primary over a window that ends 14 days after the fold month starts. Primary p in pass r gets combination (p + r) % 12 of a depth of 0.15, 0.30, or 0.50 of capacity and a length of 3, 7, 15, or 30 days. Passes 0 to 11 raise the power and passes 12 to 23 lower it, so across the 24 passes every Primary meets every depth and length with both signs. The shift enters the inputs only, and the targets are never shifted. Experiment 713 ran passes 0 to 11 only, so this experiment runs all 24 passes and reports every reading by sign.

    The follow share of a network at an event is the mean, over the scored rows, of the median forecast on the shifted panel minus the median forecast of the same weights on the unshifted panel, times the sign of the shift, divided by the depth. This is follow_q50 of e00581_tails._aggregate. A network that ignores recent power scores 0, and a network that carries the whole shift forward scores 1. The unshifted forecast is the network's ordinary stored forecast, so no second prediction is needed. Quantiles are averaged over seeds before the difference is taken, as in experiment 581.

    At origin days 14 and 15 no input shows that the shift has ended, so T1 mixes two behaviours. The newest visible power reading at d = 14 lies inside the shift, and the newest usable history row of M1 is 2 days old. T1 therefore mixes how far a network followed during the shift with how fast it lets go after the end. The follow share by origin day (report-only reading 4) separates the two.

    The lasting-step slice stands in for a lasting step. Experiment 581's bank holds no shift that lasts beyond day 14, and both neural networks were fine-tuned with experiment 581's synthetic shifts, so both were trained not to follow a shift of the bank's depths and lengths. L1 therefore measures how far each neural network learned to ignore a shift of 7 to 28 days' standing. Report-only reading 10 adds the follow share on experiment 709's true level steps.

    The lead band is experiment 581's: at least 24 and under 120 hours from power_fcst_init_time. The bands of 120 to 240 hours and 24 to 240 hours are report-only. A fold month holds, in the after-switch slice, 144 events at the six signed-MW Primaries and 192 at the eight MVA-only Primaries, and half of each count in the lasting-step slice.

    What is run

    Every job is prediction only, with stored weights. No fit, refit, or fine-tune runs, and no stored state was trained on a row of the bank.

    The counted Candidate run shifts the host only. The Candidate's neighbour summaries read the neighbours' power, and experiment 581's bank shifts all 16 Primaries in one direction, which feeds the Candidate a regional move that no real switch makes. A real transfer moves the donor the opposite way. The all-shifted world therefore inflates the Candidate's follow share and biases T1 and L1 towards zero. The host-only world removes the bias:

    • The 16 Primaries are split into sets in which no member is an electrical neighbour of another member. The neighbour lists are those of c650.world(), which e00400_blend.nged_neighbours builds from the interconnection table, so the lists are symmetric. A greedy colouring by degree gives 4 sets, and the triangle D10, D12, D15 means that no colouring needs fewer than 3. The implementer confirms the count on the CPU in check KP.
    • For each fold month, seed, pass, and set, the new module builds the panel in which only the set's members carry their shifts of that pass, rebuilds the Candidate's lag columns and signal block from that panel, and forecasts the test rows of the set's members with the saved weights.
    • The host-only forecast of a pass is the union of the sets' files, with one forecast of every Primary, and that Primary the only shifted reader of its own neighbourhood.

    The Candidate in the all-shifted world is report-only. The module also runs the panel of e00581_bank.contaminate unchanged, at seeds 0 and 1, on all 8 fold months and all 24 passes. That panel compares with experiment 709, whose bank shifted every input of the Candidate in the same way. Its prediction is that the Candidate follows more in the all-shifted world than in the host-only world.

    The Candidate's test rows are cut to the rows that the scorer reads. The module keeps the origins with d from 0 to 20 and lead times up to 240 hours, and in the host-only world keeps the rows of the set's members. The cut roughly halves the disk (an uncut file is about 12.9 MB, so 960 uncut files would be about 12.4 GB) and the Candidate's time per file. The check worlds forecast every test row.

    M1, K, and E run in the all-shifted world only. For each fold month, seed, and pass, the new MSA command standardises the all-shifted panel with the fold's stored mean and standard deviation, swaps the panel into the source as the history power (as experiment 713's cmd_bank does), and forecasts all 16 Primaries. M1 reads no neighbour, so its forecast of a Primary is the same in both worlds, and check KM4 shows it.

    Experiment 713 clips the standardised history power at 5 standard deviations, and a shift of 0.50 of capacity reaches the clip. In the coldest fold month the standard deviation of a Primary's cleaned power is 0.07 to 0.21 of capacity, so a shift of 0.50 is 2.4 to 7.0 standard deviations. The clip caps the follow share of M1 mechanically. The Candidate's lag encoder also clips its standardised lag at 5 standard deviations of one global standard deviation. Both arms are clipped, and the clip diagnostics below give each arm's share. A shift of 0.50 also leaves the physical range at some Primaries. Three measures answer these effects:

    • The MSA command prints, for every job, the share of shifted history half-hours beyond 5 standard deviations, by depth and sign. The first Candidate job of each all-shifted month writes the matching share for the Candidate's lag encoder, which the scorer carries beside it.
    • The scorer prints the share of shifted half-hours above capacity or below zero, by group and sign.
    • T1 and L1 on the shifts of depth 0.15 and 0.30 alone are a pre-registered sensitivity reading, which the 0.50 sign rule of the decision rule uses.

    The scorer joins four frames on time_series_id, power_fcst_init_time, and valid_time: the Candidate on the shifted panel, the Candidate's stored forecast, the MSA arm on the shifted panel, and the MSA arm's stored forecast. The MSA command reads its origins from the Candidate's stored forecast on the unshifted panel, so the MSA jobs do not wait for the Candidate's jobs. A key that any frame lacks is dropped for every network, and the scorer prints the dropped count per fold month, pass, and slice and asserts five properties: every network of a contrast holds the same key set; no origin lies before the month start; every valid time lies before the holdout months; every forecast quantile is finite; and no in-month origin with d from 0 to 20 is dropped.

    Decision rule and verdict

    The two counted contrasts are T1 and L1, each at the six signed-MW Primaries and at the eight MVA-only Primaries. T1 is the follow share of M1 minus the host-only follow share of C in the after-switch slice, and L1 is the same difference in the lasting-step slice, both at seeds 0 to 4. Each contrast is the mean over events of the paired difference, with every fold month weighted equally. The interval is a percentile bootstrap that resamples the 8 fold months and then the 24 passes within each fold month, with 20,000 draws, at 98.75%. A positive T1 means that M1 carries a finished shift forward more than the Candidate, and a positive L1 means that M1 carries a standing shift forward more than the Candidate. A percentile bootstrap with 8 clusters tends to give intervals slightly too narrow, by about a factor of the square root of 7/8 in standard error. The write-up states the factor and applies no correction.

    The smallest difference that counts is 0.05 of the depth, and the stored follow shares of M1 show that the interval may be wider than that. The 16 stored score files of M1 give a mean follow share of 0.181 over 8 fold months and 2 seeds, a standard deviation of 0.066 across the 8 fold-month means (a standard error of 0.023), and a root mean square difference of 0.038 between seed 0 and seed 1 within a fold month. These files cover passes 0 to 11 and origins from day 4 to the end, including some before the month start, so they guide scale only. A paired contrast removes part of the month-to-month spread, so the expected half-width of a counted interval lies between the seed noise of about 0.007 and about 0.07.

    The reading "no detectable difference" may be unreachable, and then not supported is too. That reading needs an interval inside +-0.05, and the expected half-width runs up to 0.07. A result with no difference would then read inconclusive.

    The split-seed guard stays. Before any contrast, the scorer prints, for C and for M1 separately, the difference between the follow share from seeds 0 and 1 and the follow share from seeds 2 to 4. A counted cell whose own split-seed difference exceeds 0.025 in absolute value reads "cannot tell". The guard confounds seed noise with the weights' root, because seeds 0 and 1 of the Candidate come from experiment 667's chain and seeds 2 to 4 from experiment 713's candidate_seeds/ chain. The Candidate's failed seed stays in, as in experiment 713 (seed 0 at the eight MVA-only Primaries in the first fold month), and the scorer prints every counted cell with that fold month left out as a report-only reading. The guard can fire by chance in roughly 1 cell in 20.

    A cell is one counted contrast in one group, and a cell's reading is the first row that holds. D is the point estimate and [L, U] the 98.75% interval. A single-seed D pairs seed s of M1 with seed s of C.

    Order Cell reading Condition
    1 cannot tell The split-seed rule fires, or the cell would otherwise read row 2, 3, or 4 and D changes sign when the 0.50 shifts are left out
    2 M1 follows more L > 0, D >= +0.05, and D is positive for at least 4 of the 5 single seeds
    3 M1 follows less U < 0, D <= -0.05, and D is negative for at least 4 of the 5 single seeds
    4 small difference The interval excludes zero, and the sign holds for at least 4 of the 5 single seeds
    5 no detectable difference L > -0.05 and U < +0.05
    6 cannot tell Any other case

    The sign rule of row 1 applies only to cells that would otherwise read rows 2 to 4. A "no detectable difference" cell has a point estimate near zero, and noise flips its sign whether or not the 0.50 shifts are present, so the rule would turn nearly every such cell into "cannot tell".

    Order Verdict Condition
    1 refuted "M1 follows less" in both groups of T1 or in both groups of L1
    2 supported "M1 follows more" in all four cells
    3 partly supported "M1 follows more" in at least one cell, and "M1 follows less" in no cell
    4 small gain, written as "M1 follows slightly more" "small difference" with a positive D in all four cells
    5 not supported "no detectable difference" in all four cells
    6 inconclusive Any other case

    The refuted row comes first on purpose, because the hypothesis names both slices: the row fires on "M1 follows less" in both groups of one contrast even when the other contrast reads "M1 follows more".

    What each outcome would mean.

    • supported, partly supported, or "M1 follows slightly more": the MSA network is more exposed than the Candidate to a switch that has ended, and tracks a standing shift further. The size of T1 times a typical depth gives the bias in points of capacity that a real switch leaves in the week after the switch ends.
    • not supported: the two networks carry the same share of a shift. The CRPS difference of +1.28 on experiment 709's bank then comes from the width of the bands, from the mirrored neighbours that the Candidate reads, or from differences between the two banks.
    • refuted: the Candidate follows more. The audit's explanation of the lasting-step gain would need another cause, and audit trigger 2 of the ml-experiment skill fires.

    Every reading carries experiment 713's disclosure. Experiment 713's known-answer checks KA3 and KA12 failed and its gate was waived, so no reading of the MSA line shows that the network can learn to follow a lasting step. The follow share here is measured on fitted states and needs no such learning, and the write-up states both facts.

    Leakage and disclosures

    No forecast reads power stamped later than 24 hours before the native origin, and no weight was trained on the bank. The target-row weather of the MSA arms comes from the origin's own ensemble run, and check KW prints the statement, because experiment 404's scores above 24 hours were voided for a weather look-ahead.

    The write-up states nine limits.

    • The MSA arms read ERA5 and CAMS history weather, a lab shortcut of experiment 713 at history ages of 2 to 5 days, which does not touch the power path that the bank shifts.
    • Both neural networks were fine-tuned with experiment 581's synthetic shifts, so L1 measures how far each learned to ignore a shift of 7 to 28 days' standing.
    • The counted contrasts shift one host with unshifted neighbours, and no world includes the opposite move of a donor.
    • Every shift ends 14 days after a month starts, and the lasting-step slice stands in for a lasting step.
    • At origin days 14 and 15 no input shows that the shift has ended, so T1 mixes following with letting go.
    • Both arms clip their standardised power history at 5 standard deviations, and a shift of 0.50 reaches either clip and can leave the physical range.
    • Shifts are constant in megawatts across the day, which matches the working assumption for a load transfer and ignores the shape of the transferred load.
    • A percentile bootstrap with 8 clusters gives intervals slightly too narrow.
    • Experiment 713's KA3 and KA12 disclosure, and experiment 715's KN6 and KN7 disclosure for reading S6.

    Order of cuts, if the projection is above the ceiling: drop phase 5; then the all-shifted world of the Candidate; then restrict the lead days to the 24 to 120 hour band; then the two-seed plan, which keeps seeds 0 and 1 of C and M1, all 8 fold months, and all 24 passes. Under the two-seed plan both single seeds carry the sign of D, and no split-seed guard applies. No fold month and no pass is cut from a counted reading, because the 8 fold months are the resampled unit and the 24 passes balance depth, length, and sign. The manager approves any cut before phase 1.

    The host-only fallback fires only after the first two cuts. The colouring gives 4 sets, so a count trigger cannot fire. The remaining trigger is a projection of phase 1 above 1.5 GPU-process-hours after phase 5 and the all-shifted Candidate run have been cut. The fallback would swap the counted world for the biased all-shifted world to save about half an hour, so it comes last. Then the counted Candidate run is the all-shifted world at seeds 0 to 4, the host-only world runs at seeds 0 and 1 on all 24 passes as a guard, a cell reads "M1 follows more" only if the host-only contrast at seeds 0 and 1 has the same sign, and the write-up names the fallback in its first paragraph.

    No seed, fold month, or pass is added or dropped after a follow share is printed. The experiment has no screen and no stop rule on a score, because prediction is short and every fold month is needed for the interval.

    Timing. The run starts straight after F4's counted fits, on the manager's word. Phase 0 can run as soon as the second code review passes. The S6 reading waits for F4's stored MSA forecasts on the second generator. Before phase 1 the implementer books the GPU slots with the manager against the streams of F4 and F7, and sends "predictions starting" with the frozen checkout's commit to the manager before phase 0 and again before phase 1.

    Machine rules, all through run.sh:

    • Every command runs under systemd-run --user --scope with MemorySwapMax=0 and OOMPolicy=continue. MemoryMax is 12G for the MSA command and 9G for the Candidate command, a default that run.sh sets per script and that the MEMORY_MAX environment variable overrides. The MSA jobs run one at a time, because the smoke test measured a host peak of 8.0 GB for one MSA job.
    • One thread for every numerical library (OMP_NUM_THREADS, MKL_NUM_THREADS, OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS, POLARS_MAX_THREADS, and RAYON_NUM_THREADS are 1, and torch.set_num_threads(1)).
    • The wrapper calls the virtual environment's Python directly after uv sync --group gpu, and never uv run inside the scope. The preflight KP needs the gpu group as well, because the neighbour lists come from a module that imports torch. Run every script through run.sh or uv run --no-sync, never plain uv run, which would re-sync and remove the gpu group.
    • At most two GPU processes run at once, each under 6 GB of GPU memory, and the wrapper refuses to start when under 25 GB of host memory is available.
    • One process per job, atomic writes, and a finished job is skipped only when its own output file exists under this experiment's data folder.
    • The Candidate's seeds 2 to 4 run one seed per process, because three seeds in one process reached the 12 GB cap in experiment 713.
    • No download, no new data source, no spend, and no change under src/ or packages/. The experiment writes nothing into the data folders of experiments 581, 650, 667, 709, 713, 714, or 715, and edits no file of experiment 715.

    Method

    Architecture.

    • M1, K, E, and N are the stage 3 states of experiment 713's axial-attention network over a matrix of the last 14 days by 48 half-hours, unchanged, and run from the frozen checkout.
    • The Candidate is experiment 667's demand network (arm P7+D50), which reads a day-old lag, weekly lags, and electrical-neighbour summaries, run from its saved weights models/P5/<month>_d0_s<seed>.pt.
    • The shift enters the power inputs only, through the same visibility rule as the real power in both networks, and the targets are never shifted.

    Training regime.

    • No network is trained, refitted, or fine-tuned. GPU time is prediction only, 3.62 GPU-process-hours in all.
    • The stored weights were fitted by experiments 667 and 713 on a monthly refit, with experiment 581's synthetic shifts in fine-tuning.

    Measurement.

    • The bank is experiment 581's, 24 passes over 8 fold months, and the counted rows are the origins with d from 0 to 20 at leads from 24 to under 120 hours.
    • The score is the follow share of the median forecast, and the counted contrasts are T1 and L1 by group, by the decision rule above.
    • The counted Candidate run shifts the host only; the all-shifted run is report-only.
