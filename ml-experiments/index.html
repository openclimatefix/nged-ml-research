    ML experiments

    Each folder here is one small experiment that tests one hypothesis about improving the production forecast, and the experiments are grouped into themes. A theme has three pages: a Summary (where one has been written) that states the answer so far, an Experiments page that lists every experiment tried, and a Research plan that lists what is planned next. The folders are named <id>-<slug>, where the id is a five-digit integer that orders the experiments by the order in which they were started. A folder holds result.md and its figures.

    Themes

    • Calibration and uncertainty: Whether the forecast's quantiles and ensemble members are well calibrated, and how to widen or sharpen them.
    • Curtailment cost: Scoring a forecast by the curtailment cost of its errors, and by how well its tails are calibrated.
    • Demand inputs: Which extra inputs lower the error of the substation demand forecast.
    • Differentiable physics: long history and drift: Whether a differentiable-physics model of Primary substation demand can use old years of history and cope with drift in the demand level.
    • Generator forecasting: Forecasting the output of solar and wind farms.
    • Hierarchy and pooling: Forecasts that share information across substations or across levels of the electricity network hierarchy.
    • Lagged power and error correction: Where recent measured power should enter the forecast, and how to correct the forecast's recent errors.
    • Production reference and best guess: How the production forecast compares with the best-guess recipes and with a perfect weather input.
    • Retraining and training history: How often to refit the XGBoost models, and how much training history to give them.
    • Solar disaggregation: Separating embedded solar from substation demand.
    • Switching detectors: Detecting load transfers between substations, called switching events, from power data alone.
    • Switching labels in the forecast: Using switching labels to forecast the demand under the normal running arrangement.
    • Weather products and blending: Which weather data improves the forecast, and whether blending several weather products helps.

    How an experiment is recorded

    The front matter of result.md is the only place that records an experiment's theme, verdict, and status, and the theme pages build their tables from it. The Experiments table of each theme, the planned-experiments table of each Research plan, and the candidates table below are generated from the front matter when the documentation is built, so adding a finished experiment means writing its result.md and editing nothing else. The ml-experiment skill (.claude/skills/ml-experiment/SKILL.md) lists the front matter fields and their allowed values, including the optional kind and promoted_to fields of the research default rule.

    uv run python scripts/mkdocs_hooks/experiment_tables.py --check validates every front matter block, and the documentation build runs the same check. uv run python scripts/mkdocs_hooks/experiment_tables.py --theme <theme> prints a theme's Experiments table as markdown.

    The research default

    The research default rule fixes one forecast per target family and one test for adding a change to it. The log of versions lists each family's versions, for example "Primary demand research default 1". An experiment with kind: change in its front matter is measured against the current version, and the rule says when a win is adopted.

    Data processing

    The data-processing page lists the processing that the experiments showed the data needs. Each entry says whether it applies to experiments, to production, or to both.

    Background

    The background pages state what earlier experiments established about the data sets and the scoring rules, and a planner reads them before planning an experiment.

    Glossary

    The glossary defines the terms that the experiment pages and the theme Summary pages use without redefining. Each definition links the experiment or code that fixes its meaning.

    Planned experiments and their order

    Each theme's Research plan page holds its own priority table and the detailed proposals for that theme. A planned experiment has a result.md with status planned, committed before any code runs. An idea that has no folder yet is marked "proposed" in the priority table and carries a tentative id, P1, P2, and so on, which the proposal sections use as headings. A proposal receives its numeric id when its plan is committed, and the tentative id then disappears. The design under test is the staged probabilistic forecast, in which stage 1 turns weather into power, stage 2 calibrates the result, uses lagged power, and blends weather products, and a coupling step restores coherent ensemble members.

    The sample resolves gains of roughly 0.3 points of capacity or more, and no smaller gains. A point of capacity is 1% of a series' capacity. The sample is 16 Primary substations, 9 generator series, and one validation year with 12 months. The Research plan pages flag the experiments whose planned contrasts the sample cannot resolve.

    Candidates for the next release

    These experiments are flagged candidate and are not yet adopted. The flags are proposals for the maintainer to confirm. The maintainer decides which candidates to port into a new model release. The release log records which experiments each release adopted.

    00494 simplify-demand-network-refit-cadence-and-pretrain-once retraining-and-training-history partly supported In five scored months, the demand neural network pretrained once and fine-tuned each month scored within 0.03 points of the same network pretrained at every refit, and would drop about 42 of the 80 GPU seconds that refitting one network takes.     00493 solar-annual-against-quarterly-refits metered-solar-farms supported The solar neural network can be refitted once a year instead of every quarter: for both networks we tested, the quantile score changes by less than 0.6%, inside the 1% limit, with little margin for the encoder and from one refit date and one scored year. One further comparison fails: at Solar E the one-pass network with annual refits is 1.3% worse than the 51-member encoder with quarterly refits, and most of that gap comes from the network design and not from the schedule.
    00492 two-constant-tail-widening-in-place-of-table-p-for-demand calibration-and-uncertainty partly supported At the eight MVA-only Primaries, one lower constant and one upper constant per metering group, fitted on earlier months, recalibrate the demand forecast as well as table P (20 factors per metering group) does, so table P can be dropped there. At the six signed-MW Primaries, the constants leave q95 coverage 0.011 to 0.013 below table P's, so table P stays. Wind and solar were not tested.
    00483 uncertainty-rerun-on-the-first-release-demand-candidates calibration-and-uncertainty partly supported The first-release demand candidates are too wide on the lower tail and too narrow on the upper tail, so a widening of both tails makes the lower tail worse while it repairs the upper one. Widening only the upper tail, or applying one factor per tail, fixes the eight MVA-only Primaries for A-pool and A-gam-in alike. At the signed-MW six, one factor per tail narrowly misses its pass rules, and cold winter nights stay under-covered there.
    00481 seed-averaging-diminishing-returns-for-demand-and-wind production-reference-and-best-guess partly supported A second seed gives the largest gain and later seeds add little: for Primary demand, two seeds score 0.035 points better than one (the 99.55% interval is +0.025 to +0.053, across the 0.03 bar for porting), and each later step gains at most 0.011. For wind, one seed scores within the minimum detectable effect of ten, though a second seed gains 0.041 points. Averaging seeds of the lagged neural network S1 is weaker than adding the lagless head: at two networks, S1 with the lagless head scores 3.441 against 3.628 for two averaged S1 seeds. Each added seed costs another fit per monthly refit.
    00472 anchor-plus-correction-with-a-strong-penalty-and-a-seed-averaged-anchor lagged-power-and-error-correction partly supported A stronger penalty does not make the lag correction safe. A penalty of 100 shrinks the correction to almost nothing and keeps only 18% of its gain, so the penalties tried (1e-3 to 1 never bound, and 100 binds) do not both limit the correction and keep the gain. Averaging the lagless forecast over three seeds and correcting that average with the recent readings scored better in this experiment. At the six Primaries metered in signed megawatts it scores 3.09 points of capacity on rows with no labelled event, against 3.62 for the averaged lagless forecast, 3.39 for K2-S1, and 3.51 for B-mix, and AvgAnchor serves four neural networks, like K2-S1. Inside real load-shift events it is not shown to be safe (0.30 points worse than its anchor on corrected actuals, inconclusive). It fails the coverage clause of the tail gate as written and is unreplicated, so it is a candidate for the demand assembly's held-out test and not an adopted design. The results are interim, over five scored months.
    00470 first-release-demand-forecaster-assembly-and-comparison production-reference-and-best-guess inconclusive Pretraining the neural network on past weather and solar data (ERA5 and CAMS) gets the first-release forecaster almost as far as adding a second, lagless forecast does. The pretrained network also holds up better on cold winter nights and 12 to 36 hours ahead. K2-425A scores 0.07 to 0.10 points of capacity better on ordinary rows, but that gap is within the noise of five months, so the lagged-plus-lagless design adds nothing the data can confirm over pretraining. The first release therefore rests on a choice between the plain five-seed pretrained network (simplest, no lagless forecast, no GAM) and the same network with the GAM forecast as an extra input (0.9% better at the eight, inside the noise, and needs the GAM at serving time). Every score here is selection-biased, because the five months that choose the candidate also score it.
    00461 wind-and-solar-retrain-with-p99-9-effective-capacity generator-forecasting partly supported Setting each wind farm's capacity to the 99.9th percentile of its earlier power and retraining keeps the forecast as accurate and changes the share of actuals above the 99% quantile only slightly (0.96% to 0.88%; all three farms were already under 1.5%). For solar the same change meets the pre-registered rule only by a hair (share 1.50%, score 0.4% worse), fails it if any one series is dropped, makes Solar A's score 2.3% worse, and leaves Solar E at 2.4%.
    00457 gam-stage-1-forecast-as-an-input-to-the-pooled-mixture-density-network production-reference-and-best-guess small gain Giving the pretrained neural network the GAM's forecast as one extra input improves the forecast a little: CRPS falls by 0.036 points at the eight MVA-only Primaries, and the pooled cold-night and tail scores do not get worse. The gain is as large as the fit-to-fit noise, so it is real but marginal. At the signed-MW six the gain (0.035) is half that group's minimum detectable effect (0.070). The input needs the GAM in the live service with its monthly refit. Using the GAM as the base that the neural network corrects made forecasts worse (+0.291), because the GAM starts from a worse forecast than the pretrained neural network and fine-tuning does not recover.
    00454 lag-trust-mixture-and-ensemble-constructs-for-lagged-and-lagless-heads lagged-power-and-error-correction partly supported Mixing lagged and lagless forecasts beats averaging several lagged forecasts, so the lagless forecast adds accuracy that more lagged networks do not: two lagged networks averaged recover only 30% of the gain of a 50/50 mixture. Giving the lagged network the distance between each of its three lagged power readings and the lagless forecast (S1) and mixing two S1 networks with two lagless forecasts scores 0.12 points of capacity better than B-mix on rows with no labelled event at the six metered-in-megawatts Primaries, at twice the networks per forecast. Whether the gain survives inside real events is not settled, because the event rows are few.
    00425 best-mdn-architecture-assembly-with-ablations production-reference-and-best-guess partly supported Pretraining the pooled mixture density network on ERA5-and-CAMS rows before fine-tuning it on ensemble rows lowered the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) by 0.050 points of capacity (-0.078 to -0.021) at the six signed-MW Primaries and by 0.124 (-0.219 to -0.048) at the eight MVA-only Primaries. The gain is clear at the MVA-only eight and probable at the signed-MW six, where the interval just misses the registered bar of -0.03. The assembled network beat the monthly XGBoost recipe and BG2 at both of those groups and trailed BG2 at D15 and D18, so pretraining is a candidate for a numbered release of the production forecast and the Primary embedding is the component to protect.
    00424 neural-mva-export-gated-signed-ens-serving-test production-reference-and-best-guess supported With ensemble weather forecasts instead of recorded weather, the neural network with the export gate still forecasts signed power on reverse-flow half-hours much better than the same network with the gate shut (a gain of 15.7 points of capacity, 95% interval 13.8 to 17.4, against 19.2 with recorded weather), and it harms the forecast by no more than 0.05 points where no solar farm exports. The gate opened at the same two of the eight apparent-power-only substations as before. An unplanned finding is that the network trained on recorded weather and applied unchanged to ensemble weather scored 5.5 points better (95% interval 4.4 to 6.6) than the network retrained on ensemble weather, so how to train on ensemble weather is the open question.
    00422 mdn-train-with-varied-origin-hour-and-age-inputs-stale-input-step-2 production-reference-and-best-guess partly supported Training the pooled neural network with the forecast made at eight different hours of the day removes almost all of its extra error when the newest power reading is 12 hours late (0.18 down to 0.04 points at the six signed substations). The two extra age inputs did not help, so the simplest recipe is enough. Training this way raised the error on fresh inputs by 0.04 to 0.11 points at the eight MVA-only substations, which is within the range that seed-to-seed noise could produce for the lower values, and training with some readings missing cut the missing-readings error from 2.4 to 0.3 points. These are five test months, so every number is interim.
    00420 neural-mva-export-gated-signed-final production-reference-and-best-guess partly supported A neural network with a gate that decides once per Primary substation whether it exports power forecasts signed power much better than the same network with the gate shut on the half-hours with reverse flow (7.1 against 26.3 points of capacity) and does no harm beyond the registered tolerance (+0.03 points at D5) where no solar farm exports. Most of that gain comes from letting the forecast go below zero at all, because the ungated network scores the same on reverse-flow half-hours, and the gate's job is to stop invented reverse flow where there is none. Adding a PV-proportional variance did not help, so the next step is a serving test with real ensemble forecasts, not a further change to the network.
    00418 mdn-pretrain-on-era5-and-cams-then-fine-tune-on-ens retraining-and-training-history supported Teaching the neural network first on six years of past weather and power, then on the forecast archive, lowers its error by about 0.06 points of capacity (2.1%) at the six signed-direction substations and 0.16 points (4.2%) at the eight apparent-power-only substations over 12 test months. Changing how much of the network is retrained, or adding forecast-like noise, changes the score by less than the interval of the estimate. With only 6 months of forecast archive on 4 test months the gain is about 1.3 points, although the ENS-only baseline degrades badly there.
    00399 xgboost-short-history-start-cut-d15-d18 retraining-and-training-history partly supported Dropping the noisy first weeks of D15 and D18 from the training rows helps the monthly XGBoost forecast only a little: about 1% lower error at both, mostly in the first scored months, when the history was shortest. Cutting 6 months is not safe, because it made D18 worse. The larger problem at D15 is a level error that no start cut fixes: the median forecast is about 21 points of capacity too low, against 3 for the generalised additive model (GAM) stack, so the next experiment should look at D15's level, not its start.
    00396 xgboost-recipe-screen-demand-calendar-winter-hyperparameters-ensemble-summary production-reference-and-best-guess partly supported Giving the weekly XGBoost demand model the ensemble's view of the weather, either its mean and spread or a model trained on the ensemble mean, lowered the error by 0.4% to 2.7% in both test months, and no other change we tried did so as clearly. Holiday columns help on Easter bank holidays but raised the error on all days in January and at the signed-MW six at Easter, and winter weather columns do not help, so a full-year test should start with the ensemble-input arms.
    00381 simple-weekly-xgboost-demand-against-gam-stack production-reference-and-best-guess partly supported A single XGBoost model per Primary, refitted weekly, is as good as the GAM stack at 9 of 14 Primaries and better on average at the eight whose meters hide reverse flow (D8 by 1.6 points; clearly so at D8, D12, and D22), so the stack's extra parts do not pay at those eight. At the six Primaries with signed readings the XGBoost model is level on average (+0.08, not distinguishable from zero) but worse at D11 and D14 and has a narrower upper tail, so the stack still protects against curtailment better there: its avoidable curtailment cost at a limit of 0.9 times the maximum observed export is lower at 4 of 6. The input changes that helped in practice were a daily solar summary (-0.05) and a 1-year half-life (-0.02); an Economy 7 input did nothing, and no input tried fixed the 4.9-point under-forecast of sunny demand at D12.
    00357 wind-default-2-monthly-head-longer-retest generator-forecasting partly supported Retraining Wind farms research default 2's head every month improves the forecast slightly, by about 0.04 to 0.05 points over the scored year and about 0.10 points in the latest 2.5 months, and no scope shows harm to the score. The scored-year interval still touches zero, so the change is a candidate and is not adopted. The upper tail stays too heavy (1.7% to 2.0% of observations above the 0.99 quantile), so retraining does not fix it.
    00348 wind-plant-mdn-upper-tail generator-forecasting partly supported The frozen physics plant with a mixture head stays about 0.23 points of capacity better than the research default over days 1 to 10, but none of the four tail fixes inside the head brings the top quantile into calibration without losing score at that quantile, and none of their intervals excludes zero. Blending the plant's residual band with the default's XGBoost quantiles gains less (0.15 points) but is the arm of the five whose gain is clear and whose 0.99 quantile is no worse, so that recipe, not a better head, is the next step.
    00347 wind-physics-hybrid-plant-then-mdn generator-forecasting partly supported A physics model of each wind farm, run on every ensemble member and corrected by a small neural network, forecasts 0.27 points of capacity (2.4%) better than the research default over leads of 0.5 hours to 15 days, and 0.23 points over true days 1 to 10, but the neural network given only the plant's median and the farm already gets 83% of the gain, and the upper tail is still over-confident. The next step is to fix the upper tail (the neural network caps its quantiles at capacity, and 0.9% of observations exceed it) before any plant-based arm is adopted.
    00346 wind-research-default-1-offset-table-refit-cadence generator-forecasting partly supported Refitting the wind forecast's offset table every month on recent runs makes the forecast a little better (a 0.4% to 1.1% lower quantile score), using only data the service already stores. It does not fix the upper quantiles, which still miss in 1.7% of half-hours against 1% intended. So the change between the training years and the scored year is a minor cause, and the upper-tail miss needs another explanation. A monthly job that refits the table on all earlier daily runs passes the adoption conditions and is a candidate.
    00345 pooled-solar-wind-mixture-density-network metered-solar-farms partly supported One neural network trained on all six solar farms forecasts each farm as well as a separate network on the physical plant model (within 0.8%), so a production forecast could use one neural network per fuel. Adding wind farms to the same network does not help either fuel and makes solar 0.7% to 2.3% worse, so solar and wind are better trained separately. In this experiment, a wind farm with no history scored 18% worse at day 1 when trained on public wind farms alone than when trained on other metered wind farms.
    00339 wind-research-default-1-adapter-plus-mixture-head generator-forecasting partly supported A small neural-network head that re-draws the physics-plus-adapter wind forecast's quantiles gains 0.27 points of capacity (2.4%) on the farms' normal half-hours and keeps that gain on the replicate months, but the same head given no weather summaries does as well, so the gain is a recalibration that a simpler correction partly reproduces. A simple rule that predicts the idle floor at low wind recovers none of the head's loss on the idle-band readings, and training the head on those readings removes the gain.
    00337 primary-demand-standard-architecture-168-to-240-hours production-reference-and-best-guess partly supported The standard architecture (a neural network that corrects the forecast of the convex stack, with a learned input adapter and a mixture-density output) forecast Primary demand at 7 to 10 days ahead with a lower error than the current research default over the 12 months scored. Most of the gain comes from the mixture-density output and from two Primaries (D10 and D16). The 12 months of data cannot establish the gain, so the standard architecture is a candidate for demand across NGED's 1-to-10-day range, not yet a replacement.
    00336 wind-research-default-1-below-cut-in-zeroing generator-forecasting partly supported Setting the wind forecast, or the training target, to zero below the turbines' cut-in speed does not improve the forecast, because the farms still produce small real output there. Leaving the low-wind rows out of training gains 0.12 points of capacity (about 1% of the default's score) on the kept rows, but mostly by lifting the quantiles upward, which a constant upward shift may reproduce (not yet tested).
    00327 lsc-label-free-detect-mask-train-pipeline lsc-detectors partly supported A forecast of the normal running arrangement recovers faster after a load shift when the readings that a label-free detector flags are treated as missing: during and after injected shifts the 95% pinball loss falls from 1.79 to 1.04 points of capacity. On ordinary days the masked forecast is no worse, and slightly better. Without one site (D11) the gain shrinks to 0.30 points, and retraining the demand model on masked power adds nothing beyond masking the filter.
    00326 wind-research-default-1-change-arms generator-forecasting supported Feeding XGBoost the neural adapter's corrected wind speed lowers the wind forecast's score by 0.10 points of capacity, about 0.9% of the default's score, at 1 to 10 day leads (99.75% interval -0.19 to -0.01). The change is negative at every farm and at short and long leads. The three other changes were not separable from zero. Only a port that puts the adapter in the production service can use the gain, so the maintainer decides.
    00322 primary-demand-default-2-daylight-only-heuristic-blend-and-sunshine-graft-replication production-reference-and-best-guess partly supported At 7 to 10 days the blend with the manual heuristic gains about 4% at all hours, and about 2.4% when it is used only by day, which is the version with no harm on the coldest winter nights. Counting how many of the 51 ensemble members are cold finds 72% of those nights (the ensemble mean finds 41%), and a hard switch on that count keeps the whole 4% with almost no cold-night harm, but that switch was not pre-registered. The sunshine response of the depth-1 XGBoost refit explains about half of the refit's advantage on the worst April-to-June half-hours in both years, so a flexible sunshine term in the convex fit is worth testing.
    00321 metered-farm-lower-tail-recalibration-and-tail-head metered-solar-farms partly supported The solar-farm forecast's lowest quantile was already close to correct: only 1.2% to 1.4% of actual values fall strictly below the 0.01 quantile, because the earlier 3.3% counted the 3.0% of half-hours that read exactly zero. A recalibration table, a zero-inflated head, and a tail head each lower the own-history score by 0.04 points or less (the unshrunk table raises it by 0.03 to 0.05 points), so none is adopted. A shrunk recalibration table does lower the score by about 0.05 points for a farm with no history, and two heads lower the 0.99 pinball loss by 3.7% to 8.5% while covering the 0.99 quantile only 98.3% to 98.9% of the time.
    00320 primary-demand-default-2-night-aware-heuristic-blend-and-night-band-width production-reference-and-best-guess partly supported A blend with the manual heuristic still improves the 7 to 10 day Primary demand forecast by about 4%, and the harm it does on the coldest winter nights cannot be removed by switching it off when the weather forecast says the night will be cold, because at 7 to 10 days that forecast spots only between one and two cold nights in three. Narrowing the night prediction bands helps one Primary (D10, by 0.25 points on night half-hours), leaves the other five unchanged or slightly worse, and makes the cold-night tails worse.
    00319 primary-demand-default-1-solar-summary-and-heuristic-blend-at-7-to-10-days production-reference-and-best-guess partly supported The demand forecast for 7 to 10 days ahead improves by about 2% when its solar term also uses the spread between the 51 weather-forecast members, and the gain is largest on sunny days (6.6%), where the forecast had been worse than the manual heuristic that National Grid Electricity Distribution (NGED) staff use. Blending in the manual heuristic improves the forecast by 4.5%, but the blend worsens the coldest winter nights beyond the agreed tolerance, so only the solar change is adopted.
    00318 metered-farm-mdn-head-more-features metered-solar-farms partly supported Giving the solar-farm head more inputs does not improve its overall score at any lead from 1 to 10 days, so the head's inputs are not what limits the forecast. The extra clock and cloud-spread inputs do lower the loss at the 0.99 quantile (the tail that matters for curtailment) by about 5% at days 3 to 7, and none of the changes fixes the lower tail, where 3.3% to 3.7% of actual values fall below the 0.01 quantile, where 1% is expected.     What the experiments taught us so far

    • Retrain the Primary demand model weekly, and do not add a 14-day error correction. Weekly retraining without the correction scored 0.20 points of capacity below the best-guess stack, whose frozen model has the correction (99.44% interval -0.40 to -0.035), and a 7-day correction on the weekly model raised the score by 0.16 (-0.06 to +0.36), unresolved (00048). Experiment 34 found the 14-day correction stops helping once the model is retrained, and hurts at weekly and daily cadence. The 14-day correction (00019, 00021) was measured on a frozen model, and the retraining results supersede it for Primary demand.
    • The 14-day correction on a frozen model is a historical result. It lowered the quantile score of N1, a model frozen at its training end, by 0.99 points (00019). At 4 bulk and grid supply point series it did not lower the score (00021), and at the supply points in experiment 87 adding it raised the score by 0.305 points.
    • A weather product as accurate as ERA5 would lower the Primary demand score by 1.9%. ERA5 weather in place of ENS weather lowered the quantile score by 0.10 points, nearly all from shortwave radiation and none up to 2 days ahead (00022).
    • Check for calendar memorisation. When the same valid time repeats across many ENS runs, a model with calendar columns can memorise it. Give each experiment a climatology row, and group folds by valid time.
    • Choose a row set that covers every weekday. The 53 Tuesday runs of 00001 and 00002 put every case of the first day on a Tuesday or Wednesday, which does not suit demand.
    • Training on ERA5 weather helps solar and wind, but a longer history does not add to it, and for demand a long history hurt. The solar and wind gain appeared even when the ERA5-trained model saw only the ENS-era months, so the gain comes from the weather it was trained on and not from the years. For demand, training from 2019 was worse than training from 2023, probably because the customer mix and embedded generation drifted.
    • Put recent power in the stage 2 calibrator, and calibrate before judging it. For Primary substations, the calibrator with recent-origin inputs beat a stage 1b correction, and a lag-free calibrator is worth about 1 point of capacity at short leads (00005).
    • The term and return-to-work columns showed no detectable gain on top of the Christmas column for Primary demand. Over the three validation years, the return and term columns moved the Primary error by +0.06 points from 2 to 20 January (99.2% interval -0.17 to +0.30), +0.20 at Easter (-0.19 to +0.59), and -0.29 over the half terms (-0.68 to +0.07), and Both was not 0.2 points better than a calendar shifted by 17 days (00011). The arm with the full history and the COVID indicator scored 0.26 points higher on the whole year than the base arm.
    • A change that helps the point forecast of solar or wind need not help the pooled mixture's CRPS. CAMS-trained solar lowered the member-average error by 0.14 points (00012) and the mixture's CRPS by 0.009 (00020), and a stage 0 mapping widened the member spread by a quarter. A power-curve base margin lowered the wind CRPS by 0.055 (00020).
    • Attach the forecast solar term to the XGBoost model as a base margin, with a weight fitted on the training window and zero beyond 10 days. The base-margin arm lowered the Primary demand quantile score by 0.152 points (98.75% interval -0.199 to -0.111) against the best guess d0 and 0.256 against d1l, where the fixed-coefficient shift of 00046 lowered d1l by 0.056, and subtracting the forecast solar from a model trained on gross demand was not shown to match the best guess (00064).
    • Do not expect a zero start to help the production XGBoost models. Experiment 78 found that a zero base margin lowered its reference model d0 by 0.071 points, but on the production recipe the zero start changed the Primary score by -0.017 (-0.072 to +0.054) and the solar score by -0.010 (-0.036 to +0.028), and worsened wind models without a power-curve margin by 0.049 (00080).
    • Refresh the capacity table that feeds the base margin once per season-year. A table refreshed that often captured almost all of the gain from a current capacity (00078).
