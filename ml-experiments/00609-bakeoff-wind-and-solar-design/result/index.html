    How should the final bake-off score the best forecasts of the 3 metered wind farms and the 6 metered solar farms, and what would it cost?

    The wind and solar part of the bake-off is cheap to fit and costly to prepare: about 0.4 GPU-h for the cheapest design and about 3 GPU-h for the most thorough, against a ceiling of 8 GPU-h. The fit times come from the logs of earlier experiments and are estimates, so the first worker must time one fit of each kind before relying on them. No weather model was fitted and no forecast was scored for this page. The page is a design that a later worker can follow once the list of release candidates is frozen.

    The bake-off scores each frozen candidate once, on forecasts issued from 2026-07-01 to 2026-10-31 (the final test, opened once). The five development months are 2025-10, 2025-12, 2026-01, 2026-03, and 2026-06. July 2026 is the first month of the final test. Nothing here is adopted for production, and the generators appear only as W1 to W3 (wind) and Solar A to F (solar).

    1. Candidates, stored artefacts, and cost

    Every candidate must be re-run for the final test, because no stored forecast reaches past 2026-08. The first table lists the candidates. "Stored" means a file under data/ml-experiments/ that holds the forecasts or fitted weights for the development period.

    Technology Candidate What it is Stored Needs for the final test
    Wind Wind default 2 Experiment 347, arm y2: the frozen physics plant, run on each of the 51 ensemble members, then a three-component mixture-density head; capacity at p99.9 of earlier power (experiment 461) Plant files (plant_stage3.json), ensemble summaries, and predictions_y2.parquet for 53 Tuesday runs Plant summaries for the new runs (CPU), a head refit (GPU), and a daily-origin run for the development months
    Wind Wind default 1 ERA5-trained XGBoost on the 51 members plus the offset table of experiment 259 Predictions for 53 Tuesday runs (experiments 303 and 347) A refit on CPU
    Wind climatology Per-farm climatology Not stored as forecasts Build on CPU
    Wind Persistence, hours 0 to 6 Last reading held Not stored Build on CPU
    Solar a2_member The 51-member encoder network of experiment 430, refitted once a year (experiment 493 and experiment 498) Weights annual_a2_member_s0.pt to s5.pt and predictions, six seeds One annual refit per seed
    Solar a2_member quarterly The same network refitted every quarter Predictions of experiment 430 (seeds 0 to 5, four refits) Two refits per seed in the test window
    Solar GAM reference The additive-curve arm a3_gam_full of experiment 272 Predictions, three seeds, per farm A refit on CPU
    Solar XGBoost reference The XGBoost reference of experiment 243 with the floors of experiment 224 Predictions under 00243-*/predictions/ A refit on CPU
    Solar manual_heuristic and climatology The two baselines of the solar protocol Not stored as forecasts Build on CPU

    Two stored sets do not match the bake-off's rows. The wind default was scored on 53 Tuesday runs, not on daily origins, so the development months need a daily-origin run of the plant summaries. The solar forecasts were scored over 2025-09 to 2026-08 on refits dated 2025-09, 2025-12, 2026-03, and 2026-06, so the stored solar forecasts cover the five development months only where those refits apply. The stored annual forecasts come from a single refit dated 2025-09, so they match that rule even less well. The worker must check the refit dates against the coarse-refit rule of the common brief (day 0 and day 14 of each test month) and refit where the dates differ.

    Fit costs are small. The numbers below are read from logs, and the totals are estimates.

    Fit Cost per fit Source
    Solar a2_member, one refit, one seed 29 to 61 s wall time (24 s of GPU time) Logs of experiment 430 and the cost table of experiment 493
    Solar one-pass network, one refit, one seed 8 s of GPU time Experiment 493
    Wind head, one seed, one refit about 25 s Log of experiment 347 stage 4 (the 45 timed head fits took 1,102 s in all)
    Wind plant summaries, one farm, hindcast and scored sets about 96 s of CPU inputs_W1.log of experiment 347
    XGBoost, GAM, climatology, manual_heuristic CPU only, minutes Earlier pages

    The projection has three designs, ranked.

    Rank Design GPU-h Notes
    1 Final test only. Solar: one annual refit and two quarterly refits, six seeds. Wind: one head refit at 2026-07-01, five seeds. Stored forecasts stand in for the development months where their rows match. about 0.4 The cheapest design: 18 solar fits at 61 s and 5 wind fits at 25 s, plus 30% for data loading.
    2 Design 1, plus re-running the five development months with the same code (day 0 and day 14 refits, two seeds for wind and six for solar), so that the development and final-test numbers come from one script. about 1.9 Adds 60 solar fits and 20 wind head fits (10 refits). Catches a mismatch between the stored forecasts and the new code before the test is opened.
    3 Design 2, plus a wind head refit every quarter inside the test window, and ten seeds for Solar E (experiment 498 needed ten seeds to resolve a 1% contrast). about 3.1 Adds 1 wind refit and 4 more solar seeds for 13 refits. The extra seeds matter only if a contrast at one farm decides a ranking.

    All three designs are below the 8 GPU-h ceiling, so no stop is needed. The estimate counts the longest fit time seen (61 s) for every solar fit and 25 s for every wind head fit, and adds 30% for data loading. The first worker confirms it with one timed fit of each kind.

    2. Scoring rules

    Every candidate is scored by the quantile score over 11 levels in points of capacity, on the same rows, with the same capacity. The levels are q01, q05, q10, q25, q40, q50, q60, q75, q90, q95, and q99. The continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) is twice the mean pinball loss over those levels. Capacity is the p99.9 rule of experiment 461 for the wind farms. For the solar farms the capacity follows experiment 505: the old capacity at Solar A and Solar E, where the rolling and all-history p99.9 rules failed (Solar A on score, Solar E on the tail test), and the worker chooses the rule for the other four farms from experiments 461 and 505 and records the rule. The scoring code reads each capacity from one file, so no candidate can use another.

    Metric Definition Why
    Quantile score Mean pinball loss over 11 levels, in points of capacity The headline of both protocols
    CRPS Twice the mean pinball loss Comparable with the demand pages
    Interval coverage Share of rows with the observed power inside q05 to q95; target 0.90 Calibration
    Tail calibration Share of rows above q99 (target 0.01) and below q01 (target 0.01); the 0.99 and 0.01 pinball losses Experiment 347 found 2.3% above q99, so the worker reports both shares for every candidate
    Threshold-weighted CRPS Weight on observed power above 80% of capacity (this page's own choice; no earlier page sets a threshold) The part of the distribution that matters for curtailment

    No curtailment-cost metric exists for generators yet. The avoidable curtailment cost of experiment 601 is defined for demand nodes with an import limit, and experiment 347 lists a generator version as "not computed". The design therefore reports the 0.99 pinball loss and the threshold-weighted CRPS instead. The maintainer must choose whether to define a generator curtailment cost before the test is opened; this page does not choose.

    Every metric is reported in every slice below, and every slice row shows its half-hour count. Intervals resample whole calendar months of origin, as in the earlier pages. The final test has only 4 months, so intervals will be wide. The page states the minimum detectable effect beside every contrast and counts the contrasts, so that the Bonferroni level is a number. Bonferroni count: the number of contrasts is the number of candidates compared with each technology's default, times the 3 lead bands, and the worker computes it from the frozen list.

    Slice Definition
    Lead band 0 to 24 h, 24 to 120 h, 120 to 240 h; the headline is the whole range 0 to 240 h
    Month Each of 2026-07, 2026-08, 2026-09, 2026-10; the development months beside them
    Season Summer and autumn from the final test; winter and spring from the development months. The final test holds no winter and no spring, so no season is compared across the two periods
    Hour of day Each hour 0 to 23 UTC; daylight half-hours only for the solar farms
    Very sunny days Days on which the observed solar output reaches 90% of the clear-sky output on at least half the daylight half-hours (the clear-sky definition of solar-v1 is not spelled out on its page, so the worker states the rule used)
    High-wind days Days on which the observed wind output of at least 2 of the 3 wind farms exceeds 80% of capacity on any half-hour
    Near a data gap Half-hours within 24 hours of a gap of at least 3 hours in the same series
    Series Every farm on its own row; the pooled number is a mean over farms, not over rows

    Slices use the observed power only to label days, never as a forecast input.

    3. Missing readings and the experiment 605 gaps

    The nine generators have had no exact-zero reading since 2026-03-26, so the final test cannot be compared with the development months unless both are filtered the same way. Experiment 605 found that the last exact zero at all nine series falls between 2026-03-23 and 2026-03-26. Solar farms read zero in 47% to 68% of half-hours in the six months before March, and wind farms in 0.3% to 4.8%. The cause is unknown, and Solar E also misses about 70% of its four middle daylight hours in July and October 2026.

    Three versions of each score are reported, and a fourth is reported for the development months.

    Version Rows kept Comparable with the development months?
    A, rows present Every half-hour that has a reading Only after the development months are filtered the same way (version A-dev)
    B, night filled Version A plus the solar night half-hours (sun elevation at or below 0 degrees) added with an observed power of 0 Yes for solar, with the development months scored as they were before
    C, calm filled (wind only) Version A plus wind half-hours that have no reading and whose ensemble-mean 100 m wind speed is below the plant's cut-in speed, added with an observed power of 0 Yes for wind, as a sensitivity run only, because the fill rule uses a weather forecast
    A-dev The development months with every exact-zero reading dropped The match for version A

    Version A is the headline, and A-dev is its development-period match. Dropping zeros removes the easy half-hours, so version A scores are higher than the scores in the earlier pages and must never be set beside them. Version B is a bound: night half-hours are scored with zero forecasts and zero actuals, so the night fill lowers the score and is reported separately, never pooled with daylight half-hours. The solar protocol already scores daylight half-hours only, so version A and version B differ only through the dawn and dusk half-hours that read exactly zero.

    Solar E is reported on its own row, and every pooled solar number is shown with and without it. Its missing midday readings are the largest gap in the final test, and the missing readings remove the half-hours that carry most of its score. A pooled solar mean that includes Solar E is not comparable with the development months.

    What is comparable with the development months.

    • Comparable: version B for solar, and A against A-dev for both technologies.
    • Not comparable: version A against the development scores of earlier pages; any pooled solar number that includes Solar E; and any season comparison.
    • Open question: whether the source stopped reporting zeros or the farms stopped reading zero. The manager's question to NGED is listed in the risks.

    4. Plots

    Every plot follows the standing plot rules of the common brief and the dataviz skill. The plots are anonymised: the axis shows the day of the week (1 to 7, repeated for the second week) and never a calendar date. Plots are drawn from the saved forecasts, not from refits.

    Group Panels Stretch
    Wind farms W1 to W3, one figure per farm Two panels, each 280 px or taller: the default 2 median with its q05 to q95 band in one panel, and the same for wind default 1 in the other. Observed power in black; no other mark is black or grey A 14-day high-wind stretch, a 14-day calm stretch, and a 14-day stretch around a gap
    Solar farms A to F, one figure per farm Two panels: a2_member annual with its band, and a2_member quarterly with its band A 14-day very sunny stretch, a 14-day cloudy stretch, and a 14-day stretch around a gap; Solar E gets two gap stretches (July and October)
    Score by lead band, month, and hour Bars with 95% intervals, one panel per technology The whole final test
    Coverage and tail calibration Points against the 0.90 and 0.01 targets, one row per candidate The whole final test

    Each figure has a legend outside the data area, a plain caption saying what to look at, and a gate or gap strip checked against the data. The gap strip is a thin bar under the panel and is drawn from the rows with no reading. A strip that is on across the whole axis is a bug until checked against the stored rows. A fresh Sonnet reviewer opens every figure rendered to PNG and answers the figure-review checklist before the pull request is marked ready.

    5. Outputs

    Forecasts are saved as Parquet in the layout of backtest_export_2026-11-06/, one file per candidate and month, with a README. The README states the candidates, the seeds, the refit dates, the version of the missing-reading rule, and the caveats of section 3. The files hold:

    • series (W1 to W3 or Solar A to F), power_fcst_init_time, valid_time, and lead_hours;
    • q01 to q99, in points of capacity and in the generator's unit (MW);
    • observed_power (null where there is no reading) and observed_filled (the version B or C value);
    • sun_elevation_degrees, reading_present, and near_gap (true within 24 hours of a gap of at least 3 hours).

    No file holds a generator's name, its identifier, or its location. The generator files are private: they stay under data/ and are not published.

    6. Dependencies and risks

    Dependencies.

    • Experiment 607 (extend the cached inputs to 2026-10-31) must finish first. Experiment 605 found the ensemble summary, wind-component, and cloud-cover tables ending with the run of 2026-06-30.
    • The inputs of the two networks are not on experiment 605's list of 11 cached inputs. Wind default 2 reads the 51-member farm-point tables that experiment 303's builders write, and a2_member reads the member arrays of experiment 430 (member_arrays, 402 MB). The first worker checks the newest date of both and adds them to experiment 607's list if they stop early.
    • The experiment 605 gaps (section 3) fix the scoring rules. The filter must be agreed before the first candidate is scored.

    Risks.

    • Capacity can leak. Wind recomputes the p99.9 capacity at each refit, and the solar capacity is fixed or rolling according to the rule the worker records. A capacity computed from power that includes the test months would leak, so the worker asserts that every capacity uses only readings from before the refit.
    • Only 4 test months and 3 wind farms. The intervals are wide. Experiment 347 found a gain of 0.23 points with a 99.75% interval that included zero. The bake-off may not separate close candidates, and the page reports ties as ties.
    • Upper tail of wind default 2. 2.3% of observations exceeded q99 in experiment 347. The worker reports this share for every candidate, and it is not a pass or fail rule here.
    • Solar E. Solar E has the fewest scored rows, and ten seeds were needed to resolve one contrast. Design 3 adds those seeds; designs 1 and 2 do not.
    • One question for NGED. Whether the source stopped reporting exact-zero readings on about 2026-03-26. The answer decides whether version A or version B is the headline.
    • Frozen list. The candidates must be frozen before the first score is read. Any change after that is a new bake-off.

    Checks

    • Median forecast minus mean forecast, and the mean-forecast error beside each quantile-score contrast: not applicable, because this page reads no forecast.
    • Triggers fired: none of the seven audit triggers applies to a design with no control, no contrast, and no earlier result to contradict.
    • Sources read: the pages of experiments 347, 461, 464, 466, 493, 498, 499, 505, 506, 508, 605, and 607, the solar evaluation protocol, and the file listings under data/ml-experiments/ of experiments 272, 347, 430, 461, and 493. The fit times are read from the logs of experiments 347 and 430 and the cost table of experiment 493.
    • Pages not changed: no other page or script is edited.

    What the next experiment should answer

    Whether a fit, a score, and a plot of the three wind farms and six solar farms run end to end on the final test with the rules above, once experiment 607 has extended the inputs.
