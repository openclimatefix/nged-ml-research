    Experiment 400: blend a forecast with power lags and a forecast without them, by lag surprise

    Plan

    • Question. Can a smooth, threshold-free weight on a forecast without power lags, driven by how surprising the three lagged readings are, give a forecast that is better around load-shift events and no worse elsewhere? A binary detector reached recall 0.42 with 1.7 false alarms per Primary-year (experiment 391). A forecast that steps loses the trust of NGED's operators.
    • Forecast with lags. Experiment 381's weekly bundle (arm_preds/weekly/bundle_solar+sunny+hl1), stored as fractions of effective capacity, unwidened. It is not experiment 383's plain weekly member (arm d, which is experiment 87's stored weekly member): that member has 64 stored files and cannot cover all rows.
    • Forecast without lags. Experiment 383's raw lagless arm (work/arms/lagless.parquet, 4,099,680 rows, 16 Primaries). Arm c2 of experiment 383 is not used: it applies this arm only on rows with a lag inside an event and arm d elsewhere.
    • Widening. Both forecasts are used raw (no 1.15 widening): experiment 381's bundle carries none and the lagless arm is raw. The 1.15 sensitivity run planned here was not run (see Caveats).
    • Row set. The 2,824,844 rows of experiment 395's Part F slice_table.parquet (16 Primaries, valid times 2025-07-02 to 2026-06-30). The plan checks before scoring that both forecasts hold every key and reports any gap. Scoring actuals come from Part F's actual_mw; half-hours flagged in the cleaned power table are dropped from scoring, and the count dropped is reported per Primary.
    • Suspicion score. Experiment 391's surprise_tables/half_hourly/<site>.parquet: u, the normal score of a reading in the lagless stage-1 quantile forecast, at each lag's valid time. A row's three lags (origin, day, and week lags, found with experiment 87's origin_lags) give three own-Primary values. The neighbour surprise uses experiment 391's NGED interconnection list. Leak check. Each u comes from a monthly refit that trained on no row at or after its refit time, and a lag's valid time is at or before the forecast origin, so no u uses information after the origin. The monthly stage-1 tables are indexed by valid time, so they align with the weekly forecasts through the lag's valid time. A lag in the first stage-1 month (2024-10) has u from a model trained on 2024-04 to 2024-09; the scored months start nine months later.
    • Arms. Mixture weight on the forecast with lags: w = 1 / (1 + exp(a + b s + c lead_term)), with lead_term = log(lead hours / 24). B1: s is the largest absolute own surprise over the three lags. B2: s is B1 plus the largest absolute neighbour surprise, as two inputs (b1, b2). B3: the signed own surprise, the sum of the neighbours' positive and negative surprises, and the largest of each sign, as five inputs, so a fit can learn the neighbour sign logic; the rule is not hand coded. Fixed-weight control: w = 0.5. Endpoints: the two pure forecasts. Each of B1 to B3 and the control is scored as a mixture and as a quantile average of the same weights.
    • Fit. The exact CRPS of the mixture (the integral of the squared difference between the mixture CDF and the step at the actual value, with the piecewise-linear CDFs below), plus a ridge penalty of 1e-3 times the squared parameters, minimised by BFGS (at most 60 iterations) on a fixed random subsample of 200,000 training rows (seed 0), with an expanding window: the model for scored month m uses rows whose valid time is before the first of m. July to September 2025 is warm-up and uses w = 0.5; October 2025 to June 2026 is scored. Warm-up months are reported separately. The label table never enters a fit.
    • Reported score. The protocol's CRPS from the mixture's 11 quantiles, so every arm is scored as every earlier experiment is. For a mixture of two well-separated forecasts the 11-quantile score is lower than the exact CRPS, so the report also gives the exact CRPS of the mixture arms.
    • Mixture quantiles. Each forecast's CDF is linear between its 11 quantiles, with the end segments extended to levels 0 and 1 at the slope of the outer segment. The mixture CDF is w F1 + (1 - w) F2, inverted by bisection at the 11 levels.
    • Events. The corrected label table sw_block_detector_label_table_corrected.csv from the head of nged-ml-research PR 49 (112 rows with an end time and a depth), unpadded, with the corrected signs. It is used only to evaluate.
    • Checks are the seven in the brief: the harm table by number of lags inside the event, the cost outside events, the median share of the labelled depth, the tails during events, smoothness at onset and end, avoidable curtailment during events and in the 7 days after (differences against the forecast with lags and the GAM stack), and the cold-night rows separately.
    • Compute. Stored forecasts only: no refit, no GPU, 2 cores, MemoryMax=6G. Chunks of one Primary and one month.
    • Earlier advice followed. Cleaned actuals; both forecasts and the weight named in full in every table; the absolute score beside every contrast. Departures are listed under Caveats.

    Results

    The blend gives no usable gain, and the surprise scores do not drive the weight. Every number below is for days 1 to 10, valid times from 2025-10-01 (the first scored month), CRPS (continuous ranked probability score) in points of effective capacity. The six Primaries with signed readings are D5, D9, D10, D11, D14, and D16. The eight with MVA-only readings are D2, D3, D4, D8, D12, D19, D21, and D22. Intervals resample whole events (event rows) or months (pooled rows) and are at 95%. The decision rule asked for 99% intervals at k = 12 and a pooled contrast on event-lag rows; neither was computed, so the verdict rests on the failed cost condition below and on the absence of any gain at the eight. The printed reports are in the private folder (work/report.txt and work/curtailment.txt). This page has no figures.

    • Flagged-zero handling. Flagged exact-zero half-hours are dropped from the scoring actuals and from the fits. The scored rows lose 96 half-hours (D12 8, D18 24, D22 40, D8 24).
    • Widening. Both forecasts are raw. Experiment 381's weekly bundle and experiment 383's raw lagless arm carry no 1.15 widening.
    • Label table. The corrected table sw_block_detector_label_table_corrected.csv from nged-ml-research PR 49: 112 labelled pairs of Primaries (51 with a corrected sign), expanded to 184 per-Primary unpadded events. It is used only to evaluate, never as an input.
    • The weight barely moves around events. In arm B2 the mean weight on the forecast with lags is 0.615 outside events, 0.611 in the first 6 hours of an event, and 0.597 after 24 hours. Its 5th to 95th percentile range is 0.56 to 0.68. The fitted coefficients on surprise and on the log of lead time are both negative, so a larger surprise and a longer lead each raise the weight on the forecast with lags.
    • Absolute CRPS, all 16 Primaries. Forecast with lags 3.867; forecast without lags 4.338; fixed 50/50 mixture 3.938; mixture B1 3.898, B2 3.892, B3 3.886; quantile averages 3.907 to 3.968.
    • Event-lag rows (1 to 3 lags in an event, target outside), arm minus forecast with lags (C1 to C4):

    • Control comparison (C9). The control is a fixed 50/50 mixture that was not fitted, and no fitted constant-weight arm was run, so the weight level and the surprise response are not separated. On event-lag rows, mixture B2 minus the fixed 50/50 mixture is +0.15 (-0.02 to +0.39) at the six and -0.05 (-0.10 to -0.01) at the eight. Outside events it is -0.046 (-0.065 to -0.024) over all 16 Primaries.
    • Cost outside events (C5 to C8, all 16 Primaries, month resampling): fixed 50/50 mixture +0.078 (+0.013 to +0.138); B1 +0.038, B2 +0.033 (-0.013 to +0.076), B3 +0.027 (-0.018 to +0.070). The B2 upper end exceeds the +0.05 tolerance, and the pooled cost is dominated by D15, which has a short history: B2 minus the forecast with lags is +1.40 points at D15, +0.15 at D18, and +0.10 at D22, with 9 of 16 Primaries below zero.
    • Tails. Mixture B2 has q95 coverage 0.936 outside events against 0.922 for the forecast with lags, because the mixture is wider (90% width 28.4 against 25.2 points). The quantile averages keep the width of the forecast with lags (26.1) and gain little coverage. During events with 0, 1 to 2, and 3 lags in the event, the share of readings inside the 1-99% band is 0.88, 0.79, and 0.81 for mixture B2 and 0.87, 0.78, and 0.82 for the forecast with lags. For the 5-95% band it is 0.65, 0.56, and 0.40 for mixture B2 and 0.62, 0.53, and 0.40 for the forecast with lags.
    • Depth missed by the median. The share of each event's labelled depth that the forecast median misses, with the corrected signs, is 0.75 (forecast with lags), 0.56 (forecast without lags), and 0.59 (mixture B2) with no lag in the event. With three lags in the event it is 0.63, 0.57, and 0.66.
    • Smoothness. The blends do not step more than the pure forecasts. The typical (median over events) largest half-hour change of the forecast median at an onset is 6.2 points for mixture B2 against 6.7 for the forecast with lags, and the 95th percentile is 13.3 against 15.8. The pure forecasts already change by several points at event edges.
    • Avoidable curtailment (limit 0.9 times each Primary's maximum observed power), arm minus the forecast with lags. Only four of the six Primaries with signed readings returned a cost during events and in the 7 days after (D5, D10, D14, D16; D9 and D11 returned "no cost" from the protocol function), and D16 supplies most of it. During events mixture B2 is +£61,000 and the fixed 50/50 mixture +£89,000, against a total of £6.56 million for the forecast with lags (about 1%, within noise), and the forecast without lags is +£1.16 million. In the 7 days after events every blend is between -£3,800 and -£3,000, and the GAM stack is +£112,000. Over all rows mixture B2 is +£40,000. The GAM stack is £3.7 million below the forecast with lags during events and £4.9 million below it over all rows.
    • Cold nights. Outside events mixture B2 minus the forecast with lags is +0.042 (-0.182 to +0.146). On event-lag rows at the six Primaries with signed readings it is -1.43 (-2.57 to -0.06), from 538 rows.
    • Warm-up (July to September 2025, weight 0.5): fixed 50/50 mixture 2.883 against 2.864 for the forecast with lags.

    Predictions

    • P1 (gain of 0.3 to 1.5 points on event-lag rows, growing with lags). Met at the six only (B2 -0.75); not met at the eight (+0.09); the gain does not grow steadily with the number of lags.
    • P2 (cost under 0.02 pooled and under 0.05 at every Primary). Not met: +0.033 pooled, and D15, D18, and D22 exceed 0.05.
    • P3 (B3 beats B2 by under 0.2 points). Met: -0.78 against -0.75 at the six.
    • P4 (no larger step than the pure forecasts). Met.
    • P5 (mixture tails reach the event level more often). Not tested as written; the band shares above are the nearest measure and show 0.01 to 0.03 differences.

    Caveats and what to try next

    • Leak check. Each surprise value comes from a monthly stage 1 refit that trained on no row at or after its refit time, and every lag time is before the forecast origin, so no row leaks.
    • Departures from the plan. The 1.15 widening sensitivity run was not run, so the relative result under widening is untested. The exact CRPS of each mixture was computed (work/exact_crps.parquet) but is not tabulated here. The 99% intervals and the pooled event-lag contrast were not computed.
