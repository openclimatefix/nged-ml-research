    Post hoc: does filling the gaps in the stage-1 median, so that more load-shift events are scored, change the counterfactual-lag gain on harm rows?

    Status: done. Filling the gaps added no scored event. The sections from "The fill rule" onward are the plan, written before any score of the filled run was read. The experiment is post hoc: it exists because experiment 411 scored only 15 of 184 corrected events, and the design change (the fill rule) was chosen after that result was read. The fill rule below was fixed from counts of the input gaps, not from any score.

    Results

    The filled run scored the same 15 events as experiment 411, with the same numbers. The prediction of at least 40 events scored failed, because the gaps were not what limited the sample. The prediction of a gain between -0.5 and +0.3 at the six also failed, because the gain is -0.836. Intervals are 95% event-cluster bootstraps (2,000 resamples). This run's random stream differs from experiment 411's, so its intervals differ from experiment 411's by up to about 0.1 (experiment 411 reports -2.578 to +1.226 at the six for the same rows).

    Six signed-MW 5,354 9 5.061 5.897 -0.836 -2.468 to +1.301
    Eight MVA-only 3,241 6 6.514 6.489 +0.024 -0.568 to +1.550
    All 14 8,595 15 5.609 6.120 -0.512 -1.798 to +0.863
    • Added events: none. The report's "added events" section is empty.
    • Leave-one-event-out gain range: -1.278 to +0.460 at the six, and -0.354 to +0.877 at the eight. Dropping D5's one event still flips the six-Primary sign.
    • No figure: the scored events are experiment 411's, so its figure applies unchanged.

    Why no event was added

    Only 36 of the 184 corrected events have a lag that any test forecast origin reads. A forecast origin reads lags up to about 7.1 days older than the origin. The audit listed every grid index read by the 123 daily origins of the 4 test refit months (2025-10, 2026-01, 2026-03, 2026-06) and checked each event. This leaves 148 events that no origin reads: 129 lie outside the span from the first to the last origin, and 19 fall in the untested months between refits. The audit's counting script is not part of the experiment's code. Of the 99 events that have a gap in the stage-1 median, 96 are among the 129, so experiment 411's count of "99 events lost to gaps" mostly counted events that no origin could read. The 36 events that are read divide as follows:

    • Gap in the observed power: 2 events. Each also has a gap in the stage-1 median, and the fill cannot repair observed power.
    • Sign, step share, and bias filters: 6, 7, and 3 events.
    • Pass every event filter: 18 events. The 15 scored events are among them. The other 3 either produce no harm rows in the sampled runs or belong to D15 and D18, which this run did not score.

    The fill repaired none of the events that are read, so it changed no score. That is why the rows match experiment 411 exactly.

    Checks

    • Median minus mean forecast per site and the error of the mean forecast: not computed, because the boosters' mean forecast is not stored; the scored forecasts are the mixture quantiles.
    • Reproduction: the 8,595 row scores are bit-identical to experiment 411's (a full join on Primary, event, origin, and valid time leaves no unmatched row, and the largest difference in either CRPS is 0.0), so every point estimate in the table matches.
    • Triggers fired: three of the seven.
      • Trigger 2 (the result contradicts its prediction): 15 events were scored against at least 40 predicted, and the gain is -0.836 against a predicted -0.5 to +0.3. The reason is explained and checked above (the lag-reach count).
      • Trigger 4 (leaving one out flips the sign): dropping D5's one event flips the six-Primary gain from -0.836 to +0.460.
      • Trigger 7 (a later design depends on the headline): the experiment 404 plan skips batches 1 and 2 on this number.
    • Reviews: a fresh Sonnet agent reviewed the code before the first run and found that the fill would change the pre-event bias of the original events; the fix computes the bias on the unfilled median, and the reproduction above confirms it. A second fresh Sonnet agent then re-derived the table from the row scores, rebuilt the event accounting with its own script, and reviewed this page's prose. It found that the first draft misdescribed the reach count, claimed the fill worked on 3 events, and quoted two different intervals for the same rows. All findings were applied.

    The fill rule

    The rule is fixed before any score is read. Experiment 411 scored 15 of the 184 events, and 99 events have a gap in the stage-1 median. The plan chose the fill rule because 46% of the event half-hours (14,184 of 30,832) have no stage-1 median. The gaps are median-only in 75 events and in both the median and the observed power in 24 events; the observed power alone is never missing. Of the 75 median-only events, the longest gap is at most 12 half-hours in 8 events, at most 48 in 30 events, at most 96 in 39, and at most 336 in 70. Linear interpolation across gaps that long would invent a median, so the fill is different:

    • Where the stage-1 median is missing, it is replaced by the mean observed reading at the same half-hour of the previous 4 weeks. The mean uses only readings that are finite and outside every labelled event of the Primary. The fill leaves the value missing when fewer than 2 of the 4 weeks qualify.
    • Every reading used is older than the half-hour it fills, so a lag never receives a value from after itself.
    • The pre-event bias is computed on the unfilled median, so the 15 original events keep the bias that experiment 411 used and should reproduce its scores.
    • Nothing else changes. Events with missing observed power stay skipped (24 events). The 25% step filter, the sign filter, the bias filter, the run sample (at most 6 runs per event and refit month), and the scoring are the same as in experiment 411.

    What the experiment answers

    The experiment tests whether the fill rule changes the answer, not whether the filled median is the true clean load. A seasonal profile ignores weather and holidays, so the clean lags are an estimate with an error that grows with the gap. The report therefore gives the gain separately for the 15 original events, which have no filled values, and for the added events, which do.

    Caveats written before the run

    • The sample is selected. Events with missing observed power stay out, and the sign, step, and bias filters still apply, so the scored events are not a random sample of the 184.
    • The filled median for a long gap is a seasonal average. It is as good as the previous weeks are for the event's own weeks; an event with a holiday in it is badly filled.
    • Single seed and four refit months. Every number is interim.

    Method

    • Code. The scripts copy experiment 411's, with one added function (fill_median). The scripts re-predict experiment 392's R1 lagged boosters twice per sampled run, with the observed series and with the clean series, and score both forecasts against the observed power on harm rows (target outside every event, at least one lag inside it, lead up to 240 hours).
    • Reviews. A fresh Sonnet agent reviewed the code before the first run. A second fresh Sonnet agent audited the results and reviewed the write-up in one pass.

    Problems caused by the move
