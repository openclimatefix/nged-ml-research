    Does a search that also starts from each unpaired rise, and looks for the matching fall at a neighbouring Primary, pair more injected load-transfer megawatts than the one-way search of experiment 670?

    Searching from each unpaired rise for the matching fall at a neighbouring Primary pairs 0.38 of the injected transfer megawatts in the long and deep cell, against 0.29 for the one-way search of experiment 670. The gain of 0.09 is just under the 0.10 needed to adopt the search, and the search adds 442 decoded transfers, most of which match no injected end. A Primary substation steps electricity down to a local distribution network. A load transfer moves part of one Primary's demand (the donor) onto a neighbour (the recipient). Experiments 672 and 676 closed the neural-network graph-learning approach at 15 Primaries; the search in this experiment uses no neural network. A noise unit is a Primary's usual difference between successive 6-hour means. The long, medium, and short cells are over 7 days, 1 to 7 days, and 1 day or less, and deep means 8 noise units or more, as in experiment 670. Found means an injected event has a decoded event at the right Primary, sign, and start; paired means the decoded donor and recipient are the right two Primaries.

    E1-J-sym adds the mirror search: start at each unpaired rise, and look for the matching fall at a neighbour. Experiment 670's search (E1-J) starts at a large fall and looks for the rise at a neighbour. Experiment 675 found that the donor is the bottleneck. This experiment adds the mirror search (E1-J-sym): it starts at each rise of 4 noise units or more that is still unpaired, and looks for a fall of 2 noise units or more that starts up to 36 half-hours before that rise (the window is widened by 6 half-hours on each side to allow for start error). The verdict is partly supported: both deep-cell differences exceed +0.05, neither reaches +0.10. The pre-registered stage 1 rule (adopt at +0.10 in both deep cells, otherwise continue) therefore sends the line on to part 2, a model that reconstructs both Primaries' residuals jointly from flows on the edges between Primaries.

    Results

    • Gate passed. E1-J, re-run here on the same 40 default worlds (seeds 900 to 939), reproduces experiment 670's paired share in every cell to four decimals.
    • Paired share, E1-J-sym minus E1-J (95% interval from resampling worlds): long and deep +0.094 (0.043 to 0.151); medium and deep +0.071 (0.025 to 0.139); long and shallow +0.076 (0.038 to 0.117); medium and shallow +0.028 (0.004 to 0.061); short and deep +0.003 (0.0 to 0.011); short and shallow +0.003 (0.0 to 0.011).
    • Absolute paired share, E1-J-sym: long and deep 0.379 (0.281 to 0.500); medium and deep 0.316 (0.186 to 0.478); long and shallow 0.263; medium and shallow 0.159; short and deep 0.083; short and shallow 0.030.
    • Found share barely moves (long and deep 0.503 to 0.515). Donor megawatts found in the long and deep cell rise from 0.441 to 0.481, and in the medium and deep cell from 0.399 to 0.434. Most of the gain is therefore pairing of donors that the event table already held.
    • Decoded transfers. E1-J-sym decodes 1230 transfers with 283 correct ends, against 788 and 220 for E1-J. The 63 additional correct ends come with 442 additional decoded transfers.
    • Placebo. On a rewired graph E1-J-sym pairs 0.057 (long and deep) and 0.064 (medium and deep), with 730 decoded transfers and 39 correct ends. The gain depends on the real graph.
    • Robustness. On 20 long-heavy worlds the long and deep difference is +0.043 (0.019 to 0.072); in short-heavy worlds it is +0.018 (0.0 to 0.054), whose interval includes zero, and in constant-MW square worlds +0.083 (0.010 to 0.193). The rest is in injected_by_cell.json.

    Real record

    • Decoded transfers (2024-01 to 2026-06, 36.0 Primary-years): 8 for E1-J, 12 for E1-J-sym, 0 for E1-J-sym on the stored rewired graph (median 2 over 100 rewired graphs, which keep 15.7% of the real links), or 0.22 and 0.33 per Primary-year for E1-J and E1-J-sym.
    • Verified D5 to D16 transfer (December 2025): kept in the right direction, with the same late starts and ends as E1-J (D5 start 24 hours late, D16 start 12.5 hours late, both ends 24 hours late), so the symmetric search adds nothing there.
    • Logged shifts longer than 1 day (77 in the scored years): 9 of 77 have an event start within 2 hours (E1-J: 8) and 19 within 24 hours (E1-J: 18). NGED's event labels are an incomplete dump, so every count of real events here is a lower bound.
    • Event-free time: neither method calls a transfer in the 27.6 Primary-years more than 14 days from any logged or verified event.

    Pre-registered predictions

    • Held: long and shallow +0.03 (got +0.076); medium and shallow +0.02 (+0.028); short cells no worse than -0.03; rewired graph at a third or fewer of the real record's transfers; D5 to D16 kept.
    • Did not hold: long and deep +0.10 (+0.094); medium and deep +0.10 (+0.071); donor megawatts found 0.54 or more (0.481).

    Method

    • Code: ml-experiments/00680-event-table-reconstruction-from-edge-flows-part-1-symmetric-search/, importing the folders of experiments 653, 660, 665, and 670. Unchanged from experiment 670: the panel, the residual, the worlds, the cells, and the found and paired definitions.
    • Thresholds, fixed in the plan: rise at least 4 noise units; donor fall at least 2 noise units; donor start from 42 half-hours before to 6 half-hours after the rise; share 0.3 to 2.0; loose conservation cost at most 2.5. No tuning was done.
    • Reviews. A fresh Sonnet code review ran before the first scored run. It found that the gate only printed, that the stage 1 branch letters were swapped against the plan, that the verdict rule was not encoded, and that the conservation cost used the found donor's step where experiment 670 uses the anchor event's step. All four were fixed before the scored run.
    • Compute: CPU only, about 7 seconds per world on one core, under 10 minutes in all.
    • Not used: the final-test window from 2026-07-01 is never read, and no NGED generator is named.

    What the next experiment should answer

    Whether a joint reconstruction of both Primaries' residuals by an edge flow, with a fixed cost per change, pairs more megawatts than E1-J-sym without adding as many transfers that match no injected end (part 2).
