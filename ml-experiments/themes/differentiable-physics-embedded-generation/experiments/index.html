    00420 neural-mva-export-gated-signed-final Experiment 417's arm A (a Primary-level export gate in front of the folded likelihood) gives a large gain in signed CRPS on reverse-flow rows over the plain network read as signed, and keeps the no-injection CRPS unchanged. Adding one shared variance term that grows with the fitted PV term improves on A, because the false calls sit next to zero crossings and the q01 coverage on reverse rows is about 0.05 against a nominal 0.01, so the signed distribution is too narrow where PV is large. partly supported candidate A neural network with a gate that decides once per Primary substation whether it exports power forecasts signed power much better than the same network with the gate shut on the half-hours with reverse flow (7.1 against 26.3 points of capacity) and does no harm beyond the registered tolerance (+0.03 points at D5) where no solar farm exports. Most of that gain comes from letting the forecast go below zero at all, because the ungated network scores the same on reverse-flow half-hours, and the gate's job is to stop invented reverse flow where there is none. Adding a PV-proportional variance did not help, so the next step is a serving test with real ensemble forecasts, not a further change to the network. also relevant
    00417 hidden-sign-mva-export-gate-zero-crossing-labels The false reverse flow that the signed network of experiment 412 produced at MVA-only Primaries came from a capacity fitted on the rows it labels, so a constraint that fixes the sign with information the capacity cannot fit from the same rows removes the false calls and keeps the skill. Two constraints are tested: a once-per-Primary export gate in front of the folded likelihood (design A, with a hard high-irradiance row gate H layered on it) and training labels taken from the two daily zero crossings of an export day (design C). not supported not-for-production Neither of the two constraints stopped the signed mixture density network from inventing reverse flow at MVA-only Primaries. A Primary-level export gate left a placebo with no injected farm clean, but the injected pair kept 12.0% false reverse flow, and training labels taken from the zero crossings of the daily reading reached the same balanced accuracy (0.82) as the unconstrained reference, with 85% false reverse flow at the no-injection D5. This is the eighth treatment of the MVA problem that did not work, so the stop rule applies. also relevant
    00416 mdn-explicit-solar-term-better-powered-follow-up The pooled plain mixture density network can keep its flexible irradiance input and gain an interpretable explicit solar term without losing skill: on sunny-midday half-hours at the six signed-direction Primaries, the network with the explicit solar term (C3), and the hybrid that also keeps the free irradiance input (H1), score within a stated margin of the network with the free irradiance input alone (A0), and they lower the 99th-percentile pinball loss. inconclusive not-for-production Adding an explicit solar-power term to the neural network did not beat letting the network read the sunshine forecast directly: over 12 test months the two score within 0.03 CRPS points overall, and on sunny middays the explicit term is nominally 0.23 points worse, with an interval too wide to call (the design cannot resolve differences under about 0.3 points). The explicit term's upper-tail (99th percentile) loss is slightly lower, but not reliably. Stronger capacity priors made no difference, so the learned capacities cannot yet be read as physical solar capacity. primary
    00412 mdn-signed-mw-from-mva-meters-known-answer A pooled mixture density network that writes signed power as X = D - C_p u, with D the plain 3-component mixture, u the CAMS unit PV shape, and C_p a learned capacity per Primary, trained with a folded likelihood where u is large, recovers the sign of reverse flow at signed-MW Primaries whose sign is hidden from it, when the sky is real and not when the sky is shuffled. not supported not-for-production A mixture density network that writes signed power as demand minus a learned solar capacity times the CAMS solar shape could not recover the sign of reverse flow without inventing it. With the sign hidden at two Primaries, it found most injected reverse flow in June but also called reverse flow on 17% of the half-hours that had none, and with no farm injected it called it on 18% to 47% of sunny half-hours at D5. Fixing the capacity, or holding it near a prior, removed the false calls and the skill together, so this is the seventh fold treatment of the MVA problem that did not work. also relevant
    00409 mdn-solar-term-day-of-year-and-anomaly-known-answer A pooled mixture density network that has a day-of-year by hour term in its demand module can attribute the monthly daylight profile of a farm to the demand module, so the explicit solar term learns capacity only from the day-to-day sky. Under that repair the known-answer test can be passed, and the solar term then lowers the net-demand CRPS at the six signed-direction Primaries against the same network given no solar term. not supported not-for-production Adding an explicit solar-power term to the neural network did not reliably lower the net-demand error at the six signed-direction substations: the change was about -0.06 points of capacity (about 2%), with an interval that includes zero. C3's pinball loss at q99 was 7% lower than A0-noirr-doy's (0.427 against 0.461; no interval was computed), and the test could not show that the network reads the sky, because an injected solar signal was too small to see above seed noise. The free irradiance input remains about as good as the explicit term. primary
    00405 mdn-explicit-pv-term-learned-capacity-known-answer A pooled mixture density network that forecasts net demand as a demand mixture minus a physical solar-power term, with the term's capacity learned per Primary and season-year under a prior, scores a lower net-demand CRPS at the six signed-direction Primaries than the same network given the irradiance as an ordinary input. A small adapter that modulates the physical solar forecast by weather scenario lowers the score further. inconclusive not-for-production We could not test whether a solar term with a learned capacity improves the forecast, because the known-answer test that gates it could not be passed. On the 2026-06 fold, with 3 seeds and 6 Primaries, adding a real solar farm's output made the network's capacity rise by about the right share, and it rose by 60 to 91% as much when the farm's days were shuffled. A fresh audit found that the shuffled series keeps most of the real series' daylight profile, so the test as built cannot separate a read of the sky from a read of the profile. The next experiment needs a test calibrated to the network. primary
    00376 all-primaries-gam-stack-with-mva-treatment-arms One recipe, experiment 252's stack (stage 1 plus stage 2) with the MVA treatment chosen by experiment 366's rule, scores as well as BG2 at the 6 Primaries that report signed megawatts, and at the 8 Primaries that report only apparent power the gap to BG2 comes from the MVA treatment and from Marsh Lane (D8), not from the architecture. Every result holds for reactive power zero. inconclusive not-for-production One recipe gives the same average score as BG2 at the six Primaries that report signed megawatts, but one year of data cannot rule out a gap of up to 0.13 points, and the stack is worse than BG2 on sunny half-hours and better on cold nights. At the eight Primaries that report only apparent power, the gap to BG2 is a Marsh Lane (D8) gap, and the rule that picks the fit scores 0.03 points better than the naive fit, within noise and all of it at Stickney (D12). This holds for reactive power zero only. also relevant
    00374 mva-fold-stage-two-at-eight-primaries At the eight Primaries that report only apparent power, the fit that the rule of experiment 366 chooses (the fit through the meter at D12, the naive fit at the other seven), followed by experiment 252's stage-2 corrections, scores no worse than experiment 252's arm A1 over all half-hours, pooled over the eight Primaries. supported not-for-production Choosing the fit by experiment 366's rule and then applying stage-2 corrections gives forecasts as good as the stack's A1 at the eight Primaries that report only apparent power. The forecast still trails BG2 at Marsh Lane (D8), where stage 2 makes the score worse, and slightly at Stickney (D12) and Langrick (D22). This holds for reactive power zero only; experiment 366's rule fails at power factor 0.97, and real MVA-only meters have unknown reactive power. primary
    00372 mva-fold-rule-chosen-fit-at-all-primaries At the eight MVA-only Primaries, the fit that the rule of experiment 366 chooses (the fit through the meter at Stickney, D12, and the naive fit that reads the meter reading as signed power at the other seven) scores better than the naive fit at D12 on sunny daylight half-hours, and scores no better than BG2, the production-style forecast, at most of the eight. not supported not-for-production Using the fit through the meter at Stickney, the one MVA-only Primary where it is chosen, lowers the score by 0.57 points on sunny half-hours, but twelve months of data cannot tell that from zero. At five of the eight MVA-only Primaries the stored raw fits already score within 0.1 points of BG2 over all half-hours, so the large gaps sit at D8, D12, and D22, not across the board. Experiment 372 repeats most of experiment 152, and the stack with its stage-2 corrections was not rerun. primary
    00370 mva-fold-pv-capacity-and-eight-primaries When the registered rule of experiment 366 chooses between the fit through the meter (L0) and the naive fit (N), the solar capacity of the chosen fit (the naive fit N or the fit through the meter L0) stays close to the capacity of the fit to the signed series at the six Primaries with signed MW readings, and at the eight MVA-only Primaries the fit through the meter finds reverse flow at the substations NGED marks "Yes" or "Maybe", not only at Stickney (D12). partly supported not-for-production With the meter's reactive power at zero, the rule of experiment 366 recovers each signed-MW Primary's solar capacity to within 3.1% of the signed fit, and the fit through the meter fits Stickney's sunny-half-hour readings with a reading error 9.3 points of capacity lower than the naive fit's. The fit through the meter does not find reverse flow at Marsh Lane (D8) or Langrick (D22), so either those substations have little reverse flow or the fit cannot see it. The pinned solar capacity is 45% and 58% above the signed fit's at D9 and D16, which is a reason to treat the pin as a reference and not as the truth. primary
    00369 d8-cross-site-step-rate-against-stack-gap The best-guess stack trails BG2 most at the Primaries that show the most generator-like steps, so D8's gap is part of a pattern across Primaries and not only a property of D8's residual. If the hypothesis holds, a step detector's rate is a cheap flag for the Primaries where the stack's uncertainty bands need widening. inconclusive not-for-production The test was inconclusive. A simple step counter at a fixed 1.5 MW does not flag the Primaries where the best-guess forecast trails the production-style forecast, but a counter scaled to each site's capacity does correlate with the gap (post hoc, 14 sites). Shuffled copies of the readings show as many steps as the real ones either way, so the counter mostly measures how volatile demand is, and a step-based explanation needs a detector that survives its placebo. primary
    00366 mva-fold-irradiance-correlation-reverse-flow-rule Reverse flow can be told apart from invented reverse flow using the meter readings and the irradiance forecast alone: where a substation has reverse flow, its sunny-midday meter readings rise with irradiance (the magnitude of a negative flow grows), and where it has none, they fall. partly supported not-for-production A test on how the midday meter readings move with sunshine, together with a test for readings near zero, picks the right fit at the substation with no reverse flow and at every substation where solar output was added, and it picks reverse flow at Stickney (D12) and at no other MVA-only Primary. All registered pass criteria are met with reactive power zero. The verdict is "partly supported" only because an unregistered check, at power factor 0.97, fails. The rule only works when the meter reading has no reactive power in it. At power factor 0.97 the readings no longer show the reverse flow, so the rule stays with the naive fit and loses badly at moderate injected solar. The 1.0 threshold on the low readings was chosen after seeing experiment 365's numbers, and it carries the moderate-injection cases. primary
    00365 mva-fold-readings-only-reverse-flow-rule Reverse flow can be told apart from invented reverse flow using the meter readings alone: if the training readings on sunny midday half-hours never come close to zero, the substation has no reverse flow, and the naive fit (reading the magnitude as signed power) is right. If they do come close to zero, the fit through the meter is used. partly supported not-for-production Looking for midday meter readings near zero does not reliably tell whether a substation has reverse flow. The rule still picks the wrong fit at the substation with none (D5), because that substation's readings also dip towards zero. The rule also misses heavy reverse flow, because heavy reverse flow gives large readings, not small ones: the rule picks the naive fit at Stickney (D12), the MVA-only Primary expected to have it. The registered pass test is met only because it allows one of five substations to be wrong. A rule that uses how the readings move with sunshine is an idea to test next. primary
    00363 d8-segment-level-term-in-the-gam-stack D8 (Marsh Lane) is a Primary substation whose demand carries on/off steps of about 4 MW and multi-day drops of about 2.7 MW. Experiment 361 removed or masked the detected steps in the training target and the score did not move. Giving the stack one penalised level term for each stretch between detected steps, fitted jointly with the weather and calendar terms, lets those terms be estimated within stretches, so D8's quantile score falls against experiment 354's best arm. not supported not-for-production Giving the forecast for D8 a separate level for each stretch between detected steps makes the forecast worse, not better, and also worsens the forecasts at two Primaries that have no engine. The detected steps do not carry a level that a monthly refit can extrapolate. One untested option is to estimate the generator's state at forecast time instead of fitting its past levels. primary
    00362 mva-fold-gated-fit-known-answer A gate that accepts reverse flow only where an independent solar forecast exceeds the fitted demand stops the fit through the meter from inventing reverse flow at a substation with none, without losing the reverse flow that real solar output produces. refuted not-for-production A fit to meter readings that give only the size of the power flow sometimes invents reverse flow (power flowing back to the grid) at a substation that has none. Rejecting such fits with an independent solar forecast does not work. The registered gate (ratio 0.75) rejected no fit, and the stricter gate (ratio 1.0) rejected the correct fits where reverse flow is heavy. The next test is a rule that uses the meter readings alone: accept reverse flow only if the training readings come close to zero at midday. primary
    00361 d8-step-catalogue-detector-injection-and-gam-stack D8 (Marsh Lane) is a Primary substation whose demand carries on/off steps of about 4 MW and multi-day drops of about 2.7 MW that look like a dispatchable generator. A plain, as-of step detector finds such components, and a GAM stack fitted on a training target with the detected steps removed scores better at D8 than the same stack fitted on the raw target, with the removed component returning through the own-residual band as the chance that the generator is running. not supported not-for-production A plain step detector finds injected 3 MW and 4 MW generator-like spells most of the time and 2 MW spells about half the time. Removing the steps it finds from D8's training target does not improve D8's forecast, so this test gives no support for building a particle filter that separates the generator. A detector that rejects ordinary demand swings, and a longer record of the generator's own output, would be the next things to try. primary
    00358 mva-fold-known-answer-at-folded-mw-primaries When the signed power of a Primary substation is folded to apparent power (its magnitude), the convex model fitted through the meter (experiment 110's L0) recovers the sign of the flow and the solar capacity, and a model that reads the folded reading as signed power does not. partly supported not-for-production A substation meter that reports only the size of the power flow, not its direction, still lets the model fitted through the meter recover the direction and the solar capacity about as well as a model that sees the signed power, as long as reactive power is zero. The model also invented reverse flow at the 1 of 5 substations that has none, so reverse flow it reports at a real substation needs an independent check. The pre-registered test could not be passed as written, because with the larger injection almost every sunny half-hour has reverse flow, so only one flow direction is present and balanced accuracy cannot be computed. primary
    00353 d8-research-default-with-mva-fold At D8 (Marsh Lane, a Primary metered only in MVA), a stack that fits its demand model through the fold-aware meter operator (experiment 152's arm A) and then applies the stack's night offset and recipe R scores better than the same stack fitted to the folded reading as if it were signed demand, and recovers reverse flow where the meter's fold hides it. not supported not-for-production D8's meter reports apparent power with no sign, so a flow back into the network is folded onto a positive reading. Modelling that fold changes D8's forecast score by less than half a point (1 point is 1% of D8's capacity), and the 95% interval includes zero, so this experiment found no evidence that the fold explains D8's poor score. The uncertainty bands are the larger problem: the stack takes their width from six other substations, and they are far too narrow at D8. Refitting the bands on D8's own earlier months cuts the gap to the production-style forecast (BG2) from 3.5 to 1.3 points, so the evidence favours trying that change first. also relevant
    00352 primary-demand-default-3-capacity-response-surface The score of the first (convex) stage of Primary demand research default 3 on sunny daylight half-hours is nearly flat in the solar capacity over 0.8 to 1.2 times the pinned capacity when the Kalman filter is on, because the filter absorbs the difference. Experiments 349 and 350 found no reliable gain from a different capacity, and a stored-data analysis suggested a flat response. Without the filter the response is steeper. partly supported not-for-production The demand forecast's score on sunny half-hours moved by only 0.010 points (-0.104 to +0.088) when the assumed solar capacity was 20% too low, but rose by 0.124 points when it was 20% too high and by 0.358 when it was 40% too high, so a capacity estimate above the pin is the risk and a lower capacity estimate is not. The Kalman filter halves the rise from too much capacity, which supports the idea that the filter absorbs part of the capacity error, but leaving out D5 cuts the rise at 20% too high from +0.124 to +0.074. Chasing a more exact capacity cannot be justified by this result: no scale from 0.6 to 1.4 beat the pin by more than 0.03 points with the filter on. primary
    00351 primary-demand-capacity-physical-shape-readout Experiment 349 read the solar capacity with the empirical plant shape of six metered farms (experiment 269) but multiplied it by the stack's physical unit shape, a mismatch its page lists as a lab shortcut. Reading the capacity out in the physical unit shape the stack itself uses, with the same monthly as-of refit and its own injection divisor, removes the mismatch. If the mismatch explained experiment 349's null result, the sunny-half-hour score improves. not supported not-for-production Estimating how much unmetered rooftop solar sits behind each substation with the clear-sky daily output curve that the forecast already uses did not improve the demand forecast on sunny half-hours. The quantile score was 0.065 points worse than with the fixed capacity from experiment 45 (95% interval +0.017 to +0.126). The two output curves give scores within 0.018 points of each other (-0.020 to +0.054), so mixing two curves in experiment 349 does not explain why it did not improve the forecast. Leaving out either of two substations (D16 or D10) makes the interval include zero, so the harm rests on those two. primary
    00349 primary-demand-default-3-exp-269-capacity-in-the-stack Primary demand research default 3 subtracts a solar capacity pinned to experiment 45's frozen vintage (a hindsight growth prior) times a unit solar shape. Replacing that capacity with the experiment 269 estimate (the mean plant response of six metered farms, read at the Primary's satellite irradiance, with the capacity fitted from rows before each fold's origin) lowers the quantile score on sunny daylight half-hours, because the experiment 269 read-out recovers 0.89 to 0.97 of an injected farm where the physical fleet shape recovers 0.78 to 0.83. inconclusive not-for-production Using the capacity estimated from six metered solar farms in place of the capacity pinned from earlier experiments did not improve the demand forecast on sunny half-hours: the score was 0.047 points worse, within the noise (-0.029 to +0.135), and the replicate year (2024-07 to 2025-06) pointed the same way. The estimate is lower than the pin at several Primaries, most of all at D16 where the forecast got worst. This was tested on the convex stage out to 7 days only, so the full stack and the 7 to 10 day range are untested. primary
    00306 summer-gap-solar-interactions-with-temperature-and-day-type The April-to-June daylight excess of arm D (the convex demand model with a Kalman filter on recent errors) over BG2 (the production-style XGBoost forecast) at the substations D5 and D9 is an additive-structure problem. On cold clear spring days heating demand and rooftop solar output are both large, and an additive model must give the solar term one weight on every day. Experiment 299 found the D5 excess largest on clear midday half-hours (+1.67 points). Letting the solar shape interact with temperature (shape times the heating hinge) and with day type (shape times a weekend indicator) lets the model give those days a different solar weight. refuted not-for-production Letting the rooftop-solar term vary with temperature and with weekdays versus weekends does not close the April-to-June gap to BG2: at the two substations with the gap, the extra columns close about 0% of it. Two linear interaction columns therefore do not explain the gap. The next test is what BG2 uses that the convex demand model does not (item 5 of the summer-gap brief). also relevant
    00299 summer-gap-timing-night-level-and-error-correction The April-to-June excess of arm D (the convex demand model with a Kalman filter on recent errors) over BG2 (the production-style XGBoost forecast) at the substations D5 and D9 is a step that starts at the March clock change and sits at short leads, which would point at a local-time mismatch or at BG2's use of recent power. D10's night-time level offset of about -2 points (21:00 to 07:30 UTC) is a step on one date, not a smooth drift with temperature. The Kalman filter and the correction head are net harmful at D5 and D9 in April to June. not supported not-for-production The spring excess of the best-guess demand forecast over BG2 grows with sunshine, appearing on clear midday half-hours at D5 and across the whole day at D9. It is not concentrated in the 4 weeks after the clock change or at short leads, so the next test is the solar term's response on clear half-hours at D5. The Kalman filter and the correction heads are not the cause: removing the filter changes little, and at D5 the heads reduce the excess. D10's night-time offset varies partly with month and has no single date on which it changes. also relevant
    00294 regime-aware-model-at-d8-engine-state-term D8's metered power excludes a gas engine's output (experiment 194) while the engine, which runs in spells (experiment 291), takes net load below what the weather and calendar predict. A single model trained across those spells has no way to know which half-hours the engine ran, so its forecast and its bands carry the engine as noise. A model that keeps the calendar, weather, and solar shapes shared across the whole history but adds one regime-specific term, an offset per half-hour the engine runs (non-negative, taking power off net load), forecasts D8 better than the single model. The offset is estimated from the data, and its size is not assumed. not supported not-for-production Telling the forecast when D8's gas engine is running would make it much better when the state is read at the half-hour being forecast, but the detector builds that state from the very reading being forecast. With only the state of the last settled day, the engine term made the forecast 10% worse; trained on that stale state, it gained only 3%, no better than widening the bands from past residuals. A forecast of whether the engine runs, or NGED's own record of its running hours, would be the data to test next. primary
    00291 d8-drops-engine-spells-load-shift D8's 29 multi-day drops (experiment 159) are spells in which a gas engine of up to three units runs, and D8's own metered power no longer includes the engine's output (experiment 194). If so, the engine detector of experiment 196 flags the drops' half-hours far more often than matched windows elsewhere on D8, the drops' depths cluster at multiples of one engine unit, and most of D8's load-shift alarms that fall inside drops are engine edges. partly supported not-for-production D8's multi-day drops coincide with the engine detector's running states, which is consistent with the gas engine running: the detector flags 27 of the 28 drops that began after the engine left D8's reading, against about 1 in 5 comparable stretches of D8, and it flags almost nothing at three other Primaries on the same dates. This cannot be called proved, because the drops and the detector both read D8's residual, and the depths of the drops do not fall at whole multiples of the engine's 1.97 MW unit. D8's drops should be flagged as a probable generator regime and reported apart from the load-shift scoring until NGED confirms the engine's running hours, and the next step is a forecast model for D8 that is told when the engine is running. also relevant
    00289 summer-gap-weather-input-discriminator The best-guess stack's weaker April to June score than BG2 at the decision Primaries comes from the ensemble's sunshine forecast (or its ratio calibration), and not from demand absorbing the solar term or from the error-correction layers. partly supported not-for-production Giving the best-guess stack the sunshine that actually happened would close about 70% of its April to June gap to BG2, but the same change helps the stack nearly as much in July to September, when the stack already matches BG2. Most of the April to June gap beyond the July to September gap therefore stays. That points to the solar term in the demand model, or to the error-correction layers, and away from the ensemble sunshine forecast. A refit with realised sunshine as the solar driver, and a check of the solar term at D5 and D9 in April to June, are the next steps. also relevant
    00282 pv-plant-curve-audit The curve that turns sunshine into power in our solar models departs from the metered farms' measured curve by more than 5% of capacity somewhere in the clear-sky range, and the departure is the flat-top level (the inverter limit), not the panel tilt or the temperature loss. not supported not-for-production The curve that turns sunshine into solar power stays within 5 percentage points of capacity at 3 of the 6 metered solar farms and departs from their measured output at the other 3 in different directions, so no single fix to the inverter limit repairs it. The best-guess demand stack assumes panels tilted 35 degrees and the farms fit best at about 20 degrees, which makes the stack over-predict output on clear half-hours in December (measured is 0.66 of the stack, median over farms) and under-predict it from April to July by 5 to 8%.
