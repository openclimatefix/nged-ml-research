    00633 bakeoff-final-test-bg2-and-lag-enabled-bsp-gsp-network Part 1.     00631 bsp-group-sum-with-metered-generators-against-direct-bsp-forecast Experiment 629 found that sums of the 16 Primaries beneath two bulk supply points (BSPs) put 31% to 79% of actual flows below their q05 (nominal 5%), and that the metered wind and solar series beneath the BSP explained much of the gap (R squared 0.94 at Skegness). The claim here is that, once those metered farms are added to the sum together with a fitted remainder, a coupled scenario set (J3, the Schaake shuffle, or J5, the fitted copula) puts the q05 and q95 exceedance of the BSP flow within the binomial error of nominal at both BSPs, and beats independent draws (J0) on CRPS. not supported not-for-production Adding the nine metered wind and solar farms to the sum of Primaries fixes most of the export-side tail miss of Primaries-only sums (Skegness 41.7% to 3.0% of actual flows below the q05, on this experiment's rows), but the import side swings from too wide or about right (Boston 0.1%, Skegness 4.4% above the q95) to too narrow (14.3% and 32.6%), and no sum has both tails near 5% at both bulk supply points (BSPs). Coupling the scenarios (Schaake shuffle or fitted copula) improves the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) by 2.6% to 7.7%, so coupling matters once the farms are in. The direct forecast of each BSP meter is still the safer tail forecast, which means the deliverable should be a direct BSP forecast beside any node scenarios. also relevant
    00629 bsp-group-sum-of-nodes-against-direct-bsp-forecast Experiment 436 found 13.9% of actual group net flows above the q95 of independent-draw sums (J0), against 6.6% for the Schaake shuffle (J3). The claim here is that, summed to a bulk supply point (BSP) and scored against the BSP's own meter, a coupled scenario set (J3 or the fitted copula J5) puts the export-side q95 exceedance within the binomial error of 5% and has a lower threshold-weighted CRPS at the scaled limit than J0, and that the direct forecast of the BSP meter does not beat every summed set by 5%. not supported not-for-production Summing coherent scenarios of the 16 Primaries did not give a usable export tail for either BSP: 31% to 79% of actual flows fell below the sum's q05 where 5% should, in every coupling and every demand network, while the direct forecast of each BSP's own meter was close to nominal (4.6% and 6.9%). The missing metered generators explain much of the gap (R squared 0.94 at Skegness), so the next step is a sum that includes them, or to deliver the direct BSP forecast alongside the node scenarios. also relevant
    00628 quantile-form-client-side-scenario-rehydration NGED reads stored forecasts from S3 and builds scenarios in Python. The stored forecasts of experiments 425, 429, and 430 hold 11 quantiles (0.01, 0.05, 0.10, 0.25, 0.40, 0.50, 0.60, 0.75, 0.90, 0.95, 0.99) per site and half-hour. The claim is that a client holding only these 11 quantiles, plus a small dependence table, draws 1,000 coherent scenarios per run that score within 1% of the 51 scenarios the pipeline builds (experiment 436's J5 and J3) on CRPS, and within 1 percentage point on exceedance at the 0.01, 0.05, 0.95, and 0.99 levels, provided the client extends the quantile function past the outer levels with a good tail rule. partly supported not-for-production NGED can rebuild the marginal forecasts and a Schaake-shuffle coupling from 11 stored quantiles per site and half-hour plus a small dependence table. 1,000 client-drawn scenarios score within 0.3% of the 51 scenarios the pipeline builds on CRPS, and a Schaake shuffle of 51 stored past paths matches the pipeline's shuffle on the variogram score. The only loss we measured is for the fitted copula on solar farms (6.6% worse on the variogram score). The pipeline's version also uses the weather ensemble's member ranks, which the quantiles do not carry, and that is the likely cause. Mixture parameters were not needed for any of this. The tails of the stored quantiles are too narrow for any of the three tail rules to fix. also relevant
    00627 scenario-subset-selection-from-51-coherent-paths NGED will run a handful of power-flow studies by hand, so they need K of the 51 coherent member paths (K = 3, 5, 10) that cover the distribution of the 51, without labelling any path. A path that is low wind on day 1 need not be low wind on day 2, so a label such as "the low-wind scenario" does not carry over. We test whether picking K paths by stratifying on one summary of the run (S) or by clustering the 51 joint paths (C) covers the 51-path distribution better than a simple random sample (R). partly supported not-for-production Picking 5 of the 51 forecast paths by cutting them into 5 equal groups on one summary and taking one path from each covers their spread better than picking 5 at random, in every month, but still misses the tails: the stratified rules leave a gap of 11% to 12% of the range at the 5th percentile and 13% to 14% at the 95th. For a bulk supply point's limit, an observed daily peak export beat the largest of 5 chosen paths on 12% of days (1.6% for all 51); 10 paths chosen by that substation's own peak export came closest (6.3%, against 7.3% for a random 10). NGED should treat 10 paths as a minimum when the tail matters. also relevant
    00626 widen-the-tails-of-the-51-scenario-member-values In experiments 434 and 436 the 51 values per node and half-hour were too narrow in the tails: the q95 of the 51 values was exceeded by 7.6% to 8.8% of actuals (target 5%). A factor per family and lead band, fitted on earlier months only, that rescales the 51 values about their median (arm B), or re-draws them from a wider quantile function (arm C), brings the q05 and q95 exceedance rates to within 1 point of 5% in every family and lead band on later months, while the variogram and energy scores of the Schaake-shuffled paths move by less than 2% and the CRPS of the 51 values moves by less than 1%. partly supported not-for-production Rescaling the 51 scenario values with a separate upper and lower factor (arm D) brought demand's q05 and q95 exceedance to 4% to 6% in every lead band, but did not fix wind or solar. Demand's q90 is still exceeded for 11% to 16% of half-hours (target 10%), which points to a forecast that sits too low rather than too narrow. In experiment 436, most of the apparent q95 excess came from its quantile definition, not from the forecast. The next step is a bias correction for demand, then a re-check of the tails. also relevant
    00625 coherent-members-from-quantiles-k-sweep-and-path-checks Experiments 434 and 436 already scored independent draws (IND), ensemble copula coupling (ECC), a Schaake shuffle (SS), and member-wise quantiles (MW) at 51 paths. A Schaake shuffle gave the wind farms and solar farms variogram scores 28% to 43% below IND but gave the 16 Primaries nothing. This experiment tests three new claims. (1) SS paths cut to K = 10 lose under 2% of the variogram score of K = 51. (2) A Schaake shuffle that orders by anomaly paths (observed power minus the mean of the same clock time on the four preceding same-weekdays) gives the Primaries a variogram score at least 5% below IND, because observed-power paths mix the seasonal level with the weather anomaly. (3) SS paths put neighbouring solar farms in opposite sky states less often than IND does and about as often as the observations do. partly supported not-for-production NGED can get coherent wind-farm and solar-farm paths from a Schaake shuffle of past observed paths, which scores 34% below independent draws across the farms, and 20 paths lose 2% to 3% of the 51-path score across the farms (10 paths lose 5% to 11%). The 16 Primaries gain nothing from any coupling we tried, including a shuffle of anomaly paths, because their errors are only weakly correlated, so independent draws are close to right there. Experiment 436's joint-block scores were wrong (a reshape mixed members and nodes) and are replaced by the values here, which show no difference between couplings on the joint block. also relevant
    00622 bsp-gsp-lagged-against-lag-free-matched-d5-thermal-lag-rematch At the 2 bulk supply points (BSPs) and the 2 grid supply points (GSPs), a demand network that reads the power of one day and one week earlier forecasts better than a lag-free network at days 1 to 3, once both arms use the D5 offset and the thermal-lag and solar-gain columns, and the two arms tie from day 4. partly supported not-for-production Once the lag-enabled and lag-free networks differ only in the lags, the lags lower the error at days 1 to 3 by a small but measurable amount at the 2 bulk supply points (0.11 points of capacity, about 2%), and by a larger but unproven amount at the 2 grid supply points (0.73 points, about 4.5%). From day 4 the BSP gain fades to zero by about day 6, while the GSP gain (-0.32 at days 4 to 10, interval includes zero) is neither shown nor ruled out. No effect of the thermal-lag columns was detectable in either arm. primary
    00619 bsp-gsp-lag-enabled-network-for-the-bake-off At the 2 bulk supply points (BSPs) and the 2 grid supply points (GSPs), a demand network that also reads the power of one day and one week earlier forecasts better than the lag-free network at days 1 to 3, and no better after day 4. partly supported not-for-production Giving the network the previous day's and the previous week's power lowered the forecast error a little at both bulk and grid supply points, in 19 of the 20 group-and-day cells (days 1 to 10), but by less than the 5 development months can resolve. The lag-enabled network is the lowest-scoring forecast in the table, yet the evidence does not separate it from the lag-free network, so the choice between them rests on cost and on the maintainer's judgement. The 90% band is still too narrow. primary
    00611 bsp-gsp-pilot-backtests-for-the-bake-off The lag-free network with the steadier offset O2 (experiment 577), pooled per group, forecasts the 2 bulk supply points (BSPs) and the 2 grid supply points (GSPs) at least as well as a month-by-half-hour climatology, and at the BSPs as well as the weekly XGBoost model (BG2). partly supported not-for-production A neural network that reads weather and the calendar but no recent power forecasts the two bulk supply points better than the weekly XGBoost model, and the two grid supply points about as well. Giving it the previous day's and the previous week's power gave no clear further gain. The 90% band is too narrow everywhere, so calibration deserves scoring beside accuracy at these four series. primary
    00604 bsp-gsp-pilot-design-for-the-bake-off The 4 series (2 bulk supply points, 2 grid supply points) have enough clean history to backtest the five development months with a neural network of the Primary recipe, inside 6 GPU-h. inconclusive not-for-production The pilot is affordable and the data are clean, but the 4 series differ from the Primaries in two ways that matter. A grid supply point carries much more than the bulk supply point under it, and one of the two grid supply points mostly exports power. The best-evidenced recipe is experiment 415's pooled network with lags, which beat the weekly XGBoost model by 0.47 points at the bulk supply points and tied it at the grid supply points. I recommend a pilot that fits the lag-free network with the output offset first, as the cheapest and safest design, and the lag network second. primary
    00570 price-encoder-part-1-known-answer-test-and-pretrained-stack Price-following assets such as batteries and gas engines put price-driven variation into a series' power that weather and calendar cannot explain. A price encoder that sees the true price while the modular lag-free network is pre-trained can absorb that variation, so the weather encoder and the datetime encoder learn cleaner patterns from the same targets. The price encoder is discarded before fine-tuning, so serving needs no price feed. not supported not-for-production Training a price encoder (a neural network part fed electricity prices) beside the weather and datetime encoders did not make those encoders cleaner: after fine-tuning, the forecast was no better, and at the six MW Primaries it was worse by 0.29 points of CRPS (the probabilistic forecast error, as a percentage of each Primary's capacity) than a forecast trained without price. Most of that harm also appears when the encoder sees only noise, so the extra encoder itself lowers accuracy, and at the eight MVA Primaries the price content adds nothing the data can resolve. The same encoder could not recover an injected price-driven generator in a controlled test (it recovered 45% of the injected battery rule's output and none of the threshold and rank rules'), so the next step is a controlled test with a larger injected generator before any further real-data price work. also relevant
    00564 pooled-primary-bsp-gsp-m2-reduced Adding the 2 bulk supply points (BSPs) and 2 grid supply points (GSPs) to the training rows of the modular lag-free network M0 (experiment 545, replicated in 554), with a one-hot input saying whether a series is a Primary, a BSP, or a GSP, lowers the Primaries' CRPS at leads of 24 to 120 h, because the weather and calendar encoders see more examples of how weather and calendar drive load. not supported not-for-production Adding two bulk and two grid supply points to the training rows of the demand network did not make its forecasts at the 16 Primaries better: the change is smaller than the experiment could resolve, and its sign differs between the two groups of Primaries. The same network forecasts the four extra series better than a climatology, so pooling is a way to cover them with one network, not a way to improve the Primaries. A test of whether pooling helps needs many more extra series than NGED's data holds. primary
    00563 pooled-primary-bsp-gsp-data-check At least 8 bulk supply point (BSP) and grid supply point (GSP) series in NGED's data are clean for 2 years before each fold cutoff and have a weather location, so that a pooled neural network (arm M2 of the modular-architecture item) can learn from more series than the 16 Primaries. inconclusive not-for-production NGED's metadata holds four bulk and grid supply point series. Adding them to the 16 Primaries gives 20 series, too few to test whether many extra series help. By the hierarchy file, each grid series should include one bulk series, so the four are not independent. The test was not run. Experiment 415 already fitted a pooled neural network at the 16 Primaries and these four series. primary
    00525 metering-type-flag-lagless-demand-network The 8-wide Primary embedding of the lag-free network is mostly its random start, so it may not carry the one fact that separates the eight MVA-only Primaries from the six signed-MW Primaries: apparent power cannot be negative, and signed power can. One extra 0/1 input column (1 for apparent power in MVA) tells the network that fact directly. If the embedding already carries it, the column adds nothing; if the column improves the forecast or removes the mixture's probability mass below zero at the eight, the embedding is too thin. not supported not-for-production Telling the lag-free demand network which Primaries report apparent power (MVA) and which report signed MW did not make its forecast better. At the eight MVA-only Primaries the flag made the score slightly worse, as a randomly permuted flag did, and it did not remove the forecast probability below zero. At the six signed-MW Primaries it helped by about 0.03 points, a gain the permuted flag did not reproduce but that is too small to port. primary
    00522 primary-embedding-data-look-lagless-demand-network The Primary embedding of the lag-free neural network does more than label the 16 Primaries. The embedding is the 16 x 8 table of vectors that tells the network which Primary (primary substation) a row belongs to. The network is the L0PT network of experiment 448: it reads no lagged power and is pretrained on ERA5 and CAMS estimates of past weather. Each vector starts as a random N(0, 1) draw. The hypothesis is that the movement from that start is small, is not arranged by a Primary's physical behaviour, and is not needed by the network, because a random fixed label is enough to separate 16 Primaries. partly supported not-for-production The lag-free demand network's Primary embedding is not just a random label: pretraining moves each vector about half its length, and scoring a Primary with its own random start vector instead of its trained vector raises the error by 0.8 to 1.3 points of capacity, so the movement is used. The movement is only weakly arranged by physical behaviour (winter daily shape, through the size of the movement and not its direction) and does not tell signed-MW from MVA-only Primaries, so giving the network per-Primary descriptors, and not only a learned label, is worth testing next. primary
    00475 recipe-on-bulk-and-grid-supply-points-against-pooled-plain-network The first-release candidate recipe (experiment 425's arm A: ERA5-and-CAMS pretraining, the Primary-and-node embedding, hand-made calendar columns, the D15 and D18 cut, and flagged exact-zero half-hours dropped), fitted pooled with the 16 Primaries and a node-type input, scores lower CRPS at days 1 to 10 than experiment 415's N1 at both bulk supply points and does not lose at the grid supply points. not supported not-for-production The assembled recipe (ERA5-and-CAMS pretraining, cuts, flagged zeros dropped) does not improve on the plain pooled network at the two bulk supply points: it is better at D17 (-0.14) and worse at D13 (+0.09), both inside the noise. At the two grid supply points the recipe is about 0.5 points of capacity better than the plain network, which is close to the size of the refit noise (about 0.4). Against BG2 the recipe is better at all four nodes, with the largest gain at the grid supply points. primary
    00459 load-shift-conservation-check-donor-and-recipient-sum When NGED moves load from one Primary substation to another, most of the donor's step is still visible in the sum over the donor and its labelled recipients, so a forecast of that group sum, split by a slowly changing share, would be immune to a transfer between members. refuted not-for-production When NGED moves load from one Primary substation to another, the sum over the donor and its labelled recipients still steps by about half of the donor's step, as it does in windows with no logged event. A forecast of that sum split by a share therefore cannot absorb a transfer, and even with the true sum known, the split is no more accurate than the lagged XGBoost forecast of experiment 392, so the group-sum forecast does not reach its threshold. also relevant
    00457 gam-stage-1-forecast-as-an-input-to-the-pooled-mixture-density-network If the pooled mixture density network of experiment 425's arm A is given the out-of-fold stage-1 GAM forecast of Primary demand, either as one extra input feature (A-gam-in) or as a zero-initialised additive base that the network corrects (A-gam-base), its CRPS at days 1 to 10 is lower than arm A's on the eight MVA-only Primaries, because the GAM carries the smooth calendar, temperature, and solar shapes that the network learns less well from five years of data. small gain candidate Giving the pretrained neural network the GAM's forecast as one extra input improves the forecast a little: CRPS falls by 0.036 points at the eight MVA-only Primaries, and the pooled cold-night and tail scores do not get worse. The gain is as large as the fit-to-fit noise, so it is real but marginal. At the signed-MW six the gain (0.035) is half that group's minimum detectable effect (0.070). The input needs the GAM in the live service with its monthly refit. Using the GAM as the base that the neural network corrects made forecasts worse (+0.291), because the GAM starts from a worse forecast than the pretrained neural network and fine-tuning does not recover. also relevant
    00436 joint-scenarios-across-demand-wind-solar-and-leads The stored marginal forecasts of demand (experiment 425 arm A), wind (experiment 429), and solar (experiment 430) can be turned into 51 scenarios that are one coherent realisation across the 16 Primaries, the three wind farms, the six solar farms, and the leads, without changing any marginal. A single historical ordering (a Schaake shuffle, J3) or an ensemble ordering corrected by a fitted error copula (J5) matches the observed joint behaviour better than the ensemble's own member ranks (J2) do. Tying scenario k to ensemble member k (J4) loses no coherence for demand, and helps for wind and solar. partly supported not-for-production To give NGED scenarios that are coherent across wind farms and solar farms, use a Schaake shuffle from past observed paths (J3) or the copula-corrected ensemble ranks (J5): both scored 28% to 43% lower than independent draws across the farms, although J3 gave no wind gain at 12 to 36 hours. Across the 16 Primaries nothing beat independent draws, and tying scenario k to ensemble member k (J4) scored the same as J3 across Primaries and wind farms, so it brought no gain for its extra complexity. Ramps scored 14% better under J3 to J5 (9% under ensemble ranks, J2), and group-sum CRPS 1% to 2% better, but the other proxies showed no gain. The 51 values themselves are too narrow in the tails (q95 exceeded by 7.6% to 8.8% of actuals), and the intervals are optimistic because they rest on 10 calendar months. Four of the five registered predictions (P1 to P4) failed. also relevant
    00434 demand-ensemble-copula-coupling-and-schaake-reordering-of-stored-forecasts The stored forecast of experiment 425's arm A holds 11 quantiles per Primary and half-hour. Drawing 51 values from those quantiles and assigning them to the 51 ensemble members by the rank of each member's 2-metre temperature (ensemble copula coupling, ECC) gives sample paths that match the observed dependence across Primaries and leads better than independent draws do, by more than the 5% coherence margin on the variogram score, without changing the marginal CRPS. A Schaake shuffle from past observed paths does at least as well as ECC. not supported not-for-production Re-ordering the stored demand forecast's 51 values by member temperature or by past observed paths does not give clearly better sample paths overall. Ordering by temperature made the paths about 7% less coherent across the 16 Primaries and 1.7% more coherent across leads at days 1 to 10 (13% at 12 to 36 hours, an interim band), and the effects cancel in the combined score. A Schaake shuffle from past paths was no worse than independent draws across Primaries and 3.5% better across leads at days 1 to 10 (interval -6.3% to -0.9%, below the 5% margin), so it is the stronger reference candidate if a step 2 runs the 51 members through the neural network. also relevant
    00426 mdn-neighbouring-primaries-graph-and-bottom-up-design Information from the Primaries connected to a Primary improves the pooled plain mixture density network's forecast at days 1 to 2, the only leads at which recent power helps the signed-MW six, by more than simple neighbour statistics do. Residuals of the stored forecast of interconnected Primaries correlate more strongly than residuals of Primaries joined at random or by distance alone, and a network that passes messages along the interconnection graph beats both its own rewired-graph control and the simple-statistics control. not supported not-for-production In these tests, the recent forecast errors of a Primary's interconnected neighbours predicted none of its day-ahead forecast error, and adding them to a simple regression made it slightly worse on ordinary half-hours. A graph network over the Primaries therefore has no lagged-error signal to pass along, and none was fitted. A scaled sum of the forecasts of the Primaries beneath a bulk supply point scored 34% to 60% higher error than the network that sees the node's own readings, at two bulk supply points. Whether neighbours help inside load-shift events was not measured here, and experiment 404 tests it. primary
    00425 best-mdn-architecture-assembly-with-ablations The pooled mixture density network that adds ERA5-and-CAMS pretraining (experiment 418) to the pooled plain network with the Primary embedding, the ensemble-summary inputs, the hand-made calendar columns, and the 1.15 widening (experiments 397, 403, 406, 407, 423) scores lower CRPS at days 1 to 10 than the same network without pretraining, and removing the Primary embedding or the widening makes it worse. partly supported candidate Pretraining the pooled mixture density network on ERA5-and-CAMS rows before fine-tuning it on ensemble rows lowered the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) by 0.050 points of capacity (-0.078 to -0.021) at the six signed-MW Primaries and by 0.124 (-0.219 to -0.048) at the eight MVA-only Primaries. The gain is clear at the MVA-only eight and probable at the signed-MW six, where the interval just misses the registered bar of -0.03. The assembled network beat the monthly XGBoost recipe and BG2 at both of those groups and trailed BG2 at D15 and D18, so pretraining is a candidate for a numbered release of the production forecast and the Primary embedding is the component to protect. also relevant
    00423 pooled-mdn-shared-date-time-encoder A small encoder that turns the half-hour of day, day of week, day of month, day of year, and the bank-holiday name into one learned vector, shared across the 16 Primaries, lets the pooled plain mixture density network share what it learns about holidays across Primaries and across holidays of one kind, so it forecasts bank-holiday weeks better than the hand-made calendar columns and is no worse elsewhere. not supported not-for-production A small calendar encoder shared by the 16 Primary substations did not forecast demand better than the 18 hand-made calendar columns. With holiday names and dates it scored 0.27 points of capacity worse (8% worse, in a range of 0.10 to 0.43) on the continuous ranked probability score at the six substations that report signed power, and with dates only it scored 0.37 worse. One possible reason, not tested, is that each day of the year appears only once or twice in the 24-month training window.
