    Hierarchy and pooling: experiments

    This page lists every experiment tried in the theme, newest first. An experiment that belongs mainly to another theme but bears on this one is marked "also relevant".

    Experiment Hypothesis Verdict Production status Take-home message Relevance
    00434 demand-ensemble-copula-coupling-and-schaake-reordering-of-stored-forecasts The stored forecast of experiment 425's arm A holds 11 quantiles per Primary and half-hour. Drawing 51 values from those quantiles and assigning them to the 51 ensemble members by the rank of each member's 2-metre temperature (ensemble copula coupling, ECC) gives sample paths that match the observed dependence across Primaries and leads better than independent draws do, by more than the 5% coherence margin on the variogram score, without changing the marginal CRPS. A Schaake shuffle from past observed paths does at least as well as ECC. not supported not-for-production Re-ordering the stored demand forecast's 51 values by member temperature or by past observed paths does not give clearly better sample paths overall. Ordering by temperature made the paths about 7% less coherent across the 16 Primaries and 1.7% more coherent across leads at days 1 to 10 (13% at 12 to 36 hours, an interim band), and the effects cancel in the combined score. A Schaake shuffle from past paths was no worse than independent draws across Primaries and 3.5% better across leads at days 1 to 10 (interval -6.3% to -0.9%, below the 5% margin), so it is the stronger reference candidate if a step 2 runs the 51 members through the neural network. also relevant
    00426 mdn-neighbouring-primaries-graph-and-bottom-up-design Information from the Primaries connected to a Primary improves the pooled plain mixture density network's forecast at days 1 to 2, the only leads at which recent power helps the signed-MW six, by more than simple neighbour statistics do. Residuals of the stored forecast of interconnected Primaries correlate more strongly than residuals of Primaries joined at random or by distance alone, and a network that passes messages along the interconnection graph beats both its own rewired-graph control and the simple-statistics control. not supported not-for-production In these tests, the recent forecast errors of a Primary's interconnected neighbours predicted none of its day-ahead forecast error, and adding them to a simple regression made it slightly worse on ordinary half-hours. A graph network over the Primaries therefore has no lagged-error signal to pass along, and none was fitted. A scaled sum of the forecasts of the Primaries beneath a bulk supply point scored 34% to 60% higher error than the network that sees the node's own readings, at two bulk supply points. Whether neighbours help inside load-shift events was not measured here, and experiment 404 tests it. primary
    00425 best-mdn-architecture-assembly-with-ablations The pooled mixture density network that adds ERA5-and-CAMS pretraining (experiment 418) to the pooled plain network with the Primary embedding, the ensemble-summary inputs, the hand-made calendar columns, and the 1.15 widening (experiments 397, 403, 406, 407, 423) scores lower CRPS at days 1 to 10 than the same network without pretraining, and removing the Primary embedding or the widening makes it worse. partly supported candidate Pretraining the pooled mixture density network on ERA5-and-CAMS rows before fine-tuning it on ensemble rows lowered the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) by 0.050 points of capacity (-0.078 to -0.021) at the six signed-MW Primaries and by 0.124 (-0.219 to -0.048) at the eight MVA-only Primaries. The gain is clear at the MVA-only eight and probable at the signed-MW six, where the interval just misses the registered bar of -0.03. The assembled network beat the monthly XGBoost recipe and BG2 at both of those groups and trailed BG2 at D15 and D18, so pretraining is a candidate for a numbered release of the production forecast and the Primary embedding is the component to protect. also relevant
    00423 pooled-mdn-shared-date-time-encoder A small encoder that turns the half-hour of day, day of week, day of month, day of year, and the bank-holiday name into one learned vector, shared across the 16 Primaries, lets the pooled plain mixture density network share what it learns about holidays across Primaries and across holidays of one kind, so it forecasts bank-holiday weeks better than the hand-made calendar columns and is no worse elsewhere. not supported not-for-production A small calendar encoder shared by the 16 Primary substations did not forecast demand better than the 18 hand-made calendar columns. With holiday names and dates it scored 0.27 points of capacity worse (8% worse, in a range of 0.10 to 0.43) on the continuous ranked probability score at the six substations that report signed power, and with dates only it scored 0.37 worse. One possible reason, not tested, is that each day of the year appears only once or twice in the 24-month training window. The planned test on bank-holiday weeks could not detect any difference smaller than 0.34 points, and all five test months are interim. also relevant
    00421 mdn-history-fingerprint-for-pooled-primary-demand A 1D convolutional encoder over the last 7 days of a Primary's own demand gives the pooled plain mixture density network enough information to infer which Primary it forecasts, so that the network without a learned Primary embedding recovers most of the embedding's gain, and a Primary held out of training is forecast better than by a network given a placeholder embedding. not supported not-for-production The last 7 days of a substation's power do tell a pooled neural network which substation it is forecasting, but the network still forecasts worse with that history than with a learned substation label: 3.64 against 3.32 points of capacity at the six signed-direction substations. The raw history identifies the substation as well as the learned encoder does, so the encoder adds nothing, and giving it to the network that has the label made the forecast slightly worse. For a substation the network never trained on, the simplest network (no label, no encoder, three recent readings) forecast best, and a placeholder label made the forecast much worse. All five test months are interim. primary
    00418 mdn-pretrain-on-era5-and-cams-then-fine-tune-on-ens The pooled plain mixture density network of experiment 397, pretrained on the Primaries' own power and ERA5 and CAMS weather from 2019-09 and then fine-tuned on the ENS rows, scores lower CRPS at days 1 to 10 than the same network trained on the ENS rows alone, because the ENS archive starts on 2024-04-01 and the pooled network sees at most 18 to 24 months of it. supported candidate Teaching the neural network first on six years of past weather and power, then on the forecast archive, lowers its error by about 0.06 points of capacity (2.1%) at the six signed-direction substations and 0.16 points (4.2%) at the eight apparent-power-only substations over 12 test months. Changing how much of the network is retrained, or adding forecast-like noise, changes the score by less than the interval of the estimate. With only 6 months of forecast archive on 4 test months the gain is about 1.3 points, although the ENS-only baseline degrades badly there. also relevant
    00416 mdn-explicit-solar-term-better-powered-follow-up The pooled plain mixture density network can keep its flexible irradiance input and gain an interpretable explicit solar term without losing skill: on sunny-midday half-hours at the six signed-direction Primaries, the network with the explicit solar term (C3), and the hybrid that also keeps the free irradiance input (H1), score within a stated margin of the network with the free irradiance input alone (A0), and they lower the 99th-percentile pinball loss. inconclusive not-for-production Adding an explicit solar-power term to the neural network did not beat letting the network read the sunshine forecast directly: over 12 test months the two score within 0.03 CRPS points overall, and on sunny middays the explicit term is nominally 0.23 points worse, with an interval too wide to call (the design cannot resolve differences under about 0.3 points). The explicit term's upper-tail (99th percentile) loss is slightly lower, but not reliably. Stronger capacity priors made no difference, so the learned capacities cannot yet be read as physical solar capacity. also relevant
    00415 pooled-mixture-density-network-at-bulk-and-grid-supply-points A pooled plain mixture density network fitted across the 16 Primaries and the 4 nodes (D13 and D17 are bulk supply points; D1 and D6 are grid supply points) with a node-type input scores lower CRPS at days 1 to 10 at the nodes than the same network fitted at the 16 Primaries only and applied to the nodes, and is not worse than the stored monthly XGBoost control of experiment 386 by more than 0.30 points of effective capacity at either group. supported not-for-production A pooled neural network trained on the readings of two bulk supply points and two grid supply points together with the 16 Primaries forecasts the bulk supply points a little better than the weekly XGBoost forecast (0.47 points of capacity lower, in all four months) and the grid supply points about as well, with very wide bands. A copy fitted at the Primaries alone fails at these points, partly because of how its inputs were scaled. Giving the network the readings of the Primaries beneath a node did not help. primary
    00413 differentiable-physics-demand-network-part-1 A pooled network that forecasts Primary demand as a Primary-specific mix of shared learned daily shapes plus a Primary-specific mix of shared learned temperature and solar responses, with a plain mixture density head on the residual, scores within 0.03 points of the pooled plain mixture density network on the 2026-01 fold, and its learned curves can be read. not supported not-for-production A neural network built from shared, readable parts (learned daily shapes and temperature responses) forecast substation demand less accurately than the plain neural network on five test months: by 0.17 points of capacity at the 6 substations that record the direction of flow, and by 0.23 points at the other 8. Its error in the middle of the distribution was larger at both groups. At the six it covered the highest outcomes better, but at the eight it was worse in both tails. Under the default rule the design does not advance, and whether to pursue it is the maintainer's call. also relevant
    00409 mdn-solar-term-day-of-year-and-anomaly-known-answer A pooled mixture density network that has a day-of-year by hour term in its demand module can attribute the monthly daylight profile of a farm to the demand module, so the explicit solar term learns capacity only from the day-to-day sky. Under that repair the known-answer test can be passed, and the solar term then lowers the net-demand CRPS at the six signed-direction Primaries against the same network given no solar term. not supported not-for-production Adding an explicit solar-power term to the neural network did not reliably lower the net-demand error at the six signed-direction substations: the change was about -0.06 points of capacity (about 2%), with an interval that includes zero. C3's pinball loss at q99 was 7% lower than A0-noirr-doy's (0.427 against 0.461; no interval was computed), and the test could not show that the network reads the sky, because an injected solar signal was too small to see above seed noise. The free irradiance input remains about as good as the explicit term. also relevant
    00406 pooled-mixture-density-network-without-embedding-and-with-short-history Most of the gain that one pooled mixture density network shows over one network per Primary (experiment 403) comes from sharing parameters, not from the learned Primary embedding, and the gain does not grow when each Primary's training history is cut to 3, 6, or 12 months. not supported not-for-production One neural network shared across all 16 substations only beats separate networks if it knows which substation it is forecasting: without a substation input it is 0.57 points of capacity worse than separate networks at the six signed-direction substations, and with a learned substation embedding it is 0.22 better. The advantage of sharing grows when one substation has little history: with 3 months of its history the shared network is 1.15 points better, because the separate network loses 1.77 points and the shared one only 0.83. All five test months are interim. primary
    00405 mdn-explicit-pv-term-learned-capacity-known-answer A pooled mixture density network that forecasts net demand as a demand mixture minus a physical solar-power term, with the term's capacity learned per Primary and season-year under a prior, scores a lower net-demand CRPS at the six signed-direction Primaries than the same network given the irradiance as an ordinary input. A small adapter that modulates the physical solar forecast by weather scenario lowers the score further. inconclusive not-for-production We could not test whether a solar term with a learned capacity improves the forecast, because the known-answer test that gates it could not be passed. On the 2026-06 fold, with 3 seeds and 6 Primaries, adding a real solar farm's output made the network's capacity rise by about the right share, and it rose by 60 to 91% as much when the farm's days were shuffled. A fresh audit found that the shuffled series keeps most of the real series' daylight profile, so the test as built cannot separate a read of the sky from a read of the profile. The next experiment needs a test calibrated to the network. also relevant
    00403 pooled-versus-per-primary-mixture-density-network One network trained across all 16 Primaries scores lower CRPS than one network per Primary, with the same inputs, rows, folds, and seeds, and the gain is largest at the Primaries with the shortest history. supported not-for-production One neural network trained across all 16 Primaries forecasts demand better than 16 separate networks: at the six signed-direction substations the error score falls by 0.185 points of capacity (about 5%), at the eight apparent-power-only substations by 0.161 points (about 4%), and 11 of 14 long-history substations gain. The gain does not grow where the history is shorter, and at the two short-history substations the result is mixed. These are five test months, so every interval is interim. primary
    00393 gam-mva-fold-data-analysis The GAM stack trails BG2 at the eight MVA-only Primaries (D2, D3, D4, D8, D12, D19, D21, D22) because apparent power (MVA) is a folded, non-linear function of the additive effects that a GAM learns for signed active power (MW), so the stack loses relative skill whenever demand is near zero. Stop-rule count for the idea "the MVA fold explains the GAM's gap": 3 of 3 already, because experiments 152, 372, and 374 each tested a fold treatment and none closed the gap. This experiment is a data analysis on stored forecasts, not a fourth fold treatment. not supported not-for-production The MVA fold does not explain why the GAM trails BG2 at the MVA-only Primaries. Folding the signed series at the six Primaries that report signed megawatts moves the gap by only -0.015 points pooled, because four of the six almost never come near zero; D5 and D10 do (4.6% of half-hours each), yet only D5 moves (-0.092). The gap is larger when demand is low at D12 and D22, but D8, the biggest gap, comes from mid-to-high demand, so a next test could target low-demand half-hours at D12 and D22 and look separately at D8. also relevant
    00386 bsp-gsp-demand-v1-top-down-bg2-gam-simple-xgboost At the four bulk and grid supply points that NGED forecasts, fitted and scored top-down on the aggregate series, the best Primary-demand GAM stack is not worse than BG2 (the weekly-refitted XGBoost model alone, b_weekly) by more than 0.20 points of effective capacity at days 1 to 10, separately for the two bulk supply points (BSPs: D13, D17) and the two grid supply points (GSPs: D1, D6). refuted not-for-production At the two bulk supply points and two grid supply points, the GAM stack fitted on each aggregate reading does much worse than BG2 (the weekly XGBoost model), by 1.0 points at the bulk supply points and 2.0 at the grid supply points, and scores about level with the manual heuristic and climatology. The gap is largest on day 1 and shrinks with lead, which is consistent with the stack lacking the recent-power input that BG2 has; this explanation is untested. Both forecasts have tail errors: BG2's 5% quantile covers 2.8% to 4.4% of readings (too wide), and the stack's covers 5.5% to 10.4% (too narrow, most clearly at D1). primary
    00223 dp-bsp-generators-as-children-and-double-count-test The metered generators that NGED lists under a bulk supply point (BSP) are children of that BSP as much as the Primaries are: the BSP's reading is the Primaries' demand plus the unmetered load minus the generators' output. If the Primaries' readings already include a generator's output, adding that generator to the sum double-counts it. This experiment first tests, from the readings alone, which generators the BSP's remainder carries and which a Primary already carries, and then asks how much the BSP's forecast improves when the generators the BSP carries are summed as children. inconclusive not-for-production The two bulk supply points' readings move with the metered generators that NGED lists under them, and the Primaries' readings show only small coefficients on the same generators. The size of the movement is not the clean one-for-one that adding the generator to the sum needs, so the experiment did not test whether adding the generators improves the bulk supply point forecast. A better test is to separate the unmetered solar and the battery's price-driven dispatch first. primary
    00204 dp-bsp-full-bottom-up-with-signed-mva-children A bulk supply point (BSP) is forecast better bottom-up than top-down when the sum covers every child Primary. Experiment 161 summed only the three children per BSP that report real power (MW) and have a long history, which reached 42% of D13's effective capacity and 56% of D17's, and the bottom-up forecast scored 0.5% to 0.6% worse than the top-down forecast. This experiment adds the children that report apparent power (MVA). A child's apparent-power reading is the magnitude of its flow, so a child with reverse flow at midday needs a signed flow before it can enter a sum. The fold-aware model of experiment 152 supplies that signed flow as a latent variable. inconclusive not-for-production Adding every child Primary to the sum did not improve the forecast of either bulk supply point: the bottom-up score matched the top-down score (+0.1% at D13, -0.15% at D17), and the intervals include zero. Converting the children that report apparent power to signed flow changed the score by at most 0.03 points, at the one bulk supply point where a child has reverse flow. Even with every child's reading known exactly the BSP's score gains only 12% to 24%, and the children's forecasts gave no detectable gain over the BSP's own chain, so the next gain may lie in forecasting the generators and the unmetered load beneath each BSP. primary
    00161 dp-bsp-top-down-against-bottom-up A bulk supply point (BSP) is forecast better bottom-up than top-down. The top-down forecast is the best-guess GAM-K chain (experiment 155's arm D) applied to the BSP's reading as a stand-alone series. The bottom-up forecast adds the arm D forecasts of the BSP's child Primary substations that report real power, each times a weight learned from earlier readings, to arm D's forecast of the remainder (the BSP's reading minus those children's weighted readings). The mechanism is that the child Primaries' own forecasts are better than the BSP's chain can infer from the BSP's sum, because each child has its own heating, solar, and calendar response and its own recent errors, and the sum is not exact (losses, unmetered load, children that report apparent power), so the remainder and the weights absorb the difference. inconclusive not-for-production Forecasting a BSP from its children's forecasts plus a remainder did not beat forecasting the BSP as a stand-alone series at either BSP: the bottom-up score was 0.6% worse at D13 and 0.5% worse at D17. The likely reason is that the children this test could use (three real-power Primaries per BSP) cover 42% and 56% of the BSP's capacity, and the BSP's error lives in the rest. The oracle control also missed its pre-registered threshold, which was set too high. The verdict is inconclusive because both intervals include zero. also relevant
    00158 dp-partly-pooled-gam-shapes-heating-and-calendar-curves A GAM-K forecast (the convex demand model with a Kalman filter on recent errors, experiment 111) fitted jointly across six Primary substations, so that each substation's heating curve, lighting curve, daily profile, holiday effects, and Christmas shape are a fleet mean plus a penalised deviation of its own, forecasts better than the same model fitted to each substation alone, now that the solar capacity is pinned and the filter handles recent switching. The mechanism is that a substation sees only two winters and two Christmases in a 24-month window, so its own curves are noisy, and five other substations carry information about the shapes that physically are shared. Capacity, level, and the band means stay per substation. The gain is largest for a substation with 3 months of history and on Christmas rows, and no pooled arm is worse than the per-site fit by more than 0.02 points on ordinary rows. partly supported candidate In this test on six Primary substations, fitting the demand model's shared curves (heating, lighting, daily profile, holidays) across all six let a substation with only 3 months of history score 3.01 points instead of 3.64 (lower is better), within 0.17 points of the same substation with 24 months of its own history. The gain was largest in winter and on Christmas rows. For a substation with 24 months of history, pooling changed the score by less than 0.02 points, which is too small to justify making each forecast depend on five other substations' data. The test cannot say whether pooling the heating curve alone would help a 3-month substation. also relevant
    00154 differentiable-physics-n0-gate-2-heating-error-against-truth When the partly pooled convex regression model of experiment 109 is fitted to only 4 winter weeks of one substation, its fitted heating curve is closer to the injected truth than the curves of the no-pooling and complete-pooling fits. The curve is as close to the truth as a fair oracle's curve (within 10%). The model recovers the sign of the substation's own heating offset relative to the fleet's curve. The fleet's shared curve is recovered as well as an average of per-site fits recovers it. partly supported not-for-production On synthetic substations with a known answer, a partly pooled demand model fitted to 4 winter weeks of one substation estimated that substation's heating response with a smaller error than fitting the substation alone (2.8 to 3.6 against 4.1 to 11.5 points of capacity) and than copying the fleet's curve (3.4 to 4.6), on every seed. It recovered the fleet's shared curve about as well as separate fits did. Neither the pooled fit nor the fair oracle recovered the sign of the substation's own heating offset reliably (the pooled fit got the sign right at 6 or more of 8 substations on only 2 of 5 seeds), so on these synthetic fleets and one January, a partly pooled model can share the heating curve but cannot learn a substation's own offset from 4 weeks. also relevant
    00150 differentiable-physics-n0-gate-rerun-fair-oracle-five-seeds When the reference estimator estimates every coefficient from the same 4 weeks of rows (so that no coefficient is fixed at its true value), the partially pooled convex model of experiment 109 recovers the injected truth about as well as that reference on most of 5 random seeds, and its tuning rule picks a deviation penalty within one grid step of the best one on most of the 5 seeds. inconclusive not-for-production The rerun of the synthetic known-answer test could not say whether the pooled model shrinks substation deviations correctly, because a control that keeps no deviation at all passed the shrinkage line on 2 of 5 seeds when 4 June weeks were held. Capacity from 4 weeks was within 25% of the truth at 8 of 8 synthetic substations on every seed, the tuning rule picked a penalty within one grid step of the best on 5 of 5 seeds, and the pooled fit's heating error was below a fair reference estimator's on 5 of 5 seeds. The full-history line passed on only 2 of 5 seeds. A post hoc rerun on 4 January weeks separated the controls, so a test that scores heating error against the injected truth in winter should settle the question. also relevant
    00124 differentiable-physics-h-bsp-closure-audit-and-bsp-hint A bulk supply point (BSP) reading, fitted jointly with the Primaries beneath it as the sum of their demand terms plus the metered generators plus a penalised remainder, with a tuned weight, limits the damage that a metering error at one Primary does to that Primary's forecast, without raising the Primaries' forecast error when no error is present. not supported not-for-production The bulk supply point reading halved the damage from injected scale errors, but it made forecasts on clean data worse at every weight above zero: by at least 0.385 points of capacity in the corrected re-run, about 17 times the 0.023-point limit (2.355 points or more in the first run). A synthetic system that closes exactly loses clean accuracy the same way, so the loss does not come mainly from a wrong multiplier. The cause is untested. As a detector of metering errors the reading is weak: a 25% scale error lasting 30 days is found in 0% of cases by a fit that includes it and in 50% by a fit trained only on earlier rows, which then false-alarms on 61% of later windows. also relevant
    00120 differentiable-physics-sh-convex-daily-shape-library A convex model in which each Primary's daily profile is a non-negative mix of K = 3 or 4 shared daily shapes per day type, plus a penalised deviation, forecasts Primary demand with 2 years of history at least 0.05 points better than experiment 109's shrinkage towards one fleet-mean profile. That needs the library to beat the per-site profile by about 0.02 points, because the fleet-mean model is 0.031 worse than it; the plan predicts a tie or a small gain, so supported is a long shot. The stability of the shapes and the shape mix of each substation are descriptive. inconclusive not-for-production In a test with synthetic substations, 5 Primaries were too few for a library of 3 shared daily shapes to recover the shapes we planted, so the check failed on 7 of 10 lines and the verdict is inconclusive. In the scored year the library scored the same as the fleet-mean model and 0.030 points worse than a profile per substation (0.068 worse in winter), and its forecast reproduced a tenth to a half of the Economy 7 night step on cold winter nights. also relevant
    00117 differentiable-physics-h2-graph-prior-on-pooled-convex-parameters A penalty on the differences between interconnected Primaries' parameters (heating slopes, PV capacity as a share of effective capacity, the profile's scale, and the holiday coefficients) narrows those parameters' month-block bootstrap intervals without breaking their coverage when neighbouring Primaries are alike, and worsens the full-history quantile score by at most 0.03 points. refuted not-for-production At the weight tested, tying the parameters of interconnected substations together did not improve the forecast: in the scored year it worsened the quantile score by 0.020 points on all days (the interval spans zero) and by 0.173 points in winter evening peaks (the replicate year gave -0.021). On synthetic substations with a known truth it narrowed only heating-slope intervals, and the 80% intervals of PV capacity held the truth 17% of the time against 78% without the tie. A tie on heating slopes alone worsened the real score by 0.006 and 0.001 points, an exploratory lead and not a result. also relevant
    00109 differentiable-physics-n0-partially-pooled-convex-model A convex model whose per-substation coefficients are a fleet mean plus a ridge-penalised per-substation deviation forecasts Primary demand as well as the per-substation convex model when both have 2 years of history, and clearly better when the substation has 4 weeks or 3 months. inconclusive not-for-production The known-answer test of this method failed under its pre-registered definition, and only one of five runs (each on a new seed, runs 2 to 5 being post hoc redefinitions) passes every line, so the verdict is inconclusive. Taking the numbers alone, for the five MW Primaries tested, one convex model shared across substations forecast a new substation with only 4 weeks of history 3.3 points better than a convex model fitted to that substation alone (and 0.8 points better with 3 months), far more than the 0.5 to 2.0 predicted, and scored 0.03 points worse on substations with 2 years of history. On the six held-out Primaries it came within 0.2 points of experiment 105's neural pooled model with 4 weeks (the neural model 0.13 better) and 0.12 better with 3 months, without that model's loss on long histories, which suggests that the loss in experiment 105 came largely from the neural fit. Complete pooling scored 0.07 points worse than partial pooling at 4 weeks in the scored year (interval touching zero) and no differently in the replicate year. The gains should be relied on only after a rerun of the gate. also relevant
