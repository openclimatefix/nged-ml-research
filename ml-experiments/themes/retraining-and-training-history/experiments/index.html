    Retraining and training history: experiments

    This page lists every experiment tried in the theme, newest first. An experiment that belongs mainly to another theme but bears on this one is marked "also relevant".

    Experiment Hypothesis Verdict Production status Take-home message Relevance
    00577 static-between-steps-offsets-and-probabilistic-offset-widening The learnt offsets of D5 (experiment 565) wander from month to month, although a substation's true offset should stay constant for months and then step. A penalty that allows only sparse steps (a total-variation penalty on the first differences, or gated jumps) therefore gives a steadier offset without costing accuracy. Separately, the offset is the least certain part of the forecast, so adding its uncertainty to each mixture component's variance, and a small heavy-tailed component for a step that happened since the refit, improves the tails and the calibration beyond what a constant widening does. refuted not-for-production The slow offset (D5) is already as good as these refinements can make it on this data. Making the offset steadier or wider does not help the forecast, so keep D5 as it is; the penalty and widening ideas are not worth carrying into the next model release. primary
    00575 stepwise-offset-large-step-and-mirrored-pair-recovery Experiment 573 injected steps of 2% to 6% of capacity, which may drown in the weather-error noise of the forecast's daily residuals. Steps as large as the labelled load-shifts (a median of 19% of capacity) are found by the daily-knot offset (S1) or the gated-jump offset (S2) when they last a week or more. not supported not-for-production A free per-day offset on top of the frozen demand network cannot find load-shift-sized steps. Even a step of 20% of capacity that lasts a month is only partly followed (median 49% of its size at the better penalty, 9% at the heavier one), and the labelled load-shifts mostly last under 4 days. Do not build a detector on this offset; a detector needs the step to be an explicit part of the model, such as a labelled-event input or a state estimate, rather than a by-product of an offset. primary
    00574 part-d-primaries-only-modular-recipe-lag-encoder-offset-and-add-one-arms Four add-ons to the modular lag-encoder neural network with the D5 offset (P0) each lower CRPS: the multi-variable ensemble spread columns in the mixer (P1), thermal-lag and solar-gain columns through the weather encoder (P2), an expected signal for the lag encoder taken from encoders trained on ERA5 and CAMS only (P3), and a three-step schedule with a 3-day embargo (P4). The factors that meet the keep rule, plus P2 by the maintainer's policy, form a final candidate (P5) that stays within 0.04 of A-pool-24 at 24-120 h at the eight MVA Primaries. partly supported candidate The best demand recipe we have fitted (P5) combines experiment 572's lag-encoder neural network and offset with a fresh mixer fitted on the whole ensemble history up to the cutoff, a 3-day embargo, and the thermal-lag and solar-gain columns. At the six MW Primaries it improves on P0, the best earlier recipe, by 0.22 points of capacity at 1 to 5 days (interval excludes zero), and at the eight MVA Primaries by a smaller 0.09 (interval includes zero). P5 beats the daily-refit references at every lead band, and follows an injected 7-day shift of the history less than half as much (2.9 against 7.3 points). The multi-variable ensemble spread columns do not help the network and should stay out. also relevant
    00573 stepwise-output-offset-known-answer-step-recovery D5 adds one offset per Primary and month, with a slope penalty, so a step in a Primary's level (a new estate, a persistent transfer, a metering change) is smeared over months and a short step cannot be seen. An offset that is constant between days and changes abruptly (daily knots held constant between knots, with an L1 total-variation penalty, or a sparse set of jumps chosen by hard-concrete gates) recovers the date and size of an injected step. not supported not-for-production At the penalties we tried, an offset that is allowed to jump did not find steps of 2% to 6% of capacity in the demand forecast's residuals. The weights that stop apparent false steps also stop the real ones. The next step is to test whether a second signal, such as a neighbour series or a label, separates a step from slow drift, before relying on a free offset to date level changes. primary
    00572 two-offset-part-1-lags-versus-the-slow-output-offset A slow per-Primary output offset (D5) carries the Primary's current level into the forecast, as lagged power does. If so, a lag-free modular network with an offset learnt in pre-training (E2) scores about as well as the best lag network (v3) at 24 to 240 hours, and adding the offset to the lag network (v3+D5) gains little. Lags should still matter in the first 24 hours. partly supported candidate A slow output offset does not make lags unnecessary: at the eight MVA Primaries the best forecast uses both, and the lag network with the offset beat the offset alone by 0.224 points of capacity at 1 to 5 days. At the six MW Primaries the offset alone and the lag network are not distinguishable. The stop rule of the brief (the gain at both groups) is not met, so continuing on the lag network with the offset rests on the eight MVA Primaries alone. also relevant
    00571 drift-offset-and-embedding-hybrid-known-answer-worlds A slow per-Primary output offset (D5 of experiment 565) absorbs level shifts, and a drift embedding that modulates the encoders (A10) absorbs changes in behaviour, such as a growing temperature sensitivity or a changed daily shape. Experiment 565's joint fit of both (the hybrid) was worse than the offset alone, but a joint fit lets the two components compete for the same slow variance, so that failure does not refute the hypothesis. Building the hybrid so the components cannot compete (staged, multiplicative-only, or orthogonal) should recover both kinds of injected change in worlds where the answer is known. not supported not-for-production When we inject level steps (and a growing temperature sensitivity, which the steps dominate) into real substation histories, the monthly drift embedding recovers nearly all of the oracle's gain and the output offset alone recovers under a third, so the embedding is not limited to changes in behaviour and the offset is not the better carrier of levels. The joint hybrid that did badly on the real data did not do badly in this world, so competition is not shown to be the cause. The growing-sensitivity part of the question stays open until a readable sensitivity-only world is built. primary
    00569 modular-network-inputs-through-matching-encoders-in-pretraining-and-fine-tuning-run-1 Experiment 562 added five input groups to the modular lag-free network at fine-tuning only. Each group may help once it is also pre-trained on ERA5 and CAMS rows and enters through the encoder that matches its content: thermal-lag and solar-gain windows (B1) and look-ahead windows (B2) through the weather encoder, school terms and holidays (B3a) through the datetime encoder, national signals of the previous day (B3b) and the whole-day price shape (B4) through a small parallel encoder. A sixth group (B3c) adds, to B3a, the number of schools within 2 km of each Primary as a gate on the school columns. not supported not-for-production Letting the demand network read thermal-lag, solar-gain, look-ahead, school-term, national-signal, and price-shape columns during pre-training as well as fine-tuning, through the encoder that matches each group, did not help: none of them beat a shuffled-day placebo of the same width on all rows, cold nights, Christmas week, or high-spread days, and a school-proximity input made the eight MVA Primaries worse. This test cannot see small gains (one seed), and the network pre-trained from scratch with extra columns scored worse than plain M0's stored weights, which is the larger effect to understand first. The next step is a second seed and a plain M0 re-pre-trained under the same recipe, so that the control and the arms differ only in the columns. also relevant
    00568 attention-mixer-modular-network-with-lead-and-spread-token The modular network M0 of experiment 545 concatenates its encoders' outputs and feeds them to a multi-layer-perceptron (MLP) mixer. A self-attention mixer that reads each encoder's output as a separate token, plus a token built from the lead time and the ensemble spread of several weather variables, extracts more from the same encoders, and does so without raising the injected-shift or real-event leak. not supported not-for-production A self-attention mixer over the encoder outputs does not forecast Primary demand better than the simpler MLP mixer, and at the eight MVA Primaries it is worse. The attention mixer's small gain at the six MW Primaries also appears when the ensemble spread is shuffled between days, so it does not come from the spread. A next experiment could look for what the relative-spread block lets the networks do with the recent shift, not for a better mixer. primary
    00567 neighbour-lag-encoders-synthetic-load-shifts-in-pretraining-and-fine-tuning A load transfer lowers the donor Primary's lags and raises a neighbour's lags by the same amount, while a genuine level change shows at one Primary only. A network that reads its neighbours' lags as well as its own can check whether the sum is conserved, so it can discount a transferred lag and still follow a genuine level change. Training it on synthetic load transfers, in both pretraining and fine-tuning, lowers its CRPS inside real labelled load-shift events whose recipient is another Primary, at no cost outside events. not supported not-for-production Adding the neighbours' lags to the lag-encoder demand neural network raised CRPS across all rows, and synthetic load-shift training did not give a shown gain at the real events that name a recipient. The neural network does not use the neighbours' lag values, so the next attempt needs a way to force their use, or a different signal. also relevant
    00566 combination-block-lag-encoder-with-three-step-schedule-and-embargo-real-event-leak The lag encoder of experiment 560 (variant 3, capped 24-hour lags, block dropout) lowers 24-120 h CRPS at the eight but leaks an injected load shift more than L-1d does. Training the mixer once on the whole history (the three-step schedule, experiment 559's A1), leaving the newest 3 days out of each fine-tune (the embargo, experiment 556), or both, lowers the leak measured on real labelled events and keeps most of the lag encoder's accuracy gain. partly supported not-for-production A larger refit leak for the lag encoder than for the lag-free network is not shown on real labelled load-shift events, and the three-step schedule and the 3-day embargo cut the leak by a third to a half with no accuracy cost shown. That is the part of the leak that comes from refitting. The larger part, 0.12 to 0.13 of an event's depth, comes from the network reading corrupted lags at forecast time, and neither protection touches it. also relevant
    00565 drift-embedding-variants-six-designs Substations change slowly over years, and the Primary embedding is static, so the modular lag-free demand network follows slow change only through its daily fine-tune. A designed slow signal can carry that change. Stage 1 asks the cheapest version: an exponentially weighted mean of the network's own past residuals, older than 24 hours, added to the forecast quantiles. partly supported candidate A slow, per-Primary offset added to the demand forecast improves it at the eight MVA Primaries, and in the injected-shift fits we ran it leaked no more of a recent load shift than the plain network. The other four ways of carrying slow change showed no gain, so the next step is the offset alone, on more random seeds and at the six MW Primaries, where the direction holds but the interval still includes zero. primary
    00564 pooled-primary-bsp-gsp-m2-reduced Adding the 2 bulk supply points (BSPs) and 2 grid supply points (GSPs) to the training rows of the modular lag-free network M0 (experiment 545, replicated in 554), with a one-hot input saying whether a series is a Primary, a BSP, or a GSP, lowers the Primaries' CRPS at leads of 24 to 120 h, because the weather and calendar encoders see more examples of how weather and calendar drive load. not supported not-for-production Adding two bulk and two grid supply points to the training rows of the demand network did not make its forecasts at the 16 Primaries better: the change is smaller than the experiment could resolve, and its sign differs between the two groups of Primaries. The same network forecasts the four extra series better than a climatology, so pooling is a way to cover them with one network, not a way to improve the Primaries. A test of whether pooling helps needs many more extra series than NGED's data holds. also relevant
    00562 modular-lag-free-network-null-input-retry-with-placebos The modular lag-free network of experiment 545 (arm M0) has a weather encoder and a datetime encoder, so it may extract signal from four groups of inputs that earlier networks found null or harmful: thermal-lag and solar-gain windows (B1), look-ahead weather windows (B2), school-term and national-signal columns (B3, one arm for each), and a whole-day price shape that is present only while training (B4). Each real arm is compared with a shuffled-day placebo of the same number of columns, so a gain counts only if it exceeds what extra columns give regardless of content. not supported not-for-production Giving the modular lag-free demand network extra inputs that earlier networks found useless did not help over a whole year: no group beat a shuffled-day control of the same width by the threshold. The exception is the thermal-lag and solar-gain group (B1), which lowered the error on cold winter nights at the six signed-MW substations by about 0.2 points of capacity (3 months only, an effect the data can barely resolve). On all rows at the eight it made no difference against plain M0. The next step is a larger cold-night test of that group on the best lagged network. also relevant
    00560 modular-lag-encoder-capped-lags-and-block-dropout A lag encoder trained in ERA5/CAMS pretraining, next to the weather and datetime encoders of experiment 545's M0, lets the modular network use power lags that are at least 24 hours old. Feeding the encoder what the weather and datetime encoders say about each lag's own time ("expected") lets it read the lag as a deviation from normal, which a lag alone does not give. partly supported not-for-production Adding a lag encoder to the modular demand network let it use power readings at least 24 hours old, and it then forecast the 1 to 5 day horizon at the eight MVA-only Primaries better than the network with no lags (0.07 to 0.15 points of capacity), and better than the 24-hour-capped reference networks of experiment 558. The best version also reads the weather and calendar at the time of each lag, and its gain over lags alone is not shown at the eight and absent at the six. Every version that reads lagged power leaks an injected load shift more than L-1d does (6.1 to 8.1 against 4.49 points of capacity), so whether to adopt a lag version depends on cutting that leak. also relevant
    00559 m0-leak-ladder-three-step-schedule-frozen-encoders-and-penalty M0's injected-shift leak (7.3 to 7.7 points of capacity, against 4.49 for the single network L-1d) comes from the fresh mixer that is trained only on the recent days at every daily refit. Training that mixer once on the whole ensemble history (step 2) and then fine-tuning gently (step 3) lowers the leak while keeping most of M0's gain at 24 to 120 h. partly supported not-for-production The changes that make the lag-free network react less to a recent shift in demand also make its forecasts worse at the eight MVA-only Primaries, so none of them is ready for production. The simplest, a one-off training of the mixer on the whole history followed by daily fine-tuning, loses about a fifth as much gain as the strongest penalty. Adding trailing temperature or a per-Primary drift term did not lower the leak, and wind lowered it no more than its placebo did. primary
    00556 lag-free-finetune-absorption-of-load-shift-events-and-unlearn-time When a load-shift event lies in the rows the lag-free demand neural network is fine-tuned on, the network absorbs part of the event into its median forecast, and the absorbed share grows with the event length. A rule inside the scheduled fine-tune (an embargo on the newest 3 days of targets, or weights clipped by a label-free residual rule) cuts the absorbed share without slowing adaptation to a genuine lasting level change or worsening the score. partly supported not-for-production A short load-shift event in the data the lag-free neural network is refitted on moves the next week's forecast by a small share of the event (about one in ten for a 3-day step of 10% of capacity at all Primaries together, and 1.5% of the depth on average for real labelled events, though one of 74 absorbed 27.6%), and the effect fades over about 2 weeks. Leaving the newest 3 days out of each refit removes the effect for short events but costs 0.057 points of CRPS and delays adaptation to a genuine change by about 3 days, so the maintainer decides whether the protection is worth the cost. Down-weighting rows that look like outliers cut the leak by only 14%. primary
    00554 m0-replication-five-seeds-leak-mitigations-and-leave-d8-out The modular network M0 (a weather encoder, a datetime encoder, and a fresh mixer at fine-tuning) keeps most of its gain over the single network L-1d when both are averaged over 5 seeds instead of 2, and the gain does not depend on D8 alone. One of M0's two changes (the separate encoders, or the fresh mixer) raises its injected-shift leak, and a longer recency half-life or a 3-day refit cadence brings the leak down to the production limit. partly supported not-for-production The modular demand network that reads weather and calendar through separate encoders still beats the single network at 1 to 5 days ahead at the eight MVA-only Primaries when both are averaged over 5 seeds (0.18 points of capacity), and most of that remains with the slower learning rate. About half of the gain comes from one Primary, D8, and the other seven gain by about 0.09 with an interval that includes zero, so the gain is real but narrower than the headline. The experiments that would find which part of the network raises the injected-shift leak were not run. primary
    00545 modular-lag-free-demand-network-weather-and-datetime-encoders A lag-free demand network built from a weather encoder, a datetime encoder, and a mixer, where the mixer is replaced at fine-tuning by one that also reads ensemble spread and lead time, forecasts as well as the single multilayer network L-1d does (M0 is the modular network). Adding an auxiliary forecast head after each encoder (M1) forces each encoder to carry information about the power distribution on its own, and so improves the forecast at 24 to 120 hours. partly supported not-for-production Giving the lag-free demand network separate weather and datetime encoders improved the 1 to 5 day forecast at the eight MVA-only Primaries by about 0.14 to 0.21 points of capacity, and most of the gain remained when the new mixer learned no faster than the old network. The network also has a higher point estimate of the leak of an injected load shift (7.6 against 4.49 points of capacity, with overlapping fold ranges), and more than half of the gain at the eight comes from one Primary, so the next step is to find out which of the two changes makes the network follow recent data harder and whether the gain holds without D8. The auxiliary heads did not help. primary
    00543 lag-free-network-auxiliary-recent-level-and-reverse-flow-heads The lag-free network trails the lagged network (A-pool) because it never sees recent power. A second output that predicts the recent power level (the newest observed value and the day-ago and week-ago values, used as training targets only and never as inputs) makes the shared hidden layers carry level information, so the lag-free network closes part of the gap without any lag input at forecast time. refuted not-for-production Training the lag-free network to also predict the recent power level did not give it the benefit of lag inputs: its forecast got slightly worse (0.05 points of CRPS at the eight), and the gap to the lagged network stayed at 0.27 points. The recent level has to enter as an input, so the next step is to choose which lagged inputs to feed (the day-ago and week-ago lags closed 76% to 80% of the gap in experiment 539), not to try to learn them as a side task. also relevant
    00524 two-stage-lagless-forecaster-stage-1-and-ensemble-calibrator A lag-free demand network can be split in two. Stage 1 maps ERA5 and CAMS weather, calendar, solar geometry, and the Primary embedding to a three-component Gaussian mixture of power. It has no lead input and no ensemble-spread input. Stage 2 is a small network that takes stage 1's mixture, the lead, the ensemble spread, and the half-hour of day, and returns a calibrated mixture. The split is a simpler and more explainable forecaster: one part says what the weather does to demand, the other says how much to trust the ensemble weather at each lead. The claim is that the split loses no CRPS against the single-stage network L-Ww. partly supported candidate The lag-free demand network does not need lead time or ensemble spread as inputs to its main weather-to-power network: removing both and adding a small calibrator that sees them scored the same as the current network (within noise, if anything 0.03 to 0.10 CRPS points better at the eight MVA-only substations), as long as the main network keeps being fine-tuned on recent ensemble forecasts. Freezing that network and letting only the calibrator adapt made the forecast 0.06 to 0.15 points worse, so the recent-data refit cannot be dropped. primary
    00523 lead-awareness-of-the-lag-free-network-widening-and-pretraining-by-lead The maintainer's hypothesis is that pretraining on ERA5 (where the weather is the truth at every lead) never teaches the lag-free network that a longer lead means a larger weather error, so the network does not widen its forecast with lead. Experiment 521 already found that L-1d (the daily-refit lag-free network) and K2-S1 (the network with lagged and lag-free heads) widen by about the same amount from 12 to 24 h to days 6 to 10, so this experiment asks the narrower question of whether the stored bands are the right width at each lead, and whether the network's output changes when its lead input changes. partly supported not-for-production The lag-free neural network does not make its forecast bands wider at longer lead times, but neither does K2-S1, and re-fitting the band width per lead time recovers almost none of K2-S1's advantage at days 5 to 10. ERA5 pretraining does not explain the gap either: it lowers the error at every lead time. The next question is why the more complex networks score better at days 5 to 10; the centre of the forecast, not the band width, is the suspect. also relevant
    00521 lag-advantage-by-lead-time-k2-s1-vs-daily-lag-free Experiment 511 left the daily-refit lag-free network L-1d 0.091 points of CRPS behind K2-S1 at the eight MVA-only Primaries and 0.071 at the six signed-MW Primaries. The gap is not a lag advantage and so not concentrated at the shortest leads. L-1d beats K2-S1 below about 2 days, and the whole pooled gap sits at days 5 to 10 (stored finding, interim and unaudited, from a first look at experiment 511's row scores). The open question is whether the long-lead gap comes from the four-head mixture width of K2-S1 or from the monthly refit age of K2-S1's lag-free heads. partly supported not-for-production K2-S1, the neural network that reads lagged power, beats the daily-refit lag-free neural network mainly at leads of 5 to 10 days. Below 2 days the point estimates favour the daily lag-free network, so a short-horizon correction on a lag-free forecast is not what the gap calls for. At the eight MVA-only Primaries the long-lead gap is consistent with head count, and at the six signed-MW Primaries it is not. also relevant
    00519 ens-share-in-lagless-network-pretraining-ratio-sweep Experiment 511's mixed pretraining (L-mix: half of every batch from ENS rows, half from ERA5 and CAMS rows) scored worse than ERA5-and-CAMS pretraining alone, although ENS rows are what the network sees when serving. A smaller ENS share, or no source flag, gives a pretraining at least as good as ERA5-and-CAMS alone; the L-mix deficit comes from the 50% share, not from the flag. The sweep cannot separate the share from the double use of the fine-tune rows, because every ENS pretraining row also lies in the fine-tune window in every arm. not supported not-for-production Showing the lag-free neural network ECMWF ENS rows during pretraining made its forecast worse at the eight MVA-only Primaries (0.06 to 0.22 points of CRPS), and the loss grew steeply from a 50% ENS share upwards. ERA5 and CAMS pretraining alone therefore stays the choice for the mean score. The ENS rows did lower the loss at the 99th percentile by up to 0.03 points, but only with the source flag set at fine-tuning, so a tail-focused variant needs its own test. Five folds and two seeds leave seed-to-seed noise of 0.06 to 0.08 points, as large as the 10% and 25% differences. primary
    00515 lagless-demand-network-trailing-weather-window-features A demand network that reads no lagged power sees only the weather at the valid time, but a building warms and cools slowly, heating is switched on late, and sunshine through windows offsets heating on a cold day. Trailing means of temperature and shortwave over the previous 6 to 72 hours, or the 24-hour product of the two, therefore let the lag-free network forecast cold winter nights and cold sunny days better. Earlier neural tests of trailing weather (experiments 419, 437, 441, 442) all fed the network three origin lags of power, which may have carried the same information, so their nulls may not transfer. inconclusive not-for-production Trailing temperature windows lowered cold-night CRPS at the eight MVA-only Primaries by 0.150 points in 10 of 10 folds, but a shuffled-day placebo lowered it by 0.071, and nothing resolved at the six signed-MW Primaries or on all rows, so the effect is not resolved. also relevant
    00514 load-shift-probability-detector-and-calibration Four signals computed from the scaled residual series (a step at each end of a pulse, a return to the original level, edges at the same times in other series, and the symmetry of the sizes) can be combined by a logistic regression or a depth-limited tree into a probability that is calibrated on injected load-shift pulses, including held-out pulse shapes, seeds, and months, and that stays low on genuine level changes, on the D8 engine dips, and on rows with no injected event. partly supported not-for-production A probability that a half-hour sits inside a load-shift event can be calibrated, but the four signals are too weak to make it sharp: it finds about a quarter of injected pulses as large as the residual's spread and lasting 6 hours or more (about half of the largest, 3 times the spread), and on real rows it leaves nearly every weight at 0.9 or above. A soft-weight fine-tune on these tables would therefore be close to a repeat of the baseline; a sharper detector (or a different use of the same signals) is the thing to try before spending GPU time. also relevant
    00512 lagless-weekly-finetune-december-2024-sensitivity The gains that experiment 510 found for the lag-free demand neural network in the months of 2025-10 to 2026-06, refitting weekly instead of monthly and weighting recent rows with a 90-day half-life, also appear in December 2024, where the network has only 7 months of ensemble-forecast (ENS) history to fine-tune on. partly supported candidate A second Christmas confirms the direction of experiment 510's weekly-refit gain at the six signed-MW Primaries (0.70 points in 2024, 0.28 in 2025) but not at the eight MVA-only Primaries (0.02 and 0.31), and the 2024 result came with only 7 months of ensemble history.
