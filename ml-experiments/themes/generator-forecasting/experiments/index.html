    Generator forecasting: experiments

    This page lists every experiment tried in the theme, newest first. An experiment that belongs mainly to another theme but bears on this one is marked "also relevant".

    Experiment Hypothesis Verdict Production status Take-home message Relevance
    00433 solar-idle-noise-data-look H0: the six metered solar farms read exact zeros, or smooth small values, when they are not generating, so the wind farms' idle-noise texture (exact zeros and repeats, quantised values, and a floor above zero that does not follow the season) is absent and no detector or cleaned-zero score is needed for solar. supported not-for-production The metered solar farms read exact zeros at night, unlike the wind farms' noisy floor, so solar forecasts need no idle-noise detector and no cleaned-zero scoring. One farm (Solar B) shows small negative night readings on 8% of night half-hours, and two farms (Solar D and Solar E) have idle runs of 17 to 24 days on clear days, which is an outage question, not a noise question. also relevant
    00432 wind-cleaned-zero-rescore The maintainer wants a wind forecast to say zero where the farm should output zero, and an evaluation that never asks the forecast to predict the idle noise. If the half-hours that the experiment 210 mask flags as idle are scored against a target of 0 (the cleaned-zero row set), then a forecast that puts its lower quantiles at zero below the turbines' cut-in speed is no longer penalised for the farms' idle floor, so experiment 336's zeroing arm x1, which lost on the raw readings, wins or ties on the cleaned-zero row set. partly supported not-for-production Scoring idle half-hours as zero output does not change which wind forecast is best, and forcing the forecast to zero below cut-in gains nothing: that arm ties the default (-0.002 points, interval -0.008 to +0.004), because the default already forecasts near zero there. The mixture-head arms keep at most a 0.02-point lead on cleaned zeros (none if every ERA5-flagged half-hour is zeroed), against 0.04 on raw rows and 0.23 on masked rows, because those heads rarely forecast zero when the farm is idle. Cleaned zeros can be a headline row set for experiment 429 beside the raw set, but not in place of it. primary
    00431 wind-idle-data-look H1: the idle floor of each metered wind farm has a texture (exact zeros, repeats, a quantised step, small first differences) that genuine low output at the same power level does not have. H2: idle half-hours at high wind (a whole-farm outage or maintenance) follow a weekly cycle, because maintenance is done on weekday daytimes and weather-driven idling has no weekly cycle. partly supported not-for-production A cheap rule that looks at how jumpy a wind farm's recent readings are can tell idle readings from genuine low output at the same power level at two of the three farms, so the texture detector stays in the design. Idle readings at high wind do not follow a weekday-daytime cycle, and the stored series show no idle run of 7 days or more; Farm C has runs of several days that low wind cannot explain, so the whole-farm outage class stays for Farm C at the 1.5-times-cut-in speed (not at the empirical cut-in) and is weak at Farm A and Farm B. Farm A cannot be tested for texture from its power alone. primary
    00430 unified-forecaster-solar-and-pretraining-ablations A pooled mixture-density network for the six metered solar farms needs, beyond experiment 345's solar-only pooled network (the demand-style default: one pass on the ensemble mean and spread, no physics, no pretraining, a clipped Gaussian mixture), only those of four switches that an ablation shows to help: the 51 ensemble members passed through a shared encoder one by one (A2), a PyTorch fleet plant that adds a power channel (A5), ERA5 and CAMS pretraining followed by fine-tuning on the ensemble (A6), and point masses at zero and at capacity in the output (A7). refuted not-for-production One pass of a neural network on the ensemble mean and spread is enough for the six solar farms: none of the four richer designs we tested beats it by the 1% margin. For the 51 coherent member paths that NGED's power-flow modelling needs, reorder the one-pass network's 51 quantiles with a Schaake shuffle, which scores 13% better than ensemble copula coupling on the variogram score of the paths and the same on the energy score. Passing each member through the network alone gives paths that score 11% worse on the energy score and a marginal quantile score 1.5% worse. also relevant
    00429 unified-forecaster-wind-ablations A single pooled neural network can forecast the three metered wind farms as well as Wind farms research default 2 (experiment 347's y2: a frozen ERA5-trained power curve run on each of the 51 ensemble members, followed by a mixture-density head). Each difference between that design and the Primary-demand design (experiment 425's arm A: one pass on the ensemble mean and spread, no physics, an unbounded head) is kept only if an ablation shows it is needed. partly supported not-for-production Neural wind forecasts do not need the extra machinery. A plain pooled network fed the ensemble members ties Wind farms research default 2 (10.533 against 10.518 points), and neither passing members through the network (-0.008 against the one-pass design), a power-curve layer (+0.000), nor a censored head (+0.018, worse) changes the score by the 0.10-point margin. The weakness all arms share is the upper tail: 2.2% to 2.8% of observations exceed q99, because the forecasts under-predict windy half-hours, and censoring did not fix it. The plain network is the design that matches the default; the upper tail is the open problem, and the architecture comparison does not explain it. primary
    00357 wind-default-2-monthly-head-longer-retest Retraining Wind farms research default 2's mixture head on the first day of every month (experiment 356's arm u3) gains over default 2 at true days 1 to 10, because a recent head tracks the farms' drift. Experiment 356 measured -0.038 points on 53 Tuesday runs (interval -0.104 to +0.024) and -0.120 on about 16 Tuesday runs of 2026-07 onwards. partly supported candidate Retraining Wind farms research default 2's head every month improves the forecast slightly, by about 0.04 to 0.05 points over the scored year and about 0.10 points in the latest 2.5 months, and no scope shows harm to the score. The scored-year interval still touches zero, so the change is a candidate and is not adopted. The upper tail stays too heavy (1.7% to 2.0% of observations above the 0.99 quantile), so retraining does not fix it. primary
    00356 wind-default-2-blend-and-monthly-refit-retest Wind farms research default 2 (experiment 347's arm y2) gains 0.234 points over Wind farms research default 1 at true days 1 to 10 but puts 2.28% of observations above q99 over every lead (2.15% at true days 1 to 10). Either a per-quantile, per-lead-band blend of the two defaults (u2), experiment 348's arm z5 (u1), a monthly offset layer on default 2 (u4), or a monthly retrained head (u3) lowers the 0.99 pinball loss and the share above q99 without losing the gain. not supported not-for-production Blending Wind farms research default 2 with research default 1, or refitting it every month, does not fix its upper tail: the share of observations above the 0.99 quantile only falls from 2.15% to between 1.77% and 2.04%, against 1% intended, and no arm clearly beats default 2 over days 1 to 10. The blend's small gain (0.05 points) is the same with shuffled weights, so it is averaging, not learning. The top quantile needs new information, not a blend or a refit; the monthly retrained head is the one arm worth a longer retest. primary
    00348 wind-plant-mdn-upper-tail Experiment 347's best arm (y2: Stage 3 frozen physics plant, 51 ensemble members through it, a three-component Gaussian mixture head on the summary of the resulting power values) beats Wind farms research default 1 by 0.234 points over true days 1 to 10 but is over-confident in the upper tail (2.3% of observations above q99 over every lead, and a 0.99 pinball loss up to 0.084 worse in experiment 347's report). One of five tail fixes (z1 tail-weighted loss, z2 capped quantiles with a point mass at the plant's scale, z3 monotone increments above q90, z4 z1 plus z2, z5 the solar recipe of a plant residual band blended with the default's XGBoost quantiles) brings the share above q99 under 1.5% and the 0.99 pinball loss to within the tolerance, while keeping the headline gain. partly supported candidate The frozen physics plant with a mixture head stays about 0.23 points of capacity better than the research default over days 1 to 10, but none of the four tail fixes inside the head brings the top quantile into calibration without losing score at that quantile, and none of their intervals excludes zero. Blending the plant's residual band with the default's XGBoost quantiles gains less (0.15 points) but is the arm of the five whose gain is clear and whose 0.99 quantile is no worse, so that recipe, not a better head, is the next step. primary
    00347 wind-physics-hybrid-plant-then-mdn A physics plant model of each metered wind farm (a smooth cubic speed-to-power curve with cut-in, rated, and cut-out speeds, a direction-dependent loss, and priors that make the untrained model already sensible), fitted on ERA5 before 2024-04-01 and frozen, turns each of the 51 ensemble members into a power value. A three-component Gaussian mixture-density network (MDN) fed the summary of those 51 values, the lead, and the farm then scores within 0.15 points of Wind farms research default 1 on the kept rows at 1 to 10 days, without a neural adapter. A second plant fitted after masking the blocks where the farm fell short of the first plant (a healthy-farm plant) lets the head learn the shortfall. partly supported candidate A physics model of each wind farm, run on every ensemble member and corrected by a small neural network, forecasts 0.27 points of capacity (2.4%) better than the research default over leads of 0.5 hours to 15 days, and 0.23 points over true days 1 to 10, but the neural network given only the plant's median and the farm already gets 83% of the gain, and the upper tail is still over-confident. The next step is to fix the upper tail (the neural network caps its quantiles at capacity, and 0.9% of observations exceed it) before any plant-based arm is adopted. primary
    00346 wind-research-default-1-offset-table-refit-cadence Experiment 342 found that the default's offset table, fitted once on the 442 daily runs of 2024-04-01 to 2025-06-16, leaves the upper tail too low in the scored year (observed power above q99 in 1.8% of half-hours against 1% intended) although it fits the hindcast, and blamed drift between the hindcast years and the scored year. The ensemble table holds daily runs up to 2026-10-24, so the table can be refitted each month on runs that had already finished. A table refitted monthly on a trailing 6-month window (x2) scores lower than the table fitted once. partly supported candidate Refitting the wind forecast's offset table every month on recent runs makes the forecast a little better (a 0.4% to 1.1% lower quantile score), using only data the service already stores. It does not fix the upper quantiles, which still miss in 1.7% of half-hours against 1% intended. So the change between the training years and the scored year is a minor cause, and the upper-tail miss needs another explanation. A monthly job that refits the table on all earlier daily runs passes the adoption conditions and is a candidate. primary
    00345 pooled-solar-wind-mixture-density-network One mixture-density network (MDN) trained once for all leads on the six metered solar farms and the wind farms together, with a fuel-type indicator and weather inputs common to both fuels, scores as well on each fuel as that fuel's best single-family model, and scores better for a farm with no history. partly supported candidate One neural network trained on all six solar farms forecasts each farm as well as a separate network on the physical plant model (within 0.8%), so a production forecast could use one neural network per fuel. Adding wind farms to the same network does not help either fuel and makes solar 0.7% to 2.3% worse, so solar and wind are better trained separately. In this experiment, a wind farm with no history scored 18% worse at day 1 when trained on public wind farms alone than when trained on other metered wind farms. also relevant
    00344 wind-research-default-1-speed-dependent-offsets Experiment 340 found that a constant upward shift of the wind quantiles gains 0.09 to 0.12 points on the kept rows but worsens the rows below cut-in by 0.51 to 0.78, and that the default's median is too low at 8 to 15 m/s and too high below 5 m/s. An offset that depends on the ensemble-mean 100 m wind speed at forecast time lifts the quantiles where the power curve is steep and leaves them alone where the forecast is near zero, so it keeps the gain without the low-wind harm. The starting point is Wind farms research default 1, the three metered wind farms W1 to W3 are scored, and the lead range is 1 to 10 days. not supported not-for-production Letting the wind forecast's quantile offsets depend on the forecast wind speed gains only about 0.02 points of capacity when fitted on the same retrospective forecasts (hindcast runs) that fit the quantile offset table, and the gain cannot be told from zero. Fitted on the six months just before the scored year (July 2025 to June 2026), the same speed-dependent correction gains 0.146 points and does not worsen the half-hours where the forecast wind speed is below the turbines' cut-in speed. The speed-dependent bias is therefore real, and it changes from year to year. Refitting the correction on recent months is the untested option that could capture it. primary
    00343 wind-research-default-1-train-on-ensemble-mean Wind farms research default 1 trains XGBoost on ERA5 and runs it on each of the 51 ensemble members, then corrects with the offset table. The ensemble's 100 m wind speed at the grid cell is lower than at the turbines (a factor of 1.13 closed 78% of the gap to the ERA5 oracle in experiment 307). An XGBoost model trained on the 51-member mean of the ensemble's own history (arm M, 2024-04 to 2025-06, no calendar columns) would learn that bias and need less offset-table correction. Arm MS adds the member standard deviation of the 100 m and 10 m speeds. The three metered wind farms W1 to W3 are scored at 1 to 10 days. not supported not-for-production A wind model trained on the ensemble mean needs almost no offset table (the table changes its score by 0.017 points, against 0.345 for the default), but its forecast is no better than the default's: 0.08 points worse on the headline rows, an amount the data cannot distinguish from zero, and worse at low winds and in the 0.99 quantile. The default's recipe (ERA5 training plus the table) stays; mean training is a simpler recipe that scores about the same. primary
    00342 wind-research-default-1-upper-tail-offsets-and-head The upper quantiles of Wind farms research default 1 are too low: in experiment 324 the observed power exceeded q99 in 1.8% of half-hours (1% nominal) and q95 in 6.7% (5% nominal). Three corrections fitted on the hindcast can fix that without harming the headline score. x1 is a table of offsets for q90 to q99 that also depends on the ensemble-mean wind speed. x2 is a small boosted-tree head, fed the default's quantiles and summaries of the ensemble members' wind speed, that adds offsets to q90, q95, and q99. x3 lets the share of ensemble members above the farm's saturation speed set a probability mass at the farm's scale. not supported not-for-production The wind forecast's upper quantiles miss too often (observed power exceeds q99 in 1.8% of half-hours against 1% intended), and corrections fitted on past forecasts do not fix that: the offsets keyed by wind speed, the tail head, and the mass at the farm's cap all leave the share at 1.8% to 1.9% and change the upper-tail loss by about 1% or less. A head that sees the ensemble members does no better than one that sees only the default's quantiles, so in these 3 farms the members add no information about the upper tail. The remaining miss is consistent with the scored year differing from the hindcast years, which the next experiment could attack by refitting more often or by training on the most recent months. primary
    00341 wind-capacity-steps-from-strong-wind For the three metered wind farms (W1, W2, W3), a change-point search on the upper quantiles (99.9th percentile and maximum) of observed power in the half-hours when ERA5 wind is well above the speed at which the fitted power curve saturates finds a loss of 25% of capacity within 60 days at W1 and W2, with fewer false alarms than experiment 233's ratio monitor under a 9% seasonal swing. Steps come in whole turbines. Weighting lower-wind half-hours by the inverse of their speed-error variance speeds detection up. A neighbouring farm in the same ERA5 cell cancels the shared speed error. not supported not-for-production A change in a wind farm's usable capacity shows up in time only when it is large: a loss of half the capacity is found within 60 days at 91% to 93% of start dates at all three farms, and a loss of a quarter at 26% to 77%. Looking only at the strongest winds, as proposed, does not help, because strong wind fills 0.18% to 1.1% of half-hours and most 28-day blocks hold too few of them. The real records show repaired episodes (a 2-week outage at W1, a loss of 17% and of 31% at W2, a loss of about one of two turbines at W3 from October 2024 to January 2025) and no permanent step. primary
    00340 wind-research-default-1-constant-shift-then-low-wind-drop Experiment 336 found that dropping the ERA5 training rows below the turbines' cut-in speed (x2) gains 0.117 points on the kept rows, and its audit traced about 85% of the gain to a constant upward shift of the quantiles. If that is right, a constant shift fitted on the 442-run hindcast (arm S) reproduces most of the gain without changing the training rows. Arm S+X2, which also drops the low-wind rows, then adds little beyond the shift. Wind farms research default 1 is the starting point, the three metered wind farms W1 to W3 are scored, and the lead range is 1 to 10 days. not supported not-for-production A single upward shift of the wind quantiles, fitted on past forecasts, does nothing, because the forecast's offset table already fits those runs. Moving the quantiles up by 0.8 to 1.2 points of capacity does reproduce most of the gain of dropping low-wind training rows, but it also worsens the forecast at low winds by 0.5 to 0.8 points, the same harm that stops X2 being adopted. An untested option that could lift the forecast at 8 to 15 m/s and leave it alone below 5 m/s is a correction that depends on wind speed. primary
    00339 wind-research-default-1-adapter-plus-mixture-head A three-component Gaussian mixture head on the hindcast, fed the quantiles of the physics core plus adapter and the ensemble members' summaries of speed, direction, and temperature (experiment 324's W4), lowers the quantile score of Wind farms research default 1 on the kept rows of the three metered wind farms W1 to W3 at 1 to 10 day leads. The head loses on the idle half-hours because it is trained on kept rows only, and the maintainer's view is that a forecast-time rule (predict the idle floor where the ensemble-mean wind speed is below cut-in) needs no idle detector. Three arms are tested: x1 the head as in experiment 324, x2 x1 plus the rule, x3 the head trained on all hindcast half-hours. partly supported candidate A small neural-network head that re-draws the physics-plus-adapter wind forecast's quantiles gains 0.27 points of capacity (2.4%) on the farms' normal half-hours and keeps that gain on the replicate months, but the same head given no weather summaries does as well, so the gain is a recalibration that a simpler correction partly reproduces. A simple rule that predicts the idle floor at low wind recovers none of the head's loss on the idle-band readings, and training the head on those readings removes the gain. primary
    00338 wind-adapter-column-without-neural-network The gain of experiment 326's arm (c), the neural adapter's effective wind speed as an extra XGBoost column, can be kept without a neural network: a lookup table of speed scale by direction sector, speed ratio bin, and temperature bin (x1) keeps at least half of the gain, and a small monotone gradient-boosted tree (x2) keeps at least 80%. not supported not-for-production A lookup table of wind-speed corrections keeps almost none (5%) of the gain that the neural adapter gave the wind forecast, and boosted trees keep about half (0.051 of 0.099 points of capacity, scored at 1 to 10 day leads). Neither passes the adoption rule, so the neural adapter of experiment 326 is the only arm tested that does, and the maintainer decides whether it belongs in the production service. A non-neural column would need to reduce the physics-based wind-power model's error on ERA5 as much as the neural network does. Boosted trees with more inputs might approach that. primary
    00336 wind-research-default-1-below-cut-in-zeroing The maintainer proposes that, for wind, rows where the wind speed is below the turbines' cut-in speed carry only noise, so zeroing them lowers the quantile score of Wind farms research default 1 on the kept rows of the three metered wind farms W1 to W3 at 1 to 10 day leads. Three readings are tested, each added alone: x1 sets the forecast to zero power whenever the ensemble-mean 100 m speed is below the farm's cut-in speed, x2 drops the ERA5 training rows below cut-in from the XGBoost model and the offset table, and x3 sets the training target to zero on those rows. At most one is adopted, in the order x1, x2, x3. partly supported candidate Setting the wind forecast, or the training target, to zero below the turbines' cut-in speed does not improve the forecast, because the farms still produce small real output there. Leaving the low-wind rows out of training gains 0.12 points of capacity (about 1% of the default's score) on the kept rows, but mostly by lifting the quantiles upward, which a constant upward shift may reproduce (not yet tested). primary
    00326 wind-research-default-1-change-arms Each of four changes, added alone to Wind farms research default 1, lowers the quantile score on the kept rows of the three metered wind farms W1 to W3 at 1 to 10 day leads: (a) 2 m temperature and an air-density column as XGBoost inputs, (b) hour of day and day of year as XGBoost inputs, (c) the ERA5-trained neural adapter's effective wind speed as an extra XGBoost column, and (d) one wind-speed scale factor, fitted on the hindcast to the implied wind, applied to every member's 100 m speed before the XGBoost model. At most one is adopted, in the order c, b, a, d. supported candidate Feeding XGBoost the neural adapter's corrected wind speed lowers the wind forecast's score by 0.10 points of capacity, about 0.9% of the default's score, at 1 to 10 day leads (99.75% interval -0.19 to -0.01). The change is negative at every farm and at short and long leads. The three other changes were not separable from zero. Only a port that puts the adapter in the production service can use the gain, so the maintainer decides. primary
    00324 wind-farm-solar-cross-breeds-long-run The solar-farm findings (a mixture-density network (MDN) fed by a physical plant forecast beats the plant alone, lead features help it, and one pooled head across leads matches separate heads per lead band) carry over to the three metered wind farms W1 to W3, when the plant is the physics core with the experiment 285 wind-speed adapter and the weather is the 51-member ensemble. partly supported not-for-production A small neural network that corrects the quantiles of the wind farms' physics-based forecast lowers the quantile score by 0.27 points of capacity against XGBoost plus a lookup table of offsets, on the half-hours when the farm is not idle, at days 1 to 2 and at days 3 to 10. The gain disappears when the idle half-hours are included. The upper tail gets no better. The network needs the farm's own forecast history to learn from. primary
    00313 metered-farm-pure-xgboost-and-mdn-vs-plant A predictor built only from weather and calendar inputs, with no plant model anywhere in the pipeline (not even as an input column), scores close to the plant-based forecast in own history and falls behind it where the plant carries what missing history or a long lead cannot. partly supported candidate A forecast that sees only weather and the calendar, with no physical plant model anywhere, scores almost as well as the plant-based forecast for the six solar farms, up to 10 days ahead, even when the farm has no history. The plant's added value is small, between 0% and 3.4%, and its intervals include zero except for XGBoost. For wind, the same kind of network also matches the best plant-based forecast, so a plant model is not needed to be competitive on these farms. also relevant
    00307 wind-farm-power-as-wind-sensor The wind that the three metered wind farms' own power implies (the fitted physics core's power curve run backwards) is closer to a high-resolution analysis than to ERA5 at 9 to 14 m/s, and at least half of the ensemble's shortfall (observed power below the forecast's median on 34.1% of rows instead of 50%) is in the spread and timing of the members, not in their mean speed. not supported not-for-production The ensemble's low wind-power forecast comes mostly from wind speeds that are too slow, by about 1.1 m/s on average and more at longer leads. Multiplying every member's wind speed by a factor fitted on the 14 months before the scored year closed about 78% of the gap to the ERA5 oracle, while widening the members or shifting them in time closed almost none. The median is slightly better calibrated when the factor is fitted to the wind that the farms' power implies than when it is fitted to ERA5 on the same rows (1.7 percentage points), but the quantile score is not better. primary
    00303 wind-adapter-in-production-setting Experiment 285 found that, with ERA5 as the weather, a small neural adapter in front of the physics core of a wind farm scored better than XGBoost. On the 51 ensemble members in the scored year, an adapter fine-tuned on the ensemble hindcast beats XGBoost plus experiment 259's offset table (the benchmark that matters), because the adapter corrects the wind speed itself and the table corrects only the power. partly supported not-for-production A small neural correction of the wind speed, fitted to ERA5 and run on the 51 ensemble members, does about as well as XGBoost with a lookup table of offsets (0.09 points better, too little to justify porting). Fitting the correction on the one year of ensemble hindcasts made the forecast worse, probably because that year differs from the scored year; we did not test the cause. primary
    00288 pooled-head-on-public-wind-farms A neural head that adds 11 quantile offsets to the XGBoost wind forecast (R0) and was trained on dozens of public onshore wind farms (metered output from Elexon's Balancing Mechanism Reporting Service) lowers the quantile score of the three metered wind farms (W1 to W3) against R0 alone, and a head trained on the pool beats the same head trained on W1 to W3 only, because experiment 259's head had too few farm-years to learn a correction that transfers. A second hypothesis: XGBoost keeps improving as the training history grows from 12 to 48 months while the physics core is flat after 24 months, and a pool of public farms replaces the missing history. not supported not-for-production Training the correction on 40 public wind farms did not help the forecasts for the three NGED wind farms. Applied directly, it made them worse, because the public farms' capacity, turbine rating, and hub height let the head memorise each farm. Without those inputs it did no harm, and after a short fine-tune on the three farms' own data it gained about as much as a simple offset table fitted on those farms, so the public farms added nothing measurable. primary
    00285 wind-effective-wind-adapters A small bounded correction of the wind speed that the physics core of a wind farm reads, learned from the wind direction, the 10 m to 100 m speed ratio, the 2 m temperature, and the hour of day, closes at least half of XGBoost's lead over the core on the three metered wind farms (W1, W2, W3) when ERA5 is the weather. A correction of each ensemble member's wind speed towards ERA5's removes the ensemble's low bias. partly supported candidate The physics model of a wind farm matched XGBoost once a small neural network adjusted the wind speed it takes as input, so XGBoost's lead can be reproduced by a smooth correction of wind speed.
