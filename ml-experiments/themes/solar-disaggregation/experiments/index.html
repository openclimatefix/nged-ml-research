    Solar disaggregation: experiments

    This page lists every experiment tried in the theme, newest first. An experiment that belongs mainly to another theme but bears on this one is marked "also relevant".

    Experiment Hypothesis Verdict Production status Take-home message Relevance
    00479 pv-capacity-anomaly-regression-rung A capacity estimate that regresses the within-month anomaly of net demand on the within-month anomaly of the physics PV shape, with the temperature response fitted jointly, recovers an injected metered farm (raw recovery of 0.85 or more), and its placebo passes. A placebo that passes here is a property of the construction. The estimator is useful only if it also recovers the injected farm (the removed means did not erase the signal) and its 80% interval covers the injected capacity when the load follows the clouds. not supported not-for-production An estimate of solar capacity built only from day-to-day weather within a month cannot be fooled by the season, and it lands close to experiment 474's estimate at most Primaries with a signed reading. It still finds only 0.62 to 0.83 of a solar farm we added as a test, so it cannot validate any Primary's capacity, and experiment 474's estimates stay unvalidated. A load that follows the clouds produces a spurious capacity of 0.2 to 0.3 of a 2 MW farm, unless the estimate also fits two cloud-response columns. primary
    00477 neural-pv-module-placebo-gate-and-known-answer-on-stored-fits The PV module of experiment 416's C3 (a learned capacity per Primary times a physics curve of ensemble-mean irradiance and temperature, subtracted from a demand module that has no direct irradiance) recovers an injected farm's capacity at the signed-MW six, while a placebo (the farm's daylight profile plus its day-to-day anomaly shuffled within the calendar month) stays under a limit set by the same placebo on A0-doy (the network with free irradiance inputs and no PV module). A network without the power lags recovers more of the injected capacity than C3, because the lags let the demand module absorb the PV effect. not supported not-for-production The PV module inside the net demand neural network does not give a capacity per Primary that can be trusted: a placebo made of the farm's daylight profile and a shuffled sky is read out as strongly as the real farm, by the module-free network too. The explicit split neither helps nor hurts the forecast beyond noise (sunny middays +0.22 points, interval -0.14 to +0.56), so the case for it rests on explanation, and the capacities cannot yet carry that case. also relevant
    00427 neural-mva-trajectory-labeller-and-two-channel-inputs A small labeller that reads a whole day of folded readings and outputs a probability that each half-hour is reverse flow, trained on the visible signed Primaries with solar farms injected, gives the export-gated neural network of experiment 420 (arm A) a better sign signal than the gate alone. Feeding the neural network separate MW and MVA input channels with a measured flag, and training it with MW masked on pseudo-Primaries that contain injected reverse flow, lets the network treat the hidden Primaries and the visible Primaries as the same kind of input. Together (arm TDL) the two ideas lower the signed CRPS on reverse-flow half-hours below arm A's. not supported not-for-production A small neural network that reads a whole day of meter readings and marks each half-hour as reverse flow or not ranks the half-hours about as well as the gated forecast network of experiment 420, but no better, and it wrongly marks about three in five of D5's sunny middays as reverse flow when no solar farm was added. The labeller was trained on four higher-demand Primaries, so the next step is to train a sign labeller on a mix that includes low-demand Primaries, or to accept arm A as the candidate and test it on ensemble weather. also relevant
    00424 neural-mva-export-gated-signed-ens-serving-test Experiment 420's export-gated network (arm A) still forecasts signed power on reverse-flow half-hours much better than the same network with its gate shut (P0) when both are trained and scored on ECMWF ensemble (ENS) mean and spread weather, which is the weather a live service would hold, instead of the recorded CAMS irradiance and ERA5 temperature of the laboratory test. The gain shrinks, because the ENS forecast of cloud is less accurate than recorded cloud, but most of the laboratory gain comes from letting the forecast go below zero, which does not depend on weather accuracy. supported candidate With ensemble weather forecasts instead of recorded weather, the neural network with the export gate still forecasts signed power on reverse-flow half-hours much better than the same network with the gate shut (a gain of 15.7 points of capacity, 95% interval 13.8 to 17.4, against 19.2 with recorded weather), and it harms the forecast by no more than 0.05 points where no solar farm exports. The gate opened at the same two of the eight apparent-power-only substations as before. An unplanned finding is that the network trained on recorded weather and applied unchanged to ensemble weather scored 5.5 points better (95% interval 4.4 to 6.6) than the network retrained on ensemble weather, so how to train on ensemble weather is the open question. also relevant
    00420 neural-mva-export-gated-signed-final Experiment 417's arm A (a Primary-level export gate in front of the folded likelihood) gives a large gain in signed CRPS on reverse-flow rows over the plain network read as signed, and keeps the no-injection CRPS unchanged. Adding one shared variance term that grows with the fitted PV term improves on A, because the false calls sit next to zero crossings and the q01 coverage on reverse rows is about 0.05 against a nominal 0.01, so the signed distribution is too narrow where PV is large. partly supported candidate A neural network with a gate that decides once per Primary substation whether it exports power forecasts signed power much better than the same network with the gate shut on the half-hours with reverse flow (7.1 against 26.3 points of capacity) and does no harm beyond the registered tolerance (+0.03 points at D5) where no solar farm exports. Most of that gain comes from letting the forecast go below zero at all, because the ungated network scores the same on reverse-flow half-hours, and the gate's job is to stop invented reverse flow where there is none. Adding a PV-proportional variance did not help, so the next step is a serving test with real ensemble forecasts, not a further change to the network. also relevant
    00417 hidden-sign-mva-export-gate-zero-crossing-labels The false reverse flow that the signed network of experiment 412 produced at MVA-only Primaries came from a capacity fitted on the rows it labels, so a constraint that fixes the sign with information the capacity cannot fit from the same rows removes the false calls and keeps the skill. Two constraints are tested: a once-per-Primary export gate in front of the folded likelihood (design A, with a hard high-irradiance row gate H layered on it) and training labels taken from the two daily zero crossings of an export day (design C). not supported not-for-production Neither of the two constraints stopped the signed mixture density network from inventing reverse flow at MVA-only Primaries. A Primary-level export gate left a placebo with no injected farm clean, but the injected pair kept 12.0% false reverse flow, and training labels taken from the zero crossings of the daily reading reached the same balanced accuracy (0.82) as the unconstrained reference, with 85% false reverse flow at the no-injection D5. This is the eighth treatment of the MVA problem that did not work, so the stop rule applies. also relevant
    00416 mdn-explicit-solar-term-better-powered-follow-up The pooled plain mixture density network can keep its flexible irradiance input and gain an interpretable explicit solar term without losing skill: on sunny-midday half-hours at the six signed-direction Primaries, the network with the explicit solar term (C3), and the hybrid that also keeps the free irradiance input (H1), score within a stated margin of the network with the free irradiance input alone (A0), and they lower the 99th-percentile pinball loss. inconclusive not-for-production Adding an explicit solar-power term to the neural network did not beat letting the network read the sunshine forecast directly: over 12 test months the two score within 0.03 CRPS points overall, and on sunny middays the explicit term is nominally 0.23 points worse, with an interval too wide to call (the design cannot resolve differences under about 0.3 points). The explicit term's upper-tail (99th percentile) loss is slightly lower, but not reliably. Stronger capacity priors made no difference, so the learned capacities cannot yet be read as physical solar capacity. also relevant
    00412 mdn-signed-mw-from-mva-meters-known-answer A pooled mixture density network that writes signed power as X = D - C_p u, with D the plain 3-component mixture, u the CAMS unit PV shape, and C_p a learned capacity per Primary, trained with a folded likelihood where u is large, recovers the sign of reverse flow at signed-MW Primaries whose sign is hidden from it, when the sky is real and not when the sky is shuffled. not supported not-for-production A mixture density network that writes signed power as demand minus a learned solar capacity times the CAMS solar shape could not recover the sign of reverse flow without inventing it. With the sign hidden at two Primaries, it found most injected reverse flow in June but also called reverse flow on 17% of the half-hours that had none, and with no farm injected it called it on 18% to 47% of sunny half-hours at D5. Fixing the capacity, or holding it near a prior, removed the false calls and the skill together, so this is the seventh fold treatment of the MVA problem that did not work. also relevant
    00409 mdn-solar-term-day-of-year-and-anomaly-known-answer A pooled mixture density network that has a day-of-year by hour term in its demand module can attribute the monthly daylight profile of a farm to the demand module, so the explicit solar term learns capacity only from the day-to-day sky. Under that repair the known-answer test can be passed, and the solar term then lowers the net-demand CRPS at the six signed-direction Primaries against the same network given no solar term. not supported not-for-production Adding an explicit solar-power term to the neural network did not reliably lower the net-demand error at the six signed-direction substations: the change was about -0.06 points of capacity (about 2%), with an interval that includes zero. C3's pinball loss at q99 was 7% lower than A0-noirr-doy's (0.427 against 0.461; no interval was computed), and the test could not show that the network reads the sky, because an injected solar signal was too small to see above seed noise. The free irradiance input remains about as good as the explicit term. also relevant
    00405 mdn-explicit-pv-term-learned-capacity-known-answer A pooled mixture density network that forecasts net demand as a demand mixture minus a physical solar-power term, with the term's capacity learned per Primary and season-year under a prior, scores a lower net-demand CRPS at the six signed-direction Primaries than the same network given the irradiance as an ordinary input. A small adapter that modulates the physical solar forecast by weather scenario lowers the score further. inconclusive not-for-production We could not test whether a solar term with a learned capacity improves the forecast, because the known-answer test that gates it could not be passed. On the 2026-06 fold, with 3 seeds and 6 Primaries, adding a real solar farm's output made the network's capacity rise by about the right share, and it rose by 60 to 91% as much when the farm's days were shuffled. A fresh audit found that the shuffled series keeps most of the real series' daylight profile, so the test as built cannot separate a read of the sky from a read of the profile. The next experiment needs a test calibrated to the network. also relevant
    00370 mva-fold-pv-capacity-and-eight-primaries When the registered rule of experiment 366 chooses between the fit through the meter (L0) and the naive fit (N), the solar capacity of the chosen fit (the naive fit N or the fit through the meter L0) stays close to the capacity of the fit to the signed series at the six Primaries with signed MW readings, and at the eight MVA-only Primaries the fit through the meter finds reverse flow at the substations NGED marks "Yes" or "Maybe", not only at Stickney (D12). partly supported not-for-production With the meter's reactive power at zero, the rule of experiment 366 recovers each signed-MW Primary's solar capacity to within 3.1% of the signed fit, and the fit through the meter fits Stickney's sunny-half-hour readings with a reading error 9.3 points of capacity lower than the naive fit's. The fit through the meter does not find reverse flow at Marsh Lane (D8) or Langrick (D22), so either those substations have little reverse flow or the fit cannot see it. The pinned solar capacity is 45% and 58% above the signed fit's at D9 and D16, which is a reason to treat the pin as a reference and not as the truth. also relevant
    00366 mva-fold-irradiance-correlation-reverse-flow-rule Reverse flow can be told apart from invented reverse flow using the meter readings and the irradiance forecast alone: where a substation has reverse flow, its sunny-midday meter readings rise with irradiance (the magnitude of a negative flow grows), and where it has none, they fall. partly supported not-for-production A test on how the midday meter readings move with sunshine, together with a test for readings near zero, picks the right fit at the substation with no reverse flow and at every substation where solar output was added, and it picks reverse flow at Stickney (D12) and at no other MVA-only Primary. All registered pass criteria are met with reactive power zero. The verdict is "partly supported" only because an unregistered check, at power factor 0.97, fails. The rule only works when the meter reading has no reactive power in it. At power factor 0.97 the readings no longer show the reverse flow, so the rule stays with the naive fit and loses badly at moderate injected solar. The 1.0 threshold on the low readings was chosen after seeing experiment 365's numbers, and it carries the moderate-injection cases. also relevant
    00365 mva-fold-readings-only-reverse-flow-rule Reverse flow can be told apart from invented reverse flow using the meter readings alone: if the training readings on sunny midday half-hours never come close to zero, the substation has no reverse flow, and the naive fit (reading the magnitude as signed power) is right. If they do come close to zero, the fit through the meter is used. partly supported not-for-production Looking for midday meter readings near zero does not reliably tell whether a substation has reverse flow. The rule still picks the wrong fit at the substation with none (D5), because that substation's readings also dip towards zero. The rule also misses heavy reverse flow, because heavy reverse flow gives large readings, not small ones: the rule picks the naive fit at Stickney (D12), the MVA-only Primary expected to have it. The registered pass test is met only because it allows one of five substations to be wrong. A rule that uses how the readings move with sunshine is an idea to test next. also relevant
    00362 mva-fold-gated-fit-known-answer A gate that accepts reverse flow only where an independent solar forecast exceeds the fitted demand stops the fit through the meter from inventing reverse flow at a substation with none, without losing the reverse flow that real solar output produces. refuted not-for-production A fit to meter readings that give only the size of the power flow sometimes invents reverse flow (power flowing back to the grid) at a substation that has none. Rejecting such fits with an independent solar forecast does not work. The registered gate (ratio 0.75) rejected no fit, and the stricter gate (ratio 1.0) rejected the correct fits where reverse flow is heavy. The next test is a rule that uses the meter readings alone: accept reverse flow only if the training readings come close to zero at midday. also relevant
    00358 mva-fold-known-answer-at-folded-mw-primaries When the signed power of a Primary substation is folded to apparent power (its magnitude), the convex model fitted through the meter (experiment 110's L0) recovers the sign of the flow and the solar capacity, and a model that reads the folded reading as signed power does not. partly supported not-for-production A substation meter that reports only the size of the power flow, not its direction, still lets the model fitted through the meter recover the direction and the solar capacity about as well as a model that sees the signed power, as long as reactive power is zero. The model also invented reverse flow at the 1 of 5 substations that has none, so reverse flow it reports at a real substation needs an independent check. The pre-registered test could not be passed as written, because with the larger injection almost every sunny half-hour has reverse flow, so only one flow direction is present and balanced accuracy cannot be computed. also relevant
    00352 primary-demand-default-3-capacity-response-surface The score of the first (convex) stage of Primary demand research default 3 on sunny daylight half-hours is nearly flat in the solar capacity over 0.8 to 1.2 times the pinned capacity when the Kalman filter is on, because the filter absorbs the difference. Experiments 349 and 350 found no reliable gain from a different capacity, and a stored-data analysis suggested a flat response. Without the filter the response is steeper. partly supported not-for-production The demand forecast's score on sunny half-hours moved by only 0.010 points (-0.104 to +0.088) when the assumed solar capacity was 20% too low, but rose by 0.124 points when it was 20% too high and by 0.358 when it was 40% too high, so a capacity estimate above the pin is the risk and a lower capacity estimate is not. The Kalman filter halves the rise from too much capacity, which supports the idea that the filter absorbs part of the capacity error, but leaving out D5 cuts the rise at 20% too high from +0.124 to +0.074. Chasing a more exact capacity cannot be justified by this result: no scale from 0.6 to 1.4 beat the pin by more than 0.03 points with the filter on. also relevant
    00351 primary-demand-capacity-physical-shape-readout Experiment 349 read the solar capacity with the empirical plant shape of six metered farms (experiment 269) but multiplied it by the stack's physical unit shape, a mismatch its page lists as a lab shortcut. Reading the capacity out in the physical unit shape the stack itself uses, with the same monthly as-of refit and its own injection divisor, removes the mismatch. If the mismatch explained experiment 349's null result, the sunny-half-hour score improves. not supported not-for-production Estimating how much unmetered rooftop solar sits behind each substation with the clear-sky daily output curve that the forecast already uses did not improve the demand forecast on sunny half-hours. The quantile score was 0.065 points worse than with the fixed capacity from experiment 45 (95% interval +0.017 to +0.126). The two output curves give scores within 0.018 points of each other (-0.020 to +0.054), so mixing two curves in experiment 349 does not explain why it did not improve the forecast. Leaving out either of two substations (D16 or D10) makes the interval include zero, so the harm rests on those two. also relevant
    00350 primary-demand-capacity-free-irradiance-curve The convex model of Primary demand research default 3 subtracts a PV term, a pinned PV capacity (from the frozen weekly vintages of experiment 45) times a unit solar shape built from irradiance (35 degree tilt, south facing, temperature derated). Experiments 110 and 139 found that no fitted or as-of capacity improves on the pin, so the capacity estimate is the part of the stack most likely to be wrong. Replacing the pinned capacity and the fixed unit shape with a learned, monotone function of irradiance, scaled per Primary by a number the fit learns, keeps the forecast as good on sunny daylight half-hours and keeps it as good elsewhere, so the capacity estimate is not needed. not supported not-for-production Dropping the PV capacity estimate is not yet safe: a learned curve of irradiance does no better than the pinned capacity over five of the six Primaries (+0.025 points, interval -0.252 to +0.346). Only D5's interval excludes zero (-0.969), where either the pinned capacity or the unit solar shape is probably wrong; D11 is significantly worse with the curve (+0.223 [+0.100, +0.360]). The useful next step is to check the D5 pin and unit shape, and to keep the curve as a low-effort sanity check on each Primary's pin and as a fallback for a Primary with no trustworthy capacity. primary
    00349 primary-demand-default-3-exp-269-capacity-in-the-stack Primary demand research default 3 subtracts a solar capacity pinned to experiment 45's frozen vintage (a hindsight growth prior) times a unit solar shape. Replacing that capacity with the experiment 269 estimate (the mean plant response of six metered farms, read at the Primary's satellite irradiance, with the capacity fitted from rows before each fold's origin) lowers the quantile score on sunny daylight half-hours, because the experiment 269 read-out recovers 0.89 to 0.97 of an injected farm where the physical fleet shape recovers 0.78 to 0.83. inconclusive not-for-production Using the capacity estimated from six metered solar farms in place of the capacity pinned from earlier experiments did not improve the demand forecast on sunny half-hours: the score was 0.047 points worse, within the noise (-0.029 to +0.135), and the replicate year (2024-07 to 2025-06) pointed the same way. The estimate is lower than the pin at several Primaries, most of all at D16 where the forecast got worst. This was tested on the convex stage out to 7 days only, so the full stack and the 7 to 10 day range are untested. also relevant
    00289 summer-gap-weather-input-discriminator The best-guess stack's weaker April to June score than BG2 at the decision Primaries comes from the ensemble's sunshine forecast (or its ratio calibration), and not from demand absorbing the solar term or from the error-correction layers. partly supported not-for-production Giving the best-guess stack the sunshine that actually happened would close about 70% of its April to June gap to BG2, but the same change helps the stack nearly as much in July to September, when the stack already matches BG2. Most of the April to June gap beyond the July to September gap therefore stays. That points to the solar term in the demand model, or to the error-correction layers, and away from the ensemble sunshine forecast. A refit with realised sunshine as the solar driver, and a check of the solar term at D5 and D9 in April to June, are the next steps. also relevant
    00282 pv-plant-curve-audit The curve that turns sunshine into power in our solar models departs from the metered farms' measured curve by more than 5% of capacity somewhere in the clear-sky range, and the departure is the flat-top level (the inverter limit), not the panel tilt or the temperature loss. not supported not-for-production The curve that turns sunshine into solar power stays within 5 percentage points of capacity at 3 of the 6 metered solar farms and departs from their measured output at the other 3 in different directions, so no single fix to the inverter limit repairs it. The best-guess demand stack assumes panels tilted 35 degrees and the farms fit best at about 20 degrees, which makes the stack over-predict output on clear half-hours in December (measured is 0.66 of the stack, median over farms) and under-predict it from April to July by 5 to 8%. Swapping in the pooled shape of the other farms does not help, so the next experiment should try the 20 degree tilt inside the stack. also relevant
    00280 pv-empirical-shape-and-capacity-in-arm-d The April to June 2026 gap between arm D (the monthly-refit generalised additive model of experiments 155 and 220, scored on experiment 178's rows) and BG2 comes from its solar term: the pinned solar capacity is too high and the south-facing fleet shape is not the shape the metered farms show. Replacing the fleet shape by the pooled empirical shape of experiment 269, at the capacity that shape's real-series fit supports, lowers arm D's April to June error at the substations where the pinned capacity looks too high (D5, D9, and D16). not supported not-for-production Putting the empirical solar shape into the forecast, at the pinned capacity or at the lower capacity the real series supports, does not lower the April to June error at the substations where the pinned capacity looked too high. The change is under 0.01 MW against an error of about 0.5 MW, and the intervals are wide. A capacity cut that is correct for the whole of April to June is not the fix either: at D5 it raises the error. This experiment finds no sign that the shape or the capacity of the solar term closes the summer gap to BG2. also relevant
    00269 pv-capacity-gain-decomposition Most of the 0.090 gain that experiment 238 found from using a nearby farm's recorded output as the solar shape comes from the farm's plant shape (tilt, orientation, flat top) and from the capacity unit in which recovery is read, and little comes from the farm's measured sunshine. partly supported not-for-production For the five substations and six farms tested, the measured sunshine at a nearby farm explains almost none of the extra solar output that its recorded output helped to find. The extra output found comes from the farms' plant shape (panel tilt, panel orientation, and the flat top of the output curve). A table learned from other farms' output and driven by the satellite irradiance reproduces that plant shape. Using the table finds 0.966 of an injected solar farm's output when recovery is read as the capacity ratio of experiment 231 (0.826 with the physical fleet shape), and 0.995 when it is read over clear-sky daylight energy. In these tests the satellite irradiance was not the limit, and a metered farm beside each substation was not needed. Switching to the table lowers the fitted solar capacities of the five decision substations by 0.2 to 0.4 MW. The effect on the sunny-half-hour forecast score has not been run. also relevant
    00267 pv-capacity-early-years-and-register-floor The inferred capacity of unmetered solar at the five decision Primaries falls below the register floor in season-year 2021 (July 2021 to June 2022) because the capacity fit recovers only about 0.78 of an injected farm and the 2021 fit window is thin, not because the register is shared to the wrong district. Dividing by the median recovery of the metered farms, and using the output of a metered farm within 25 km as the sunshine shape, lifts the estimates to the floor. not supported not-for-production The 2021 shortfall at D10 and D11 is not a missing recovery correction and not a thin fit window: the card already divides by the metered farms' recovery, and the 2021 season-year has 12 months of data at every Primary. Using a nearby metered farm's output as the sunshine shape makes the shortfall no smaller (the estimates fall by 7% to 22%, partly because the two shapes are divided by different recoveries), so the shortfall at those two Primaries remains unexplained. Candidate causes are the register's postcode-district share, a gap of about 20 months in the early data, and a fit that is too low in its first full year. Show 2021 at D10 and D11 as a known shortfall. also relevant
    00266 summer-gap-diagnostics-solar-term The best-guess demand stack (experiment 251's arm 1) trails BG2, the production-style XGBoost forecast, in April to June 2026 mainly because of its explicit solar term (the subtracted estimate of unmetered rooftop solar output), and not because of the neural-network head, the two-number summary of the ensemble's solar response, the Kalman filter, or the heating and lighting terms. The same defect explains the loss at D5, an anonymised Primary substation, on sunny half-hours. partly supported not-for-production The best-guess stack trails BG2 in April to June because of the solar term at two of the six substations (D5 and D9 on daylight half-hours) and a night-time error at a third (D10), not because of the neural-network head, the two-number summary, the Kalman filter, or the ensemble sunshine forecast; the head and summary actually reduce the gap. The pinned solar capacity looks too high in those months, not too low, and clear mornings are forecast worse than clear afternoons. Candidate next experiments are the export clip at D9, a morning-and-afternoon solar shape, and a look at D10's night residuals; the stack's added parts need no change for this gap. also relevant
    00241 pv-capacity-calibrated-interval The recoveries that experiment 129 measured when it injected real solar farms into a Primary substation's power can be turned into a calibrated estimate of unmetered solar capacity: dividing the capacity found by the Primary's median recovery over the other farms corrects its bias, and the spread of those recoveries gives an 80% interval that covers the truth about 80% of the time. partly supported not-for-production Dividing the solar capacity found by the typical recovery of other real farms removes most of the bias: the estimate lands within 10% of the truth at all five Primaries, against a typical error of 19% without the correction. The 80% interval built the same way covers the truth 59% to 79% of the time, which is too uncertain to label as an 80% interval, and it fails completely when the demand follows cloud (0% to 2%).
