    Demand inputs: state and next steps

    Extra weather, calendar, price, and satellite inputs have given the demand forecast little beyond the inputs already in the final model. The overview page holds the XGBoost results. This page covers the neural-network results, which came later, and ranks the options for further tests. Final models describes what the production port builds. In this page, "the Candidate" is the demand neural network that the final models use for the 16 Primary substations.

    Terms. CRPS is the continuous ranked probability score, an error measure for probabilistic forecasts, in points of capacity. A negative contrast means the first arm is better. The six MW Primaries are the six Primary substations whose meters report signed megawatts (MW). The eight MVA Primaries report apparent power only, in megavolt-amperes (MVA). A cold night is a half-hour below 5 degrees C between 18:00 and 08:00 local time. A placebo is the same columns with the values taken from a shuffled other day, so the level is right and the timing is wrong. The minimum detectable effect is the smallest difference that seed spread alone would resolve. ENS is the ECMWF ensemble weather forecast, ERA5 is the fifth-generation ECMWF reanalysis, and CAMS is the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service satellite estimate of sunlight (glossary). M0 is the plain modular neural network that the add-on experiments use as their control.

    What we know

    No input group tested on a neural network beat its control by the registered bar over a whole year. Eight bundles of thermal-lag, solar-gain, look-ahead, calendar, tariff, and national-signal columns did not lower the CRPS of the pooled neural network by 0.03 points in 3 of 4 folds, and seven of the eight were worse at the six MW Primaries (Experiment 437).

    When the same columns entered pre-training through a matching encoder, no group beat its placebo by the minimum detectable effect. The closest was the thermal-lag and solar-gain group (B1) at the six MW Primaries: -0.135 (interval -0.31 to +0.04), against a minimum detectable effect of 0.30 (Experiment 569).

    Adding the 14 thermal-lag and solar-gain columns to the modular neural network changed error little and lowered the leak of injected shifts. Against the control without them, the columns moved the CRPS by +0.010 at the eight MVA Primaries and -0.046 at the six MW Primaries in the 0 to 24 hour band. The leak of an injected shift into the forecast fell from 7.7 to 5.4 points against the placebo (Experiment 574). Experiment 677, which builds up the Candidate one component at a time, is pending, and Final models will state its result.

    The one candidate input group is the thermal-lag and trailing-temperature group, and only on cold nights. The gain is small and shifts between experiments:

    • Experiment 437 found a gain of 0.022 at the eight MVA Primaries in 2 of 4 folds.
    • Experiment 442 found the cold-night contrast at the eight fell from the 0.05 or more of Experiment 441 to -0.014 (-0.102 to +0.138), and at the six it was -0.086 (-0.241 to +0.141).
    • Experiment 515 found trailing temperature windows lowered cold-night CRPS at the eight by 0.150 (10 of 10 folds) while a placebo lowered it by 0.071, so the experiment called the effect not resolved.
    • Experiment 562 found a thermal-lag and solar-gain group 0.231 better than its control on cold nights at the six, from 3 monthly blocks, at a minimum detectable effect of 0.227.

    Experiment 526 supersedes Experiment 515 because it used 11 seeds. The two experiments share folds. Experiment 526 found the real windows beat the placebo by 0.093 (-0.133 to -0.053, 9 of 10 folds) at the eight, only just above the minimum detectable effect of 0.083. Nothing resolved at the six or in summer. The seam check failed: that check compares the windows built from lagged weather with windows that fill the hours before the forecast origin with the run's first value.

    Satellite sunshine helps only when it is read at the target half-hour. Two columns of realised CAMS irradiance lowered the XGBoost CRPS at all 16 Primaries in the point estimates, with the interval wholly below zero at 15 (Experiment 480: -0.355 at the eight, -0.476 at the six). A live service lacks that input for the first day ahead, because the satellite data arrives about a day late. Satellite values a live service could hold, from whole days at least 36 hours old, raised the neural network's CRPS by 0.006 at the eight and 0.010 at the six (Experiment 504).

    The ensemble mean of temperature and shortwave carries the XGBoost gain from the ensemble. Two mean columns lowered the CRPS by 0.052 at the six and 0.029 at the eight, and the spread columns added almost nothing (Experiment 394).

    What does not work

    Learned or larger components did not beat the hand-made inputs.

    • A convolutional encoder over a 24-hour weather window scored 0.07 to 0.13 worse than the 37-column base on all rows, with every interval including zero (Experiment 516).
    • A shared calendar encoder scored 0.27 worse than the hand-made calendar columns at the six (Experiment 423).
    • Larger encoders did not help beyond seed noise at the eight (Experiment 579).

    Price did not help, even as a training-time-only input. A hindsight price shape for the whole day gave -0.048 at the six and +0.037 at the eight, against a minimum detectable effect of about 0.17. The arms that would have trained a teacher neural network on the price (price dropout and a teacher network) were therefore not run (Experiment 513). A price encoder trained beside the weather encoders made the fine-tuned forecast 0.29 worse at the six (Experiment 570). The XGBoost price tests are on the overview page.

    The lockdown flag is neither shown to be needed nor shown to be removable. In Experiment 517, arm C0 keeps the flag, C1 removes the flag and keeps the 2020 to 2021 lockdown rows, and C2 removes the flag and drops those rows. C1 scored 0.029 worse than C0 at the six. C2 scored 0.044 better than C1 at the eight, and 0.033 better than C0 at the eight. The experiment judges that it cannot rank the arms, because leaving out one Primary at a time weakens both results and every difference is below the minimum detectable effect of 0.12 to 0.13.

    The 95% and 99% upper quantiles of the six MW Primaries cover only 76% and 83% of cold winter-night half-hours (Experiment 441). That shortfall is larger than any input's gain, so calibration is a likelier lever on cold nights than another input.

    Open questions

    • Does the trailing-temperature gain at the eight survive on a held-out winter? Experiments 515 and 526 share folds, so a new winter is the first independent test.
    • How much of Experiment 480's satellite gain survives when the columns come from the ensemble forecast? The experiment named this as its next step.

    Next-experiment options

    The options are ranked by how much a production port depends on the answer.

    1. An option is to wait for Experiment 677 and then test removing the 14 thermal-lag and solar-gain columns from the Candidate. Experiments 574 and 569 found the columns neutral on error, so the question is whether the port can drop them and the pipeline step that builds them. Experiment 677's build-up is pending (see Final models), and it may answer the question first. The plan page lists no separate experiment for this option.
    2. An option is to retest trailing temperature on more winter folds with the placebo and seam check of Experiment 526. The question is whether the 0.093 gain at the eight is real. It depends on option 1, because a column the final model drops needs no retest. Experiment 562 asked for a larger cold-night test on the best lagged neural network.
    3. An option is to re-run Experiments 569 and 562 with a second seed and a re-pretrained plain control. Experiment 569 named this step, because its pre-trained control scored worse than the stored weights of plain M0. It matters mainly if option 1 shows the columns help.
    4. An option is to score Experiment 480's satellite columns with ensemble-forecast values. The question is what remains of the 0.355 to 0.476 gain in a live service. Its result applies to the XGBoost path more than to the final neural network.
    5. An option is to add more seeds for the larger datetime encoder at the six. Experiment 579 suggested it, and its own conclusion is that no further architecture arms are needed, so the production value is small.

    What is parked

    The convolutional window encoder is parked. Experiment 516 recommended spending no more fits on learned windows until Experiment 515's placebo question was resolved, and Experiment 526 resolved it only partly.

    Price inputs are parked. The oracle bound of Experiment 513 is not resolvable from zero, and Experiment 570 failed its known-answer test, so the next step named there is a controlled test with a larger injected generator before any real-data price work. Modelling batteries and price-following generators as a separate target is a roadmap question, not an experiment of this theme.

    The lockdown flag stays as the default. Experiment 517 cannot rank the arms, and the flag is zero in every scored month, so the production port can follow the default of the final model.

    Load-shift event detection is paused until January. It is the subject of the lsc-detectors theme, and the options above do not depend on it.
