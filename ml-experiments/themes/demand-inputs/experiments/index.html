    Demand inputs: experiments

    This page lists every experiment tried in the theme, newest first. An experiment that belongs mainly to another theme but bears on this one is marked "also relevant".

    Experiment Hypothesis Verdict Production status Take-home message Relevance
    00632 gru-attention-sequence-network-for-primary-demand A sequence neural network (Seq: an encoder gated recurrent unit over the last 7 days, a decoder gated recurrent unit over the next 7 days that starts from the encoder's state, and three self-attention layers over all tokens), pre-trained on ERA5 and CAMS weather with one Primary embedding for all 16 Primaries, forecasts the next 7 days of demand better than the existing pre-trained networks, because it reads the last 6 days of power and not only a day lag and a week lag. refuted not-for-production The sequence neural network is not worth porting. Seq is not shown better than the per-row lag neural network P2-pre (CRPS 2.90 against 2.86 at the eight MVA Primaries, 2.12 against 2.15 at the six MW Primaries) and loses to P2-hist6, a per-row neural network that reads six daily lags (2.80 and 2.13). No ablation (no attention, no recurrence, neighbour inputs) and not the 3-week history beat Seq by more than seed noise. Seq follows an injected load-shift more than P2-pre does (a share of 0.40 against 0.27 of the shift at the eight MVA Primaries from day 4 of the event), and training it on synthetic shifts cuts that to 0.15 but raises CRPS by about 0.12 points. Stage 3 (ensemble weather) does not run. also relevant
    00585 lag-count-and-year-ago-lags-through-the-lag-encoder The lag encoder of the modular network (experiment 572's v3+D5, the control N2) reads one day-old and one week-old reading of a Primary's power. A reading from 364 days earlier (the same weekday and half-hour) carries information that the two recent lags cannot at long leads, where the week lag is 2 to 3 weeks old, and on holidays that repeat each year. The manual heuristic already uses last year's values. partly supported not-for-production Reading the power from a year ago did not make the forecast better on average and did not make it safer around load-shift events; it narrowed the 90% interval and lowered the share of readings on the safe side. It did lower the error at Christmas and New Year at the six MW Primaries (0.53 at 120-240 h for the weekday-aligned lag), but that is one December. Dropping the day lag reduced following most (0.116 against 0.196), at the cost of 0.137 CRPS. also relevant
    00579 encoder-capacity-and-architecture-arms-on-the-modular-network The weather encoder and the datetime encoder of the modular network are small (about 2,500 weights each). The datetime encoder has the most to learn for demand forecasting, so capacity may limit it, and the inputs it is given (hour harmonics 1 and 2, a day-of-week one-hot, solar geometry, holiday columns) may limit it too. The weather encoder has only 7 inputs, so capacity should not limit it. not supported not-for-production Making the weather and datetime encoders of the modular demand network larger, or giving the datetime encoder richer inputs such as an Economy 7 proxy, did not lower the forecast error beyond what a different random seed changes at the eight MVA Primaries, so capacity is not what limits the forecast there. At the six MW Primaries a larger datetime encoder lowered the error by 0.135 points of capacity, but a copy with the same depth and no extra width achieved more than half of that, so the gain may come from depth or from training noise rather than capacity. The next step is more seeds for DL and DT at the six MW Primaries, and no further architecture arms. primary
    00570 price-encoder-part-1-known-answer-test-and-pretrained-stack Price-following assets such as batteries and gas engines put price-driven variation into a series' power that weather and calendar cannot explain. A price encoder that sees the true price while the modular lag-free network is pre-trained can absorb that variation, so the weather encoder and the datetime encoder learn cleaner patterns from the same targets. The price encoder is discarded before fine-tuning, so serving needs no price feed. not supported not-for-production Training a price encoder (a neural network part fed electricity prices) beside the weather and datetime encoders did not make those encoders cleaner: after fine-tuning, the forecast was no better, and at the six MW Primaries it was worse by 0.29 points of CRPS (the probabilistic forecast error, as a percentage of each Primary's capacity) than a forecast trained without price. Most of that harm also appears when the encoder sees only noise, so the extra encoder itself lowers accuracy, and at the eight MVA Primaries the price content adds nothing the data can resolve. The same encoder could not recover an injected price-driven generator in a controlled test (it recovered 45% of the injected battery rule's output and none of the threshold and rank rules'), so the next step is a controlled test with a larger injected generator before any further real-data price work. primary
    00569 modular-network-inputs-through-matching-encoders-in-pretraining-and-fine-tuning-run-1 Experiment 562 added five input groups to the modular lag-free network at fine-tuning only. Each group may help once it is also pre-trained on ERA5 and CAMS rows and enters through the encoder that matches its content: thermal-lag and solar-gain windows (B1) and look-ahead windows (B2) through the weather encoder, school terms and holidays (B3a) through the datetime encoder, national signals of the previous day (B3b) and the whole-day price shape (B4) through a small parallel encoder. A sixth group (B3c) adds, to B3a, the number of schools within 2 km of each Primary as a gate on the school columns. not supported not-for-production Letting the demand network read thermal-lag, solar-gain, look-ahead, school-term, national-signal, and price-shape columns during pre-training as well as fine-tuning, through the encoder that matches each group, did not help: none of them beat a shuffled-day placebo of the same width on all rows, cold nights, Christmas week, or high-spread days, and a school-proximity input made the eight MVA Primaries worse. This test cannot see small gains (one seed), and the network pre-trained from scratch with extra columns scored worse than plain M0's stored weights, which is the larger effect to understand first. The next step is a second seed and a plain M0 re-pre-trained under the same recipe, so that the control and the arms differ only in the columns. primary
    00562 modular-lag-free-network-null-input-retry-with-placebos The modular lag-free network of experiment 545 (arm M0) has a weather encoder and a datetime encoder, so it may extract signal from four groups of inputs that earlier networks found null or harmful: thermal-lag and solar-gain windows (B1), look-ahead weather windows (B2), school-term and national-signal columns (B3, one arm for each), and a whole-day price shape that is present only while training (B4). Each real arm is compared with a shuffled-day placebo of the same number of columns, so a gain counts only if it exceeds what extra columns give regardless of content. not supported not-for-production Giving the modular lag-free demand network extra inputs that earlier networks found useless did not help over a whole year: no group beat a shuffled-day control of the same width by the threshold. The exception is the thermal-lag and solar-gain group (B1), which lowered the error on cold winter nights at the six signed-MW substations by about 0.2 points of capacity (3 months only, an effect the data can barely resolve). On all rows at the eight it made no difference against plain M0. The next step is a larger cold-night test of that group on the best lagged network. primary
    00561 wind-components-era5-and-ens-summary-data-prep The wind components (east and north, at 10 m and 100 m) can be added to the ERA5 pretraining table and to the ensemble summaries from data already on disk, and total cloud cover can be added to both from ERA5 on disk and from the Dynamical.org ensemble store. partly supported not-for-production The wind direction inputs and ERA5 cloud cover are ready for every run and every hour. Ensemble cloud cover is ready only for the 222 most recent runs (2025-11-21 to 2026-06-30), so an experiment that uses it cannot train on the earlier ensemble history. The production table does not store cloud cover yet. primary
    00552 icon-eu-four-runs-a-day-lag-free-demand NGED wants the forecast updated every 6 hours. The ECMWF ensemble runs once a day, so a lag-free forecast issued at 06, 12, or 18 UTC repeats the same weather inputs. ICON-EU runs four times a day, so a small adaptor that maps the latest ICON-EU run to the seven mean-weather inputs of experiment 524's frozen stage-1 network could improve the forecast at the issue times that the ECMWF run does not refresh. not supported not-for-production A fresher weather run did not improve the forecast: with 4 months of ICON-EU history, the ICON-EU-based forecast was worse than the ECMWF-based one in March and April and level with it in May and June. This is spring only and gives no evidence about winter. The next step is to wait for more ICON-EU history, or to test the same question on a source with years of runs. also relevant
    00550 lag-light-network-trailing-temperature-windows-with-placebo The three trailing temperature windows (means of the ensemble-mean 2 m temperature over the 6, 24, and 72 hours ending at the valid time) still lower the error on cold winter nights when the network also reads the day-ago and week-ago power lags (R-DW of experiment 539), because the windows describe the weather of the last three days and a lag of one day or one week carries only one reading of it. supported candidate The three trailing temperature windows still help the lag-light demand network on cold winter nights by about 0.10 points of capacity, the same size as without the lags, so the day-ago and week-ago lags do not already carry that information. Over all rows the windows do nothing we can show. The next step is to judge the windows on cold nights, with more winter folds. also relevant
    00549 k2-s1-blend-and-climatology-stored-check Part 1. Averaging the 11 stored quantiles of R-DW (experiment 539) and the retrained K2-S1 (experiment 541, a different kind of network: monthly lag-free heads plus lagged heads) with a fixed weight per lead band forecasts better than either, because the two networks have different inputs and architectures and so make less correlated errors than L-1d and R-DW did in experiment 547. Part 2. A blend with a calendar-only or climatology forecast helps the demand forecast only at 120 to 240 h. partly supported not-for-production Averaging R-DW with the rebuilt K2-S1 improves the 1 to 5 day forecast by about 0.02 points of capacity, less than half the 0.05 we asked for, so serving a second neural network for it is not justified. No stored blend of climatology or a calendar-only forecast into a demand network helped by 0.05 at 5 to 10 days; the one climatology gain, for wind farms in experiment 262, vanished once the offset table was applied. The proposed climatology head and weather-input dropout therefore have no stored evidence behind them. also relevant
    00548 lag-free-release-candidate-assembly-with-trailing-temperature-windows The lag-free recipe assembled from parts that already have evidence (ERA5 and CAMS pretraining, daily refit with a 90-day half-life, trailing temperature windows, flat 1.15 widening) reaches the score of the lag-enabled network A-pool at 120 to 240 h of lead but not at 24 to 48 h, where the gap is mostly the recent-power inputs. partly supported candidate A demand forecast that sees no recent power but does see the recent temperature scored slightly better than the same forecast without the temperature, although no interval excluded zero. At 5 to 10 days ahead its gap to the forecast that does see recent power was inside what the seeds can resolve. At 0 to 5 days ahead, for the eight Primary substations metered in MVA only, it stayed 0.13 to 0.34 points of capacity worse. The temperature columns did not close that gap. Of the approaches tested in experiments 539 and 541, only adding the power from a day and a week ago closed most of it, and that approach also made the forecast follow an injected load shift more closely (5.68 against 4.49 points of capacity). also relevant
    00547 lag-light-and-lag-free-blend-by-lead-band-stored-forecasts Averaging the 11 stored quantiles of L-1d (a daily-refit lag-free network, experiment 511) and R-DW (the same network plus day-ago and week-ago power, experiment 539) with a fixed weight per lead band forecasts better than either parent, because the two networks make partly different errors and the best weight on the lag-light network differs by lead. not supported not-for-production Averaging the lag-free network with the lag-light network gives no better forecast than the lag-light network alone: at 1 to 5 days ahead the two scores differ by under 0.01 points of capacity, far below the 0.05 we asked for. In this test the lag-free network added nothing the lag-light network lacks. A blend with a network of a different kind, such as K2-S1 rebuilt on origin-time weather, is untested. also relevant
    00546 teacher-student-distillation-into-lag-free-demand-network A lag-free network (the student) fine-tuned against the forecast of a teacher that sees the day-ago and week-ago lags (experiment 539's R-DW), in addition to the real target, scores closer to A-pool than the lag-free network L-1d does, because the teacher's forecast carries information about the weekly pattern that the weather and calendar inputs can recover but the real target alone teaches slowly. The student reads no lag at any time, so it adds no load-shift leak route. refuted not-for-production A demand network that cannot see recent power learned nothing useful from a teacher that can: it scored slightly worse than the same network trained on the real target alone, and stayed 0.26 to 0.38 points of capacity behind A-pool at 1 to 5 days ahead. The day-ago and week-ago power must therefore be an input at forecast time, or the gap stays. The next step is to choose between accepting the injected load-shift leak that those lags bring and a robust same-slot summary of the recent weeks. also relevant
    00544 lag-light-demand-network-robust-same-slot-median-and-lag-dropout Experiment 539's lag-light network (the lag-free network plus the day-ago and week-ago power readings, each with a missing flag and an age) closes 76% to 83% of the lag-free network's gap to the lag-enabled network A-pool, but it follows an injected load shift more than the lag-free network does. Two changes could keep the gain and cut the weakness. (1) R-MED: replace each single reading by the median of the same half-hour over many days, which one shifted day cannot move. (2) R-DROP: train with the readings removed in 30% of rows, so the network also works when they are late or absent. not supported not-for-production Averaging the same half-hour over several past days did not make the lag-light demand network safer against load-shift events: it forecast worse and followed an injected shift more. Randomly removing the readings during training made the network lean less on them, so it kept under half of the gain. It does work with no readings at all, at the level of the lag-free network. The next step is a different guard against shifted readings, such as the detector's probability, or accepting experiment 539's leak. also relevant
    00540 lag-gap-what-closes-the-gap-to-lag-enabled-models The lag-enabled demand models beat the lag-free network L-1d at 24 to 120 h because of their recent-power (lag) inputs, and the gap is not shown to come from head count, recency weighting, restart, or network family. Refit cadence is not separated from the lags. supported not-for-production The demand network that sees no recent power scores 0.2 to 0.3 points of capacity worse (CRPS) than the network that does, at 1 to 5 days ahead for the eight Primary substations metered in MVA only. Adding the power from the same time a day ago and a week ago closes about four fifths of that gap (a point estimate with wide intervals). In one seed, those inputs also made the forecast follow an injected load shift more. An earlier apparent advantage at 5 to 10 days came from weather that was issued after the forecast was made. also relevant
    00526 lagless-demand-network-trailing-weather-window-placebo-repeat Experiment 515 found that trailing temperature windows (W-T) lower cold-night CRPS at the eight MVA-only Primaries by 0.150 points, but a shuffled-day placebo over nine columns (W-null) lowered it by 0.071, and W-T minus W-null was -0.079 [-0.290, +0.089]. That comparison mixes real against shuffled windows with three columns against nine. The claim here is that W-T beats a shuffled-day placebo built from exactly W-T's three columns (W-Tnull) on cold nights at the eight, so the gain comes from the content of the windows and not from extra input columns or from the fold in which W0 scores badly. partly supported candidate Trailing temperature windows lowered cold-night error at the eight MVA-only Primaries by 0.093 points of capacity more than a shuffled-day placebo of the same three columns did (11 seeds, 9 of 10 folds), which is a small gain only just above what the seeds can resolve. Nothing resolved at the six signed-MW Primaries or in summer, and the check that persistence-filled windows match the default build failed, so this is a candidate and not a settled result. primary
    00525 metering-type-flag-lagless-demand-network The 8-wide Primary embedding of the lag-free network is mostly its random start, so it may not carry the one fact that separates the eight MVA-only Primaries from the six signed-MW Primaries: apparent power cannot be negative, and signed power can. One extra 0/1 input column (1 for apparent power in MVA) tells the network that fact directly. If the embedding already carries it, the column adds nothing; if the column improves the forecast or removes the mixture's probability mass below zero at the eight, the embedding is too thin. not supported not-for-production Telling the lag-free demand network which Primaries report apparent power (MVA) and which report signed MW did not make its forecast better. At the eight MVA-only Primaries the flag made the score slightly worse, as a randomly permuted flag did, and it did not remove the forecast probability below zero. At the six signed-MW Primaries it helped by about 0.03 points, a gain the permuted flag did not reproduce but that is too small to port. also relevant
    00522 primary-embedding-data-look-lagless-demand-network The Primary embedding of the lag-free neural network does more than label the 16 Primaries. The embedding is the 16 x 8 table of vectors that tells the network which Primary (primary substation) a row belongs to. The network is the L0PT network of experiment 448: it reads no lagged power and is pretrained on ERA5 and CAMS estimates of past weather. Each vector starts as a random N(0, 1) draw. The hypothesis is that the movement from that start is small, is not arranged by a Primary's physical behaviour, and is not needed by the network, because a random fixed label is enough to separate 16 Primaries. partly supported not-for-production The lag-free demand network's Primary embedding is not just a random label: pretraining moves each vector about half its length, and scoring a Primary with its own random start vector instead of its trained vector raises the error by 0.8 to 1.3 points of capacity, so the movement is used. The movement is only weakly arranged by physical behaviour (winter daily shape, through the size of the movement and not its direction) and does not tell signed-MW from MVA-only Primaries, so giving the network per-Primary descriptors, and not only a learned label, is worth testing next. also relevant
    00517 lagless-demand-network-lockdown-flag-removal The flag is 0 in every scored month and in every fine-tune row, so it can only matter through the pretraining rows from 2019-09, which hold the 2020-03-23 to 2021-07-19 lockdown. A lag-free network pretrained on those rows without the flag may fit them worse, or may be pulled off its normal behaviour by them. Dropping the lockdown rows may then help the network more than keeping the flag. inconclusive candidate No arm is a clear replacement for the lockdown flag, and the experiment cannot rank the arms. Removing the flag while keeping the lockdown rows scored 0.029 points worse at the six signed-MW Primaries, and dropping the lockdown rows as well scored 0.033 points better than keeping the flag at the eight MVA-only Primaries, but leaving out one Primary at a time removes the first difference and weakens the second. Every difference is below the minimum detectable effect of 0.12 to 0.13 points. primary
    00516 lagless-demand-network-learned-weather-window-cnn A lag-free demand network sees weather at the valid time only, yet buildings warm up and cool down slowly and sunshine through windows offsets heating on a cold day. A small convolutional encoder over the 24 hours of ensemble-mean weather around the valid time can learn which part of that history matters, so it improves the lag-free network, and it improves it by more than the hand-built trailing windows of experiment 515 (the second PR of the maintainer's 2026-11-02 weather-window brief; the hand-built arms are experiment 515's). not supported not-for-production A small convolutional encoder over 24 hours of ensemble-mean weather did not improve the lag-free demand network: on all rows both encoders scored 0.07 to 0.13 CRPS points worse than the base, with every interval including zero. The same encoder reading shuffled-day windows scored numerically better than the one reading real windows, but that difference is also unresolved. primary
    00515 lagless-demand-network-trailing-weather-window-features A demand network that reads no lagged power sees only the weather at the valid time, but a building warms and cools slowly, heating is switched on late, and sunshine through windows offsets heating on a cold day. Trailing means of temperature and shortwave over the previous 6 to 72 hours, or the 24-hour product of the two, therefore let the lag-free network forecast cold winter nights and cold sunny days better. Earlier neural tests of trailing weather (experiments 419, 437, 441, 442) all fed the network three origin lags of power, which may have carried the same information, so their nulls may not transfer. inconclusive not-for-production Trailing temperature windows lowered cold-night CRPS at the eight MVA-only Primaries by 0.150 points in 10 of 10 folds, but a shuffled-day placebo lowered it by 0.071, and nothing resolved at the six signed-MW Primaries or on all rows, so the effect is not resolved. primary
    00513 lagless-demand-network-whole-day-price-shape-at-training-time The Market Index price of a whole local day, known exactly at training time, carries information about price-following embedded batteries and gas engines behind a Primary, so a lag-free demand network that sees it in training forecasts those Primaries better. At serving time no price exists, so this experiment first asks the upper-bound question: does the true shape, given at both training and scoring time, help at all? not supported not-for-production Even a perfect, hindsight price shape for the whole day does not lower the lag-free demand network's error at NGED's Primaries by a measurable amount, and the oracle gains no more at the Primaries with a known battery or gas engine. The oracle gain is the bound for a training-time-only price arm (price dropout, an auxiliary price head, or a teacher network), and that bound is not resolvable from zero, so those arms were not run. An option for batteries and price-following generators is to model their output as a separate target or a subtracted term; whether to test it belongs to the roadmap. primary
    00504 neural-demand-leak-free-recent-cams-irradiance-inputs Giving the pooled demand neural network (experiment 425's arm A, which already reads the ensemble forecast of shortwave radiation) three extra inputs built only from Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service (CAMS) satellite irradiance that a live service would have at the forecast origin lowers its continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) at days 1 to 10 at the eight Primaries that report only apparent power in megavolt-amperes (MVA): D2, D3, D4, D8, D12, D19, D21, and D22. The reason is that recent observed sunshine says how much embedded solar generation hides in those readings, which the ensemble forecast does not. not supported not-for-production Satellite sunshine measurements that a live service could have at forecast time (whole days at least 36 hours old) did not help the demand neural network in this fine-tuning design: its error rose by 0.1% at the eight Primaries that report only apparent power. The same satellite columns read at the forecast's own half-hour would cut the error by 6.8%, so the improvement comes from knowing the sunshine of the target half-hour, not from older sunshine, and better sunshine forecasts or a nowcast are the next place to look. The experiment did not test a fresher feed (the previous day). primary
    00496 simplify-demand-network-input-and-output-pruning Four inputs or outputs of the demand neural network can be removed one at a time, each within the equivalence limit: (a) the seven ensemble-spread inputs (the mean forecast stays), (c) the four holiday columns that describe the Christmas period and bank holidays, merged into one day-type column, (d) the three inputs that describe the weekly lag (its value, its missing flag, and its age), and (b) the second and third Gaussian of the three-Gaussian output. Serving then needs fewer inputs and a smaller output layer. partly supported candidate The demand neural network can drop its 7 ensemble-spread inputs (46 inputs down to 39) with no loss on the scored whole sample: the CRPS change was +0.012 points at the 8 MVA-only Primaries and -0.043 at the 6 signed-MW Primaries, both inside the limit, although it scored 0.187 points worse at the six on cold winter nights (3 folds, almost all from December) and 0.408 worse at the six on rows with a labelled event. The other three simplifications were not shown equivalent: merging the holiday columns (at both groups), dropping the weekly lag (at the eight), and using one Gaussian instead of three (at the eight). also relevant
    00480 xgboost-cams-columns-at-mva-only-primaries-lab-conditions Giving the monthly XGBoost demand model two extra input columns per half-hour, the unit photovoltaic (PV) output shape and the clear-sky index, both computed from realised Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service (CAMS) irradiance, lowers the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) at days 1 to 10 at the eight MVA-only Primaries (D2, D3, D4, D8, D12, D19, D21, D22), because embedded solar generation hides inside their apparent-power readings and the model has no input that says how much of it a half-hour had. supported candidate Giving the XGBoost demand model the satellite-measured sunshine of each half-hour lowered its error at all 16 primary substations in the point estimates, by 0.36 points of capacity over the eight that report apparent power and by 0.48 over the six that report real power. A live service cannot have this input for the first day ahead, because the satellite data arrives about a day late, so the gain is an upper bound. The next step is to measure how much of the gain remains when the columns come from the ensemble weather forecast. primary
    00442 thermal-lag-cold-night-test-on-six-winter-folds The maintainer expects thermal lags to help mostly on difficult winter rows, with no harm elsewhere. Experiment 441 (rescoring experiment 437) found that bundle T gains at least 0.05 CRPS points on cold winter nights at both metering groups, but its intervals include zero, not every fold was negative, and the slice's minimum detectable effect (0.12 to 0.26) is above 0.05. Adding winter months to the folds lets the cold-night slice resolve a gain of that size. not supported not-for-production Thermal-lag columns are not supported as a help on cold winter nights on these six monthly folds, which is not the same as no effect.
