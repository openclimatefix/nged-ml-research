    Load-shift labels in the forecast: state and next steps

    Telling the Primary demand forecast about load shifts has not improved it on real data, and the final demand models use no label table or detector. On injected load shifts, labels recover most of the lost accuracy only when the megawatts (MW) moved are known exactly. The final models page describes how the final models cope without labels. The theme page holds the full results, and the state of the event detector records where the detector work paused.

    A load shift is a transfer of demand between neighbouring Primary substations for hours or days. The normal running arrangement (NRA) is the electricity network configuration without any load shift. A lag is an earlier power reading used as an input. RO-NC is the refit-offset neighbour-count detector, which flags a load shift from a 24-hour step in a Primary's forecast error (see the detector page). The gap is the extra error of a forecast on lags that a shift has changed, against the same forecast on unchanged lags.

    Scores are quantile scores, which are twice the mean pinball loss over 11 quantile levels. They are in points of effective capacity (1% of a substation's capacity), and a positive change is a worse forecast. NGED's event labels are incomplete, so every count of real events is a lower bound. Unless a paragraph says otherwise, results come from 16 Primary series and 292 daily forecast runs. Verdict words in backticks, such as inconclusive, follow the glossary's definitions.

    What we know

    On real data, no label-based change lowered the score by an amount the data can show. Dropping labelled load-shift half-hours from the training targets changed the score by +0.004 (98.33% interval -0.026 to +0.025), and only 22 of 82 labelled events fall in the scored year (Experiment 75, inconclusive).

    A perfect detector could lower the quantile score by little in the year tested. In the band of events that the event labeller (a script that marks load-shift events from NGED's logs and the power series) finds most reliably, a perfect detector would lower the quantile score by 0.42% (95% interval -0.47% to 1.96%), against a 3% threshold. These events touch 3 of the 16 series. Over the 22 events visible in the power data, the bound is 2.35% (-0.22% to 5.73%), an interval that includes zero and straddles the threshold (Experiment 44, refuted).

    Only the true MW recovered most of the gap on injected load shifts. With the true MW and a forecast trained on NGED's logged shifts, 83% (70% to 91%) of the gap closes. With the MW from RO-NC as built, 16% (-17% to 67%) closes, which cannot be told from zero (Experiment 88, inconclusive). These shares rest on 2,452 forecast origins inside injected transfers (4 onset months, 13% of origins).

    Taking one MW per half-hour from the largest overlapping step recovered more, but the choice was made after seeing the results. The loss comes from overlapping detector intervals that add up (21% of marked cases). The lag correction alone closed 48% (18% to 71%) of the gap, and the RO-NC-trained booster that also drops those half-hours from its training targets closed 43% (17% to 71%) (Experiment 88).

    A calibrated probability of a lag inside a load shift did not beat the detector's yes-or-no flag. On injected transfers, the probability lowered the Brier score (the mean squared error of a probability forecast) by 3.3% (97.5% interval -7.6% to +1.6%), against a 20% requirement. Its area under the curve against NGED's logs (where 0.5 is chance) was 0.53, so the experiment advises against a band-widening rule built on it (Experiment 91, inconclusive).

    Feeding the detector's alarm into the GAM-K Kalman filter did not help. GAM-K is the generalised additive demand model whose Kalman filter corrects each forecast with its recent errors. The alarm helped inside a transfer and hurt for 72 hours after it ended. The alarm came 48 hours after the step, which was no better than a random placebo (Experiment 141, not supported).

    After the alarm, subtracting the detected MW closes much of the gap, but the weather alone closes more. Subtracting closed 71% (25% to 85%) of the gap to a forecast fed the true NRA series, and the weather model alone closed 78%. Counting missed transfers, subtracting gained nothing and masking gained 21% (Experiment 151, partly supported).

    A second Kalman filter state for the transfer added nothing over masking. It closed 78% of the gap and masking the readings inside the alarm closed 81% (Experiment 153, partly supported).

    What does not work

    Hiding lags that lie inside a load shift made the forecast worse. Setting them to missing worsened the score by 0.409 (+0.281 to +0.517) on the 352,765 rows with a lag inside an abnormal running arrangement (the union of RO-NC and the logs). (Experiment 89, refuted).

    Giving the forecast the unusualness of each lag worsened the score by 0.355 (+0.209 to +0.513) (Experiment 90, refuted).

    Three times as many synthetic shifts in training did not help. The score changed by +0.079 (-0.031 to +0.153) (Experiment 96, inconclusive).

    Random lag dropout in training did not widen the band enough. The 5% to 95% band covered 0.799 on rows with a missing lag for the plain forecast and 0.836 with 30% of training rows hidden (the best arm), against 0.90 nominal. The band width did not change (Experiment 100, inconclusive).

    Every label experiment on this theme used hindsight labels (labels known only after the event), so nothing here is ready for the serving path.

    What the final models do instead

    The final demand models for Primaries contain no label table and no detector input. They use three substitutes.

    • Injected shifts in training. The lag encoder, the part of the neural network that reads the lagged power, trains on random synthetic shifts covering 5% of each Primary's rows.
    • A refit embargo. The newest 7 days of training rows are held back and the 7 days before them are down-weighted by half. This cut the share of a shift leaked into the median forecast 7 days after it ends from 0.16 to 0.114 (Experiment 599, partly supported).
    • A prevailing output. A second output mixes the NRA quantiles with a shifted copy, and extra lagged inputs make it react to a new shift 20.4 hours sooner on injected events, below the 24 hours set in advance. Which extra-lag set to port is open, because the 8-input set scored worst inside the 2 real events that can be read (Experiment 681, small gain).

    The embargo's leak of 0.114 is above the proposed 0.10 bar, and the six MW Primaries lose 1.3 points of q05 to q95 coverage (Experiment 599).

    Detector features for the prevailing output were not adopted. The features reacted 1.59 days sooner than the earlier head, but extra lagged power alone reacted 1.25 days sooner (Experiment 687, partly supported). Its reaction times are not comparable with Experiment 681's, which used different events and folds.

    The prevailing output is better overall, and December 2025 is the exception. It scored 0.17 points better than the NRA output for origins in 2026-01 to 2026-06 and 0.42 better in 2026-07 to 2026-10 (Experiment 668). After the one verified shift, at D5 and D16 in December 2025, it scored worse than the NRA output for 7 days after the shift ended. December 2025 is a fallback month (B-haz fallback head).

    Open questions

    • Whether the detector's MW, with each shift counted once and ended promptly, recovers more than the 43% of the gap is unknown. Only a post hoc rule has been scored.
    • Whether any label improves the forecast on real shifts is unknown. The logged events in one year are too few (Experiment 100).

    Next-experiment options

    Each option below comes from the "What to try next" section of the experiment named. The final models come first and load-shift event detection is paused until January, so options that need a better detector wait for it.

    1. An option is to re-run Experiment 88 with the de-duplicated MW. The rerun would keep the largest of overlapping same-sign intervals and let the MW decay after the end (from Experiment 88). It depends on the detector work.
    2. An option is to replace a masked lag with a forecast of the NRA (from Experiment 89).
    3. An option is to train synthetic shifts with the detector's errors (from Experiment 96).
    4. An option is to widen the band where a lag is missing, with an indicator column (from Experiment 100).
    5. An option is a Kalman-filter correction that decays over the typical transfer duration (from Experiment 141).
    6. An option is a detector that alarms sooner, paired with a forecast that drops recent power while a transfer is suspected (from Experiment 151).
    7. An option is a gated embargo that starts only when a gate fires (from Experiment 599).

    What is parked

    Hindsight-label results are parked until a causal detector is better, which is January work. The detector state page records why.

    Experiment 116 (differentiable physics, edge flows between Primaries) is inconclusive. Its known-answer gate failed: the fit cannot see transfers shorter than 7 to 9 days, and it found 0.22 of the injected MW over 1 day. The network research plan holds its follow-ups, a drift term and a topology check.
