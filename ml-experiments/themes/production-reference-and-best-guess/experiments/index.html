    Production reference and best guess: experiments

    This page lists every experiment tried in the theme, newest first. An experiment that belongs mainly to another theme but bears on this one is marked "also relevant".

    00494 simplify-demand-network-refit-cadence-and-pretrain-once A pooled demand network that was refitted 3 months before the scored month forecasts as well as a freshly refitted network, and so does a network fine-tuned from one pretraining run that stops before the first scored month. Staler networks (6 months) lose a little more. If so, production needs one pretraining run, fine-tunes at most every 3 months, and no ERA5-and-CAMS replay at refit. partly supported candidate In five scored months, the demand neural network pretrained once and fine-tuned each month scored within 0.03 points of the same network pretrained at every refit, and would drop about 42 of the 80 GPU seconds that refitting one network takes. A network refitted 3 months earlier scored about 0.07 points worse at the eight MVA-only Primaries than a freshly refitted one, with an interval across months too wide to say how much worse. Whether to refit less often than monthly is therefore open until a larger test. also relevant
    00493 solar-annual-against-quarterly-refits Refitting the pooled solar network once a year, on data to the end of August 2025, scores within 1% of refitting it every quarter, for the one-pass network and for the 51-member encoder, so a production solar forecast needs one refit a year instead of four. supported candidate The solar neural network can be refitted once a year instead of every quarter: for both networks we tested, the quantile score changes by less than 0.6%, inside the 1% limit, with little margin for the encoder and from one refit date and one scored year. One further comparison fails: at Solar E the one-pass network with annual refits is 1.3% worse than the 51-member encoder with quarterly refits, and most of that gap comes from the network design and not from the schedule. also relevant
    00491 weight-averaging-in-demand-network-fine-tuning Experiment 481 found that averaging the quantiles of several networks that differ only in fit seed lowers the score at the signed-MW six, and that the first-release demand forecaster serves five such networks. Averaging the weights of one network along one training trajectory costs one network at serving time, and may recover a useful share of the gain from seed averaging. inconclusive not-for-production One neural network trained with a moving average of its weights did not match a pool of seeds. The averaged network scores 0.002 to 0.005 CRPS points better than a single network at the eight (the seed-noise minimum detectable effect is 0.083), while a pool of three averaged networks from different seeds gains 0.065 to 0.071. In this experiment, the pools that gained came from networks that differ in their seed. The gap between one network and five is 0.082 points at the eight. The plan needed a gap above 0.15 points before a closure share could settle the question, so the verdict stays inconclusive. primary
    00490 one-lag-robust-network-distilled-from-the-lagged-plus-lagless-mixture A single lagged network (A0-inj's architecture: the Primary's measured power at three earlier times, no lagless input) trained on injected load-shift worlds, with the forecast of experiment 454's K2-S1 mixture as an extra training target, forecasts as well as that mixture, so the live service could run one network and store no lagless table, mixture step, or store of past lagless forecasts. not supported not-for-production Training one neural network to imitate the four-network mixture K2-S1 did not remove the need for the lagless forecast. On rows with no labelled event the imitation network scores 0.28 points of capacity worse than B-mix (a 50/50 mixture of a lagged network and a lagless forecast) at the 6 Primaries metered in signed megawatts, beyond the minimum detectable effect, and 0.15 worse at the 8 Primaries metered in megavolt-amperes, where the interval just reaches zero. It recovers only a small part of the gap to K2-S1, so the lagless forecast probably carries information that the 3 raw power readings do not. Keep the lagless forecast in the live service. also relevant
    00489 distill-the-five-network-pool-into-one-network The pool of five pretrained-then-fine-tuned networks (A-pool, experiment 425's arm A) can be replaced at serving by one network of the same shape, fine-tuned to match the pool's forecast distribution, without losing forecast skill beyond the seed noise. This moves the seed averaging from the serving path into the training regime. not supported not-for-production One network trained to copy the five-network pool forecasts no better than a single network trained normally, so the pool's gain cannot be moved into training with this recipe. The student matches a single teacher network's tail coverage but not the pool's accuracy, probably because the teacher is in sample on every row it teaches from. Serving the pool of 5 networks stays the way to get that gain. primary
    00487 era5-and-cams-replay-rows-during-demand-network-fine-tuning Fine-tuning experiment 425's arm A on the ensemble (ENS) rows alone may make the network forget what its ERA5-and-CAMS pretraining taught it about rare cold winter nights, which the ENS history (from 2024-04) holds few of. Replaying ERA5-and-CAMS rows during fine-tuning, especially cold-night rows, lowers the CRPS on cold winter nights. inconclusive not-for-production Mixing ERA5-and-CAMS rows back into the fine-tuning batches does not help the cold winter nights at the eight MVA-only Primaries, and replaying only cold nights makes the eight slightly worse. At the signed-MW six, plain replay lowers CRPS on rows with no labelled event by about 2 to 2.5%, a gain below the seed-noise minimum detectable effect that halves without the 2026-01 fold but keeps its sign. Cold nights stay badly under-covered at the six whatever the replay. also relevant
    00485 christmas-period-demand-forecast-regime-arms Experiment 425's arm A (A-pool) already has Christmas flags and day-of-week inputs, yet its lags on 26 to 31 December point at holiday days, and its Christmas weeks are seen at ordinary weight by pretraining and fine-tuning. Treating 22 December to 2 January as a regime (a day-type input, a lag taken from the latest same-type day outside the window, a Christmas-row weight with an ERA5 replay, or a Christmas-weighted pretraining) lowers CRPS on the Christmas days without harming other days. not supported not-for-production None of the five ways of treating 22 December to 2 January as its own regime lowered Christmas CRPS by more than one Christmas can detect. The three that moved the Christmas days at all also made the other days worse at the eight MVA-only Primaries. The next check is Christmas 2026. primary
    00484 hard-row-weighting-in-demand-network-fine-tuning Experiments 435 and 470 left cold nights worse than the tail target, and avoidable curtailment cost ranks the candidates differently from CRPS. Fine-tuning the pretrained network with larger weights on the rows that decide NGED's costs lowers the error on those rows without raising it elsewhere. not supported not-for-production Giving larger fine-tuning weights to cold nights, to high-flow half-hours at the six signed-MW Primaries, or to winter did not improve the forecasts that matter to NGED. Cold-night error at the eight fell by 0.068 points under W-cold, which is inside the noise floor of 0.17, and avoidable-curtailment cost did not change. Weighting winter (W-season) raised CRPS at the eight. One weight value (3) and one pretrained neural network were tested, so the result suggests the hard rows are limited by the inputs rather than the weighting. The next step to try is a different input or neural-network architecture. primary
    00482 best-models-performance-analysis-injected-shifts-and-wind-references A forecast that reads lagged power follows an injected load shift that no neighbour mirrors more than a forecast that reads no lagged power does, the forecast that mixes lagged and lagless heads (K2-S1) limits the harm of an unmirrored shift, and the pooled plain network (A-pool), which never saw an injected shift in training, follows the shift most. partly supported not-for-production A forecast that mixes lagged and lagless heads (K2-S1) was in none of the eight scenarios meaningfully worse than the lagless forecast when we injected load shifts into its lags, and it was a little better in three. The pooled neural network that was never trained on shifted lags (A-pool) is the one that suffers: it is up to 2.45 points of capacity worse, and its 95% quantile covers only 72% of actuals in the worst scenario. At the wind farms, member_plain is level with research default 2 and 0.4 points better than research default 1. primary
    00481 seed-averaging-diminishing-returns-for-demand-and-wind Averaging the quantile forecasts of K networks that differ only in fit seed lowers the score with diminishing returns. For Primary demand (experiment 425's arm A) and for the wind farms (experiment 429's member_plain), the gain from K = 5 to K = 10 is inside the minimum detectable effect, and for the lagged S1 network of experiment 454 the gain from K = 3 to K = 6 is. partly supported candidate A second seed gives the largest gain and later seeds add little: for Primary demand, two seeds score 0.035 points better than one (the 99.55% interval is +0.025 to +0.053, across the 0.03 bar for porting), and each later step gains at most 0.011. For wind, one seed scores within the minimum detectable effect of ten, though a second seed gains 0.041 points. Averaging seeds of the lagged neural network S1 is weaker than adding the lagless head: at two networks, S1 with the lagless head scores 3.441 against 3.628 for two averaged S1 seeds. Each added seed costs another fit per monthly refit. primary
    00478 load-shift-unpadded-curtailment-cost-and-after-event-slices In the 7 days after an unpadded load-shift event, the weekly bundle of experiment 381 has a higher avoidable-curtailment cost than the plain weekly member of experiment 383 at the signed-MW six (the six Primary substations that report signed megawatts), and a power lag inside the event raises the error of targets outside every event for that whole week. not supported not-for-production A power lag that falls inside a load-shift event keeps hurting the forecast for at least a week after the event ends, and the harm is largest in the first day. Experiment 391's surprise inputs did not reduce it. Avoidable-curtailment costs during events rest on 2 to 11 events per Primary and one Primary (D16, 4 events) carries most of the pounds, so no cost difference during events is established; across all rows the GAM stack costs far less than the weekly XGBoost forecasts at D14 and D16. also relevant
    00475 recipe-on-bulk-and-grid-supply-points-against-pooled-plain-network The first-release candidate recipe (experiment 425's arm A: ERA5-and-CAMS pretraining, the Primary-and-node embedding, hand-made calendar columns, the D15 and D18 cut, and flagged exact-zero half-hours dropped), fitted pooled with the 16 Primaries and a node-type input, scores lower CRPS at days 1 to 10 than experiment 415's N1 at both bulk supply points and does not lose at the grid supply points. not supported not-for-production The assembled recipe (ERA5-and-CAMS pretraining, cuts, flagged zeros dropped) does not improve on the plain pooled network at the two bulk supply points: it is better at D17 (-0.14) and worse at D13 (+0.09), both inside the noise. At the two grid supply points the recipe is about 0.5 points of capacity better than the plain network, which is close to the size of the refit noise (about 0.4). Against BG2 the recipe is better at all four nodes, with the largest gain at the grid supply points. also relevant
    00471 manual-heuristic-calibration-and-analogue-upgrades If the 13 (and later 12) equally weighted historical analogues of the manual heuristic are recalibrated with a correction fitted on a trailing window of past errors, the quantile score falls against the faithful heuristic, because the heuristic's empirical 95th percentile of 13 members is exceeded about 11.4% of the time even when the analogues are perfect. The heuristic also carries a level bias. not supported not-for-production A simple recalibration of the 13 historical analogues behind NGED's manual heuristic did not improve its forecasts over the five months we scored. Fitting a calibration on the last 28 or 56 days of errors, or shifting the analogues by their recent error, left the average error score unchanged or worse at the eight MVA-only substations and the six signed-MW substations. The Gaussian fit lowered the score on cold winter nights (0.90 points at the eight) but raised it at the six in Christmas week, so the faithful heuristic stays the benchmark that the neural-network and XGBoost models are compared against. primary
    00470 first-release-demand-forecaster-assembly-and-comparison Two pieces of evidence point to different first-release forecasters. Experiment 425's arm A (one network pretrained on ERA5 and CAMS weather, five seeds averaged) has the lowest CRPS at the eight MVA-only Primaries. Experiment 454's K2-S1 (two lagged networks mixed with two lagless networks) has the lowest CRPS on rows with no labelled event at the signed-MW six. Scored on one set of rows, the simplest candidate within the stated tolerance of the best is a pretrained network without a lagless head at the eight, and the lagged-plus-lagless mixture at the six. inconclusive candidate Pretraining the neural network on past weather and solar data (ERA5 and CAMS) gets the first-release forecaster almost as far as adding a second, lagless forecast does. The pretrained network also holds up better on cold winter nights and 12 to 36 hours ahead. K2-425A scores 0.07 to 0.10 points of capacity better on ordinary rows, but that gap is within the noise of five months, so the lagged-plus-lagless design adds nothing the data can confirm over pretraining. The first release therefore rests on a choice between the plain five-seed pretrained network (simplest, no lagless forecast, no GAM) and the same network with the GAM forecast as an extra input (0.9% better at the eight, inside the noise, and needs the GAM at serving time). Every score here is selection-biased, because the five months that choose the candidate also score it. primary
    00468 best-models-performance-analysis-with-time-series-graphs The best forecasters' failures are concentrated in a few identifiable situations (a stormy week for wind, a cloudy week for solar, and the first days of a load shift that the lagged power does not reflect), and a set of time-series graphs makes those situations visible. partly supported not-for-production The wind and solar forecasts keep their tails close to nominal over a year, but single months miss by a factor of two or three, so a service that relies on one month's tails should widen its bands. In seven real demand events, the forecast that reads lagged power (S1) was less accurate than the forecast that does not in three, and the mixture K2-S1 was more accurate in six, so lagged power helps in some events and hurts in others. The injected-shift scenarios and the wind reference contrasts are not done yet. primary
    00457 gam-stage-1-forecast-as-an-input-to-the-pooled-mixture-density-network If the pooled mixture density network of experiment 425's arm A is given the out-of-fold stage-1 GAM forecast of Primary demand, either as one extra input feature (A-gam-in) or as a zero-initialised additive base that the network corrects (A-gam-base), its CRPS at days 1 to 10 is lower than arm A's on the eight MVA-only Primaries, because the GAM carries the smooth calendar, temperature, and solar shapes that the network learns less well from five years of data. small gain candidate Giving the pretrained neural network the GAM's forecast as one extra input improves the forecast a little: CRPS falls by 0.036 points at the eight MVA-only Primaries, and the pooled cold-night and tail scores do not get worse. The gain is as large as the fit-to-fit noise, so it is real but marginal. At the signed-MW six the gain (0.035) is half that group's minimum detectable effect (0.070). The input needs the GAM in the live service with its monthly refit. Using the GAM as the base that the neural network corrects made forecasts worse (+0.291), because the GAM starts from a worse forecast than the pretrained neural network and fine-tuning does not recover. primary
    00452 pretraining-cap-and-surprise-inputs-on-the-425-arm-a-recipe Experiment 449's audit found that experiment 425's arm A (a pretrained pooled mixture density network without surprise inputs) beats PT1-pool5 (the same network with the surprise inputs), but the two used different fit loops and arm A averages five seeds' quantiles, and all 55 of 449's pretraining runs stopped at the 60-epoch cap. If pretraining was too short, then raising the cap to 150 epochs improves the network with the surprise inputs (A-S1-long) and the network without them (A-long), and the surprise inputs add to the longer pretraining. not supported not-for-production Pretraining for longer did not help: 150 epochs against 60 moved CRPS by less than the fit-to-fit noise at the eight and the six, and the surprise inputs added 0.042 points only at the signed-MW six, again below the noise. Experiment 425's arm A (no surprise inputs, five-seed quantile average) stays the pretrained demand candidate on this evidence. The comparison with arm A is confounded with the fit loop (experiment 397's against 428's). primary
    00449 pretraining-with-lags-and-surprise-inputs-on-the-s1-network Pretraining on ERA5 and CAMS weather did not help the S1 network in experiment 448 because the pretraining stage hid the lag and surprise inputs, so the lag pathway was learned only from the shorter ensemble history. If the pretraining stage instead sees the three origin lags and the surprise at each lag (the surprise computed from a cross-fitted lagless forecast on ERA5 and CAMS weather), the fine-tuned S1 network scores lower days 1 to 10 CRPS than S1. partly supported not-for-production Pretraining the S1 network on past-weather estimates helps once the pretraining rows carry real recent-power inputs: the S1 neural network pretrained this way scores 0.08 points better than S1 at the signed-MW six and 0.07 at the MVA-only eight, and 0.13 and 0.11 better than the earlier masked pretraining. Most of the gain at the six comes from one test month, and experiment 425's simpler recipe (no surprise inputs, five seeds averaged) is ahead of the pooled network at the hardest substations (D15 and D18) and over all 16 Primaries. BG2 is still ahead at D15 and D18 and on cold winter nights at the six. All numbers are interim, from four test months. primary
    00448 s1-network-assembly-ladder-with-pretraining-b-mix-and-seed-pool The S1 network (pooled plain mixture density network, Primary embedding, ensemble mean and spread once, hand-made calendar columns, monthly refits, three origin lags plus the surprise at each lag) improves with each of three components added one at a time (ERA5-and-CAMS pretraining then ensemble fine-tuning; the fixed 50/50 B-mix of the S1 and lagless output distributions; a five-seed pool), and the network with pretraining and the pool together scores lower CRPS at days 1 to 10 than every one of its parts and than the monthly XGBoost recipe. partly supported not-for-production Averaging five random-seed fits of the S1 neural network lowered its error by about 0.05 points at both groups of Primary substations, and no other add-on helped. Pretraining on past-weather estimates with the recent-power inputs masked, and the fixed 50/50 mix with the lagless neural network, each raised the error slightly at the six and eight series and much more at D15 and D18. The assembly of pretraining and the pool was worse than the pool alone (+0.059 at the six and +0.047 at the eight). The pool's gain of 0.048 at the six is below the minimum detectable effect of 0.057. On four test months the five-seed average is level with BG2 and ahead of the monthly XGBoost recipe at the six and eight. With a fifth month BG2 is ahead at the signed-MW six, so all numbers are interim and the network is not a release candidate. primary
    00447 demand-network-hyperparameter-sweep-on-the-s1-skeleton S1 (the pooled plain mixture-density network of experiment 397's setting g09, given the three origin lags and the surprise at each lag) sits near a flat optimum, so changing one of its hidden width, depth, Primary-embedding width, number of mixture components, dropout, weight decay, learning rate, learning-rate schedule, batch size, or optimiser lowers days 1 to 10 CRPS by less than the margin. not supported not-for-production Changing one setting of the S1 demand neural network at a time does not improve it by the margin we required (0.064 CRPS points): the network is insensitive to its hidden width, depth, Primary embedding, mixture components, dropout, weight decay, learning rate and schedule, batch size, and optimiser at this resolution. Of the 22 arms, only a hidden width of 256 shows a gain (0.047 on the four folds with stored S1 forecasts, 0.250 on one new fold), and it makes real load-shift events and the upper tail worse. Keep S1 as it is; combinations of settings were not tested. also relevant
    00443 solar-network-hyperparameter-and-architecture-sweep The plain pooled mixture-density network for the six metered solar farms (experiment 430's g0_onepass: two hidden layers of 64, three Gaussians, no farm embedding, learning rate 2e-3, weight decay 1e-4, 20 epochs, no dropout, equal row weights) sits near a flat optimum, so no single change of its hidden width, depth, farm-embedding width, learning rate, weight decay, number of epochs, number of mixture components, dropout, or row-recency weight lowers the quantile score by the margin. not supported not-for-production None of 25 one-at-a-time changes to the solar neural network (width, depth, farm embedding, learning rate, weight decay, epochs, number of mixture components, dropout, recency weighting) improves its forecast by 0.5%. The network stops improving after 2 or 3 training epochs, so epochs, learning rate, and weight decay have almost no effect. Combinations of settings were not tested, and the next step to try is better inputs, such as a better irradiance product. also relevant
    00441 difficult-row-rescore-of-the-neural-demand-input-bundles The annual mean score of experiment 437 hides a gain that is concentrated on difficult rows. The maintainer expects thermal-lag columns (bundle T) to help mostly on cold winter nights, solar-gain columns (bundle S) to help on cold sunny days, look-ahead columns (bundle F) to help on cold winter nights and heat waves, and calendar and tariff columns (bundle C) to help at Christmas and on bank holidays. NGED needs good forecasts on exactly those rows, with no harm on the others. inconclusive not-for-production The input bundles did not help on the difficult rows either, but the four test months are too few to say they do nothing: on cold winter nights the smallest gain these data could reliably show is 0.12 to 0.23 points of capacity, several times the 0.05 bar. Thermal lag (bundle T) was the only arm that looked better on cold winter nights at both groups of Primaries, and its signed-MW gain came from two Primaries. The larger finding is that the signed-MW Primaries' 95% and 99% upper quantiles cover only 76% and 83% of cold winter night half-hours, which is a larger shortfall than any bundle's gain. also relevant
    00437 neural-demand-thermal-lag-solar-gain-and-tariff-feature-bundles The pooled plain mixture density network of experiment 425 sees one valid time per row, so it cannot see the hours before or after that time. Adding columns that summarise those hours (bundle T, thermal lag; bundle S, solar gain; bundle F, look-ahead) or that carry the calendar, tariff, and national signals (bundle C) lowers its continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) at days 1 to 10, because the network trains across all 16 Primaries (and, in the pretrained form, across 6 years of ERA5 and CAMS history) and may find signal that the per-Primary XGBoost model and the GAM stack missed. not supported not-for-production Adding thermal-lag, solar-gain, look-ahead, calendar, tariff, or national-signal columns to the pooled plain mixture density network did not lower its continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) by the registered 0.03 points of capacity, in either the ensemble-only or the pretrained form. Seven of the eight arms were worse than their control at the six signed-MW Primaries, by 0.016 to 0.059 points. The thermal-lag bundle lowered the CRPS at the eight MVA-only Primaries (by 0.022 and 0.029) in only 2 of 4 folds, which is too weak a result to act on. The screen could not detect effects below 0.045 to 0.064 points, so the bundles are left out, not ruled out. also relevant
    00434 demand-ensemble-copula-coupling-and-schaake-reordering-of-stored-forecasts The stored forecast of experiment 425's arm A holds 11 quantiles per Primary and half-hour. Drawing 51 values from those quantiles and assigning them to the 51 ensemble members by the rank of each member's 2-metre temperature (ensemble copula coupling, ECC) gives sample paths that match the observed dependence across Primaries and leads better than independent draws do, by more than the 5% coherence margin on the variogram score, without changing the marginal CRPS. A Schaake shuffle from past observed paths does at least as well as ECC. not supported not-for-production Re-ordering the stored demand forecast's 51 values by member temperature or by past observed paths does not give clearly better sample paths overall. Ordering by temperature made the paths about 7% less coherent across the 16 Primaries and 1.7% more coherent across leads at days 1 to 10 (13% at 12 to 36 hours, an interim band), and the effects cancel in the combined score. A Schaake shuffle from past paths was no worse than independent draws across Primaries and 3.5% better across leads at days 1 to 10 (interval -6.3% to -0.9%, below the 5% margin), so it is the stronger reference candidate if a step 2 runs the 51 members through the neural network. primary
    00430 unified-forecaster-solar-and-pretraining-ablations A pooled mixture-density network for the six metered solar farms needs, beyond experiment 345's solar-only pooled network (the demand-style default: one pass on the ensemble mean and spread, no physics, no pretraining, a clipped Gaussian mixture), only those of four switches that an ablation shows to help: the 51 ensemble members passed through a shared encoder one by one (A2), a PyTorch fleet plant that adds a power channel (A5), ERA5 and CAMS pretraining followed by fine-tuning on the ensemble (A6), and point masses at zero and at capacity in the output (A7). refuted not-for-production One pass of a neural network on the ensemble mean and spread is enough for the six solar farms: none of the four richer designs we tested beats it by the 1% margin. For the 51 coherent member paths that NGED's power-flow modelling needs, reorder the one-pass network's 51 quantiles with a Schaake shuffle, which scores 13% better than ensemble copula coupling on the variogram score of the paths and the same on the energy score. Passing each member through the network alone gives paths that score 11% worse on the energy score and a marginal quantile score 1.5% worse. also relevant
    00428 day-window-convolution-attention-encoder-replacing-lagged-power-inputs A pooled mixture density network that has no hand-made lagged power input and instead reads the raw half-hourly power of whole recent days through a one-dimensional convolutional network and an attention layer (whose query is the half-hour being forecast) forecasts as well as the pooled plain network with the three origin lags on rows outside load-shift events, forecasts better on harm rows (rows whose target is outside every event but whose recent days are inside one), and follows a step added to the recent days by a much smaller share of the step, with wider bands. not supported not-for-production A neural network that reads whole days of raw power (E3) did not stop the forecast following load-shift events and scored worse than the lagged network (+0.050 on clean rows).
