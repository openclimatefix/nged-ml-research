    Gross-demand simulation: research plan

    No experiment in this theme is planned, and the options below are queued for the maintainer to choose. The project is not starting further experiments unprompted. The reasoning is on the asset-inventory simulator page. An advisor's analysis of how to propose the simulator to NGED, with phases as options, is on Options for proposing the simulator to NGED. The latest finished experiment, experiment 697 (running patterns of the D8 gas engine), found that the tracker's labels of D8's gas engine show a higher running share on weekday evenings than on weekends (+0.392 in the net era and +0.256 in the solar era). Six null controls, each a real reading with a patternless engine injected, produce a smaller difference. The running share also rises with the half-hourly system price. The evidence comes from the tracker and not from a meter on the engine, so option 3 below could test both findings against the one metered engine record. Experiment 696 (engine state in the pre-training mixer), found that telling the neural network about D8's gas-engine state during pre-training only did not change the forecast error (+0.009 means no change, in points of capacity; positive is worse). A post hoc split at a host substation with a recorded engine added showed a gain when the engine was off and a loss when it ran, which cancel when weighted by how often the engine runs. One option from the split is to combine a pre-training state with a way of putting the engine back after pre-training, tested at the host first.

    Option 6 in detail: a language-model loop

    Option 6 is an idea, not agreed work, and no experiment has been started. The maintainer's thought is that an LLM could help with the fuzzy matching between the signals in a Primary's time series and the very incomplete registers (the Embedded Capacity Register and the other registers on the asset-inventory page). The LLM could also make adjustments to the simulator's assets, parameters, and operating profiles, so that the simulator's statistical properties match the observed Primary data as closely as possible.

    The nearest evidence is one reviewer on one task, and it does not settle whether the loop would work. In experiment 692, a Claude Sonnet reviewer judged a figure of each candidate load transfer from the event table. It missed the pre-registered precision gate: 0.45 against 0.80. The reviewer's precision was 0.75, still under 0.80, after the 22 recurring candidates were removed from the calibration set, a post-hoc reading. Those candidates are events in the real record that the injected truth leaves out. The experiment did not test an LLM editing a simulator.

    The asset-inventory page's own risks apply to the loop. The risks are circularity and bands doing the work (see Risks), and fits left free that invent generators (see that section). A loop that edits assets freely could also invent generators to match the statistics. The controls the loop would need, as options:

    • Register-constrained asset types: the LLM may only choose among asset types that the register lists for the Primary.
    • Held-out months: the match is scored on months the loop has not seen.
    • A fixed budget of edits: each run has a set number of changes.
    • Every move logged: each edit and the reason given for it are stored.
    • The same known-answer test as option 1: real metered output added to the readings, with an absent decoy asset and an unlisted asset.

    The loop passes only if it matches option 1's plain fit on the known-answer test, and a proposed stop rule ends it otherwise. The prediction that could fail: the loop recovers each listed, injected asset's capacity within 20% and the engine's median spell length within 30%, as in the smallest test, and gives the absent decoy asset under 0.5 MW at 5 or more of the 6 Primaries. Stop if the decoy gets 1 MW or more at 3 or more of the 6 Primaries, the stop rule of the smallest test.
