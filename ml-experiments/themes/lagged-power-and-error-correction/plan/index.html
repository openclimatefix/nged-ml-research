    Lagged power and error correction: research plan

    A lower number in the "Priority" column runs sooner.

    Priority

    Id Question Priority Waits for State
    400 Does a smooth blend of the forecast with power lags and the forecast without them, weighted by lag surprise, beat the forecast with lags around load shifts at no cost elsewhere? High (maintainer approved 2026-10-30) Nothing Finished (not supported): The lag surprise scores from experiment 391 did not tell a blend when to trust the power lags: the fitted weight hardly moved around events, so every learned blend behaved like a mix of about 60% forecast with lags and 40% forecast without lags. See experiment 400.
    404 Can one neural mixture density network with a lagless module and a calibration module learn a continuous trust weight for each lagged power input, so that lags inside a load-shift event do not harm the forecast? High (maintainer, weekend programme 2026-10-30) Slot from the manager; experiment 402's injected-event generator if it exists Finished (not supported): No variant we trained beats a fixed 50/50 mixture of the plain network and the lagless forecast (B-mix) on real harm rows at the six substations that report signed power, so the learned trust weights add complexity without a measured gain. See experiment 404.
    402 Can a gate trained on injected load shifts set the weight of a mixture of the forecast with power lags and the forecast without them, with no NGED labels? High (maintainer approved 2026-10-30) Experiment 400 merged Finished: partly supported, see experiment 402
    411 By how much would the forecast with power lags improve on harm rows if the lags inside each real load-shift event were replaced by clean values (the most any lag-trust method can gain)? High (batch 0 of experiment 404) Nothing Finished (inconclusive): We could not measure the most that a lag-trust method can gain, because the harness scores only 15 of 184 load-shift events. See experiment 411.
    414 Post hoc: does filling the gaps in the stage-1 median, so that more load-shift events are scored, change the counterfactual-lag gain on harm rows? High (manager, after experiment 411) Nothing Finished (inconclusive): Re-scoring experiment 411 with the gaps in the stage-1 median filled changed none of its scores. See experiment 414.
    428 Can a convolutional network over whole days of recent power, read by attention, replace the hand-made lagged power inputs and stop the forecast following load-shift events? High (maintainer, 2026-10-31) Nothing Done (S1, the surprise at the three lags, carries forward)
    445 If a detector knew exactly which recent lags lie inside a load-shift event, how much would a lagged and lagless forecast mixture gain (the O-rule ceiling, no fits)? High (neural Discuss session, 2026-10-31) Experiment 404 merged Finished (refuted): A perfect record of which recent power readings lie inside a load-shift event did not help the mixture of the lagged and the lagless forecast: choosing the lagless forecast (or weighting it by the share of flagged readings) scored worse than averaging the two 50/50, inside real events (0.465 percentage points of capacity worse, 95% interval 0.198 to 0.644) and on simulated events. See experiment 445.
    447 Does changing any one setting of the S1 demand network (width, depth, embedding, components, dropout, weight decay, learning rate and schedule, batch size, optimiser), or a random search over the three most influential, lower its quantile score by the agreed margin? High (neural Discuss session, 2026-11-01) Nothing Done (not supported: no single setting beats S1; see experiment 447)
    459 Is the sum over a load-shift donor and its recipient Primaries unchanged by the event, so that a forecast of the group sum split by a slowly changing share could work? High (load-shift Discuss session, 2026-11-01) Nothing Finished: refuted, see experiment 459
    458 Can a weight per lead band, a prequential weight, or a shrunk level offset on the lagless forecast beat the fixed 50/50 mixture of the lagged and lagless forecasts (zero fits)? High (neural Discuss session, 2026-11-01) Nothing Finished: not supported, see experiment 458
    462 Can a lag network that sees the neighbours learn to follow a level change that no neighbour mirrors, when its training worlds contain genuine level changes with and without a mirror (row 4 of the experiment 404 review, G3)? High (neural Discuss session, 2026-11-01) Row 3 reported; NRA final list Done (inconclusive by design: the optimal mirror gap is 0.054 to 0.075, below the seed noise; see experiment 462)
    507 Is there enough to gain from repairing shifted power readings, rather than down-weighting them, to justify a learned 14-day history encoder? High (neural Discuss session, 2026-11-02) Nothing Interim: oracle repair helps; one total-variation repair does not; learned encoder not run (line paused)
    550 Do trailing temperature windows still help the lag-light demand network once the day-ago and week-ago power lags are in? High (manager, 2026-11-04) Nothing Finished (supported): The three trailing temperature windows still help the lag-light demand network on cold winter nights by about 0.10 points of capacity, the same size as without the lags, so the day-ago and week-ago lags do not already carry that information. See experiment 550.
    567 Do the neighbouring Primaries' lags let the modular lag-encoder demand network tell a load transfer from a genuine level change, when it trains on synthetic load shifts? High (manager, 2026-11-05) Experiments 559, 560, and 566 merged Finished (not supported): Adding the neighbours' lags to the lag-encoder demand neural network raised CRPS across all rows, and synthetic load-shift training did not give a shown gain at the real events that name a recipient. See experiment 567.
    589 Why does the P5 forecast cover Primary D5 poorly before the December 2025 load-shift, and is that one Primary or a property of the P5 recipe? High (manager, 2026-11-06) Nothing Finished (partly supported): The P5 forecast does not under-cover in general: on rows with no load-shift nearby, the observed power falls inside its 90% band slightly more often than inside the band of the earlier v3+D5 forecast (0.884 against 0.869). See experiment 589.
    595 Does the fine-tuned demand network of C1 (P5 plus lag-only synthetic shifts) or A1 (shared lag encoder) absorb less of a load-shift in its training power than plain P5's 0.219 of the depth, so that the proposed bar L4 (at most 0.10) can be met? High (manager, 2026-11-06) Nothing Finished: partly supported, see experiment 595
    597 Do C1 and A1 with the G4 gated band, assembled as single forecasts, hold the normal running arrangement inside the q05-q95 band on 0.90 of rows from day 4 of a load-shift to its end? High (manager, 2026-11-06) Nothing Finished: supported, see experiment 597
    600 Do C1, A1, and their G4 assembled forecasts still hold the normal running arrangement inside the q05-q95 band at 120-240 h ahead, where NGED's horizon reaches 10 days? High (manager, 2026-11-06) Nothing Finished (partly supported): At 5 to 10 days ahead, the neural network A1 with the G4 band covers the normal running arrangement inside its 5th-to-95th-percentile band on 0.905 of rows during injected load-shifts, but the interval includes values below the 0.90 target, so reaching it is not shown. See experiment 600.
    602 What does each encoder of the modular demand network (datetime, weather, power lag) contribute to its forecast, on the December 2025 load-shift, on injected load-shifts, and on an event-free fortnight? High (maintainer, 2026-11-06) Nothing Finished: partly supported, see experiment 602
    608 Which parts of the combined lag-and-gate, lag-without-gate, and lag-free demand neural networks are needed, when each part is removed alone? High (maintainer, 2026-11-06) Nothing Finished (partly supported): On injected load-shifts, the lag-and-gate network (K1) keeps the normal running arrangement inside its band on 0.927 of rows from day 4 of a shift to its end, and the gated band is the largest single part of that (0.041). See experiment 608.
    647 Can a small departure head on the frozen K1 neural network forecast the arrangement now in force better than K1 shifted by its own recent error? High (maintainer, 2026-11-07) Nothing Finished: partly supported, see experiment 647
    650 Does a departure head trained on shifts with random ends, varied shapes, and varied depths beat K1 shifted by its own decayed recent error, once band widening is removed? High (manager, 2026-11-08) Nothing Finished: partly supported, see experiment 650
    652 Does a departure head with an explicit survival output beat the best forecast that does not know when a shift ends, once each forecast is scored under its own rules? High (maintainer, 2026-11-08) Nothing Finished: not supported, see experiment 652
    654 Does one network with an NRA output and a prevailing output, trained end to end with a learned end detector, beat a frozen K1 plus a separate survival head? High (maintainer, 2026-11-09) Nothing Planned
    681 Do extra lagged power inputs make the prevailing output of the two-output demand network react faster to a load shift, without worsening the NRA output? High (maintainer, 2026-11-10) Nothing Finished (small gain): Giving the prevailing forecast 8 to 14 extra lagged power inputs makes it follow a new load level about 20 hours sooner on 1,152 simulated load shifts, and 120 hours sooner at D5 in one verified real transfer (D16 did not change), without making the normal-running (NRA) output or the event-free forecast worse. See experiment 681.
    691 Does a prevailing head that reads both the causal detector features and the extra lagged power inputs react to a load shift sooner than a head that reads only one of them? High (maintainer, 2026-11-10) Experiments 681 and 687 merged Finished: not supported, see experiment 691
    603 Does perturbing the lagged power in pre-training and step 2, and keeping the first half of the mixer through every training stage, make the demand network follow a load-shift less than the network of experiment 584 (C1)? Done Nothing Finished (not supported): Showing the demand network synthetic load-shifts earlier in training, and freezing the first half of its mixer, did not make the forecast band hold the normal running arrangement on more rows after a load-shift.
    606 Which inputs should the lag encoder of the demand network read so that it follows a load-shift less: the lag's identity or age, the expected-input embeddings, the gap between a lag and the lag-free forecast, weighted averages of that gap, or the gaps at the electrical neighbours? Done Nothing Finished (partly supported): Giving the demand network's lag encoder a weighted history of how far each recent lag sits from the lag-free forecast, and the same history at its electrical neighbours, cut the share of an injected load-shift that the forecast follows from 0.119 to 0.035.
    610 Can the demand network's lag encoder keep following a load-shift this little (follow at most 0.05) while its event-free forecast stays as good as the A1 network's, by changing the span of the neighbour summaries, building the gap signals out of fold, dropping the signals during training, or adding a label head? Done Nothing Finished (not supported): The neighbour-summary inputs (P7, from experiment 606) make the A1 demand network follow a load shift much less, but none of the 27 variants and the placebo that we tried keeps ordinary-day accuracy at the six MW Primaries without losing that benefit.
    613 Do five seeds give intervals tight enough to show that the class 2 candidate can replace the gate, that O2 is equivalent to D5, and that dropping E3 changes absorption? Done Nothing Finished (partly supported): The class 2 candidate matches the gate on injected load-shifts: its coverage and safe-side share are within 0.01 of K1's.
    632 Does a network with two gated recurrent units and three self-attention layers, pre-trained on ERA5 and CAMS weather, forecast Primary demand better than the existing pre-trained networks? Done Nothing Finished (refuted): The sequence neural network is not worth porting.
    648 Does a larger or differently shaped part of the K1 or class 2 candidate demand network lower its quantile score by the agreed margin, now that the lag encoder also reads neighbour summaries? Done Nothing Finished (not supported): Making the lag encoder wider or deeper, or the hidden layers bigger, does not improve the K1 or class 2 candidate demand neural networks by the margin we required (0.11 CRPS points); the best gain is 0.04 points and comes from one short-history Primary (D15), while the six MW Primaries get slightly worse.
    651 Does a graph layer over the electrical neighbours let the class 2 candidate keep its low follow of a load shift while removing its extra error on ordinary days at the six MW Primaries? Done Nothing Finished (not supported): A graph layer over the electrical neighbours did not remove the class 2 candidate's extra error at the six MW Primaries, and did not help it resist a false load shift.
    663 Does a learned end detector, reading the freshest half-hours at a Primary and its neighbours, make the prevailing forecast drop a load shift sooner after it ends, without more false alarms? Done Nothing Finished (not supported): A learned end detector made the prevailing forecast better in the 24 hours after an injected shift ends (-0.9 to -1.1 scaled points of capacity) and worse while the shift is in force (+0.2 to +0.3 for E0d), and the neighbour graph added no consistent gain, because three random rewirings of it scored within 0.08 points of the real graph.
