    Weather products and blending: experiments

    This page lists every experiment tried in the theme, newest first. An experiment that belongs mainly to another theme but bears on this one is marked "also relevant".

    Experiment Hypothesis Verdict Production status Take-home message Relevance
    00437 neural-demand-thermal-lag-solar-gain-and-tariff-feature-bundles The pooled plain mixture density network of experiment 425 sees one valid time per row, so it cannot see the hours before or after that time. Adding columns that summarise those hours (bundle T, thermal lag; bundle S, solar gain; bundle F, look-ahead) or that carry the calendar, tariff, and national signals (bundle C) lowers its continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) at days 1 to 10, because the network trains across all 16 Primaries (and, in the pretrained form, across 6 years of ERA5 and CAMS history) and may find signal that the per-Primary XGBoost model and the GAM stack missed. not supported not-for-production Adding thermal-lag, solar-gain, look-ahead, calendar, tariff, or national-signal columns to the pooled plain mixture density network did not lower its continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) by the registered 0.03 points of capacity, in either the ensemble-only or the pretrained form. Seven of the eight arms were worse than their control at the six signed-MW Primaries, by 0.016 to 0.059 points. The thermal-lag bundle lowered the CRPS at the eight MVA-only Primaries (by 0.022 and 0.029) in only 2 of 4 folds, which is too weak a result to act on. The screen could not detect effects below 0.045 to 0.064 points, so the bundles are left out, not ruled out. also relevant
    00429 unified-forecaster-wind-ablations A single pooled neural network can forecast the three metered wind farms as well as Wind farms research default 2 (experiment 347's y2: a frozen ERA5-trained power curve run on each of the 51 ensemble members, followed by a mixture-density head). Each difference between that design and the Primary-demand design (experiment 425's arm A: one pass on the ensemble mean and spread, no physics, an unbounded head) is kept only if an ablation shows it is needed. partly supported not-for-production Neural wind forecasts do not need the extra machinery. A plain pooled network fed the ensemble members ties Wind farms research default 2 (10.533 against 10.518 points), and neither passing members through the network (-0.008 against the one-pass design), a power-curve layer (+0.000), nor a censored head (+0.018, worse) changes the score by the 0.10-point margin. The weakness all arms share is the upper tail: 2.2% to 2.8% of observations exceed q99, because the forecasts under-predict windy half-hours, and censoring did not fix it. The plain network is the design that matches the default; the upper tail is the open problem, and the architecture comparison does not explain it. also relevant
    00424 neural-mva-export-gated-signed-ens-serving-test Experiment 420's export-gated network (arm A) still forecasts signed power on reverse-flow half-hours much better than the same network with its gate shut (P0) when both are trained and scored on ECMWF ensemble (ENS) mean and spread weather, which is the weather a live service would hold, instead of the recorded CAMS irradiance and ERA5 temperature of the laboratory test. The gain shrinks, because the ENS forecast of cloud is less accurate than recorded cloud, but most of the laboratory gain comes from letting the forecast go below zero, which does not depend on weather accuracy. supported candidate With ensemble weather forecasts instead of recorded weather, the neural network with the export gate still forecasts signed power on reverse-flow half-hours much better than the same network with the gate shut (a gain of 15.7 points of capacity, 95% interval 13.8 to 17.4, against 19.2 with recorded weather), and it harms the forecast by no more than 0.05 points where no solar farm exports. The gate opened at the same two of the eight apparent-power-only substations as before. An unplanned finding is that the network trained on recorded weather and applied unchanged to ensemble weather scored 5.5 points better (95% interval 4.4 to 6.6) than the network retrained on ensemble weather, so how to train on ensemble weather is the open question. also relevant
    00419 pooled-mixture-density-network-solar-summary-sunny-flag-and-half-life-input-ports The XGBoost model's gain from a two-column solar summary (experiment 381: -0.051 points at the signed-MW six on the scored year) carries over to the pooled plain neural network, which has the solar geometry but no cloud-aware summary, and the 0.25-year half-life that lowered the score at D15 and D18 and on cold nights in experiment 407 does so again with new random seeds. not supported not-for-production The XGBoost model's solar-summary input did not carry over to the pooled plain neural network: it lowered the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) at the six signed-MW Primaries by only 0.012 points of capacity (-0.043 to +0.013), one sixth of the 0.0738 minimum detectable effect. Refitting the same neural network with new random seeds changed the MVA-only eight by -0.047, about six times as far as the solar summary did there (-0.008), and changed the signed-MW six by the same amount as the solar summary did (+0.011 against -0.012). Adding the sunny flag and a 1-year half-life was worse than the solar summary alone, and the 0.25-year half-life raised the signed-MW six CRPS (+0.044) while lowering it at D15 and D18. None of the input changes is adopted, and the reference stays. All five test months are interim. also relevant
    00418 mdn-pretrain-on-era5-and-cams-then-fine-tune-on-ens The pooled plain mixture density network of experiment 397, pretrained on the Primaries' own power and ERA5 and CAMS weather from 2019-09 and then fine-tuned on the ENS rows, scores lower CRPS at days 1 to 10 than the same network trained on the ENS rows alone, because the ENS archive starts on 2024-04-01 and the pooled network sees at most 18 to 24 months of it. supported candidate Teaching the neural network first on six years of past weather and power, then on the forecast archive, lowers its error by about 0.06 points of capacity (2.1%) at the six signed-direction substations and 0.16 points (4.2%) at the eight apparent-power-only substations over 12 test months. Changing how much of the network is retrained, or adding forecast-like noise, changes the score by less than the interval of the estimate. With only 6 months of forecast archive on 4 test months the gain is about 1.3 points, although the ENS-only baseline degrades badly there. also relevant
    00396 xgboost-recipe-screen-demand-calendar-winter-hyperparameters-ensemble-summary At least one change in each of five families (calendar columns, winter weather columns, hyperparameters, ensemble-summary inputs, a split of the model by lead band) lowers the days 1 to 10 CRPS of the weekly member of experiment 87, refitted monthly, by more than the refit noise floor at the signed-MW six or the MVA-only eight Primaries, and the families that act on a specific slice (holiday days, winter and the top demand quartile, high ensemble spread) gain more on that slice than on all rows. partly supported candidate Giving the weekly XGBoost demand model the ensemble's view of the weather, either its mean and spread or a model trained on the ensemble mean, lowered the error by 0.4% to 2.7% in both test months, and no other change we tried did so as clearly. Holiday columns help on Easter bank holidays but raised the error on all days in January and at the signed-MW six at Easter, and winter weather columns do not help, so a full-year test should start with the ensemble-input arms. also relevant
    00395 xgboost-level-state-winter-spread-anchor-stored-forecasts A level state in the style of the GAM stack's Kalman filter, run on the weekly XGBoost bundle's own residuals, closes the bundle's gap to the GAM stack at D11 and D14, and a one-state filter with three numbers per Primary does so almost as well as the stack's two-state filter. partly supported not-for-production A Kalman-filter-style level state helps the weekly XGBoost model at D14 and hurts it at D11, so the filter is not the whole explanation of the gap to the GAM stack. A cheap one-state version (three numbers per Primary) recovers about half of the two-state filter's gain at D14 only, so the first production update should not carry it. Adding the winter ensemble-spread columns lowers cold-night scores but not cold-night coverage, and does not widen the band where weather is most uncertain. A short-history level anchor cuts D15's median bias but worsens the other 14 Primaries by about 0.1 points. When recent power readings lie inside a labelled load-shift event, every forecast loses q95 coverage on the signed-MW six, and only the GAM stack does on the MVA-only eight. The GAM stack's score worsens most, though its interval overlaps the others'. also relevant
    00394 xgboost-ensemble-mean-and-std-columns-separately Experiment 389's gain from four ensemble columns (the mean and standard deviation across the 51 members of temperature and of shortwave) comes mostly from the two mean columns, because the gain was largest at 0 to 24 hours and at the middle quantiles and absent at q01 and q99. supported not-for-production Giving the XGBoost demand forecast, as an extra input, the mean across 51 ensemble weather members of the temperature and of the shortwave radiation lowers its error (CRPS) by 0.052 points of capacity at the 6 substations that record the direction of flow, and by 0.029 points at the other 8. That is the whole improvement of four extra columns at the 6 and 82% of it at the 8 (0.035 points). The members' spread adds almost nothing, and it does not help more on days when the members disagree most. The tails do not materially improve with either input: on cold nights the forecast's 95% quantile lies above only 0.77 to 0.78 of outcomes at the 6. also relevant
    00381 simple-weekly-xgboost-demand-against-gam-stack A single XGBoost model per Primary, refitted weekly on the ENS control member with experiment 87's recipe (the BG2 weekly member), plus at most four small input or weighting changes, scores no worse than the GAM stack ("stack without S below 168 h", experiment 377) at every one of the 14 countable Primaries at days 1 to 10. partly supported candidate A single XGBoost model per Primary, refitted weekly, is as good as the GAM stack at 9 of 14 Primaries and better on average at the eight whose meters hide reverse flow (D8 by 1.6 points; clearly so at D8, D12, and D22), so the stack's extra parts do not pay at those eight. At the six Primaries with signed readings the XGBoost model is level on average (+0.08, not distinguishable from zero) but worse at D11 and D14 and has a narrower upper tail, so the stack still protects against curtailment better there: its avoidable curtailment cost at a limit of 0.9 times the maximum observed export is lower at 4 of 6. The input changes that helped in practice were a daily solar summary (-0.05) and a 1-year half-life (-0.02); an Economy 7 input did nothing, and no input tried fixed the 4.9-point under-forecast of sunny demand at D12. also relevant
    00379 bg2-components-all-16-primaries-to-14-days Under the demand-v1 protocol at all 16 Primaries, no single component of BG2 carries its lead over a single weekly-refitted XGBoost model, so dropping any one of the five blend members, the solar offset, or the spread factor moves the CRPS of days 1 to 10 by less than 0.10 points of effective capacity. supported not-for-production A plain weekly-refitted XGBoost model scores within 0.05 points of BG2 (about 1.5% of its error) at the six Primaries whose meters record the sign of the flow, and within 0.02 points at the eight whose meters do not, so the five-model blend adds little to the first release candidate. Two of BG2's five models (monthly XGBoost and the pooled-by-type model) add nothing measurable, and the solar offset twins act only at the five Primaries that carry them. BG2's upper tail is too narrow at the six signed-MW Primaries: 93.2% of outcomes fall below its 95% quantile and 97.6% below its 99% quantile. also relevant
    00371 d8-monthly-xgboost-input-group-ablation One input group of monthly XGBoost (the weather inputs the GAM lacks, the three origin lags, the 0.5-year recency weighting, or the 51-member mixture) accounts for most of its 0.64-point lead over the GAM stack at substation D8. supported not-for-production Monthly XGBoost beats the GAM stack at substation D8 mostly because it uses three lagged values of the power measured shortly before the forecast starts: without them its score rises from 7.70 to 8.25, within 0.1 of the GAM stack's 8.34. The five extra weather inputs (0.05) and the ensemble mean (0.01) explain little of the gap, and the recency weighting (0.23 at D8) explains a smaller part and only at D8. The lags also help at two other Primary substations (D4 +0.28, D2 +0.11), so the pre-registered expectation that only D8 would respond failed. A GAM term that uses recent power is the next candidate to test. This is one substation and one scored year. also relevant
    00343 wind-research-default-1-train-on-ensemble-mean Wind farms research default 1 trains XGBoost on ERA5 and runs it on each of the 51 ensemble members, then corrects with the offset table. The ensemble's 100 m wind speed at the grid cell is lower than at the turbines (a factor of 1.13 closed 78% of the gap to the ERA5 oracle in experiment 307). An XGBoost model trained on the 51-member mean of the ensemble's own history (arm M, 2024-04 to 2025-06, no calendar columns) would learn that bias and need less offset-table correction. Arm MS adds the member standard deviation of the 100 m and 10 m speeds. The three metered wind farms W1 to W3 are scored at 1 to 10 days. not supported not-for-production A wind model trained on the ensemble mean needs almost no offset table (the table changes its score by 0.017 points, against 0.345 for the default), but its forecast is no better than the default's: 0.08 points worse on the headline rows, an amount the data cannot distinguish from zero, and worse at low winds and in the 0.99 quantile. The default's recipe (ERA5 training plus the table) stays; mean training is a simpler recipe that scores about the same. also relevant
    00333 primary-demand-foundation-model-zero-shot A pre-trained time-series foundation model (Chronos-2, 120 million parameters, Apache-2.0) forecasts a Primary substation's half-hourly demand from the substation's own history. Without weather inputs Chronos-2 cannot match Primary demand research default 3 at leads of 1 to 10 days. With the ensemble-mean temperature, the sun's elevation, and calendar columns passed as covariates, Chronos-2 closes part of the gap, but not all of it. partly supported not-for-production A pre-trained time-series foundation model, used without any training on our data, forecasts Primary demand clearly worse than Primary demand research default 3 at every lead from 0.5 to 240 hours. Giving the foundation model the temperature forecast, the sun's elevation, and the calendar recovers about one sixth of the gap, and makes sunny-daylight half-hours worse. Chronos-2, the one foundation model we tested, is not a candidate to replace the default, and a foundation-model forecast would have to be scored alone against the default before use as a blend member. also relevant
    00329 primary-demand-default-3-member-solar-distribution-at-7-to-10-days At 168 to 240 hours the 51 ensemble members have little skill for individual days, so the forecast centres on average sunshine and the half-hours that turn out sunny have realised demand below the median. The weakness of Primary demand research default 3 on sunny daylight (5.07 against the manual heuristic's 4.82 in experiment 317, for the convex chain before any blend) is therefore a fault of the predictive distribution (the lower tail on realised-sunny half-hours), not of the median. Replacing the two-number Gaussian summary of the members' solar responses with the 51 responses themselves (X1), mixing them with a climatology of the response (X2), or adding the members of the two previous daily runs (X3) fixes the distribution. not supported not-for-production The 7-to-10-day weakness on sunny half-hours no longer exists in the research demand forecast: the two-number solar summary and the daylight-only blend with the manual heuristic already took it from 5.2% worse than the heuristic to 6.6% better, so no new distribution method is needed there. What remains is one coastal Primary (D5), where the forecast is 11% worse than the heuristic, which points back at the solar term and not at the width of the bands. also relevant
    00325 primary-demand-default-3-sunshine-response-refit-and-restriction Experiment 323's sunshine response (F1) harmed all daylight half-hours because the Kalman filter's level, the winter night offset, and the median correction were estimated on the errors of a fit without a response and so counted the same error twice (double counting), or because the response acts too widely, at forecast clear-sky index where the damped ensemble-mean forecast index mis-applies the response. Refitting those corrections on the response's own errors (F1r) removes the first cause, and restricting the response to clear-sky index above 0.6 and sun elevation above 15 degrees (F1s) removes the second. small gain not-for-production The sunshine response, once the rest of the forecast chain is refitted around it, no longer makes daylight forecasts worse, but the response improves the overall forecast by under 0.01 points, too little to adopt. The response improves spring daylight at two Primaries (D5 and D9) by about 0.11 points. also relevant
    00323 primary-demand-default-3-flexible-sunshine-response-in-the-convex-fit Primary demand research default 3 trails the depth-1 XGBoost refit on April-to-June daylight half-hours because its solar term (the pinned capacity times the derated unit shape, with the D5 clip) is too high on clear spring middays. Experiment 322 grafted the refit's sunshine response onto the default and recovered 0.50 of the refit's advantage. A smooth correction in clear-sky index and sun elevation, fitted in the same bounded least-squares fit as the solar term, recovers a useful part of that gain with no model of other inputs. not supported not-for-production Adding a flexible sunshine curve to the solar part of the demand forecast does not recover the gap to the better-performing XGBoost model: it helps the sunniest half-hours but worsens the pooled score by about 0.4%. The advantage seen in experiment 322 is therefore not recovered by adding a smooth curve in clear-sky index and sun elevation on top of the existing level corrections, and most of the harm sits in the level shift. A boosted-tree sunshine term could use more inputs at once, and whether to test it is a decision for the research plan. also relevant
    00319 primary-demand-default-1-solar-summary-and-heuristic-blend-at-7-to-10-days Primary demand research default 1 has two weak spots at 168 to 240 hours: sunny daylight half-hours, where it scores 5.2% worse than the manual heuristic, and April to October, where it beats climatology by only 4%. Default plus x1 (the two-number solar summary S, the mean and standard deviation of the 51 ensemble members' solar responses, extended from 0.5 to 168 hours out to 240 hours) lowers the sunny-daylight score at 168 to 240 hours. Default plus x2 (a blend of the convex chain's median with the manual heuristic's, with the weight fitted per 24-hour lead band and season group) lowers the pooled score at 168 to 240 hours, mostly in April to October. partly supported candidate The demand forecast for 7 to 10 days ahead improves by about 2% when its solar term also uses the spread between the 51 weather-forecast members, and the gain is largest on sunny days (6.6%), where the forecast had been worse than the manual heuristic that National Grid Electricity Distribution (NGED) staff use. Blending in the manual heuristic improves the forecast by 4.5%, but the blend worsens the coldest winter nights beyond the agreed tolerance, so only the solar change is adopted. also relevant
    00307 wind-farm-power-as-wind-sensor The wind that the three metered wind farms' own power implies (the fitted physics core's power curve run backwards) is closer to a high-resolution analysis than to ERA5 at 9 to 14 m/s, and at least half of the ensemble's shortfall (observed power below the forecast's median on 34.1% of rows instead of 50%) is in the spread and timing of the members, not in their mean speed. not supported not-for-production The ensemble's low wind-power forecast comes mostly from wind speeds that are too slow, by about 1.1 m/s on average and more at longer leads. Multiplying every member's wind speed by a factor fitted on the 14 months before the scored year closed about 78% of the gap to the ERA5 oracle, while widening the members or shifting them in time closed almost none. The median is slightly better calibrated when the factor is fitted to the wind that the farms' power implies than when it is fitted to ERA5 on the same rows (1.7 percentage points), but the quantile score is not better. also relevant
    00292 metered-farm-ens-neighbourhood A day-ahead cloud field is often right in shape and wrong in position by tens of kilometres, so summaries of the ensemble's irradiance over the hexagonal cells around a metered solar farm carry information that the farm's own cell lacks. Giving H5 (the plant plus a neural network that outputs all 11 quantile offsets) those summaries lowers its quantile score at 12 to 36 hour leads. not supported not-for-production At the six metered solar farms tested, the ensemble's irradiance on the cells around a farm adds almost nothing to the day-ahead forecast: the quantile score improves by 0.36%, against the 2% the plan needed, and the gain is not robust to dropping one farm. Adjacent cells, 15 km apart, differ from the farm's own cell by only about 3% of the irradiance, so the ensemble mean is too smooth between them to show where a cloud edge will fall. A weather source of a different kind is the next experiment to run, rather than more processing of the same ensemble. also relevant
    00272 metered-farm-weather-adapter-gam-vs-neural Extra weather variables that the solar-farm power model (the plant) ignores, fed through a learned correction to the plant's irradiance and temperature, close part of the gap between the plant driven by the ensemble weather forecast (about 0.91 of output fitted on sunny half-hours) and the plant driven by observed weather (0.973), and a small neural network does this better than additive curves. not supported not-for-production The extra ensemble weather variables (longwave radiation, humidity, precipitation, pressure, wind, and 500 hPa height) showed no usable information for the solar-farm power model at these six farms, whether they enter through additive curves or a small neural network, and the real variables do no better than placebo copies. A neural network with no plant, given the lagged power and the ensemble spread, scores 2.7% better than the plant but not better than the linear fit of experiment 257, so a correction to the plant's weather is not the route to a better forecast. also relevant
    00270 metered-farm-xgboost-vs-plant-diagnostic XGBoost beats the plant (the solar-farm power model of experiments 218 and 224) on the six metered farms because of inputs the plant never sees, namely the farm's own lagged power and the ensemble spread, and not because of a better map from the ensemble-mean weather to power. Calendar columns and extra weather variables add little once those two are present. partly supported candidate XGBoost beats the solar-farm power model by about 3% in the quantile score even when it sees only the power model's own weather inputs, and the gain is in the width of the forecast band (the 0.05 and 0.95 quantiles), not in the median forecast. The same gain comes from giving the power model a forecast band that depends on the ensemble spread or the clear-sky index, and that band also beats XGBoost at the 0.99 quantile. Lagged power, calendar columns, and extra weather variables add little in own history. also relevant
    00262 wind-aifs-members-and-climatology-by-lead For the three metered wind farms (W1 to W3), a lead-dependent mixture of the ECMWF ensemble's member forecasts, the AIFS ensemble's member forecasts (both run through experiment 230's ERA5-trained XGBoost wind model A0r), and climatology lowers the quantile score and the 0.99 pinball loss against the ECMWF members alone, after both are corrected by a table of offsets per lead day, quantile, and farm; and a small neural gate that reads the two ensembles' spread and disagreement chooses better weights than weights by lead alone. partly supported candidate Adding the AIFS ensemble's wind forecasts to the ECMWF ensemble's improves the wind forecast at 3 to 6 days ahead, by about 5% of the quantile score, but does not improve the high-power tail that matters for curtailment. Climatology and a small neural network that picks the mixing weights add nothing once the table of offsets has corrected the common low bias. The AIFS arms also need testing on a second year, because one year of Tuesday runs sets the weights. also relevant
    00246 metered-farm-ens-ukv-icond2-blend The DWD ICON-D2 regional weather forecast (2 km grid, run every 3 hours, 48 hours ahead) carries information about cloud over a metered solar farm that neither the ECMWF ensemble nor the Met Office UKV carries, so adding it to the ensemble plus UKV lowers the farm's day-ahead error. The evidence against is that at day 1 every single product lost to the ensemble mean for solar in the matched-lead study, and ICON-D2's errors are likely to be correlated with UKV's. Every gain is an upper limit for production, because the UKV archive used is the CEDA research archive. small gain not-for-production Adding the German ICON-D2 weather forecast to the ECMWF ensemble and the Met Office UKV lowers the day-ahead error of a metered solar farm by about 1%, which is under the 1.5% we set as worth a fourth weather source. ICON-D2 is available for only 12 to 29 hours of the day ahead at an 08 UTC forecast time, and the UKV archive used is a research archive, so this is an upper limit for production. also relevant
    00237 dp-two-number-solar-summary-with-clip The mean and standard deviation of the 51 ensemble members' solar responses lower the daylight quantile score of the demand forecast also when each member is passed through the clipped solar term, because cloud uncertainty and the clip both act at midday only partly on the same rows. supported candidate At D5, in one year of data, summarising the ensemble weather members as a mean and a standard deviation keeps at least about 90% of its gain when the solar output is clipped at 60% of the solar capacity held for D5 (110%, interval 91% to 141%). On the sunniest third of daylight rows the point estimate of the remaining gain is about half (interval -12% to 88%). A post hoc arm using the standard deviation alone kept the gain there, which suggests testing that form. also relevant
    00229 metered-farm-ens-ukv-blend The Met Office UKV weather model (2 km grid, 54 hours) carries information about cloud over a solar farm that the 51-member ECMWF ensemble lacks, so giving a metered solar farm's forecast both products lowers its day-ahead error against the ensemble alone, and closes part of the gap between the ensemble-driven fit (0.91 of output on sunny half-hours) and the fit with observed weather (0.973) of experiment 218. Every gain is an upper limit for production, because the UKV archive used here is the research archive on CEDA, which differs statistically from the live UKV. small gain not-for-production Adding the Met Office UKV forecast to the ECMWF ensemble lowers the day-ahead error of a metered solar farm by about 1% to 2%. The gain is under the 3% we set as worth a second weather source, and the farms' fit on sunny half-hours moves only about 6% of the way to the fit with observed weather. The UKV archive used here is a research archive, and the live UKV differs, so this result is an upper limit for production; a weather product of a different kind is the option we see for a larger gain. also relevant
    00202 dp-ens-two-number-rolling-replay Experiment 193 found that two numbers per half-hour (the mean and the standard deviation of the 51 ensemble members' solar responses) lower the daylight quantile score by 2.20% in the scored year. Its two fitted factors per lead band were fitted on interleaved months (the odd months of 2024-07 to 2025-06), some of them later than the replicate months they were applied to, and its numbers were held in float64.
