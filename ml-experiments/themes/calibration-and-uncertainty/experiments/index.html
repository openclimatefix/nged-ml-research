    Calibration and uncertainty: experiments

    This page lists every experiment tried in the theme, newest first. An experiment that belongs mainly to another theme but bears on this one is marked "also relevant".

    00492 two-constant-tail-widening-in-place-of-table-p-for-demand Table P (a factor per metering group, lead band, and tail quantile) can be replaced at the eight MVA-only Primaries by two constants per metering group, one for the lower tail (q01 and q05) and one for the upper tail (q95 and q99), fitted on earlier folds only, with no loss of calibration. It cannot be replaced at the signed-MW six, where table P already misses the q99 pinball clause. partly supported candidate At the eight MVA-only Primaries, one lower constant and one upper constant per metering group, fitted on earlier months, recalibrate the demand forecast as well as table P (20 factors per metering group) does, so table P can be dropped there. At the six signed-MW Primaries, the constants leave q95 coverage 0.011 to 0.013 below table P's, so table P stays. Wind and solar were not tested. primary
    00491 weight-averaging-in-demand-network-fine-tuning Experiment 481 found that averaging the quantiles of several networks that differ only in fit seed lowers the score at the signed-MW six, and that the first-release demand forecaster serves five such networks. Averaging the weights of one network along one training trajectory costs one network at serving time, and may recover a useful share of the gain from seed averaging. inconclusive not-for-production One neural network trained with a moving average of its weights did not match a pool of seeds. The averaged network scores 0.002 to 0.005 CRPS points better than a single network at the eight (the seed-noise minimum detectable effect is 0.083), while a pool of three averaged networks from different seeds gains 0.065 to 0.071. In this experiment, the pools that gained came from networks that differ in their seed. The gap between one network and five is 0.082 points at the eight. The plan needed a gap above 0.15 points before a closure share could settle the question, so the verdict stays inconclusive. also relevant
    00490 one-lag-robust-network-distilled-from-the-lagged-plus-lagless-mixture A single lagged network (A0-inj's architecture: the Primary's measured power at three earlier times, no lagless input) trained on injected load-shift worlds, with the forecast of experiment 454's K2-S1 mixture as an extra training target, forecasts as well as that mixture, so the live service could run one network and store no lagless table, mixture step, or store of past lagless forecasts. not supported not-for-production Training one neural network to imitate the four-network mixture K2-S1 did not remove the need for the lagless forecast. On rows with no labelled event the imitation network scores 0.28 points of capacity worse than B-mix (a 50/50 mixture of a lagged network and a lagless forecast) at the 6 Primaries metered in signed megawatts, beyond the minimum detectable effect, and 0.15 worse at the 8 Primaries metered in megavolt-amperes, where the interval just reaches zero. It recovers only a small part of the gap to K2-S1, so the lagless forecast probably carries information that the 3 raw power readings do not. Keep the lagless forecast in the live service. also relevant
    00489 distill-the-five-network-pool-into-one-network The pool of five pretrained-then-fine-tuned networks (A-pool, experiment 425's arm A) can be replaced at serving by one network of the same shape, fine-tuned to match the pool's forecast distribution, without losing forecast skill beyond the seed noise. This moves the seed averaging from the serving path into the training regime. not supported not-for-production One network trained to copy the five-network pool forecasts no better than a single network trained normally, so the pool's gain cannot be moved into training with this recipe. The student matches a single teacher network's tail coverage but not the pool's accuracy, probably because the teacher is in sample on every row it teaches from. Serving the pool of 5 networks stays the way to get that gain. also relevant
    00486 lagged-network-injection-rate-and-schedule The share of training rows whose lags lie inside an injected event sets the prior the lagged network (A0-inj) learns about how often a jump in its lags is an event. A lower share leaves more of a lag step in the median forecast, and a higher share makes the network trust fresh lags less. A schedule that trains first at a high share and ends at a low share (Cur-down) reaches the lower leak without the lost score of a constant high share. not supported not-for-production The share of training rows with an artificial load-shift event in their lags trades how much the forecast follows a step in its recent readings against its error on ordinary rows: 50% follows about a quarter as much of the step as 5% does (0.058 against 0.224) but raises the error on rows with no labelled event by 0.2 points of capacity. None of the 3 rates and 2 schedules tried beat the fixed 50/50 average of the network and a forecast built without lags (B-mix) when the network is used alone, so this experiment did not find a rate that removes the following. All numbers are interim, from five months. also relevant
    00484 hard-row-weighting-in-demand-network-fine-tuning Experiments 435 and 470 left cold nights worse than the tail target, and avoidable curtailment cost ranks the candidates differently from CRPS. Fine-tuning the pretrained network with larger weights on the rows that decide NGED's costs lowers the error on those rows without raising it elsewhere. not supported not-for-production Giving larger fine-tuning weights to cold nights, to high-flow half-hours at the six signed-MW Primaries, or to winter did not improve the forecasts that matter to NGED. Cold-night error at the eight fell by 0.068 points under W-cold, which is inside the noise floor of 0.17, and avoidable-curtailment cost did not change. Weighting winter (W-season) raised CRPS at the eight. One weight value (3) and one pretrained neural network were tested, so the result suggests the hard rows are limited by the inputs rather than the weighting. The next step to try is a different input or neural-network architecture. also relevant
    00483 uncertainty-rerun-on-the-first-release-demand-candidates Experiment 435 ended refuted for table P on clean rows. The prior is that a widening, not table P, repairs the tails of A-pool (experiment 425's arm A, five seeds averaged) and of A-gam-in (the eight MVA-only Primaries). A widening from 1.0 to 1.15 brings clean-row coverage into the target ranges on held-out months, and table P adds nothing beyond it. partly supported candidate The first-release demand candidates are too wide on the lower tail and too narrow on the upper tail, so a widening of both tails makes the lower tail worse while it repairs the upper one. Widening only the upper tail, or applying one factor per tail, fixes the eight MVA-only Primaries for A-pool and A-gam-in alike. At the signed-MW six, one factor per tail narrowly misses its pass rules, and cold winter nights stay under-covered there. primary
    00481 seed-averaging-diminishing-returns-for-demand-and-wind Averaging the quantile forecasts of K networks that differ only in fit seed lowers the score with diminishing returns. For Primary demand (experiment 425's arm A) and for the wind farms (experiment 429's member_plain), the gain from K = 5 to K = 10 is inside the minimum detectable effect, and for the lagged S1 network of experiment 454 the gain from K = 3 to K = 6 is. partly supported candidate A second seed gives the largest gain and later seeds add little: for Primary demand, two seeds score 0.035 points better than one (the 99.55% interval is +0.025 to +0.053, across the 0.03 bar for porting), and each later step gains at most 0.011. For wind, one seed scores within the minimum detectable effect of ten, though a second seed gains 0.041 points. Averaging seeds of the lagged neural network S1 is weaker than adding the lagless head: at two networks, S1 with the lagless head scores 3.441 against 3.628 for two averaged S1 seeds. Each added seed costs another fit per monthly refit. also relevant
    00472 anchor-plus-correction-with-a-strong-penalty-and-a-seed-averaged-anchor Experiment 467's correction (a 32-unit neural network on the frozen lagless forecast) gains 0.50 points of capacity over the lagless head on rows with no labelled event at the signed-MW six, but its L2 penalty from 1e-3 to 1 never limited the outputs, and it is no better than the lagless head inside real events. A penalty of 100 limits the outputs, so it keeps most of the gain and removes the loss inside events; and an anchor that is the equal-weight mixture of the lagless heads of seeds 0 to 2 carries much of K2-S1's gain with a correction on top. partly supported candidate A stronger penalty does not make the lag correction safe. A penalty of 100 shrinks the correction to almost nothing and keeps only 18% of its gain, so the penalties tried (1e-3 to 1 never bound, and 100 binds) do not both limit the correction and keep the gain. Averaging the lagless forecast over three seeds and correcting that average with the recent readings scored better in this experiment. At the six Primaries metered in signed megawatts it scores 3.09 points of capacity on rows with no labelled event, against 3.62 for the averaged lagless forecast, 3.39 for K2-S1, and 3.51 for B-mix, and AvgAnchor serves four neural networks, like K2-S1. Inside real load-shift events it is not shown to be safe (0.30 points worse than its anchor on corrected actuals, inconclusive). It fails the coverage clause of the tail gate as written and is unreplicated, so it is a candidate for the demand assembly's held-out test and not an adopted design. The results are interim, over five scored months. also relevant
    00471 manual-heuristic-calibration-and-analogue-upgrades If the 13 (and later 12) equally weighted historical analogues of the manual heuristic are recalibrated with a correction fitted on a trailing window of past errors, the quantile score falls against the faithful heuristic, because the heuristic's empirical 95th percentile of 13 members is exceeded about 11.4% of the time even when the analogues are perfect. The heuristic also carries a level bias. not supported not-for-production A simple recalibration of the 13 historical analogues behind NGED's manual heuristic did not improve its forecasts over the five months we scored. Fitting a calibration on the last 28 or 56 days of errors, or shifting the analogues by their recent error, left the average error score unchanged or worse at the eight MVA-only substations and the six signed-MW substations. The Gaussian fit lowered the score on cold winter nights (0.90 points at the eight) but raised it at the six in Christmas week, so the faithful heuristic stays the benchmark that the neural-network and XGBoost models are compared against. also relevant
    00470 first-release-demand-forecaster-assembly-and-comparison Two pieces of evidence point to different first-release forecasters. Experiment 425's arm A (one network pretrained on ERA5 and CAMS weather, five seeds averaged) has the lowest CRPS at the eight MVA-only Primaries. Experiment 454's K2-S1 (two lagged networks mixed with two lagless networks) has the lowest CRPS on rows with no labelled event at the signed-MW six. Scored on one set of rows, the simplest candidate within the stated tolerance of the best is a pretrained network without a lagless head at the eight, and the lagged-plus-lagless mixture at the six. inconclusive candidate Pretraining the neural network on past weather and solar data (ERA5 and CAMS) gets the first-release forecaster almost as far as adding a second, lagless forecast does. The pretrained network also holds up better on cold winter nights and 12 to 36 hours ahead. K2-425A scores 0.07 to 0.10 points of capacity better on ordinary rows, but that gap is within the noise of five months, so the lagged-plus-lagless design adds nothing the data can confirm over pretraining. The first release therefore rests on a choice between the plain five-seed pretrained network (simplest, no lagless forecast, no GAM) and the same network with the GAM forecast as an extra input (0.9% better at the eight, inside the noise, and needs the GAM at serving time). Every score here is selection-biased, because the five months that choose the candidate also score it. also relevant
    00469 lag-trust-cross-fitted-trust-fit-twelve-folds The out-of-fold lagged head of experiment 463 is too weak because each of its 6 folds trains on only 5/6 of the rows, so its learned trust weight sits below 0.5 and loses to the fixed 50/50 mixture (B-mix). With 12 folds, each fold trains on 11/12 of the rows, the out-of-fold lagged head is stronger, the learned lagged weight approaches 0.5, and the trust weight beats B-mix. not supported not-for-production A finer cross-fit (12 folds instead of 6) does not make the learned trust weight beat the fixed 50/50 mixture of the lagged and lagless forecasts. On rows with no labelled event at the six signed-megawatt substations the score is unchanged (+0.002 points of effective capacity against 50/50), and the learned weight falls from 0.41 to 0.34 instead of rising towards 0.5. Keep the fixed 50/50 mixture. Interim: five scored months, one seed, and NGED's event labels are incomplete, so rows with no labelled event may hold unlogged events. also relevant
    00467 anchor-plus-penalised-lag-correction-network-for-the-lagless-forecast A forecast made of the frozen lagless forecast plus a small network that reads the three origin lags, the surprise at each lag, and the lead time, and shifts and rescales the lagless mixture with an L2 penalty pulling its outputs to zero, scores as well as the lagless forecast on rows where the lags are corrupted, and better on rows with no labelled event, so one anchor plus one small correction replaces the two networks of B-mix. partly supported not-for-production A frozen forecast made without recent power readings (the lagless forecast), plus a 32-unit correction that reads the recent readings, is the best design with one lagless neural network and a small correction that we have tried on rows with no labelled event. At the six Primaries metered in signed megawatts it scores 3.21 points of capacity, against 3.71 for the lagless forecast alone and 3.50 for B-mix (a fixed 50/50 mixture of the lagless forecast and a neural network that reads the readings). The readings carry 0.40 of the 0.50-point gain over the lagless forecast. The design fails the rule we wrote before reading the scores, which required it to be no worse than the lagless forecast inside real load-shift events. On actuals corrected to the normal running arrangement (NRA), it is no better than the lagless forecast there and 0.22 points worse than B-mix (not significant). The penalty on the correction never limited it, so the next step is a penalty that does, or a correction that ignores readings inside events. Interim and unaudited, five scored months. also relevant
    00465 median-day-and-week-lags-for-load-shift-robustness Replacing the day lag and the week lag by the median of the 7 most recent same-time-of-day readings and of the 3 most recent same-time-of-week readings makes the median of an XGBoost demand forecast move by under 15% of a step added to the readings of the last 48 hours (against 38% to 41% for the single-reading lags of experiment 392), at a loss of under 0.05 points of capacity on rows with no labelled event. partly supported not-for-production Median day and week lags make the forecast more robust to short events and stuck readings, not more accurate, and the manager decides whether that is worth porting. A step of 5% of capacity in the last 48 hours moves the median forecast by 0.11 of the step at the six Primaries metered in signed megawatts, against 0.19 for the original lags (leads of 24 to 72 hours). The shielding fades at 7 days, a dropout and a spike are not shielded, and the loss on rows with no labelled event (+0.050) is of the same size as the 0.06 difference between two fits of the original lags. Interim and unaudited, four scored months. also relevant
    00464 solar-efficiency-history-stage-0-post-hoc-scaling Part of the solar forecast error is a slow change in each farm's efficiency (soiling, degradation, a level shift). The ratio of actual power to the power the observed weather should have produced, taken as a trailing median over weeks to months, estimates that efficiency, so multiplying the stored forecast quantiles by it lowers the quantile score at the farms whose output level changed (Solar A, Solar B, and Solar D, from experiment 453) and leaves the other farms unchanged. not supported not-for-production Scaling the stored solar forecasts by each farm's recent efficiency (actual power over the power the observed weather should have produced) did not lower the quantile score at any of the six farms, including the three whose output level changed between years. The best of 18 methods moved one farm by -0.46% and made the others worse, so slow efficiency change is not the error that limits the solar forecast. This was tested on the stored a2_member forecasts only. The later stages, which feed the ratio to the network, have no evidence behind them and stop. also relevant
    00463 lag-trust-cross-fitted-trust-fit-seed-zero Experiment 404's learned trust weight (B1) loses to the fixed 50/50 mixture (B-mix) partly because the lagged head is fitted in sample on the same rows where the trust weight is fitted, while the lagless table is out of sample on those rows (mechanism M11). A trust weight fitted on out-of-fold lagged heads and the cross-fitted lagless table, so that both heads are out of sample on the rows where the trust is fitted, matches B-mix on rows with no labelled event and beats it inside real load-shift events. not supported not-for-production The learned trust weight of experiment 404 lost to the fixed 50/50 mixture at least partly because the lagged forecast was fitted on the same rows as the weight. When the weight is fitted on lagged forecasts from neural networks that did not see those rows, the learned weight matches the 50/50 mixture on rows with no labelled event (-0.02 points of effective capacity at the six signed-megawatt substations) but does not beat the 50/50 mixture inside real events (+0.06 points), so keep the fixed 50/50 mixture. Interim: five scored months, one seed, and National Grid Electricity Distribution's (NGED's) event labels are incomplete, so rows with no labelled event may hold unlogged events. also relevant
    00462 lag-trust-mirrored-genuine-level-change-worlds Every training world so far (experiments 402, 404, 440, and 446) keeps a clean target under every injected jump, so discounting every jump is optimal and a neighbour mirror carries no information. When the training worlds also contain genuine level changes (the lags and the target move together) that carry no mirror at the neighbours (a mirror in the training worlds always means a load shift with a clean target, as in experiment 402), a lag network given the neighbours' surprise (S3) can use the mirror, and a network given randomised neighbours (S3-rw) cannot. A pass would show only that a network can use a mirror when the training world makes the mirror informative. It would not be evidence about real Primaries, because no labelled genuine level change exists in the real data. inconclusive not-for-production The lag network that sees its neighbours (S3-amb) did not learn to follow a level change that no neighbour mirrors any better than the same network given random neighbours (S3-rw-amb), but the worlds of this experiment could hardly have shown it: even a perfect forecaster would follow only 0.05 to 0.08 more of a step without a mirror than with one. The result is inconclusive by design. S3-amb passes the real-row checks against U1n-2, so it is not harmful, and it shows no gain that would justify adopting it. A 5% genuine share, which raises the Bayes-optimal gap, is the follow-up that could still show the effect. All numbers are interim, from four test months and two seeds. also relevant
    00461 wind-and-solar-retrain-with-p99-9-effective-capacity If effective capacity is the 99.9th percentile of each series' earlier power instead of the 99th percentile of its whole record, then retraining the best wind network and the solar networks keeps the quantile score in MW and brings the pooled share of actuals above the table-P q99 to at most 1.5%, because the networks no longer clip their upper tail at a capacity the series exceeds. partly supported candidate Setting each wind farm's capacity to the 99.9th percentile of its earlier power and retraining keeps the forecast as accurate and changes the share of actuals above the 99% quantile only slightly (0.96% to 0.88%; all three farms were already under 1.5%). For solar the same change meets the pre-registered rule only by a hair (share 1.50%, score 0.4% worse), fails it if any one series is dropped, makes Solar A's score 2.3% worse, and leaves Solar E at 2.4%. also relevant
    00460 lag-dropout-network-forecast-disagreement-as-a-trust-and-width-signal When one neural network, trained with random dropout of its three power lags, gives a different forecast median with all lags, with each lag alone, and with no lags, the rows where these forecasts disagree are the rows where the lags are wrong (rows whose target lies outside every labelled load-shift event but whose lags lie inside one). The amount of disagreement can therefore widen the bands, and can tilt a mixture of the all-lags forecast and the no-lags forecast toward the no-lags forecast, better than a fixed 50/50 mixture of a lagged and a lagless network (B-mix). not supported not-for-production When one neural network hides its power lags, its forecast barely changes (the median moves by about 4% of the width of the 90% interval), so the disagreement between its passes does not show which lags are wrong, and it cannot widen bands or choose a mixture weight better than a fixed rule. Hiding lags at random during training also made the network slightly worse than the same network trained on all lags. In one post hoc comparison at 12 to 36 hours a three-seed ensemble beat B-mix, and a lag-trust signal needs a forecast that actually depends on the lags. also relevant
    00458 constant-prequential-and-offset-corrected-combinations-of-lagged-and-lagless-heads A better combination of the stored lagged head and the stored lagless head than the fixed 50/50 mixture B-mix (a weight per lead band, a weight from the heads' recent losses, or the lagless head plus a shrunk recent level offset) matches B-mix inside real load-shift events and loses nothing on rows with no labelled event, so an explicit event detector is unnecessary for the forecast of the normal running arrangement (NRA). not supported not-for-production No simple rule for combining the lagged and lagless forecasts beats the fixed 50/50 mixture, so keep 50/50 and do not spend 50 neural-network fits on a weight per lead band. The weight that fits best falls from about 0.75 on the lagged forecast at short leads to about 0.5 at days 6 to 10, but it moves by 0.2 or more from month to month. A shrunk recent-level offset on the lagless forecast helps on rows with no labelled event at the six (-0.23 points, not significant) and costs 1.21 points inside real events. Interim and unaudited, four scored months. also relevant
    00454 lag-trust-mixture-and-ensemble-constructs-for-lagged-and-lagless-heads Part of the gain of B-mix (a fixed 50/50 mixture of a lagged network and a lagless forecast) over one lagged network comes from averaging several networks, and part from mixing two different kinds of forecast. Replacing the lagged network by S1 (the lagged network that also sees how far each of its three lags lies from the lagless forecast) makes the mixture better still. partly supported candidate Mixing lagged and lagless forecasts beats averaging several lagged forecasts, so the lagless forecast adds accuracy that more lagged networks do not: two lagged networks averaged recover only 30% of the gain of a 50/50 mixture. Giving the lagged network the distance between each of its three lagged power readings and the lagless forecast (S1) and mixing two S1 networks with two lagless forecasts scores 0.12 points of capacity better than B-mix on rows with no labelled event at the six metered-in-megawatts Primaries, at twice the networks per forecast. Whether the gain survives inside real events is not settled, because the event rows are few. also relevant
    00453 effective-capacity-upper-bound-data-look The farms' historical power holds few implausible high values, so an effective capacity set at the p99.9 or the maximum would cut the share of actuals above 1 and the share above q99 without harming the CRPS. partly supported not-for-production The wind farms hold almost no implausible high values: 98% to 100% of half-hours above p99.9 are plausible, so a capacity at p99.9 or higher is safe, and p99.9 takes the pooled share of actuals above q99 from 1.41% under the current rule to 0.85% (Farm A 1.76% to 1.05%). The solar series hold a two-half-hour spike on the second day of Solar E's record and months at an earlier, higher output level, so the maximum is unsafe, but p99.9 takes the pooled share above q99 from 3.42% under the current rule to 1.48%. Recommend p99.9 of the earlier power for wind and solar. also relevant
    00451 farm-a-upper-tail-exceedances-and-capacity-state Farm A's share of observations above q99 after table P (1.76%, experiment 450) comes either from a table that is too coarse for Farm A, or from a capacity step that puts output above a q99 scaled to the old capacity. partly supported not-for-production Farm A's upper-tail failure after table P is partly a clipping effect: raising the clip on every rescaled quantile from 1.0 to the farm's largest earlier power over capacity cuts Farm A's share above q99 from 1.76% to 1.17%, and all three farms are under the 1.5% limit (pooled 0.96%). No flagged capacity reduction explains the exceedances, and a finer table makes the share worse, so no tail-weight fits were needed. also relevant
    00450 wind-upper-tail-check-with-table-p-and-sweep The wind upper-tail rule that stopped every experiment 429 arm (more than 1.5% of observations above q99 at true days 1 to 10) is met once the stage-0 recalibration table P of experiment 435 is applied, and the plain pooled wind network (429's member_plain) has at least one setting, among hidden width, depth, encoder width, mixture components, and ERA5 pretraining, whose change lowers its quantile score by the 0.10-point wind margin. not supported not-for-production Table P nearly meets the wind upper-tail limit when the three farms are pooled (1.4% of observations above q99, limit 1.5%) but not at Farm A (1.8%), and no change to the size of the plain wind network or ERA5 pretraining moves its score by the 0.10-point margin or fixes Farm A. The wind network is at its plateau on these inputs, so the open problem is mainly Farm A's windy half-hours, not the network. also relevant
    00448 s1-network-assembly-ladder-with-pretraining-b-mix-and-seed-pool The S1 network (pooled plain mixture density network, Primary embedding, ensemble mean and spread once, hand-made calendar columns, monthly refits, three origin lags plus the surprise at each lag) improves with each of three components added one at a time (ERA5-and-CAMS pretraining then ensemble fine-tuning; the fixed 50/50 B-mix of the S1 and lagless output distributions; a five-seed pool), and the network with pretraining and the pool together scores lower CRPS at days 1 to 10 than every one of its parts and than the monthly XGBoost recipe. partly supported not-for-production Averaging five random-seed fits of the S1 neural network lowered its error by about 0.05 points at both groups of Primary substations, and no other add-on helped. Pretraining on past-weather estimates with the recent-power inputs masked, and the fixed 50/50 mix with the lagless neural network, each raised the error slightly at the six and eight series and much more at D15 and D18. The assembly of pretraining and the pool was worse than the pool alone (+0.059 at the six and +0.047 at the eight). The pool's gain of 0.048 at the six is below the minimum detectable effect of 0.057.
