    Final models: architectures and training regimes for the production port

    This page states, for each forecast type, which neural network the one-year backtest used and how it was trained. It is written for the person who ports the models into nged-substation-forecast. Each section covers one forecast type and has the same blocks: architecture, training regime, post-processing, scores, code, input tables, and port notes. Most choices link to the experiment page that decided them. The page adds no new scoring. Where two pages disagree, the page says so and names the current one.

    No neural network on this page is adopted for production. Every score comes from one backtest year that overlaps the development months. The backtest is described in experiment 668: one forecast origin per day at 12:00 UTC from 2025-11-01 to 2026-10-31, and 672 half-hours (14 days) per origin. The origin months from 2026-07 lie in the window that earlier experiments kept as a final test, so those months are descriptive and no choice rests on them. The experiment 677 page, which builds up the Candidate demand neural network one component at a time, is summarised in the Primaries section.

    Where to start. A porter should read, in order, the "Production code that exists today" section, the "Output contract" section, the section of the forecast type to port, the "Open issues for the porter" section, and the "Open questions" list. The "Tests to write" section lists the checks that a port should pass before any score is compared. Each forecast-type section has a "Code" subsection that names the research files that produced the neural network, with the pull request that last changed each, and the production code that already does the same job. Each forecast-type section also has an "Input tables" subsection that names the cached tables the fit reads, the script that builds each table, and the order to run those scripts. The research folders are experiment folders under ml-experiments/, and their scripts import modules from earlier experiment folders by file name. The appendix lists those imports for each forecast type, and it covers imports only: a script that writes a cached table but is imported by no fit appears in the "Input tables" subsections and nowhere in the appendix. The production repository has no neural-network code and no torch dependency, so every neural network on this page is new code there. Backtest files are private and live in data/ml-experiments/backtest_year_2025-11_to_2026-10/, outside git.

    Four other pages hold rules that the neural networks follow, and this page does not repeat them. The data-processing page states how the observed power, the weather, and the series are cleaned and which dates get a regime flag. The three evaluation-protocol pages, demand, wind, and solar, state the scoring rules for each forecast type. The glossary defines the project's terms.

    Nine terms recur on this page, and the definitions below fix their meaning. NGED is National Grid Electricity Distribution. A Primary is a primary substation, a BSP is a bulk supply point, and a GSP is a grid supply point. MVA is apparent power, which cannot be negative, and MW is real power. The Candidate is the demand neural network that outputs the NRA forecast. The prevailing output is a second forecast that allows for a load shift still in progress at the forecast origin, made by a small neural network called the survival head, which estimates how likely the shift is to persist. A calibrated copy of a backtest file is a copy whose quantile bands are rescaled (see "Walk-forward band calibration"). The lag-enabled neural network reads recent observed power. CRPS is the continuous ranked probability score, computed here as twice the mean pinball loss over the 11 quantile levels, divided by capacity, times 100. A CRPS of 4 therefore means 4% of capacity. The normalised MAE (mean absolute error) of the median is the mean absolute error of the 50% quantile in the same units. Smaller is better for both. The q05-to-q95 coverage is the share of observed power inside the band from the 5% quantile to the 95% quantile, and its nominal value is 0.90. NRA is the normal running arrangement, the electricity network configuration without any load shift. ECMWF is the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts, ENS is its ensemble forecast of 51 members, ERA5 is its reanalysis of past weather, and CAMS is the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service. A load shift is a transfer of demand between neighbouring Primaries, usually for days to weeks, that NGED's logs record incompletely.

"Production code that exists today" and "Output contract" describe the production repository and the file format that the backtest used. One section per forecast type follows: the 16 Primaries, the BSPs and GSPs, wind, and solar. Then come "Walk-forward band calibration", "Production-port notes across forecast types", "Open issues for the porter", "Where the pages disagree", "Open questions", "Tests to write", and the appendix.

    The backtest files identify a series by its original time_series_id, and the experiment pages mix that identifier with NGED's D-labels.     • Primaries. Eight Primaries are metered in MW (D5, D9, D10, D11, D14, D15, D16, and D18) and eight in MVA, which is apparent power and cannot be negative (D2, D3, D4, D8, D12, D19, D21, and D22). D15 and D18 have a short history, and the backtest cuts their rows before 2025-03-01, as every recent experiment does.
    • BSPs and GSPs. D13 Skegness is time_series_id 2 and D17 Boston is 1. D6 Walpole is 27 and D1 Bicker Fen is 28. All four are signed MW series, so a negative value is net export.
    • Generators. The three wind farms and the six solar farms are anonymised as Wind A to C and Solar A to F. This page lists no generator identifier, capacity, coordinate, or dated series.
    • Capacity. Every score is divided by the series' effective capacity, the production effective-capacity table with one value per series. That capacity is a normalising constant and not a limit, for demand and for generators alike. The highest quantile reaches 2.1 times the table value at D15, 2.2 times at the GSP D1, 1.4 times at the GSP D6, and 1.35 to 1.7 times at five more Primaries (D5, D12, D16, D18, and D22). The wind and solar quantiles exceed it by up to 12% (experiment 668, Caveats). The files keep these quantiles on purpose, because the maintainer decided to leave quantiles above capacity uncapped.

    Production code that exists today

    The production repository (openclimatefix/nged-substation-forecast, read at commit a0e66b220 of main) has a model interface, an XGBoost forecaster, and orchestration, and none of the neural networks, quantile output, or post-processing on this page. Paths below are relative to that repository. The sections for each forecast type say which of these pieces apply.

    • Model interface. packages/ml_core/src/ml_core/base_forecaster.py defines BaseForecaster with the class constants MODEL_NAME, CONFIG_CLASS, and feature_engineer, and the abstract train, predict, save, load, and trained_time_series_ids. Both train and predict take a pt.LazyFrame[AllFeatures], and AllFeatures is keyed by ensemble_member. BaseForecasterConfig carries selected_features, ml_flow_experiment_id, experiment_name, weather_source, training_strategy, random_seed, and model_release. predict stamps power_fcst_model_name, power_fcst_model_version (from model_release), experiment_name, ml_flow_experiment_id, and fold_id on every row. The interface allows per-series or pooled models.
    • Existing forecasters. XGBoostForecaster (packages/xgboost_forecaster, conf/model/xgboost.yaml) trains one xgb.Booster per time_series_id on 24 features and a squared-error objective. ManualHeuristicForecaster and ClimatologyForecaster are in packages/baseline_forecasters. No forecaster branches on whether a series is a Primary, a BSP, a GSP, a wind farm, or a solar farm. torch appears in no pyproject.toml.
    • Output contract. PowerForecast (packages/contracts/src/contracts/power_schemas.py, line 414) holds one power_fcst per ensemble_member, with the primary key (time_series_id, power_fcst_init_time, valid_time, ensemble_member). It has no quantile columns, although ClimatologyForecaster encodes quantile levels as member ranks (member k stands for the level (2k+1)/102), and it has no column that separates a normal-running output from a prevailing output. DELIVERY_QUANTILES in packages/contracts/src/contracts/common.py lists 13 levels (0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.35, 0.50, 0.65, 0.80, 0.90, 0.95, 0.98, and 0.99) and is used for metric labels. The research files hold 11 quantiles (0.01, 0.05, 0.10, 0.25, 0.40, 0.50, 0.60, 0.75, 0.90, 0.95, and 0.99). TimeSeriesMetadata.units is MW or MVA, and power_fcst carries the series' own unit. The next section maps the backtest file columns onto these fields.
    • Weather. Nwp (packages/contracts/src/contracts/weather_schemas.py, line 121) stores ECMWF ENS only, at H3 resolution 5, with members 0 to 50, written by packages/delta_store/src/delta_store/nwp.py. Production does not store ERA5, CAMS, or UKV. No feature code computes the mean and standard deviation over the 51 members, although packages/ml_core/src/ml_core/metrics.py computes ensemble-mean metrics. The trained model sees the control member, and every member is scored separately at predict time. _attach_containing_nwp_cell in packages/ml_core/src/ml_core/features/tabular_feature_engineer.py maps each series to its H3 cell.
    • Analysis proxy. The production analysis proxy is the control member (member 0) of the freshest NWP run that holds a value for each valid time, filled column by column from the next-freshest run where a cell is null. It is select_analysis_proxy in packages/weather_utils/src/weather_utils/analysis_proxy.py, and the weather lags of the feature pipeline use it. The research analysis proxy is different: it is the ensemble mean of the freshest run that had been published by the forecast origin, which is the first day of the previous 00Z run (ml-experiments/00437-*/e00437_features.py, module docstring). The two definitions differ in the member (control against ensemble mean) and in the fill rule, so the production function does not reproduce the research columns.
    • Power inputs. packages/ml_core/src/ml_core/features/_lags.py builds power and weather lags, and _nullify_leaky_lags nulls a power lag that is shorter than or equal to the lead time. The production code has no constant for the 6-hour power delay. NWP_PUBLICATION_DELAY_HOURS is 9 (packages/ml_core/src/ml_core/features/_nwp.py). scan_cleaned_power in packages/nged_data/src/nged_data/storage.py reads only rows without a drop_reason, and flag_nged_power in cleaning.py has one rule, substation_zero.
    • Capacity. EffectiveCapacity (power_schemas.py, line 605) is a static 99th percentile of the absolute power per series over the whole cleaned history, written by the effective_capacity asset in src/nged_substation_forecast/defs/cv_assets.py. It is a metric denominator, and AllFeatures has no capacity input.
    • Orchestration. trained_cv_model and cv_power_forecasts in defs/cv_assets.py train and score on the folds of conf/cv/default.yaml, on demand. promoted_model in defs/promotion_assets.py copies a chosen MLflow run to the production model path. live_forecasts in defs/live_forecast_assets.py loads the promoted model with load_forecaster_from_dir, runs at 00:00, 06:00, 12:00, and 18:00 UTC over a 14-day horizon (LIVE_FORECAST_HORIZON), and writes with write_power_forecasts. The ecmwf_ens asset ingests the 00Z run from 10:30 UTC and retries every 30 minutes, up to 8 times, when the run is not yet complete (defs/schedules.py), so the 06:00 UTC slot uses the previous day's run, and the 12:00 UTC slot does too when the day's run is late. No schedule refits a model.
    • Post-processing. The production code has no clamp, calibration, or band-widening step. power_fcst is the raw output of predict.
    • Release record. docs/live_service/model-releases.md records a numbered release (model-N), stamped as power_fcst_model_version. A release needs a cross-validation run named model-release-N, a promotion, a git tag, and a log row. The log has no rows yet.

The sources are the "File format" and "Known limitations" sections of the README in data/ml-experiments/backtest_year_2025-11_to_2026-10/, the file schemas read from the stored files, and the checks of experiment 667 and experiment 668.

    • Rows. Each origin has 672 rows per series, at leads of 0 to 335.5 hours in half-hour steps, and primaries.parquet holds one row of each kind (NRA and prevailing) per key. The last half-hour of a forecast ends 30 minutes before the 14-day mark. The files hold 7,848,960 rows for the 16 Primaries (16 series, 365 origins, 672 half-hours, and 2 outputs) and 490,560 rows each for the BSPs and the GSPs.
    • Sort order and compression. Each file is sorted by time_series_id, power_fcst_init_time, then valid_time, and primaries.parquet has a fourth key, NRA_or_prevailing. The Parquet metadata does not declare the order. Every column uses ZSTD compression.
    • Checks the files pass. No duplicate key, no null or non-finite quantile, no crossing in any adjacent quantile pair, and UTC throughout (experiment 667, "Checks").
    • Two conflicts with the production schema, stated as facts. The production PowerForecast constrains valid_time to be strictly after power_fcst_init_time, and the lead-0 row of the backtest files has valid_time equal to the origin. The research code also has no ensemble_member axis, because each row is a set of 11 quantiles and not a deterministic forecast per member. Neither conflict is settled on any page read for this one.

    Demand at the 16 Primaries

    Architecture

    The Primaries are forecast by the Candidate, a modular demand neural network that outputs 11 quantiles, plus a small prevailing head. The Candidate is arm P7+D50 of experiment 612 and experiment 613 (P7 is the neighbour power summaries and D50 a 50% dropout of the signal columns, described under "Neighbour and gap signals"), which experiment 620 tested on the final-test months. The Candidate's output is the NRA output. The prevailing output comes from the survival-hazard head of experiment 652, with the 14 extra lagged inputs of experiment 681. Experiment 667 is the first backtest of the two outputs, and experiment 668 restates every detail below and is the current page where the two differ.

    • One row is a (Primary, ensemble run, valid half-hour). The neural network runs once per ensemble run, on summaries of the 51 members. The weather is the ECMWF ensemble (ENS) 00Z run of the origin day, which the neural network treats as available at 09:00 UTC. The forecast is made at that native 09:00 UTC origin and relabelled to the origin of the production forecast contract, 12:00 UTC, by adding 3 hours. Valid times before 12:00 UTC are dropped. The lead runs to 339 hours, because ENS summaries reach about 351 hours.
    • Weather inputs. The mean and the standard deviation (ddof=0) over the 51 members of 7 variables: shortwave and longwave radiation, 2 m temperature, 2 m dew point, surface pressure, precipitation (after log1p of the value clipped at zero), and 10 m wind speed. All 14 columns are z-scored with the training rows' mean and standard deviation and clipped at plus or minus 5. Each series reads the weather of the H3 resolution-5 cell (a hexagonal grid cell) that holds it.
    • Calendar and sun inputs. 21 columns: the cosine of the solar zenith angle, the sine and cosine of the azimuth, the sine and cosine of the local hour (Europe/London) at periods of 24 hours and 12 hours, a one-hot day of week, a COVID flag, three Christmas columns, and three bank-holiday columns. The COVID flag is 1 from 2020-03-23 to 2021-07-19 inclusive. The three Christmas columns are a flag for a valid day within 14 days of Christmas, the signed days to Christmas over 14, and one minus the absolute value of that ratio. The three bank-holiday columns are a flag for a bank holiday, the signed days to the nearest bank holiday (clipped at 7 days) over 7, and a flag for a nearest holiday under 7 days away (e00087_common.py, e00397_prep.py). The lead enters as the lead in hours over 336 and its square root. The 7 means, the 7 standard deviations, the 21 calendar and sun columns, and the 2 lead columns make the 37 "lag-free" columns, which the lag columns below extend.
    • Thermal-lag and solar-gain inputs. 14 columns from experiment 437 and experiment 569: trailing temperature means (3, 6, and 24 hours), temperature changes (3 and 6 hours), the day's minimum and maximum, the count of cold-snap days, trailing shortwave means (3, 6, and 24 hours), the 3-hour change in the clear-sky index, and two columns from the previous day's CAMS irradiance. Before the origin they use the analysis proxy, a stand-in for observed weather. The research proxy is the ensemble mean of the freshest run published by the origin, which is the first day of the previous 00Z ensemble run (e00437_features.py). It uses no ERA5, because ERA5 arrives about 5 days late. The production proxy in packages/weather_utils/src/weather_utils/analysis_proxy.py differs: it takes the control member (member 0) of the freshest run and fills each column from the next-freshest run where the freshest run is null. A port needs the ensemble-mean version.
    • Power lags. A day lag and a week lag. Each is the latest same-half-hour reading at least 24 hours before the neural network's own 09:00 origin, entered as a z-scored value, a missing flag, and an age code. The age code is the hours from the lag's time to the target time, over 336. A missing lag takes the largest age code (24 hours times the ceiling of 375 over 24, over 336 for the day lag, and 168 hours times the ceiling of 375 over 168, over 336 for the week lag, where 375 is the longest lead of 351 hours plus 24). The 8 lag columns are the value, missing flag, and age code of each lag, plus the half-hour grid index of each lag's time (columns 43 and 44). Those two indices are not network inputs: they address the expected-weather table, which holds the standardised ensemble-mean weather (7 columns) and datetime (21 columns) at every grid time, taken from the freshest run issued at or before that time (e00558_lags.py, e00560_model.py). A knot position, the position of the valid time along the monthly offset knots, is one further input column (column 45). The newest lag is therefore the 09:00 reading of the day before the origin day, 27 hours before the contract's 12:00 origin, so the production contract's rule that power inputs be at least 6 hours old needed no change.
    • Neighbour and gap signals. 60 columns read only by the lag encoder. For each lag: the gap between the observed power and a lag-free forecast table (the median of the lags-masked neural network of the same seed at its freshest earlier origin with a lead of at most 24 hours). The signals are exponentially weighted means of that gap with half-lives of 1, 3, and 7 days. Over the electrical neighbours of the Primary, the sum, mean, minimum, and maximum of those quantities are scaled by capacity ratio, and the own-plus-neighbour sum, a neighbour count, and a flag for no neighbour are added. The neighbours are the metered Primaries that NGED's "Primary Substation Interconnections" list links to the Primary (experiment 610). The list is two NGED files in NGED/substation_hierarchy_2026-10-15/ under the research data folder. Monitor Direction.csv maps each TimeSeriesID to a SubstationNumber, and Primary Substation Interconnections.csv holds the pairs PrimarySubstation1 and PrimarySubstation2. Two Primaries are neighbours when an interconnection pair joins their substation numbers (nged_neighbours in e00400_blend.py). The capacity ratio divides by capacity_mw of site_map.csv, read through sites() in e00578_common.py. During fine-tuning, all signal columns of a random 50% of rows are set to no information (the "D50" dropout).
    • Series identity. An 8-dimensional embedding of the Primary. The target is cleaned power divided by effective capacity, then z-scored per Primary.

    The encoders and the mixer have fixed widths. The weather encoder (7 means, the 8-wide embedding, and the 14 thermal-lag and solar-gain columns, 29 inputs) and the datetime encoder (21 columns and the embedding, 29 inputs) each map to 64 hidden GELU units and then 32 outputs. One shared lag encoder runs twice, on the day lag and then the week lag. Its 143 inputs are the lag's 3 columns, the 32-wide embeddings that the weather and datetime encoders give for the weather expected at the lag's own time, the 8-wide embedding of the Primary, an 8-wide embedding of the lag's identity, and the 60 signal columns. (The lag encoder of the K1 network that the Candidate is grafted onto has 83 inputs, because it has no signal columns; see "Training regime".) It maps to 64 and then 32, and the two outputs are concatenated. The mixer takes 145 inputs (the weather and datetime outputs of 32 each, the 64 lag outputs, the embedding, the seven spread columns, and the two lead columns), has two hidden layers of 64 units with 10% dropout, and outputs 9 numbers: the logits, means, and log standard deviations of a 3-component Gaussian mixture. No merged page states the parameter count of the Candidate. The weights and biases of the linear layers named above add up to 33,385 (4,000 for each of the weather and datetime encoders, 11,296 for the lag encoder, and 14,089 for the mixer). The embeddings and the offset knots (16 Primaries times the number of monthly knots) add to that figure. The stored state dicts were not opened for this page, so a port should assert its own count against a stored .pt file.

    • Offset. A per-Primary piecewise-linear function of time, with one knot for each month start from 2019-09, is added to the three component means (experiment 565, experiment 572). Fine-tuning trains only the newest knot, and a penalty of 10 times the mean squared difference between neighbouring knots keeps the function smooth.
    • Quantiles. The 11 quantiles come from the mixture's cumulative distribution by 40 bisection steps on a range of plus or minus 40 standard deviations. The mixture weights are a log-softmax and each standard deviation is exp of the raw output clamped to plus or minus 3. The quantiles are rescaled to power and sorted within the row.
    • Loss. The negative log-likelihood of the mixture on the standardised target, weighted, plus the knot penalty.

    Truncating the output distribution at zero is an option for the Primaries that cannot go negative, and only experiment 684 has tested it. The backtest files carry a post hoc clamp instead (see "Post-processing"). Experiment 684 refitted the Candidate with the mixture conditioned on power at or above zero, so the negative log-likelihood adds the log of the mixture's mass above zero and the quantiles are read from the conditional cumulative distribution. It tested the family at four Primaries, D12, D22, D8, and D5 (identifiers 14, 15, 26, and 17), on the test months 2026-02 and 2026-05, and on the NRA output only. D5 is metered in MW and never goes negative in the observed power. The CRPS changed against the stored forecasts by +0.030 (D12), -0.015 (D22), -0.070 (D8), and -0.010 (D5), with no negative q01 left. The verdict was partly supported and the experiment's own status is candidate. A gamma mixture trained inside the same shared neural network failed its stop rule at two Primaries and was dropped, and it also worsened the Primaries that kept a Gaussian output. Whether the truncated family helps the other five MVA Primaries (D2, D3, D4, D19, and D21), and the prevailing output, was not tested.

    The prevailing output comes from a second small neural network, the head R-haz, which mixes the Candidate's quantiles with a shifted copy of them. The Candidate's 11 quantiles define a piecewise-linear cumulative distribution with two tail knots, 1.5 times the outer spacing beyond the 1% and 99% quantiles. The prevailing distribution is the mixture (1 - p) times the Candidate's distribution plus p times the Candidate's distribution shifted by a learned depth, with the depth uncertain at three Gauss-Hermite points (defined under "Layers and outputs" below; experiment 647, with the form restated in experiment 652 and experiment 668). The 11 prevailing quantiles are read off that mixture and are monotone by construction. When p is 0, the prevailing rows equal the NRA rows. R-haz sits on the frozen Candidate and has these parts:

    • Inputs, 93 in all. The head reads 58 origin features (the gap and neighbour signals above, built from lags), 4 age columns (the log of the departure's age in days, that age over 30, a gate flag that is always set to zero, and the age of the freshest lag), and 3 row inputs (the lead over 240 hours and the lower and upper half-widths of the Candidate's band). Together these make 65 columns. The 14 extra lagged power inputs of experiment 681, each as a residual plus a missing flag, add 28 columns.
    • The 14 extra lagged inputs. Every time is measured back from the neural network's origin (09:00 UTC), and every residual is the cleaned power minus the Candidate's lag-free median forecast for that time, as a fraction of capacity. The inputs are the residuals at 6, 9, 12, 18, 24, 36, 48, and 72 hours, the means of the residuals over the last 6, 12, and 24 hours (each window ending 6 hours before the origin), and three differences between the means of four adjacent 24-hour windows. A mean is missing when under half of its window holds a reading. The newest reading any input uses is 6 hours old, and a test perturbs every later half-hour and checks that no input moves.
    • Layers and outputs. Two hidden layers of 64 ReLU units and 16 outputs: a signed depth (0.5 times the hyperbolic tangent of the first output, so up to half of capacity), a spread, and 14 daily hazards. The spread is 0.01 + 0.05 * softplus(output) of the second output. Each hazard is softplus(output - 3). The weights of the last layer are multiplied by 0.1 and its bias is set to zero at initialisation. The survival probability p is the exponential of minus the hazards accumulated over the days before the lead, and the share of day d that counts is clamp(lead / 24 - d, 0, 1) for lead in hours. The value of p is 0 when the departure is younger than half a day. The shifted copy sits at the depth plus the spread times each of the three Gauss-Hermite nodes. Those nodes are -√3, 0, and √3 with weights 1/6, 2/3, and 1/6, which is the standard three-point rule for averaging over a normal distribution. The inputs are standardised with the mean and standard deviation of the training rows (the standard deviation floored at 0.001) and clipped at plus or minus 10. The head has 11,216 parameters (e00647_head.py, p667_head.py, e00647_fit.py).
    • The backtest files use the 14-input extra-lag set, and which set to port is undecided. Experiment 681 found that 14 extra inputs (set L3) make the prevailing output react 20.4 hours sooner (95% interval 16.3 to 24.8) to the start of an injected load shift, and 8 inputs (set L1) 19.1 hours sooner. The verdict was a small gain, under the 24 hours set in advance as worth porting. Only two real events could be read, and the 8-input set scored worst inside the real events. Experiment 687 measured a larger gain for extra lagged power (1.25 days sooner than the head of experiment 652) on a different bank of injected events and different folds, so the two reaction times are not comparable. The 14-input set was adopted for the backtest files by nged-ml-research PR 353 (Replace the prevailing rows of the Primaries backtest file with the extra-lag head).
    • The fallback head B-haz. With fewer than two earlier banks of injected shifts (the folds 2025-11 and 2025-12; a bank is the set of injected-shift rows built from one earlier test month, described under "Training regime"), the prevailing rows come from B-haz: the Candidate plus its own recent residual times a survival curve fitted on synthetic durations, with no fit on observed power. The fit tries every distribution family in s652.DISTS (experiment 652's list), weights each injected shift's duration, and keeps the family with the lowest negative log-likelihood. The log-normal won in both folds, with a shape of 1.147 and a scale of 21.3 days for 2025-11, and 1.142 and 22.1 days for 2025-12. The survival is conditional on the departure's age a in days: S(a + 1 + lead / 24) / S(a + 1), with a clipped to 0.5 to 100 and p set to 0 below 0.5 days. The residual is the mean of observed power minus the Candidate's median over the 2 to 8 days before the origin, using forecast rows with a lead of 24 to under 48 hours. The prevailing quantiles are sort(q + residual * p), where q holds the Candidate's quantiles and the residual is 0 where none exists (p667_fit.py, e00652_score.py, e00647_data.py). The prevailing rows equal the NRA rows on 74% of November origins and 65% of December origins, and on no origin from 2026-01. B-haz has no inputs that the extra lagged power could widen.

    Experiment 687 tried causal step-detector features for the prevailing head and did not adopt them. The features reacted 1.59 days sooner than the head of experiment 652 (95% interval 1.00 to 2.26 days sooner), but extra lagged power alone reacted 1.25 days sooner, so the features are not shown to beat plain lags (experiment 687). The prevailing head of the backtest therefore carries no detector features.

    Training regime

    Each of the 12 refits of the Candidate, one per test month, is a chain of four stages that sees only data from before its month: the K1 network, table T, the Candidate's fine-tune, and a prediction pass. The folds are the test months 2025-11 to 2026-10. Experiment 667 and experiment 668 give the settings below, which come from experiment 545 (the modular design), experiment 559 (frozen encoders and the three-step schedule), experiment 418 (ERA5 and CAMS pre-training), experiment 574 (the embargoed schedule), and experiment 599 (the refit rule).

    • The chain. chain.sh MONTH runs six steps in this order. pretrain and step2 run K1 through e00667_k1.py. table builds table T. fit is the Candidate's fine-tune with the held-out power hidden. ptable rebuilds table T over the scored window, and pfit is the Candidate's prediction pass that writes the forecasts. K1 is a separate network. e00667_k1.py is a copy of e00613_arm.py (experiment 613), which wraps arm A1 of e00591_arm.py (experiment 591): a shared lag encoder of 83 inputs with a lag-identity embedding and no signal columns, no auxiliary loss, the design 5 offset of experiment 565, the synthetic lag-only load shifts of experiment 584's design C1, and the 7-day embargo of experiment 599's design E3. e00667_k1.py asserts these settings.
    • Grafting the Candidate onto K1. The Candidate has 60 signal columns that K1 lacks. PermLagMdn.load_state_dict (e00613_nbmodel.py) pads K1's first lag-encoder layer with 60 zero-weight columns, so the Candidate starts as K1 and learns its signal weights only in the fine-tune. The 143-input lag encoder therefore does not pre-train: the 83-input encoder of K1 does.
    • Two phases. In the train phase (E00667_PHASE=train) the cleaned power is NaN from the first day of the fold month, so no code path can read a held-out reading, and the scored window stays closed. In the predict phase the window is open: table T is rebuilt over the scored window plus the month before it, the standardiser is checked against a hash stored in the train phase (check_fingerprint), and the network fitted in the train phase is reloaded. A port should hide all power after the cut-off in every refit, and test that rule.
    • Fold mechanics. e00667_fold.py patches experiment 574's pipeline to one cut-off per month (the first day of the month, at 00:00 UTC) and sets the longest lead to 339 hours. The control member's frames stop at the run of 2026-06-29, so a stub frame holding the run time stands in for later runs, and e00667_extend.py rebuilds every stored row from 2025-07-01, because the stored matrices dropped the half-hours that have no cleaned power.
    • Data. Cleaned power, with the substation_zero half-hours dropped (the one cleaning rule the backtest applied), and the D15 and D18 histories cut before 2025-03-01. The ENS history starts on 2024-04-01, so the early folds fine-tune on less ensemble data than the later folds.
    • Step 1, pre-training (K1). The weather, datetime, and 83-input lag encoders of K1 pre-train on ERA5 and CAMS (shortwave) as stand-in weather, in pseudo-runs every 2 days from 2019-09-01 at 09:00 UTC with leads of 0.5 to 351 hours (about 8.1 million rows for the 2026-07 fold). The pre-training rows end at least 3.5 hours before the refit time. Five Primaries start later (D11 on 2019-09-17, D10 on 2019-12-01, D5 on 2020-02-01, D14 on 2020-07-01, and D16 on 2021-02-01). ERA5 has no ensemble, so the seven spread columns hold the fold's median ensemble spread for each 24-hour lead band. All weights train, with AdamW, learning rate 0.0005, weight decay 0.001, batch 8,192, 20% of the rows per epoch, up to 60 epochs, patience 8, and gradient-norm clip 1.0.
    • Step 2, fresh mixer (K1). The pre-trained weights load except the mixer. A fresh mixer is drawn with seed 12345, the weather and datetime encoders are frozen, and training runs on the 730-day history with uniform weights and a 3-day embargo, at learning rate 0.0002 (0.0005 for the fresh mixer).
    • Table T. The table step runs K1's step-2 network with its lags masked, and stores the median of each (Primary, grid time) at the freshest earlier forecast origin with a lead of at most 24 hours (masked_quantiles and build_lag_free_table in e00598_signals.py). The signal columns and the 14 extra lags of the prevailing head are differences between observed power and table T.
    • Step 3, daily fine-tune (the Candidate). The fine-tune starts from the step-2 weights, padded as above, with the same frozen encoders and learning rate 0.0002. Row weights are 0.5 raised to the power (age in days divided by 90), a 90-day half-life. The refit rule drops rows whose valid time lies in the newest 7 days before the cut-off and halves the weight of rows 7 to 14 days old. Early stopping holds out one week in every 12 weeks of origin days, with 15 days purged before and 9 days after each held week, and uses at most 200,000 held rows. The ensemble weather is the input at this stage and at inference.
    • Seeds and widening. Two seeds are averaged quantile by quantile and sorted again. The backtest applies no widening. The final test of experiment 620 widened the band about the median by a factor of 1.0934, the mean of the five development-month factors of experiment 613, and experiment 668 states that it averaged five seeds.
    • Missing and delayed inputs. A flagged or absent lag reads as value 0, a missing flag, and the maximum age code. In training, 30% of origin days mask both lags and, of those, 15% mask only the day lag. Absent signal columns read as 0 with a missing flag. Neighbour-signal columns whose training spread is under 80% of their spread in the real inputs of the month before the fold receive matching Gaussian noise during training. All weather columns must be finite.
    • Injected load shifts. Each Primary receives random synthetic shifts, added to the lag inputs only, until they cover 5% of its row span: depth 15%, 30%, or 50% of capacity, length 3, 7, 15, or 30 days, and random sign. Each shift on a Primary is subtracted, scaled by the capacity ratio, from one electrical neighbour, so the neighbour summaries see the transfer.
    • Banks. Each month's R-haz trains on "banks" of injected-shift rows, one bank per earlier test month, built from the real history of that month with synthetic shifts and forecast by that month's Candidate chain (inference only), so fold M's head trains only on banks of months before M and on rows whose valid time is before the start of M. Each fit samples 60,000 real rows and 6,000 bank rows. Origin days 0 to 17 of an injected shift train the head and origin days from 21 onward validate it. The lag inputs of an injected shift are built from the same shifted power as its target, and the lag-only shifts of the bank use the month's later power for proportional shifts (experiment 667, Caveats), which stays within the months before the fold being scored.
    • Duration mix. The injected shifts have durations drawn half from 3 to 14 days and half from 28 to 112 days, an assumed mixture with none from 15 to 27 days, and each injected shift counts equally.
    • Loss and optimiser. The loss is the pinball loss over the 11 levels plus 0.05 times the binary cross-entropy of p against the observed state of an injected shift. The optimiser is Adam with learning rate 0.003, weight decay 0.0001, batch 4,096, up to 30 epochs, patience 4, and two seeds averaged by mean quantile. The fit samples with a generator seeded 1000 * seed + yyyymm % 997, and each epoch shuffles with a generator seeded seed * 100 + epoch.
    • Hardware and run time. About 0.5 GPU-hours per fold for the Candidate chains (experiment 667).
    • Capacity. The effective capacity of the production table normalises the target. The neural network does not cap its output at that capacity.

    Post-processing

    The backtest file of the Primaries has one post hoc clamp, and the calibrated copy adds a scale for the prevailing rows only. Both act on stored quantiles after the neural networks run. The band scale leaves the median unchanged. They are not the only steps that act on predicted quantiles: the prevailing head (R-haz or B-haz) rebuilds the prevailing quantiles from the Candidate's quantiles, and the seed average sorts the quantiles again. Those steps belong to the model chain above, and "Port notes" below states what the chain means for a serving path.

    • Clamp at zero. Every quantile of the eight MVA Primaries is at least zero, because apparent power cannot be negative. The clamp changes 206,487 of 7,848,960 rows (2.6%), in D12, D22, and D8 only, and the largest change is 2.80 MVA. It is monotone, so no quantile crosses. The MW Primaries keep their negative lower quantiles, and D5 and D18 never go negative in the observed power yet have lower quantiles below zero in 703 and 6 rows (experiment 668, Caveats). The clamp came from nged-ml-research PR 342 (Clamp night-time solar and MVA Primaries quantiles in the one-year backtest files (experiment 668 page)). The body of that pull request counts 202,598 changed rows, and the 668 page counts 206,487 for the current file.
    • Walk-forward band scale (calibrated copy only). See "Walk-forward band calibration" below. It applies to the prevailing Primaries rows and not to the NRA rows. The scale of arm 2 raised the NRA coverage to 87.1% and the NRA CRPS by 0.081 points of capacity (experiment 683).
    • Not applied. The widening factor 1.0934 of experiment 613, and any cap at capacity. Experiment 683 tested the fixed factor 1.0934 on the NRA rows as its arm 1: the NRA coverage rose from 82.4% to 84.5% (stored) to 86.0% to 87.6%, each a range over the four lead bands, and the CRPS fell by 0.027 points of capacity (95% interval -0.031 to -0.023). The file does not apply it.

    Port notes

    • Reproduce exactly. The input definitions and scalings above, the 24-hour minimum age of the Candidate's own lags, the chain of K1, table T, and the Candidate's fine-tune with the 60 zero-weight columns, the same frozen encoders and fresh mixer seed, the 90-day half-life, the monthly refit on data before the first of the month with a 3-day embargo at the mixer step and a 7-day embargo at the fine-tune, the 11-quantile read-out by bisection, and the construction of the prevailing mixture from the Candidate's quantiles.
    • Serving state. The signal columns and the 14 extra lags read table T, the median of the lags-masked K1 network at the freshest earlier origin with a lead of at most 24 hours. A service must therefore run K1 and store its forecast every day, for each Primary and for every neighbour that a signal reads. The walk-forward band scale reads the model's own stored forecasts of the previous 90 days beside the observed power (LOOKBACK_DAYS = 90 in e00683_calibrate.py). The prevailing head reads the banks of injected-shift rows, one per earlier month. The production storage list under "What a production forecasting service has to store" holds the weights only, so table T and the forecast history are additions to it.
    • Serving path. Inference for the Primaries is a chain, not a call to predict. K1 with its lags masked writes table T, and table T feeds the signals. Then come the Candidate for each seed, the seed average and sort, R-haz acting on the Candidate's quantiles, the clamp, and a calibration that reads 90 days of stored forecasts. That chain departs from two production design principles. The first is keeping the serving path close to "load a model, call predict". The second is putting a capability in the training loop when it could live in either place. The band scale could be fitted at refit time, and the calibrated copy fits it month by month from stored forecasts instead. What the chain buys is the lag signals and the prevailing output, whose gain is in the scores above. Whether to accept that trade is not decided.
    • Degradation. The Candidate trained only on the 09:00 native origin of the same day's run, so the other 6-hourly production slots and a stale NWP run are untrained regimes. The ecmwf_ens schedule runs at 10:30 UTC and retries every 30 minutes up to 8 times, so on a late day the 12:00 slot holds yesterday's run. Its leads would be 24 hours longer than the 339 hours trained and would pass the 351 hours that the summaries reach, and its lag ages would change. The research code states no rule for a stale run, a missing lag (the Candidate trained with 30% lag masking), a missing neighbour, missing CAMS, or fewer than two banks (B-haz covers that case in the first two folds). The production design requires degrading, widening the bands, and recording the degradation on the row, and the rule for each case is an open question below.
    • Unvalidated. The truncated Gaussian output has been tested at only four Primaries and two months, and only for the NRA output. The prevailing head's behaviour on real load shifts rests on one verified transfer. The survival probability beyond day 10 is the bank generator's duration prior and not an estimate from observed events. The backtest used two seeds and no widening, where the final test used five seeds and a factor of 1.0934.
    • July and August level miss at D18 and D14. Experiment 690 found that a masked, frozen slow-level state tracks the July to August 2026 level miss of the stored normal-running forecasts at D18 and D14 (identifiers 13 and 16; weekly correlation 0.66 and 0.87), and that adding it to q50 halves the median absolute error at both Primaries (-50.3% and -51.3%), in hindsight. The added level is a hindsight diagnostic fitted on the whole record, and not a forecast, and experiment 690 proposes no new method. The miss stays listed as not explained by a load transfer (experiment 685).

Whether the fallback head is needed in production, because the backtest needed it only for the first two folds. Which of the 00:00, 06:00, 12:00, and 18:00 UTC slots the Candidate must serve, and what the service does with a stale NWP run, a missing lag, or missing CAMS.     Demand at the BSPs and GSPs

    Architecture

    The two BSPs and two GSPs are forecast by the lag-enabled demand neural network of experiment 633, which is arm lagged_b1 of experiment 622, retrained with fresher lags. The backtest is experiment 669. The neural network has no neighbour inputs and no component that represents load shifts, and the series' readings are net of embedded generation, which no input represents. One neural network is fitted per group (the two BSPs together, the two GSPs together), so the weights are shared across the two series of a group except for the series embedding and the offset.

    • Inputs. The same columns as the Candidate, except that the 60 signal columns are absent, with one embedding of the series (2 entries per group). Each row holds the following groups.
      • The 37 lag-free columns. The mean and standard deviation over the 51 members of the 7 weather variables (14 columns), 21 calendar columns (the cosine of the solar zenith angle, the sine and cosine of the azimuth, the sine and cosine of the local hour at periods of 24 and 12 hours, a one-hot day of week, a COVID flag, three Christmas columns, and three bank-holiday columns), and 2 lead columns (the lead in hours over 336, and its square root). The weather columns are those of the Candidate, and the 51-member means and standard deviations are taken at the series' H3 resolution-5 cell.
      • The 8 lag columns. For each of the day lag and the week lag: the lag's power over capacity as a z-score (clipped at plus or minus 5), a missing flag, and an age code, which is the age of the lag in hours at the target time over 336. The remaining 2 columns are the grid indices of the two lag times. A missing lag reads as value 0, flag 1, and the age code of the longest lead: 1.0714 for the day lag and 1.5 for the week lag at the 3.5-hour cap.
      • The knot position. One column that places the row's valid time among the monthly knots of the output offset: the number of months from 2019-09-01 to the valid time, fractional within the month.
      • The 14 thermal-lag and solar-gain columns. As for the Candidate.
      • The expected-weather table for the lag encoder: the weather and calendar columns of the freshest run issued at or before the lag's own time, passed through the same encoders.
    • Lags. The stored neural network of experiment 633 read lags at least 24 hours old. The backtest retrains it with the 3.5-hour cap of experiment 572, so the newest power is the 05:30 reading on the origin day, 6.5 hours before the 12:00 label.
    • Encoders, mixer, and output. The same encoders and mixer as the Candidate, with a lag encoder of 83 inputs (3 lag columns, the two 32-wide expected-weather and expected-datetime embeddings, and the embeddings of the series and the lag) and a mixer with 145 inputs. The offset, loss, quantile bisection, and sorting are the Candidate's. No width factor is applied. The set-up follows experiment 619 (the lag-enabled neural network at the nodes), experiment 565, experiment 569, experiment 591 (the shared lag encoder), experiment 574, and experiment 572, and the node harness of experiment 564.
    • Origin. The neural network runs at its own 09:00 UTC origin (the 00Z run plus 9 hours) and its output is relabelled to 12:00 UTC. Every input read at 09:00 was available at 12:00.

    Wind

    Architecture

    The three wind farms are forecast by the simple shared neural network S of experiment 659, a two-layer network with a Gaussian mixture output. The backtest is experiment 666. The only inputs are ensemble weather and the lead.

    • Inputs, 16 in all. The mean and standard deviation over the 51 members of 10 m wind speed, 100 m wind speed, the sine and cosine of the 100 m wind direction, and 2 m air temperature (10 columns). The lead as three columns: the sine and cosine of the lead in hours modulo 24, and the lead in hundreds of hours. A one-hot wind-farm column for each of the 3 farms, in the order of the farms' anonymous labels. One wind farm is a MW series and two are MVA series. There is no power lag, no calendar column, and no physics-lite input. The per-case summaries do hold the mean and standard deviation of a physics-lite power curve (pl_mean, pl_sd), but the fit does not read them.
    • Lead definition. The wind lead is the time from the run's 09:00 UTC availability stamp to the valid time, so the first forecast half-hour (12:00 UTC) has a lead of 3 hours and the last has a lead of 338.5 hours. The solar network measures its lead from the 00Z run instead (see the solar section).
    • Standardisation. Each input column is standardised with the mean and standard deviation of the fit rows, and the standard deviation has a floor of 1e-6.
    • Layers and output. Two hidden layers of 64 units with GELU activations, feeding 9 outputs: the log weights (a log-softmax), the means, and the standard deviations of 3 Gaussian components. These describe the residual of power as a fraction of capacity from the mixture's centre, which is the mean of the training target. The last layer starts with zero weights, a bias of 0 for the weights, of -0.1, 0, and 0.1 for the means, and of log 0.08 for the log standard deviations. Each standard deviation is exp(clamp(raw, -6, 1)). The 11 quantiles are read from the mixture by 40 bisection steps on the mixture's cumulative distribution function, over a residual range of -2 to +2 (in fractions of capacity) around the centre. They are clamped to the range 0 to 1 of the neural network's own capacity scale, and then sorted within each row.
    • Weather. The 00Z ENS run of the origin day, treated as available at 09:00 UTC, with leads of 12 to 347.5 hours from the 00Z run. Where ensemble steps are 3 or 6 hours, the half-hourly value is interpolated as in experiment 310 and experiment 430. No ERA5 or CAMS pre-training, and no ERA5 or CAMS input at inference time.
    • Cases kept. Every case must hold all 51 members, numbered 0 to 50. The feature script raises an error when a case does not, and when a run from 2025-11-01 lacks any of its 672 half-hours.

    Training regime

    • Refits. Wind refits on day 0 of each month (12 refits), on ensemble runs and valid times before the 62 days that precede the refit, and uses those last 62 days for early stopping (experiment 666). Experiment 659 refitted twice a month. One pooled network is fitted over the 3 farms for each refit and seed.     • Optimiser. AdamW, learning rate 0.002, batch 2,048, weight decay 0.0001, and 40 epochs. Every epoch runs, with no patience rule, and the epoch with the lowest negative log-likelihood on the last 62 days is kept. Training leads run to 351 hours from the 09:00 stamp.
    • Seeds. Three seeds (0, 1, and 2), averaged quantile by quantile and then sorted.
    • Idle readings. Readings flagged by the idle mask of experiment 210 are set to zero in training and scoring until the mask ends.     Solar

    Architecture

    The six solar farms are forecast by the simple shared neural network PLP of experiment 659, which adds a fixed physics-lite power to the ensemble weather. The backtest is experiment 666.

    • Inputs, 18 in all. The mean and standard deviation over the 51 members of shortwave radiation, air temperature, and 10 m wind speed. The ensemble means of relative humidity, longwave radiation, precipitation, and surface pressure. The mean and standard deviation of the physics-lite power and of the Faiman cell temperature. The sun's elevation and the sine and cosine of its azimuth. The lead. There is no farm column and no calendar column. One pooled network is fitted over the 6 farms for each refit and seed.
    • Lead definition. The solar lead is the time from the 00Z run to the half-hour's valid time, so it runs from 12 to 347.5 hours. The wind network measures its lead from the 09:00 stamp instead.
    • Physics-lite power. The physics-lite power is clip((G / 1000) * (1 - 0.004 * (T_cell - 25)), 0, 1), where G is shortwave radiation in W per square metre and T_cell is the Faiman cell temperature T_air + G / (25 + 6.84 * wind speed). The formula runs on each of the 51 members separately, and the neural network reads the mean and standard deviation. The mixture is centred on the mean physics-lite power. It has no fitted parameters.
    • Layers and output. As for wind: two hidden layers of 64 units with GELU activations and a 3-component Gaussian mixture over power as a fraction of capacity, with the same last-layer initialisation, the same standard-deviation clamp, the same 40-step bisection over a residual range of -2 to +2 around the centre, and quantiles clamped to the range 0 to 1 of the neural network's capacity scale and then sorted. The input standard deviation has a floor of 1e-6.
    • Night gate inside the neural network. The neural network forecasts only the half-hours where the mean cosine of the solar zenith angle over the ensemble step is above zero, and sets the quantiles of the other half-hours to 0.

    Input tables

    The solar fit reads one table per farm that e00666_solar_inputs.py builds from the production stores, and it needs no earlier experiment's table, ERA5, or CAMS. The table is data/ml-experiments/00666-*/solar_inputs/<site>.parquet, with one file for each of the 6 farms. It holds every half-hour of every 00Z run from 2024-04-01 to 2026-10-31, in the 14 day bands described below, with daylight, the 18 inputs, and actual. The 18 inputs and actual are null where the half-hour is not a daylight half-hour or a stamp is missing, and actual is also null where power is unobserved. The script reads the ECMWF ENS Nwp Delta table (NWP), the cleaned power Delta table, the effective_capacity table, and the metadata table, all under DATA_PATH_INTERNAL.

    • Run order. e00666_solar_inputs.py, then e00666_solar_fit.py, then e00666_export.py (which writes wind.parquet and solar.parquet together). e00666_solar_gate.py is an optional check that compares the inputs with experiments 430 and 659. It imports modules from experiments 310, 430, 461, and 659 and is not part of the pipeline.
    • The farm's weather comes from the nearest H3 cell by its centre. nearest_cell picks the store's H3 cell whose centre is nearest the farm. The wind member rows instead use the cell that holds the farm (h3_res_5).
    • Complete stamps only. The script keeps a stamp only when all 51 members are present, and it raises an error when a run from 2025-11-01 to 2026-10-31 lacks a complete stamp up to a lead of 348 hours.
    • Stamps and day bands. The ensemble stamps are 3-hourly up to a lead of 144 hours and 6-hourly after. Day d (1 to 14) holds the half-hours whose lead from the 00Z run is in [12 + 24(d - 1), 36 + 24(d - 1)) hours, so each half-hour of a run appears once and a run's 14 days cover 672 half-hours.
    • Shortwave is a period mean spread by the zenith. The ensemble value is a mean over the period that ends at the stamp. The script spreads it over the period's half-hours in proportion to the cosine of the solar zenith at the half-hour's midpoint, so that the period mean is unchanged.
    • Temperature and wind speed are interpolated. Each is interpolated linearly between the previous stamp and the period's stamp, to the half-hour's midpoint, and falls back to the stamp when the previous stamp is missing. Relative humidity, longwave radiation, precipitation, and surface pressure are the ensemble means at the period's stamp, without interpolation.
    • Relative humidity is derived, not stored. The production Nwp table has no relative-humidity column. The script computes it from the ensemble-mean 2 m temperature and 2 m dew point with the Magnus formula (constants 17.625 and 243.04): 100 times the ratio of the saturation vapour pressure at the dew point to the one at the air temperature, clipped to 0 to 100.
    • Sun position. pvlib gives the apparent zenith and the azimuth at the half-hour's midpoint. The elevation input is 90 degrees minus the apparent zenith. A half-hour is a daylight half-hour when the mean cosine of the zenith over its 3-hour or 6-hour period is positive.
    • Constants. Temperature coefficient -0.004, U0 25, U1 6.84, all in e00666_common.py.
    • Imports versus inputs. The solar inputs come from the production stores through one script in the 666 folder, so the appendix statement that wind and solar need only that folder is right for solar's code and its data sources. It is wrong for wind (see the wind section).

    Training regime

    • Refits. The solar neural network is refitted quarterly, on the first day of the first month of the backtest year and of each third month after it (2025-11-01, 2026-02-01, 2026-05-01, and 2026-08-01). Each fit scores the next three months, uses three seeds (0, 1, and 2), and has no pre-training. Training uses valid times before one day earlier, with the last 61 days for early stopping, 20 epochs, daylight rows only, and leads below 348 hours from the 00Z run, as experiment 666 states (experiment 430 and experiment 659 stopped at 251.5 hours, so the lead range is extended). Solar trains on all daylight half-hours, including more low-sun half-hours than experiment 659 kept. The script asserts that no training row has a valid time at or after the refit.
    • Optimiser and seeds. As for wind (AdamW, learning rate 0.002, batch 2,048, weight decay 0.0001), keeping the epoch with the lowest negative log-likelihood on the last 61 days, with no patience rule. The target is the observed power over the farm's production effective capacity, divided by the ratio of the new capacity to that effective capacity. The network sees raw power, because solar has no idle mask.
    • Capacity. As for wind: the 99.9th percentile of cleaned power over the 730 days before each refit. The fit writes each quantile as the fitted fraction times the new capacity, in MW.
    • Hardware and run time. See the wind section.

    Post-processing

    The solar file carries a hard zero at night, set at each half-hour's own sun position, and a calibrated copy adds a band scale.

    • Night clamp. Every quantile is zero on half-hours where the sun is not above the horizon at the middle of the half-hour. The clamp changes 271,808 of 1,471,680 rows (18.5%), the largest change is 6.1% of capacity, and the daylight rows are bit-identical to the unclamped file. It is needed because the gate inside the neural network uses the mean cosine over the 3-hour or 6-hour ensemble step, so a step that contains a sunrise or sunset leaves small non-zero quantiles at night.
    • The same rule as a gate. Experiment 684 applied the rule as a gate on the stored neural networks of experiment 666 and found no non-zero night quantile left and no change to any daylight or dawn-and-dusk score, with a lower night CRPS. The share of night observations above q99 rises, because the gate sets those quantiles to zero. Dawn and dusk (sun elevation under 5 degrees) stay badly calibrated: the q05-to-q95 coverage is 0.69 and 0.75 at the two farms tested, and 0.30 and 0.24 of observations fall below q01. The gate was tested at two farms over three months of origins.
    • Walk-forward band scale (calibrated copy only). See "Walk-forward band calibration" below, applied to daylight rows from 2025-12 with night quantiles kept at zero.

    Walk-forward band calibration

    The backtest files hold one calibration, a scale on the width of the quantile bands fitted only on earlier months, which brings the four calibrated outputs close to 0.90 coverage. Experiment 683 tested it on the origin months 2025-12 to 2026-06, and the README of the backtest folder describes the four calibrated copies. The scale is post-processing on stored quantiles and is not part of any neural network above.

    • Method. Each quantile's distance from q50 is multiplied by one factor per series, output, origin month, and lead band (days 0 to 1, 1 to 3, 3 to 7, and 7 to 14). The factor is the 90th percentile of the ratio of the actual power's distance from q50 to the stored half-width of the band on that side (q95 - q50 above, q50 - q05 below), which makes the scaled band cover 90% of the fitting rows. The fitting rows are the half-hours whose valid time lies in the 90 days that end 6 hours before the month starts, because power is available only up to 6 hours before an origin. The factor is bounded to 0.5 to 3. In the code, a cell with fewer than 20 fitting days keeps a scale of 1, and rows whose stored half-width is under 0.05 of capacity are left out of the fit. There is no level shift, so q50 is unchanged. After scaling, the quantiles of MVA series, wind, and solar are clipped at zero.
    • Where it applies. The prevailing Primaries rows, the BSPs, the GSPs, and solar. It does not apply to wind, whose coverage already exceeds 0.90, and it does not apply to the NRA Primaries rows. Two arms of experiment 683 tried the NRA rows. Arm 2, the walk-forward scale, brought their coverage to only 87.1% and raised their CRPS by 0.081 points. Arm 1, the fixed factor 1.0934 about the median that experiment 613 fixed, lowered their CRPS by 0.027 points (interval -0.031 to -0.023) and raised their coverage to 86.0% to 87.6%. The backtest files apply neither arm to the NRA rows. The first month, 2025-11, has no earlier forecast to fit on and is left as stored.
    • Effect on coverage. On 2025-12 to 2026-06, the q05-to-q95 coverage and CRPS change are as in the table, each coverage a range over the four lead bands (experiment 683).

    • A scale fitted through an unexplained stretch overshoots afterwards. A scale fitted through the July and August 2026 load transfers overshoots after they end: D18 (identifier 13) reaches 97.5% coverage in 2026-09 under the scale. The calibrated copies hold D18 and D14 (identifiers 13 and 16) at their 2026-06 factor from 2026-07, a rule chosen after seeing the final-test months, so it is post hoc. Experiment 688 dropped the half-hours inside labelled and verified load transfers and shifted each band by its month's observed median error, and the coverage of the NRA Primaries rose from 80.1% to 90.1% in 2025-12 and from 80.3% to 88.6% in 2026-01. Experiment 689 took the 151 series-and-lead-band cells (of 384, 16 series by 6 refit boundaries by 4 lead bands) whose stored band width changes by more than 10% across a refit boundary from 2026-01 to 2026-06.     Production-port notes across forecast types

    • Series and units. Carry the unit with the series, because the 8 MVA Primaries and 2 of the 3 wind farms are apparent-power series and the rest are MW.
    • Origin and lags. Every neural network reads the ENS 00Z run of the origin day as available at 09:00 UTC and is relabelled to a 12:00 UTC origin. The Candidate's own lags are at least 24 hours old at its 09:00 origin, the BSP and GSP lags at least 3.5 hours old, and the prevailing head's extra lags at least 6 hours old. Wind and solar read no power lag. Experiment 572 tested a 3.5-hour lag cap at the Primaries, and none of the pages read for this one tests it together with the Candidate's neighbour signals and the prevailing head.
    • What a production forecasting service has to store. For each demand and generator neural network, the fitted weights of each monthly (or quarterly, for solar) refit. For the prevailing head, a bank of injected-shift rows per earlier month. For calibration, a scale per series and lead band refreshed each month. For the Primaries, the lag-free forecast table: the median of the lags-masked neural network at the freshest earlier origin with a lead of at most 24 hours, for the Primary and for every neighbour, produced every day. The calibration also needs the neural network's own stored forecasts from the previous 90 days beside the observed power.
    • Serving path. The chain for a Primaries forecast is longer than loading a model and calling predict. The lags-masked neural network writes the lag-free forecast table; the table feeds the neighbour and gap signals and the 14 extra lags; the Candidate runs for each seed (2 seeds) and the seeds are averaged; the survival head acts on the Candidate's quantiles to give the prevailing output; the MVA clamp acts on the quantiles of both outputs; and a calibration step reads 90 days of stored forecasts and observed power and rescales the bands. The clamps and the band scale are therefore not the only steps that act on predicted quantiles. Sources: ml-experiments/00667-*/e00667_cand.py (ensure_tables), ml-experiments/00598-*/e00598_signals.py (build_lag_free_table), ml-experiments/00683-*/e00683_calibrate.py (LOOKBACK_DAYS = 90), and experiment 668 (seeds and head). The production CLAUDE.md states two design principles that this chain trades away: keep the serving path close to "load a model, call predict", and put a capability in the training loop when it could live in either place. The band scale could be fitted at refit time, which is the second principle's case. The page records the trade and does not decide it.
    • Unmetered solar. None of the neural networks above has an explicit term for unmetered solar behind a Primary. Experiment 673 reads the capacity of unmetered solar from a neural-network probe of the response of net demand to sunshine, and calls its figures provisional. The probe gives 2.4 to 3.9 MW at six of the eight MW Primaries (D5, D9, D10, D11, D14, and D16) and 1.25 to 6.62 MW at five MVA Primaries (D2, D3, D4, D19, and D21). Experiment 679 measures the loss in recovery of an added solar farm when MW readings are converted to MVA, as 0.5 percentage points (80% interval -1.0 to 7.7) if the meters read power factor 1 with a small reactive floor. These results are context and are not inputs to any neural network above.

    Gotchas across forecast types

    Each item below caused a wrong number or a wrong assumption in the backtest, or would in a port. The per-type sections give the detail where it belongs.

    • Forecast every half-hour. The stored test matrices of the earlier experiments drop half-hours without an observed reading, because they are training matrices. Experiment 669 rebuilt them for the BSPs and GSPs in ml-experiments/00669-*/e00669_testrows.py, and experiment 667 rebuilt the Primaries' rows from 2025-07-01 onward (REPLACE_FROM in e00667_extend.py). A port must forecast every half-hour of the horizon.
    • The last 62 days of a wind refit window only stop training early. Wind cases whose origin is at or after the cut-off (62 days before the refit date) and whose valid time is before the refit date are used to choose the best epoch and never enter a gradient (STOP_WINDOW_DAYS in e00666_wind_fit.py).
    • ERA5 arrives about 5 days late. At a real cut-off, the last roughly 5 days of ERA5 and CAMS pre-training rows could not be read. The BSP and GSP section states this. The same lateness applies to any Candidate or wind input built from ERA5.
    • Injected lags use later power for proportional shifts. In the Primaries' injected-shift bank, the lag inputs of a proportional shift use the month's later power. Experiment 667 lists this as a caveat.
    • The unclamped prevailing q01 is more negative than the NRA q01 at some MVA Primaries. The lowest prevailing q01 is -2.26 MVA at one MVA Primary against -1.21 for NRA, before the clamp (experiment 667, Caveats).
    • Wind A has flat q50 runs. Runs of up to 9 identical non-zero q50 values occur at Wind A, because the quantiles are clamped at the neural network's own capacity (backtest README).
    • At least two capacities are in play. The wind and solar networks train with the 99.9th percentile of the 730 days before each refit. Every score divides by the production effective capacity, one value per series. The neighbour power ratios of the demand neural networks divide the capacity_mw column of site_map.csv (set in world() of e00647_data.py), a third capacity that is not the effective-capacity table.
    • Hide all power after the cutoff in every refit. Experiment 667 sets the power after the cutoff to NaN in its train phase (TRAIN_ONLY in e00667_fold.py). Experiment 669 has the same switch (E00669_HIDE=1), and its docstring says that only the pre-flight gate used it, so the year's fits relied on row selection alone. A port should hide the power by construction and test the rule (test 2 in "Tests to write").
    • Regime changes in the training history. D8's reading includes one gas engine's output until 2023-08-15, and the data-processing page recommends an individual start date or a flag for such a series. The Candidate's pre-training spans 2019-09 onward. Whether the Candidate's rows handle this change is an open question below.

    Open issues for the porter: where production conflicts with the research fits

    Each item states a fact about production and the matching fact about the research code, with sources. None is an agreed design.

    • The neural networks were trained on one origin, and production runs at four origins with a stale-run fallback. Every research network was trained only on a 09:00 native origin from the same day's 00Z run, relabelled to 12:00. Production live_forecasts runs at 00:00, 06:00, 12:00, and 18:00 UTC (defs/live_forecast_assets.py). The ecmwf_ens asset ingests at 10:30 UTC and retries every 30 minutes, up to 8 times, so the 12:00 slot can use the previous day's run (defs/schedules.py). With the previous day's run, every lead grows by 24 hours, past the training lead range (experiment 666 gives a cap of 348 hours for solar, and the Candidate's range was not checked), and the lag ages change. The research page set has no rule for this regime. Rule 1 of docs/design-philosophy/inherent-stability.md in the production repository requires production to degrade, widen the bands, and record the degradation.
    • Some research inputs do not exist in production at inference time. The previous day's CAMS irradiance enters two columns of the Candidate and of the BSP and GSP networks at inference, and production stores no CAMS (weather_schemas.py defines Nwp for ECMWF ENS only). The monthly pre-training reads ERA5 and CAMS from 2019-09 to the cutoff, and ERA5 arrives about 5 days late. The solar network's relative humidity is not a column of Nwp, which has temperature and dew point, so the research code derives it with the Magnus formula (constants 17.625 and 243.04 in e00666_solar_inputs.py) from the ensemble-mean values. The demand neighbour lists are read from two NGED files, Monitor Direction.csv and Primary Substation Interconnections.csv, in e00400_blend.py, and the production repository mentions the second file only in docs/roadmap/data-sources.md.
    • The analysis proxies differ. The production proxy is the control member of the freshest run, filled column by column from older runs. The research proxy is the ensemble mean of the freshest run published by the origin, which is the previous run's first day (ml-experiments/00437-*/e00437_features.py). A port needs the research definition for every column that weather lags read.
    • The serving chain is longer than load and predict. See "Serving path" in "Production-port notes across forecast types" for the chain and the two production principles it trades away.
    • The research models do not take AllFeatures. The production BaseForecaster.train and predict take a pt.LazyFrame[AllFeatures], keyed by ensemble_member. The Candidate needs pooled rows of 51-member ensemble summaries, inputs from neighbouring series, and multi-stage pre-training on ERA5 and CAMS. A different FeatureEngineer can supply a different data view, according to the production CLAUDE.md, but the page read no code that does so for these networks. The forecast-type sections list the mismatch under "Differs".
    • The output contract differs. See "Output contract": production has no quantile columns and no output-kind column, and its valid_time constraint excludes the lead-0 row of the backtest files.

    Open questions

    These are listed for the maintainer. None is decided here.

    • Whether the Primaries Candidate should read 3.5-hour lags, as the BSP and GSP neural network does, and what the answer does to the prevailing head.
    • Which extra-lag set the prevailing head should use: the 8-input set or the 14-input set (experiment 681).
    • Whether the truncated Gaussian output should replace the post hoc clamp at all eight MVA Primaries, and what it does to the prevailing output (experiment 684).
    • Whether to refit the band scale at every neural network refit, and how to treat the band-width steps at month-start refits (experiment 683 and experiment 689). Experiment 689 shows that a fixed-lookback scale does not remove the steps, and it did not test a scale fitted on the new network's own forecasts.
    • Whether the band scale belongs in the training loop or in the serving path (see "Serving path" above).
    • Which forecast slots (00:00, 06:00, 12:00, and 18:00 UTC) each neural network must serve, and what a stale ENS run does to each (see "Open issues for the porter").
    • Whether a port rebuilds the cached input tables from source or ports the lineage of experiments 607, 437, and 564 (see the "Input tables" subsections).
    • Whether the Candidate's pre-training rows handle D8's gas-engine regime change on 2023-08-15. The code was not read for this.
    • Why the NRA bands of D18 and D14 (identifiers 13 and 16) cover only about 22% to 28% of observations in July and August 2026. Experiment 668 suggests a load shift that normal running ignores. Experiment 685, an event table on the final-test window, finds no transfer between the two Primaries in those months, and names a level offset in the lag-free forecast as an untested candidate. Experiment 690 fitted a smooth slow level in hindsight, with the candidate windows masked, on a different forecast's residual. The level tracks the change (weekly correlation 0.66 at D18 and 0.87 at D14), and adding it to q50 halves the median absolute error at both Primaries, in hindsight and not as a forecast. The level ramps over months and does not follow the step on 1 July. It moved the median absolute error of two control Primaries by 5.5% and 7.5%, against a limit of 5%, so the verdict is partly supported, and the experiment proposes no new method. The miss is still not explained by a load transfer (experiment 685), and the current pages are experiments 685 and 690.
    • Whether wind needs a power lag or a different long-lead rule, given its score against climatology at days 10 to 14.
    • Whether a calendar input or more frequent refits remove the solar median's seasonal bias.
    • How to model load shifts at the BSPs and GSPs, where the largest observed miss looks like one.
    • Whether the BSP and GSP fine-tune carries synthetic lag-only shifts. The code path suggests that it does not (e00669_fit.py calls the fine-tune of experiment 574 directly, where the Candidate's chain uses synthetic shifts), and no run checked this.

    Tests to write

    A port should pass the checks below before any score is compared, and each check states the property it protects and the source that supports it. The thresholds come from the pages named. A threshold that no page states is marked as a choice for the porter.

    1. No lookahead, by perturbation. Property: no input depends on power or weather that was not available at the origin. Perturb all power after the cutoff and after the native origin minus each lag cap, which is 24 hours for the Candidate, 3.5 hours for the BSPs and GSPs, and 6 hours for the extra lags, and check that no input, no lag-free table entry, no signal, and no output moves. Repeat with the NWP of later runs. Sources: experiment 668 (a test perturbs every later half-hour for the extra lags), experiment 669 (a perturbation test found no leak; no lag index is newer than 05:30 on the origin day).
    2. Hidden-future training. Property: a refit at month M reads no row at or after M. Fit one fold with all power after the cutoff set to NaN and require identical weights. Sources: e00667_fold.py (TRAIN_ONLY), e00669_fold.py (E00669_HIDE=1), and experiment 667 (reviews found no leak into any fold's training data).
    3. Golden output. Property: the port reproduces the stored forecasts. Re-run one stored fold, for example 2026-03, and compare with the backtest file within a tolerance. The GPU re-run of the 2026-03 GSP fold matched the stored forecasts to 5e-6 of capacity (experiment 669). For the Primaries, the 12:00 NRA rows equal the native 09:00 seed-mean quantiles times capacity exactly, with a maximum difference of 0.0 (experiment 667, "Checks").
    4. Contract. Property: the output has the layout in "Output contract". Check 672 rows per origin and series, no duplicate key, no null or non-finite quantile, no crossing in any adjacent pair, UTC, Int8 identifiers, valid_time as the end of the half-hour, and one NRA row and one prevailing row per Primary key. Sources: experiments 667, 668, and 669, "Checks". Validation against the production PowerForecast needs a quantile column set that production does not have.
    5. Clamps and gates. Property: the post-processing rules hold. MVA Primaries have no quantile below zero. MW Primaries are not clamped. Solar quantiles are zero where the zenith at mid-half-hour is 90 degrees or more, and daylight rows are bit-identical before and after the clamp. Sources: the backtest README ("Post-processing and checksums") and experiment 668.
    6. Calibration. Property: the scale follows the rule in "Walk-forward band calibration". The q50 is unchanged. Each factor lies in 0.5 to 3. Each factor uses only valid times at least 6 hours before the month starts. A cell with fewer than 20 fitting days keeps a scale of 1. Coverage on the fitting rows is 90% when no bound binds. Sources: e00683_calibrate.py and the backtest README.
    7. Scores and coverage by lead band. Property: the port reproduces the published scores. Recompute the tables of experiment 668. The Opus review of experiment 667 reproduced every score to 3 decimals, and the Sonnet audit of experiment 669 reproduced its scores within 0.03 points. A tolerance for a port is a choice for the porter.
    8. Monthly refit. Property: the refit loop is walk-forward. A refit at month M trains only on data before M (check 2), and the band-width step at each refit is reported as experiments 683 and 689 measured it.
    9. Degradation. Property: the service degrades and never raises when an input is missing, as rule 1 of the production inherent-stability page requires. Check a missing lag (the Candidate trains with 30% lag masking), a missing neighbour, a stale or missing ENS run, missing CAMS, and fewer than 2 banks for the survival head. The service must always output a forecast, widen the bands, and record the degradation on the row. No page defines the widening for these cases.
    10. Architecture and parameter count. Property: the port builds the architecture that was fitted. Assert the layer widths of each network and the parameter count against the stored .pt files in data/ml-experiments/. The widths are stated in each forecast-type section, and the count is not stated on any merged page.
