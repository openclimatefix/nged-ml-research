    What does a one-year backtest of the simple shared wind network S and the simple shared solar network PLP forecast for the 3 metered wind farms and the 6 metered solar farms?

    The year-long backtest is complete. wind.parquet and solar.parquet hold forecasts for 365 daily origins. Both neural networks score sensibly at every lead out to 14 days, and solar at days 10 to 14 is only slightly better than climatology. The files are private (they hold the time series of NGED's metered generators, labelled by their original time_series_id). The backtest is descriptive, not a ranking: the year overlaps the development months of experiment 659, and origins from 2026-07-01 to 2026-10-31 lie in the window earlier experiments kept as a final test, so those months are no longer a held-out test for choosing between the networks and the defaults. No figures are included on this page; the scores below come from scores/*.csv.

    Scores fall with lead for both neural networks, and solar PLP stays below climatology in every band. CRPS (continuous ranked probability score) is the mean pinball loss over 11 quantile levels, in points of capacity (smaller is better). Capacity is the production effective capacity, the same table as the shared folder's series_capacity.parquet. Bands are half-open ranges of days from the 12:00 UTC origin to the valid time; the "7-14 d" and "10-14 d" rows overlap the other rows, so the table is not a partition. Wind scores count idle readings (experiment 210's idle mask) as zero until 2026-09-13; solar is scored on daylight half-hours only.

    Wind S (raw ensemble weather only) and solar PLP (physics-lite power plus raw weather) are the networks of experiment 659. Wind S median mean absolute error (MAE, % of capacity) runs from 8.2 at 0-1 d to 18.3 at 10-14 d, and solar PLP from 7.9 to 12.8 (climatology: 13.3). Share of observations above q99 (ideal 0.01): wind 0.016 at 0-1 d and 0.023 at 10-14 d; solar 0.012 and 0.011. Share below q01: wind 0.011 and 0.007; solar 0.028 and 0.032 (most solar rows below q01 have an observed power of zero or less). By origin month, wind S scores best in July and August (2.67 and 2.77) and worst in November (5.52); solar PLP scores best in December (1.27) and worst in March (3.95) and September (3.74).

    Checks and caveats

    • Wind S matches experiment 659 on matched rows. On the rows both cover (December, January, March, and June origins, 231,000 rows), this experiment scores 4.174 and experiment 659 scores 4.179 when both use experiment 659's scoring capacity. The absolute numbers on this page are about 5% higher than experiment 659's because the production effective capacity is 2.3% to 8.7% below experiment 659's wind capacity. Solar is 0.13 points worse than experiment 659 on 120,000 matched rows (3.350 against 3.224), possibly because each refit here is one month older for the scored months and the solar neural network here trains on leads up to 348 h.
    • Prediction (1) (within 0.15 points of 3.684 on overlapping months) cannot be judged on that basis: the overlap-month score here is 4.011 because of the capacity difference and because the 3.684 includes October 2025. On matched rows and matched capacity the two agree. Prediction (2) holds overall (3.24 against 3.39) but not from November to March, when solar PLP at 10-14 d scores worse than climatology by 0.03 to 0.42 points; it is better by 0.17 to 0.48 points from April to September. Prediction (3) holds for wind and solar coverage in every band (0.88 to 0.92; solar is just under 0.90). The share of very sunny-day observations above q99 is 2.8% on this experiment's own definition (each farm's top tenth of days by energy in its month), which is not comparable with experiment 659's 10.6% to 12.7%; the weak upper tail on very sunny days is not resolved.
    • Seasonal bias. The solar median is high in spring (+6.5 points of capacity in March) and low in autumn (-6.9 in September, -8.5 in October, all 6 farms), and the solar neural network has no calendar input; one year with quarterly refits cannot separate a seasonal effect from refit age. The wind median at 10-14 d is low in November and December (only 28% and 32% of observations fall below q50). Wide bands at long leads may explain why coverage stays near 0.90.
    • Wind idle mask ends on 2026-09-13. From that date idle readings count as observed in scoring and in the 2026-10-01 refit's training target, so September and October are scored on a different target (one farm's share below q01 rises from 1.1% to 5.4%).
    • Capacity scale. The networks scale by the 99.9th percentile of cleaned power over the 730 days before each refit, which is 1.02 to 1.12 times the stored effective capacity, so some quantiles exceed the stored capacity (the largest q99 is 1.12 times, at solar series 22). Observed solar power exceeds the stored capacity on 5% to 9% of April daylight rows.
    • Solar night. Night half-hours are zero. The daylight flag is the mean cosine of the zenith over the 3-hour or 6-hour ensemble period, so about 3,000 half-hours around dusk and dawn at leads of 6 days or more carry small non-zero quantiles (q99 at most 0.7% of capacity), and some dawn and dusk half-hours in a mostly dark period are forecast as zero. The solar all-half-hours tables in scores/ are diluted by night rows and by 3.8% negative night readings; use the solar_daylight_*.csv tables.
    • Scoring rows. Rows without observed power are dropped, so origins after about 2026-10-26 are scored on part of their 14 days. Observed power is not in the files.
    • No break at any refit boundary in the median, band width, or q99: consecutive-origin forecasts for the same valid times differ by no more than the ordinary day-to-day change at all 11 wind boundaries and all 3 solar boundaries. Median minus mean forecast per site was not computed (the mixture mean is not stored).
    • Reviews (all fresh agents; every finding was triaged against the code):
      • Before the first fit, 2 Sonnet reviews (leakage and contract) found 8 minor points. The accepted fixes were assertions that no training valid time reaches the refit, coverage and archive checks that raise, and Int8 and origin-count checks.
      • Before the first fit, 1 Opus review of leakage and fold logic found 3 low or informational points and no leakage.
      • After the files were written, 1 Sonnet file audit found 4 low points. Both files were clean, one wind fold and one solar fold re-ran from scratch to 0.0 MW difference, and the scores reproduced.
      • 1 Opus review of the files and scores found 12 points, which are the caveats above.
      • 1 Sonnet prose and number review found no wrong number; its prose findings are applied.

    Method

    • Neural networks and weather inputs. Wind S and solar PLP of experiment 659: a pooled network of two hidden layers of 64 units with a three-component Gaussian mixture over power as a fraction of capacity, 11 quantiles read from the mixture and capped at capacity, 3 seeds averaged quantile by quantile and then sorted. Wind S reads the mean and standard deviation across the 51 ensemble members of 10 m and 100 m wind speed, the sine and cosine of the 100 m direction, and air temperature, plus the lead (sine and cosine of its hour of day, and hundreds of hours) and a one-hot wind-farm column. Solar PLP reads 18 inputs: mean and standard deviation of shortwave, air temperature, and 10 m wind speed, the ensemble means of relative humidity, longwave radiation, precipitation, and surface pressure, the mean and standard deviation of the fixed physics-lite power and of the Faiman cell temperature, the sun's elevation, the sine and cosine of its azimuth, and the lead; there is no farm column and no calendar column. No ERA5 or CAMS pre-training.
    • Origins and weather. One origin per day at 12:00 UTC from 2025-11-01 to 2026-10-31, using the 00 UTC ECMWF ENS run of that day (51 members, treated as available at 09:00 UTC); valid times run half-hourly for 672 half-hours from 12:00 (lead from the 00 UTC run 12 h to 347.5 h). Where the ensemble steps are 3-hourly or 6-hourly, the value is interpolated as in experiments 310 and 430; the share of daylight half-hours lacking their own stamp is 0% at every lead.
    • Power lags. Neither network reads a power lag, so the 6-hour power delay changes no feature. The power delay sets only the training cut-off of each refit. The first forecast half-hour (valid 12:00) has already ended at the origin; no power is an input, so this leaks nothing.
    • Refits. Wind S refits on day 0 of each month (12 refits), training on runs and valid times before 62 days earlier and stopping early on the last 62 days, 40 epochs. Solar PLP refits on 2025-11-01, 2026-02-01, 2026-05-01, and 2026-08-01 (each scores the next three months), training on valid times before one day earlier, with the last 61 days for early stopping, 20 epochs, and daylight rows with leads below 348 h from the 00 UTC run (experiments 430 and 659 stopped at 251.5 h); solar trains on all daylight half-hours, including more low-sun half-hours than experiment 659 kept. Wind training leads run to 351 h from the 09:00 stamp, as in experiment 659. Capacity is the 99.9th percentile of cleaned power over the 730 days before each refit. Wind idle readings are zero in training and scoring until 2026-09-13.
    • Compute time. About 20 minutes of GPU time in 3 parallel processes (36 wind fits, 12 solar fits).
    • Scoring. The 11-level pinball loss times 100 and the normalised median MAE, by origin-to-valid lead band and origin month, against cleaned observed power (outside the files). Solar climatology is the farm's own power at the same month and half-hour before each refit.

    Files

    • Forecasts (private): data/ml-experiments/backtest_year_2025-11_to_2026-10/wind.parquet (735,840 rows = 3 series x 365 origins x 672 half-hours; ids 4, 5, and 6; MW for 4 and MVA for 5 and 6) and solar.parquet (1,471,680 rows; ids 21, 22, 23, 29, 30, and 31; MW). Columns: time_series_id (Int8), valid_time, nwp_init_time, power_fcst_init_time, then q01 to q99 in the same unit. Checks passed: no duplicate keys, nulls, non-finite values, or crossings; UTC.
    • Per-seed fit tables, saved networks, and scores: data/ml-experiments/00666-*/fits/, models/, scores/.
    • Code, in ml-experiments/00666-*/: e00666_wind_members.py, e00666_wind_features.py, e00666_solar_inputs.py, e00666_solar_gate.py, e00666_wind_fit.py, e00666_solar_fit.py, e00666_export.py, e00666_score.py.
