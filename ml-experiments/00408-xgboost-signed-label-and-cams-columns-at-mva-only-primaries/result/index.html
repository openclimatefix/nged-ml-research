    Can the monthly XGBoost model infer signed MW from MVA readings, in lab conditions?

    Take-home: two satellite solar columns lowered the forecast error at 15 of the 16 Primary substations, but only because the lab gives the model the realised sunshine, and the attempt to recover a signed MW forecast failed its known-answer test. At the eight Primaries whose meters record apparent power in megavolt-amperes (MVA-only: D2, D3, D4, D8, D12, D19, D21, and D22) the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) fell by 0.359 points of effective capacity (99.67% interval -0.500 to -0.229) when the monthly XGBoost demand model received the CAMS solar columns, and by 0.772 (95% interval -1.037 to -0.521) at D12. In production the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service (CAMS) value arrives about a day late, so this gain is a ceiling, not a forecast of what production would see. The registered rule for labelling reverse flow recovered the sign of injected solar output with pooled balanced accuracy 0.834 against a pass line of 0.85, so the stop rule applied: the signed-label arms were not run and there is still no trusted signed forecast at the MVA-only Primaries. Verdict: partly supported.

    At the eight MVA-only Primaries the meter records apparent power in megavolt-amperes, which has no sign, so reverse flow from embedded solar looks like demand. D-codes are anonymised Primary substations of National Grid Electricity Distribution (NGED), the electricity distribution network operator that supplies the data. Experiments 152, 372, 374, and 387 each tried a different fold treatment and none gave a trusted signed forecast. This experiment gave the monthly XGBoost model two CAMS columns and labelled reverse flow by a physics rule before training. It first tested that rule on six Primaries whose meters record sign, after adding the recorded output of a metered solar farm so that the true answer is known.

    What changed since the brief was written

    • Experiment 387 merged (not supported: a folded loss leaves the sign set by the start, and its custom objective scored about 0.11 points worse than the built-in quantile loss). This experiment therefore uses only the built-in reg:quantileerror loss and a label.
    • Standing rules added to common.md on 2026-10-30 are applied: monthly refits only, cleaned data with the substation_zero half-hours dropped, the D15 and D18 rows before 2025-03-01 cut, boosters saved as .ubj, tails and avoidable-curtailment cost reported, and example forecasts plotted.
    • Stored control not reused for A0. Experiment 381's monthly/control was fit on the uncleaned cache without the D15 and D18 cut, so A0 is refit here. Experiment 381's stored forecasts serve only the code check.

    The known-answer test failed

    The registered rule recovered the sign of injected reverse flow for about three half-hours in four, which is short of the pass line. At the six Primaries whose meters record sign, we added a metered solar farm's output (twice the median sunny-midday reading), discarded the sign, and asked the rule-labelled XGBoost model (A2 at tau 0.7) to recover it. The signed median's pooled balanced accuracy (the mean of the share of reverse-flow half-hours it called and the share of other half-hours it left alone) was 0.834 against 0.85. The signed median's mean absolute error was 9.3 points against 19.5 for the plain model read as signed. It did not beat a rule that flips the sign whenever realised ERA5 shortwave irradiance exceeds a threshold fitted on the training years: that rule scored 0.86 to 0.88 per Primary, 0.036 above the model, though it wrongly flipped 14% to 19% of the half-hours with no reverse flow against 2% to 5% for the model.

    • Criteria (pooled over six Primaries, 12 monthly refits, days 1 to 10, daylight half-hours). Pooled balanced accuracy 0.834 (needs 0.85: fail). Lowest Primary 0.788 at D5 (needs 0.70: pass). False-reverse rate 3.6% (needs at most 5%: pass). Margin over the shortwave rule -0.036 (needs +0.03: fail). Signed mean absolute error 9.3 against 19.5 (pass).
    • Per Primary. D5 0.788, D9 0.841, D10 0.825, D11 0.849, D14 0.846, D16 0.843, with recall on reverse flow 0.60 to 0.75.
    • The shortwave comparison favours that rule. That rule reads realised ERA5 (the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts reanalysis) shortwave at the valid time, which a 10-day forecast does not know. The gate fails on pooled balanced accuracy alone, so dropping the margin criterion would not change the verdict.
    • Most of the loss belongs to the rule. Used alone on the injected data (no model fit), the rule's labels reach recall 0.64 to 0.79 with false-reverse 3.8% to 6.1% at level 2.0. A single-fit rule scores 0.74 to 0.85 per Primary, so the 0.85 pass line sits at the ceiling the rule's own labels allow. D9 is the exception: the model (0.841) beat the single-fit rule (0.737).
    • The miss is small but not noise. A bootstrap over (Primary, refit) pairs gives a pooled balanced accuracy of 0.821 to 0.846 (5th to 95th percentile), so the miss is about 0.016.
    • The rule's gate opens at two real Primaries. Read as apparent power, real readings open the gate only at D10 (a signed-MW Primary with 130 real reverse half-hours) and at D12. At those two Primaries the rule labels 97% and 92% of sunny summer-midday training half-hours as reverse flow, because the constant C is fitted from the same rows that it then labels. The 92% at D12 is therefore not evidence that D12 exports on 92% of sunny middays.
    • Stop rule. The gate failed at level 2.0, so level 1.0 and the A2 arms (tau 0.7, tau 0.5, off, shuffled, and forced gate) were not run. This would be the fifth fold treatment after experiments 152, 372, 374, and 387, so the next step is the maintainer's decision.

    Checks

    • Dropped half-hours. The cleaning rule dropped 622 half-hours over the cache year, at D12 (4), D15 (196), D16 (1), D18 (389), D21 (13), D22 (5), D8 (9), and D9 (5). The 196 for D15 and 389 for D18 fall before those Primaries' first scored refits (2026-03-01 and 2026-01-01); within the scored windows D15 lost none and D18 lost three.
    • Median minus mean forecast. Per Primary, in megawatts, between about -0.19 and +0.01 for both arms, and A1 is closer to zero at most Primaries (D12 -0.167 for A0, -0.148 for A1).
    • Lead band. The headline lead_days_1_10 band is lead above 0 and up to 240 hours.
    • Audit triggers. Trigger 1 (a known-answer test failed) fired. Trigger 2 (a result contradicts a prediction) fired: P3 predicted -0.05 to -0.20 at D12 and the result is -0.772. The explanation is oracle weather: the lab columns carry the realised CAMS value at every lead, so the gain grows with lead where production's would not.
    • Audit of the numbers. A fresh Sonnet agent re-derived the CRPS, the contrasts, and the code check from the stored forecasts. They match within about 0.2%, except D8 (about 1% in CRPS, traced to a difference in the row set), with no leakage and no Primary worse than +0.05.
    • Audit of the gate. A second fresh Sonnet agent rebuilt the injected truth and the scores from the stored files. Every per-Primary and pooled figure matches, the injection uses only data before 2025-07-01, and "failed at level 2.0" is a sound reading. The gate auditor also noted that the reverse flow share is 17% to 26% of daylight half-hours, that Q is zero by construction (which favours the model, so real meters would do no better), and that the 0.85 line was set without knowing the rule's ceiling.
    • Prediction outcomes. P1 failed (0.834). P2 (placebos) was not run. P3 failed on D12 (-0.772 against -0.05 to -0.20) and at the other seven (each moved by more than the predicted -0.03 to +0.03; D4's -0.053 is the smallest). P4 and P5 were not run. P6 cannot be tested in the lab, because the columns carry realised CAMS values at every lead.
    • Cadence. Monthly refits only, as the brief required.
    • Saved models and data. Fitted boosters are .ubj files, one per Primary per refit, in the experiment's private data folder (work/full/real/boosters and work/full/inj_2.0/boosters), never in git. Injected farm data and per-farm scores stay in the private store, and no farm name or series is published here.

    What to try next

    • Test the CAMS gain at production lags. An arm that gives the model the CAMS value only for half-hours at least one day old, or as an anomaly against the forecast weather, would show how much of the 0.359 survives. The porting rule for the columns (the pooled interval below zero and no Primary worse than +0.05) is met formally, but the lab gain is not a production gain.
    • Whether to continue signed-latent work at the MVA-only Primaries is the maintainer's decision. The stop rule has fired on the fifth treatment. One option the gate audit points to is fitting the reverse-flow constant on rows other than those it labels; another is to stop and use a structural term (demand minus non-negative generation).

    Design

    • Base recipe (all arms). Experiment 381's monthly control: 12 refits with origins 2025-07-01 to 2026-06-01, built-in reg:quantileerror with 11 quantiles, 500 rounds, eta 0.05, depth 6, seed 0, the default constant base_score, hist, 3 threads. Fits at 3 threads, no GPU (many small per-Primary fits, as experiment 381 found), memory capped at 8 GB.
    • CAMS source and delay. cams_demand_points.parquet (CAMS solar radiation time series, hourly global horizontal irradiance at each Primary's coordinates, 2019-09 to 2026-09); each hour-ending value is repeated on the two half-hours that end inside the hour (studies.dp_features). The service publishes with about a 1-day delay, so the columns cannot describe the first day of lead in production. Lab conditions use the half-hour's own CAMS value. u_t is studies.dp_features.unit_pv_shape; k_t is CAMS GHI over the Haurwitz clear-sky GHI (studies.baselines.haurwitz_w_m2) at the half-hour's solar zenith.
    • Signed-label rule (A2), refreshed at each refit from the training window only. The gate is open at a Primary when the Spearman correlation of the reading with u on summer-midday rows with k at least 0.7 exceeds +0.3, or the 1st percentile is below 1.0 point of capacity (experiment 366's rule, not re-tuned). D_ref(h) is the median reading at UTC hour h on April-to-September training rows with k below 0.3. C is the median of (y + D_ref)/u on clear summer-midday training rows. A reading is reverse flow when the gate is open, k is at least tau, C u exceeds D_ref(h), and C u - y is at least 2 points. The label is -y for reverse flow and +y otherwise. The lags stay unsigned readings.
    • Shape check (maintainer's rule), pre-registered. For each Primary, the correlation between the inferred reverse-flow magnitude and C u on reverse half-hours is reported. A correlation of at least 0.8 counts as following the shape; below 0.5 counts as not following it.
    • Fold at serving. Build a piecewise-linear CDF F from the 11 signed quantiles and set H(x) = F(x) - F(-x) for x at least 0. The MVA quantile at level alpha is the inverse of H. This is the identity whenever q01 is at least 0.
    • Arms. A0 (control, refit here). A1 (A0 plus u_t and k_t). A2 (tau = 0.7, registered), A2 (tau = 0.5), A2-off (tau infinite; must equal A1 to the last bit, a code check), and A2-shuffle (k permuted across days within the same month and hour before labelling). A1 is also run at the other 8 Primaries as a no-harm check. The A2 arms are run only after the known-answer gate passes at level 2.0.
    • Known-answer test, run first. Sites: D5, D9, D10, D11, D14, D16. Three different metered solar farms rotate across the Primaries (the injection frame of experiment 39 that experiment 152 uses), scaled to 2.0 times, then 1.0 times, the median sunny-midday reading of the training window. The farm output never enters the inputs, C, or u. Reading is y' = sqrt(P'^2 + Q^2) with Q = 0. The farm data stay in the private store and no farm name, ID, or series is published. Placebos on the real readings: at most 1% false reverse flow on sunny middays at D5, D9, D11, D14, and D16; D10's 130 real reverse half-hours reported; forcing the gate open at D5 must leave the MVA score about unchanged and make the signed MAE worse.
    • Scoring. demand-v1 at all 16 Primaries on fold_a_daily_gap_v1; days 1 to 10 headline and by lead band; q01, q05, q95, q99 tails; avoidable-curtailment cost; cold winter nights; sunny half-hours shown three ways (realised sunny_clear_sky_index, CAMS k at least 0.7, sunny_unit_shape); D12's sunny bias by lead band for A0 and A1. Half-hours dropped as substation_zero are counted per fold.
    • Reuse. Stored GAM stack, BG2, manual_heuristic, and climatology forecasts are read as references; 16 Primaries x 12 refits x 3 fit arms are fitted here.
    • Reviews. (1) Code is written by Sonnet. (2) A fresh Sonnet agent reviews it before the first fit (leakage, the train_end cut, scoring, matching the copied recipe). (3) A second review if the first flags many problems. (4) Run. (5) A fresh Sonnet agent re-derives the headline numbers and the audit triggers are run. (6) Write-up. (7) A fresh Sonnet agent reviews the write-up for numbers and prose.

    Risks

    • D12's reverse flow is inferred, not measured (fitted PV capacity about 148 points, 6.9 MW, unverified), and D12's solar response bottoms out at 100 to 200 W/m2, so a "very sunny" gate probably mislabels moderate-sun reverse flow there.
    • A solar-only gate mislabels reverse flow from other generators; the assumption is unsafe at Primaries with other generation (D15 is negative on 78% of its half-hours, 63% of them at night).
    • Reactive power is unknown, so every result assumes Q = 0, except a power-factor-0.97 check at the July, November, and March refits.
    • The brief's exploration used ERA5 with Haurwitz, not CAMS, so k is recomputed from CAMS here.
