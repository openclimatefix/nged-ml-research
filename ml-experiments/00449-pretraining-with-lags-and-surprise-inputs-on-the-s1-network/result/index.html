    Does pretraining the S1 demand network on ERA5 and CAMS weather help when the pretraining stage itself sees the recent-power lags and the surprise inputs?

    Take-home message: pretraining helps S1 once the pretraining rows carry real recent-power inputs, but the gain is small, rests on one test month at the six, and experiment 425's simpler recipe is ahead of the pooled network over all 16 Primaries. PT1 scores 0.082 CRPS points below S1 at the signed-MW six and 0.068 below at the MVA-only eight (four screen folds, seeds 0 to 2, injected-world clean rows). Experiment 448's masked-lag pretraining scored 0.048 and 0.042 above S1, so the live lags are the difference (PT1 against PT0: -0.129 and -0.110). All numbers are interim, from four test months.

    What the evidence supports

    • Supported. Live lags in pretraining reverse experiment 448's harm. PT1 beats S1 on 3 of 4 folds at the eight and on 4 of 4 (injected rows) or 3 of 4 (real rows) at the six. PT1-pool5 beats BG2 at the six and the eight on four and five folds.
    • Not supported. PT1-pool5 as the first-release demand candidate, because 425's arm A is ahead over all 16 Primaries. Any claim at D15 and D18, where BG2 leads PT1-pool5 by about 1.2 points. Any claim on cold winter nights at the six, where BG2 leads by 0.10 to 0.13. A tie with BG2 over all 16 Primaries is all that is shown (+0.022 on four folds, -0.020 on five).
    • Production cost. Serving a pool of five needs five networks, five forward passes, and five ensemble-weather lagless modules (experiment 404's further module, one per seed), because each member reads its own seed's table. The ERA5 lagless modules are used in training only. A monthly refit needs about 75 fits against eight for S1: 5 ERA5 tables of 6 modules (30), 5 ensemble tables of 6 cross-fit modules plus 1 further module (35), five pretraining runs of 60 epochs on about 7.4 million rows, and five fine-tunes. Sharing one lagless table across the members is untested. Training also needs an ERA5 and CAMS feed.

    Checks

    • Mean-forecast checks. Median minus mean, mean bias, and mean absolute error per group are in figure_tables/mean_forecast_checks.csv. They rank the arms as CRPS does at the six and the eight (PT1 4.923 against S1 5.072 at the six; PT1-pool5 4.931 against S1-pool5 5.056). Every arm under-forecasts the mean: about -1.7 points at the six and -8.0 to -11.2 at D15 and D18. No audit trigger 3 signal. The checks omit 425's arm A and BG2.
    • Pretraining length. All 55 PT1, PT1b, and PT2 pretraining runs stopped at the 60-epoch cap, so pretraining may be too short.
    • Leakage. Each of the 25 ERA5 lagless tables (seeds 0 to 4, five folds) covers 2019-09-01 to the half-hour before the refit, fits each module on 5 of 6 contiguous blocks and forecasts the sixth, and repeats no (primary, grid). No table holds a half-hour at or after its refit.
    • Lag semantics. PT1 pretrains through the same input class as fine-tuning, so the lags come from the same origin-lag function over the same power from 2019-09. The stages differ in the surprise source, in the spread columns (set to the fold's median per lead band in pretraining), and, for PT2, in the dropout.
    • Not done. The encroachment sweep, the figures (headline chart by lead day, contrasts by fold, surprise distribution by source, example forecasts, lag-jump event plots), and the nulled-input interval check for PT2. The tables for them are under figure_tables/.

    Question

    Experiment 448 pretrained the S1 network on ERA5 and CAMS weather with every recent-power input masked, then fine-tuned it on ensemble rows with the inputs live. The result lost to S1 by 0.05 points at the signed-MW six and at the MVA-only eight. The maintainer expected that pretraining across all history and all Primaries would add signal. This experiment asks whether the loss came from the masking: the lag pathway of the pretrained network never saw a lag, so fine-tuning on the shorter ensemble history could not recover it.

    Context

    • S1 recipe. Experiment 447's width-256 finding is interim and unaudited, so the S1 reference keeps hidden width 64.
    • Reused stored forecasts. Only seeds 0 to 2 of S1 and PT0 are used (both are stored to seed 4); the minimum detectable effect comes from S1's seeds, so the PT arms' own seed spread is read from the result. S1 seeds 0 to 2 (experiment 428) and PT0, which is experiment 448's S1PT (stored networks and forecasts in experiment 448's private folder). Their rows are the same four folds, worlds, and row set, so no S1 network is refitted and PT0 is refitted for 2025-12 only. The reproduction check (a refit of one PT0 fold and seed with a validation loss equal to the stored value) was run only for 2025-12, and its result is not stored.
    • Experiment 425 pretrained with real lags (Opus audit of experiment 448). Experiment 425's pretraining rows carried real lagged power, so its pretraining gained 0.044 points at the signed-MW six and 0.107 at the MVA-only eight on experiment 448's rows, while experiment 448's masked-lag pretraining lost. 425's single-seed arm A (no surprise inputs, no pool) ties the five-seed S1 pool at the six (2.809) and is 0.069 better at the eight. PT1 is therefore 425's arm A with the S1 surprise inputs added: pretrained with real lags and the cross-fitted lagless surprise. The prediction P1 used 425's gains (-0.050 and -0.124 on its own rows) as the expected size.
    • Fifth fold for the holiday slice. The Christmas and bank-holiday slice (22 to 31 December, where BG2 leads) lies in the 2025-12 fold, which is not one of the four screen folds. Every arm is therefore also fitted for 2025-12 (experiment 448 stored S1 for it; PT0 is refitted there). The advance rule reads the four screen folds only. The fifth fold is read for the Christmas slice and for the five-fold table.
    • Standing practices followed. Monthly refits; cleaned data (flagged and start-cut half-hours dropped); the D15 and D18 cut at 2025-03-01 applied; every network saved in the data folder; draft PR; Sonnet for every review (code before the first fit, results, and write-up) and one Opus audit. Pools used the spawn method.

    How the surprise is built for the pretraining years

    The surprise at a lag is the lag minus a lagless forecast of the same half-hour, divided by the lagless forecast's half band width. The lagless forecast must not have seen the half-hour it forecasts, or the surprise shrinks towards zero in pretraining and the network learns a lag pathway that fails on ensemble rows. The plan therefore builds one cross-fitted lagless table per fold and seed on ERA5 and CAMS weather, by the method of experiment 404's table:

    • Span and blocks. 2019-09-01 to the fold's refit, split into 6 contiguous blocks. A lagless module fitted on 5 blocks forecasts the sixth. Nothing at or after the refit enters.
    • Module and inputs. Experiment 404's lagless module (a pooled mixture density network with a Primary embedding, 64 hidden units, 6 epochs, learning rate 2e-3). The inputs are the 7 ERA5 and CAMS weather means, the solar-position, calendar, and holiday columns of experiment 397, and no lag and no lead. Experiment 404's ensemble spread columns are dropped because ERA5 has no spread. Rows have equal weights.
    • Early cut. Targets before experiment 418's first-usable date of D11, D10, D5, D14, and D16 are left out of the fits, as in the pretraining targets.
    • Target. Cleaned power over capacity (world 0), with flagged half-hours and the D15 and D18 start cut as missing and left out of the fits.
    • Table. Per (primary, grid): the q50 and half the q05-to-q95 width, in the layout the S1 code already reads.
    • Rescale. The smoke check on 2026-01 seed 0 found the ERA5 table's surprise standard deviation (1.245) well above the ensemble table's (0.850), over 2025-02 to 2026-01. The ERA5 half band width is therefore multiplied, per Primary, by the ratio of the two surprise standard deviations over the half-hours before the refit where both tables exist (the ensemble years), so the pretraining surprise has the scale the network sees in fine-tuning. The ratio uses no data at or after the refit. The ratios are printed per fold and Primary; a ratio outside 0.25 to 4 stops the run (widened from 0.3 to 3 after the first 2025-12 build gave 0.295 at one Primary; the other builds gave 0.41 to 2.31).
    • Overlap years. Pretraining rows after 2024-04 use the ERA5 table in pretraining and the ensemble table in fine-tuning, for the same half-hours.
    • Non-causal and unpurged. A module forecasting a block is trained on later blocks too, as in experiment 404, and the held-out and purged weeks of pretraining are not excluded from the other blocks' fits. Neither uses anything at or after the refit.
    • What differs from experiment 404's recipe. The span is 2019-09 to the refit rather than 24 months, rows have equal weights, and the inputs are ERA5 and CAMS, so the ERA5 modules are not identical in quality to 404's. The further module of experiment 404 (scoring months) is not needed.
    • What differs from use. In fine-tuning, and in use, the surprise comes from experiment 404's table, built from ensemble weather. The pretraining surprise comes from ERA5 and CAMS weather, which is more accurate, so its half-hours are less surprising on average. A smoke check prints the surprise standard deviation by source and asserts that the pretraining table differs from the fine-tuning table. ERA5 arrives about 5 days late and is not available at forecast time; the live service uses the ensemble-based table, as the fine-tuned networks do.

    Compute

    131 networks were fitted (lagless tables, pretraining, and fine-tuning over five folds and up to five seeds), on up to three GPU processes under 8 GB each.
