    Evaluation protocol for Primary demand, version 1

    This page fixes how every experiment on Primary substation demand is scored, so that results from different experiments can be compared. An experiment records protocol: demand-v1 in its front matter. A change to any metric, fold, series list, slice, interval, or tolerance on this page makes a new version. The superseded text moves to evaluation-protocol-archive/ as demand-v1.md, and the table of versions below gains a row. The protocols for solar and wind have their own version numbers, so a change here does not move theirs. The research default rule defines the research default and the log of its versions. This page defines how a change to it is scored and when the change is adopted.

    Version Date Change
    demand-v1 2026-10-28 First version

    What is scored

    The series are the 16 Primaries, in three groups that are always reported separately. The three groups answer different questions, so no headline pools them.

    Group Primaries What the group tests
    Signed-MW D5, D9, D10, D11, D14, D16 The demand model, because the meter records the sign of the flow
    MVA-only D2, D3, D4, D8, D12, D19, D21, D22 The demand model plus the effect of hidden reverse flow, because the meter records apparent power with no sign
    Short-history D15, D18 Report-only: no adoption rests on this group. No experiment we found fitted the GAM stack at D15

    D8 is a labelled exception. D8 has no tuning month and borrows the second-stage spread of six donor Primaries (D2, D3, D4, D19, D21, and D22). D8 is scored in two labelled arms: one with donor spread, and one with own-residual bands fitted on earlier folds. The own-residual arm is the D8 row in the per-Primary table, because the donor-spread arm is known to be the weaker one and would make D8 the worst Primary for a reason that is already understood. Donor spread is fitted before D8 is scored, so D8 is fitted last.

    Every number for the MVA-only group carries the label "reactive power assumed zero". The rule that picks the fit fails at a power factor of 0.97, and the reactive power of the real MVA-only meters is unknown.

    The forecasts compared are the forecast under test, the current research default, and three fixed references. The references are BG2 (the XGBoost-based production-style forecast), manual_heuristic, and climatology. The current research default and each reference are regenerated on the folds and Primaries of this protocol, under new stored-forecast names, because the existing stored forecasts cover six Primaries on another row set and are never overwritten. Each reference is never retuned. Earlier scores of the references, which stop at 168 hours and use other row sets, are not comparable with scores under this protocol.

    The lead range is 0.5 to 240 hours, and every experiment scores all of it. NGED's horizon is 1 to 10 days. Days 11 to 14 are reported only for the arms that have forecasts at those leads, and a table lists which arms are covered.

    The first run under this protocol produces the forecasts for 168 to 240 hours that the references lack. The stored demand forecasts stop at 168 hours, so BG2 has never been scored past 168 hours, and BG2, the GAM stack, manual_heuristic, and climatology need new runs at those leads. The ML MANAGER schedules this run. Until it has finished, no experiment scores the 168 to 240 hour leads, and the days 8 to 10 and 11 to 14 slices are empty.

    The three fixed benchmarks

    Each family keeps three benchmarks whose recipes never change: manual_heuristic, climatology, and one frozen XGBoost-based reference. The rows grow as months arrive, and the benchmarks are extended with the same recipe and never retuned. Every change page reports the research default's absolute score against the three benchmarks, with intervals, because a chain of small contrasts can hide a drift in absolute skill. Diagnostic pages need not report them.

    For Primary demand the frozen reference is BG2. BG2 is the stored arm p_s6_bg2 of experiment 87. BG2 is a quantile blend of five forecasts, with an explicit solar offset at the 5 of 16 Primaries that have a clean solar capacity estimate. The five forecasts are a weekly-refitted XGBoost model, a monthly-refitted XGBoost model, an analogue model (the experiment 87 page does not state its type), an XGBoost model pooled by substation type, and the frozen stack of experiment 48. For the bulk and grid supply points, BG2 is the weekly-refitted XGBoost model alone (b_weekly).

    Production does not run BG2. Production serves a much weaker XGBoost model, stored as xgboost_baseline. Experiment 87 scored it at 10.536 points on the Primary quantile score, against 5.260 for BG2. A page that names BG2 as a benchmark says that production does not run BG2.

    The GAM stack trails BG2 on sunny April-to-June half-hours. Experiment 251 found the stack worse than BG2 in April, May, and June (+0.162, +0.220, and +0.054 points), and at D5 on sunny half-hours. Every Primary-demand change page reports this finding until a version of the research default closes the gap.

    Folds

    Folds are contiguous months with a training window that ends before the first forecast origin of the scored month. Fold k trains on data up to the end of month M(k), then scores every daily forecast run whose origin date falls in month M(k)+1, at every lead up to 240 hours. The publication lag of the power data counts as part of the training cut. The row set is every daily ensemble run, at the 00:00 UTC origin, in the scored period. It includes the runs that fold_a_every5d_v1 holds, and it gets a new row_set_id when the first experiment under this protocol stores its forecasts. The publication lag of the power data is the lag in production, and studies.eval_protocol states its value.

    The experimenter chooses the training regime and states it in the plan. An expanding window and a sliding window (for example, the last 24 months) are both allowed. The regime must be the same for the forecast under test and for its comparison arm, unless the regime is the change under test.

    A fold needs at least 12 months of training history before it is scored. The GAM has seasonal terms, and a fold that has never seen a winter measures extrapolation, not the change under test. Each Primary's report says which months were excluded by this rule.

    The scored period is 2025-07 to 2026-06, with a replicate period of 2024-07 to 2025-06 for the Primaries that have one. The ENS archive starts on 2024-04-01 at every Primary, so an arm trained on ENS forecasts has a replicate of 2025-04 to 2025-06 only, at 13 Primaries, and none at D8, D15, and D18. Only a Primary with at least 12 months of history before the replicate period has a replicate, and an adoption that needs a replicate cannot rest on the others. Rows inside hand-filtered events are excluded, as in the research default's row set, except that a detected step day at a Primary like D8 is never excluded.

    Every Primary in a group is scored over the same months, so that a pooled group score averages the same rows. A Primary whose history is too short for a month is left out of that month for its whole group, or the pooled score is labelled with the months it covers. D15 and D18 are scored from the first month that studies.eval_protocol.fold_plan returns for each of them, with no replicate. The module governs the first scorable month at every Primary, and the scored months are a few. A 95% month bootstrap on four months under-covers, so their numbers are reported only and do not count in the tally of Primaries where the forecast under test wins. D9's history starts on 2023-10-01, so it has no replicate months before 2024-10.

    The months that the 12-month rule excludes are reported as their own labelled slice. Cold-start months are where a longer fit history helps most, and a gain there is otherwise invisible.

    Stage-2 and filter forecasts for the tuning period are stored for every fold, except at D8. The GAM stack's second stage and the Kalman filter need forecasts from before each scored month, and a missing store blocks a rescore. Every experiment stores its per-run forecasts and its stage columns.

    No experiment scores a valid time on or after 2026-07-01. This is an honour-system rule. A replicate that scores later valid times is labelled "tuning-adjacent" and is not read as held-out evidence. The rule truncates the last June origins, whose leads run past 2026-07-01. A physical lock on the held-out data is a separate, later piece of work.

    Metrics

    The three scored metrics are the mean absolute error of the median, the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS), and the tail-weighted CRPS (twCRPS). CRPS is estimated as the quantile score over 11 levels, which is the headline metric of every family. The ensemble CRPS of studies.ensemble_scores is a different estimator and is not used here. Pass or fail is judged in points of effective capacity, where a point is 1% of a Primary's capacity, and every table shows megawatts beside it, as the substation cards do. The headline metric is CRPS.

    Four tail measures and the curtailment cost are reported and have no pass or fail. The tail measures are pinball loss and coverage at the 0.01, 0.05, 0.95, and 0.99 quantiles, per group and per Primary. The curtailment cost is the avoidable-curtailment-cost measure of studies.eval_protocol. It is read only for the signed-MW group. The MVA-only meters record unsigned apparent power, which hides reverse flow, so the cost is not defined there and carries the label "reactive power assumed zero".

    The implementing code is the package studies.eval_protocol. The package lives in src/studies/eval_protocol/, with the modules metrics.py and slices.py, and a separate pull request adds it. Slice masks and the twCRPS threshold are defined there. The definitions are those of the substation cards (sunny by clear-sky index, sunny by unit shape, cold nights at 3 °C or below, and bank holidays). The twCRPS threshold is fixed per Primary and named in studies.eval_protocol. The docstrings of the package are the definition, and a change to a mask or a threshold makes a new version of this protocol. The package defines the slices with the ERA5 2 m temperature at each series' location, and the ENS forecasts are scored over those slices. The slice of a half-hour is therefore the same for every forecast, because it depends on an estimate of the weather that happened and not on any forecast. ERA5 is an estimate of past weather, so a half-hour near a threshold can sit in the wrong slice, equally for every arm.

    Experiment code must import the metric definitions from studies.eval_protocol. A hard-coded copy of a metric is a defect, because copies are how a changed definition stops applying to some experiments.

    Slices

    Each metric is reported over the slices below, and every slice row shows its half-hour count. A slice with few half-hours is labelled "thin", as the substation cards do.

    Kind Slices
    Lead time Day 1 (0 to 24 hours), day 2, days 3 to 4, days 5 to 7, days 8 to 10, days 11 to 14. Days 1 to 10 is the headline band
    Time of day Night, morning, midday, and evening, in local time, with the hour boundaries stated in studies.eval_protocol
    Condition All days; sunny by clear-sky index; sunny by unit shape; sunny April to June; the months excluded by the 12-month rule; winter (December to February); winter nights; cold nights (3 °C or below); Economy 7 step hours; Christmas (24 December to 1 January); bank holidays
    Load-shift events During each hand-labelled load-shift event; in the 7 days after each event ends
    Group Signed-MW, MVA-only, and short-history, each separately
    Series Every Primary on its own row, and every Primary in each lead band (at least the day 1 band beside days 1 to 10)

    The two load-shift slices have no pass or fail. The row set of the main score excludes the rows inside hand-filtered events, so the scores during the events and in the 7 days after each event ends are reported separately. Seven logged transfers are too few for an interval, per experiment 277. The length of 7 days is a choice of demand-v1, which the maintainer may change in a later version. The event list is the hand-filtered list and never the output of a detector.

    The tolerance for "sunny daylight half-hours" applies to the sunny-by-clear-sky-index slice. The first run passes the realised clear-sky index (global horizontal irradiance over the Haurwitz clear-sky irradiance). The other sunny slices are reported.

    The verdict names the lead band it rests on. A verdict that rests on days 1 to 2 says so, because NGED's horizon is 1 to 10 days.

    Reporting in every write-up

    The twCRPS threshold and the curtailment limit need at least 12 months of readings before the scored period. A series with fewer readings gets a null value and a status, not a threshold from a short history.

    An arm that is missing at a Primary is reported as "not fitted", never dropped. The tally of Primaries where the forecast under test wins states its denominator, which is the number of Primaries scored for both arms, and the number of Primaries not fitted.

    For each pre-registered contrast, the write-up states the 99% interval of the adoption condition and the 95% interval of the reported contrast, with the direction, the lead band, and the pooling decided before scoring. The 99% interval undercovers on short periods (96.5% at 12 months), and the write-up says so beside it. The first run states, for each Primary, whether it has a replicate and which Primaries have hand-labelled events, since an empty event list gives an empty slice.

    Each write-up reports the load-shift slices beside the main score. The write-up shows the score during the events and the score in the 7 days after each event ends, for the forecast under test and for the current research default, with the half-hour count of each slice. The write-up draws no pass or fail from them.

    Each write-up carries a per-Primary table, the worst Primary, and a leave-one-out contrast. The table lists every Primary's paired difference and its 95% interval, sorted by gap. Beside the pooled score the write-up shows the worst Primary and the number of Primaries where the forecast under test wins. For each pooled group contrast, it also shows two leave-one-out contrasts: without the Primary with the largest absolute paired difference, and without the Primary with the worst score. The sign of a pooled contrast can change when one Primary is left out.

    Intervals are 95% intervals from the paired bootstrap that resamples whole calendar months. Any statistic across Primaries states the number of Primaries, because a correlation across 14 or 16 sites is weak evidence.

    The write-up names the research default's version and the protocol version. For example, "default plus x versus Primary demand research default 3, scored under demand-v1". The result.md take-home stays at one or two paragraphs with figures, and the full metric table sits in a collapsed section.

    A change aimed at one series or slice names at least two placebo Primaries in its plan. A placebo fails when it moves in the same direction as the target, beyond its own 95% interval. A change with a failed placebo cannot be adopted as a general change.

    When a change is adopted

    These conditions apply only when an experiment asks to adopt a change. Most experiments do not ask. A change is adopted only when all six conditions hold. A change aimed at named Primaries follows the targeted path below, and the other conditions still apply to it.

    1. Gain. The 99% month-block interval of the contrast (default plus x, minus default) on the CRPS of days 1 to 10 lies wholly on the better side of zero, and the point estimate is at least the minimum gain for the change's tier. The contrast is read for the signed-MW group and for the MVA-only group separately, and each group's verdict is stated on its own. With k pre-registered arms, the interval is read at the level 1 − 0.01/k. The short-history group takes no part.
    2. No harm to a group. The signed-MW group passes the gain condition. The MVA-only group stays within the tolerance of its headline, with D8 and without D8, and is not required to show a gain. Each protected slice stays within its tolerance in each group, judged by a one-sided 95% non-inferiority bound. A breach at a group makes the verdict rejected.
    3. No harm to a Primary. A Primary whose point estimate is worse than 0.05 points makes the verdict needs-human, with the Primary named. The interval is not tested at each Primary, because 16 one-sided tests would breach by chance more often than not.
    4. Replication. An arm trained on ENS forecasts can be replicated only over 2025-04 to 2025-06 at 13 Primaries. An adoption that rests on that weak replicate has the outcome needs-human and is never adopted automatically. The gain has the same sign in the replicate period at the Primaries that have one, and the replicate estimate is at least half of the smaller of the main estimate and the minimum gain. Where the minimum gain is zero, the condition reduces to the same sign. Leaving out one series at a time is a check for changes tuned on one series and does not stand in for a second period: a change with no second period stays a candidate. A change that fails replication is not retried on the same replicate period with a changed x, unless the change is pre-registered as a new experiment and the failed attempt is counted. A change that meets this condition satisfies audit trigger 7, and a candidate that fails it never becomes a base for later work.
    5. Controls. The experiment's own controls passed, the placebos did not fail, and no audit trigger in the ml-experiment skill is unresolved. The audit's findings are triaged in the pull request before the adoption.
    6. Serving. The change uses only inputs available at forecast time. If the change would need a serving-path change to port, the page says so.

    A change aimed at named Primaries has a targeted path. The gain is judged on the named Primaries instead of the group, because a gain at one Primary is diluted by 8 or 16 in a pooled score. The placebos must stay non-inferior, and the change is adopted only as an automatic per-substation choice. The choice follows a rule fixed at training time from earlier out-of-sample scores alone, and the experiment scores the rule's output, never the better arm at each Primary in hindsight.

    The tolerances and minimum gains are fixed here and tweaked only by a new version. A tolerance is the harm a protected group may suffer before an adoption fails.

    Protected group Tolerance, in points
    Headline of each group (CRPS, days 1 to 10), including the MVA-only group with and without D8 0.02
    The 0.01 and 0.99 pinball tails, the worse end scored 0.05
    Cold winter nights 0.05
    Sunny daylight half-hours 0.05
    twCRPS 2% relative
    Each lead band 0.05
    Each Primary, on the point estimate 0.05
    Per-Primary coverage at the 0.01, 0.05, 0.95, and 0.99 quantiles Flagged when the 95% month-resampled interval of the coverage excludes the nominal level; a flag does not fail the change
    Tier of the change Minimum gain, in points
    A changed setting, an added column, or a changed loss 0
    A new fitted step, a new input, or a new data source 0.02
    A new head 0.03

    The adoption outcome is adopted, rejected, or needs-human. adopted sets promoted_to in the front matter, and the outcomes rejected and needs-human appear in the first line of result.md beside the existing verdict, with production_status: candidate.

    A neutral change passes a group's harm test with probability Φ(τ/SE − 1.645), where τ is the tolerance and SE is the standard error. A tolerance of 2 standard errors passes a neutral change 64% of the time, and a change that truly harms the group by exactly the tolerance passes 5% of the time. The per-Primary rule is a flag because 16 such tests would breach by chance far more often than not.

    When the metrics disagree, the verdict is decided in this order. A breach of condition 2 makes the verdict rejected, unless the change targets a different slice and the maintainer overrules. A Primary breach of condition 3 makes the verdict needs-human. A CRPS gain with the median error moving the other way beyond tolerance gives needs-human. needs-human is also the fallback for any case this list does not cover.

    The first experiment under this protocol rescoring the references. Experiments 374 and 376 used one fixed fit and a tuning month, not monthly folds, so their scores are not comparable with scores under this protocol.

    A screening arm is allowed. Monthly retraining of 16 Primaries over 12 scored months costs about 384 stack fits per change for two arms. An experiment's plan may screen with fewer folds or Primaries, and says so. Only a full run under this protocol supports an adoption.

    The cumulative harm budget is 2 times the group's tolerance, measured against the family's first version. The no-harm condition compares each adoption only with the previous version, so n adoptions that each cost up to the tolerance could cost n times the tolerance in total. At each adoption, the current research default must also stay within the budget against the first version, using the first version's stored forecasts. The retest rules of the research default rule still apply.

    A change that wins on the gain condition but fails another condition is recorded as a candidate, not adopted. Candidates are retested when the missing evidence arrives, or when a rebase run is available, as the research default rule describes.

    A targeted adoption on a subgroup uses a short list of admissible subgroups, set in advance. The list is cold nights, the Economy 7 step, and sunny daylight. A targeted adoption on a subgroup also needs no harm on the family headline.

    An experiment may hold several default-plus-x arms, and at most one of them is adopted. Each arm is read at the level 1 − 0.01/k, and the others become candidates that rebase on the new version. A bundle of two components is adopted as one change only when it was pre-registered as one change and its leave-one-out arms show neither component harmful.

    Only a pre-registered arm can be adopted. A post hoc arm becomes a candidate that needs a fresh experiment. When an experiment tests k variants of one change (tilt 20 and tilt 27, for example), the best variant needs replication on its own after the Bonferroni read.

    An experiment that evaluates a blend reports each constituent model family alone beside the blend. The single-family table comes first, and the page states the blend's added value over the best single family, with an interval. A blend is adopted into the research default only with that table present. The first production update is a relatively simple model implemented as one model family, for example XGBoost only, while blends stay of interest.

    The default plus x is produced on the research default's full row set. Its stored forecasts then serve as the next version without a separate regeneration job.

    The ablation that follows every fifth adoption drops a component only on evidence. A component is dropped only when the interval of its removal lies wholly on the better side, or when its removal is non-inferior within the tolerance and the component sits in a higher complexity tier.

    The nominal 99% interval covers less than 99% on short periods. A percentile bootstrap that resampled independent normal months with a nominal 99% interval covered 96.5% of the time at 12 months, 92.0% at 6 months, and 83.7% at 4 months (1,500 trials of 2,000 resamples each). The real procedure also resamples seeds and has autocorrelated months, so the figures are indicative. For periods under 12 months, add a t test on the monthly paired differences or use a studentised bootstrap. Adoption contrasts use 99% whatever the number of arms, as a stated exception to the interval-level convention, and a change page says "the 99% interval lies wholly on the better side of zero" in place of the study skill's "statistically significant at the 5% level".

    The recorded gain of each version is an upper bound. Winners are selected on the scored year, so the recorded estimate overstates the true gain. In a simulation, a true gain of 2 standard errors was recorded on average as 2.7. The log therefore also reports the cumulative gain on the replicate period, which carries no selection bias, beside the scored-year sum.

    The holdout

    The frozen period is the existing holdout: valid times on or after 2026-07-01. No experiment scores those valid times, which are kept for a finalist. The release-model skill scores the holdout once per release, on the sign of the effect only, and a month may confirm one release only.

    The holdout holds under four months and no winter on 2026-10-23. It covers July to October 2026, and the months from November to February arrive by about March 2027. Until then, a change that acts in winter (the heating curve, cold nights, the Economy 7 step) is adopted on the replicate year alone, and its log entry reads "winter-unconfirmed". The holdout confirms its sign in March 2027 or later.

    The same-months shrinkage check pauses a family's adoptions only on evidence of shrinkage. The check compares the holdout months with the same calendar months of the scored year (July to October 2025 for the current holdout). Adoptions in the family pause when the one-sided 90% upper bound of the holdout months' cumulative gain lies below half of the cumulative gain on those same scored-year months. A month read for a decision, or for a pause investigation, joins the scored months, and the holdout rolls forward as new months arrive. In a simulation of a 12-month scored year and a 4-month holdout, the check paused the family after 29% of false adoptions, 7% of adoptions with a true gain of 2 standard errors, and 3% of adoptions with a true gain of 3 standard errors. The check understates the chance of a pause for a change whose effect is seasonal.

    Retraining

    Retraining is monthly for the GAM stack, and weekly only for BG2 and the production forecast. There is no separate screening tier, because monthly retraining is already the R&D practice.

    Known-answer tests

    The protocol requires tests on synthetic series whose correct scores are known. The pull request that adds studies.eval_protocol adds them, and they run in continuous integration. They cover each metric, the month bootstrap, the folds (including the 12-month rule and the training cut), and the slice masks. A change to the evaluation code that breaks a known answer fails the build.

    What this page does not cover

    • A physical lock that stops research sessions reading the held-out actuals.
    • Logging scores to MLflow, which a follow-on pull request adds.
    • Wind and solar, whose own protocols are linked at the top of this page.
    • The bulk and grid supply points. Their row set is fold_a_daily_gap_v1, their lead range is to be named with their first research default, and no adoption happens until then.
    • Scores before demand-v1. Those scores used fold_a_daily_gap_v1 on six Primaries, a scored year of 2025-07-02 to 2026-06-30, a replicate year of 2024-25, and a lead range of 0.5 to 168 hours.
