    Do C1 and A1 with the G4 gated band, assembled as single forecasts, hold the normal running arrangement inside the q05-q95 band on 0.90 of rows from day 4 of a load-shift to its end?

    We assembled two single forecasts from stored parts and tested them without any new fit. On the injected load-shifts, both hold the normal running arrangement (NRA) inside the q05-q95 band on at least 0.91 of rows from day 4 of a load-shift to its end, with pinball loss at q05 and q95 on event-free rows within the minimum detectable effect. The median still follows about 0.11 of the shift, and the two real December 2025 events show gaps that the injected shifts do not. Verdict: supported. All four pre-registered predictions held: P1 reproduced (difference 0.000000), P2 held (A1 + G4 0.928), P3 held, and P4 held for C1.

    A load-shift moves part of one Primary substation's demand onto a neighbour for days or weeks, so recent power readings stop showing the NRA, the way the electricity network is normally arranged to supply that Primary. Earlier experiments built the parts of a forecast that copes with this: the C1 neural network (experiment 584), the A1 neural network with a shared lag encoder (experiment 591), and the G4 rule that widens the band (experiment 592). None of the three experiments joined the parts and scored the result as one forecast. We did, and we exported both forecasts for others to score. Nothing is adopted for production.

    How the forecast is assembled. The median is the neural network's own median. While the G4 gate is on, and for 7 days after the G4 gate turns off, every quantile is the widest of the network's value and the value of its lags-masked twin (the same weights with every lag removed). On other days the quantiles are the network's own. G4 reads three inputs: observed power up to 4.0 hours old, the lag-free neural network E2, and a mirrored opposite-sign gap at a neighbouring Primary. The state is therefore the same for C1 and A1. The rebuilt state equals experiment 592's stored state exactly.

    Checks

    • Median minus mean forecast. Not available: the stored forecasts hold quantiles only. The median of each assembled forecast equals its network's median on every row (0 rows changed), so the median error is the same with and without the gate.
    • Mean-forecast error beside the quantile contrasts. The gated and plain forecasts share the median, so the median's absolute error is identical; the contrasts above are in the band only.
    • Triggers. None of the seven audit triggers fired: no control failed, the reproduction matched, the quantile and median contrasts agree in direction, and no nested arm beat its container.
    • Code review. A fresh Sonnet agent found no bug that changes a headline number. It noted that the gate thresholds use the whole backtest's event-free days (the readings are causal, the thresholds are in-sample), that the G4 safe-side follow ratio mixes the network's movement and the band widening, and that the intervals resample events inside the same 5 months, so they are narrower than a month-block interval would be.

    Caveats

    • The injected bank gives the lag neural networks an easier test than a real load-shift would. It changes the lag inputs only, not the power, so it says nothing about a load-shift that enters the training power. That test (target-side absorption) has not been run on C1 or A1.
    • One real pair. Real-event numbers rest on two Primary-events. The network operator's (National Grid Electricity Distribution, NGED) labels are imperfect, and NGED says load-shifts may be missing from its logs, so clean rows and training periods can hold events no label covers.
    • Horizon. The forecasts stop at 120 hours; NGED cares about 1 to 10 days.
    • The G4 false-trigger rate rests on 1 trigger in 5 Primary-years.
    • Real events at D5. C1 + G4 and A1 + G4 both miss the level before the shift and the band does not cover the NRA after it.

    What to try next

    • Target-side absorption for A1 + G4 and C1 + G4 (a shift in the training power and the inputs, 12 or more designs).
    • A second real event: score both assembled forecasts on May 2026.
    • A gate-free network that receives the gate's signals as inputs, judged at equal band width.
