    Does an era signal that tells the Candidate network when a Primary's behaviour changed for good improve the forecast in the weeks after a permanent change, without carrying load shifts forward?

    We asked whether telling a demand-forecasting neural network when a substation's behaviour changed for good improves its forecast. The answer is yes after an injected change, and not shown on the real months we scored. The network is the Candidate, the modular network of the final models page that forecasts demand at the 16 Primary substations ("Primaries") of one electricity network operator for up to 14 days ahead. An era is a sudden change that lasts, such as a large generator or solar farm connecting to a Primary. A switching event is a temporary move of demand from one Primary to a neighbour, which the forecast must not carry forward. We gave the Candidate an era signal built only from data before the forecast origin (the time at which the forecast is issued), in several forms: fixed rules (rule, soft), a hidden Markov model (hmm), a statistical detector (stat), a version that turns every detected shift into an era (all), and an oracle that reads the true dates and is an upper bound, not a deployable arm. We compared each with the same network without a signal (none). We scored four test months (2025-11, 2026-01, 2026-04, and 2026-06) with two training seeds, on the real series and on real series into which we injected synthetic eras and switching events. All numbers are interim: the plan pre-registered five seeds, and we ran two because experiment 667, which supplies the first two of the Candidate's three training stages, stores them for seeds 0 and 1 only.

    Verdict: inconclusive. The decision rule rests on two contrasts, C1b and C2, which compare each arm with that arm's prevailing output (the forecast the network makes without the era signal). We did not score C1b or C2, because we did not store the prevailing output of any arm. The scored contrasts point two ways. After injected eras, five of the seven era signals lower the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS, lower is better, in points of the Primary's effective capacity) with a 99.44% interval that excludes zero in at least one window: oracle, all, soft, stat, and hmm. After injected switching events, every learned arm that helps after eras raises the CRPS at the donor Primary (the Primary that loses the demand), which fails the pre-registered veto on switching events. On the real rows, no learned arm lowers the CRPS on all rows. An audit was required (see Checks), and it reproduced the bank table.

    On the real rows, no learned arm lowers the error on all rows, and at D8 only hmm lowers it with an interval that excludes zero. Differences are from none, with 99.44% intervals (the pre-registered level for nine contrasts) that resample whole fold months, so four fold months and two seeds support each interval.     The oracle lowers the error after eras by about 7 points of capacity, and all lowers it by 2.9 points at the price of raising it by 3.9 points at the donor Primary of a switching event. The oracle's figure pools injected steps of different sizes, so it is not the pre-registered C1a. The arm all turns every detected shift into an era, so it carries a switching event forward at the donor. An audit that scored the recipient Primary instead (the Primary that gains the demand, 96 events with a metered recipient) found that all lowers the recipient's CRPS by 0.03 points, so the harm is confined to the donor. The placebo is within 0.06 points of none everywhere. The pre-registered veto on switching events (C7 above 0.1, or its interval end above 0.2) fails for all, soft, hmm, and stat, and only rule passes it, so no arm that gains after eras can be called supported under the screen's rule. The two decision rules in this page differ on a failed C7: section 11 gives "partly supported" when C2 passes, and section 20 gives "not supported for adoption" whatever C2 shows. Section 20 refines the earlier sections, so its rule governs.

    In phase B2, the arm trained on injected eras with no signal (b2-aug) meets the pre-registered rule at 95% and fails it at 99.44%. Phase B2 mixes the curriculum (the injected world) into every training batch, and its four arms run on the four test months with two seeds. At 95%, b2-aug lowers the all-rows CRPS by 0.050 (interval -0.103 to -0.001) and changes the touched Primaries (the Primaries that received an injected era) by +0.008. At 99.44% the all-rows interval is -0.109 to +0.005. The placebo moves the same way (-0.037, 95% interval -0.127 to +0.096), and the injected rows receive a second noise draw, which could explain both moves without any era effect (we did not test this). The curriculum world held 2 eras in 6 of the 8 fold-and-seed pairs and 4 in the other 2, so 6 of the 8 pairs are weak tests of the curriculum, and the contrast aug against none is not decisive (section 27).

    At D8, e-stat lowers the error in phase E although stat raised it in the screen months, so the sign of its effect at one Primary is not stable. Phase E is a robustness check at one Primary: it has about 6 forecast origins per month and wide intervals, and its oracle reads the scored months. It scores the folds 2025-04 to 2025-06 with seeds 0 and 1, and differences are from e-none (CRPS 8.718 at D8 and 3.717 on all rows), with 95% month-block intervals. At D8, e-stat is -0.569 [-1.339, -0.242], e-rule -0.170 [-0.425, +0.049], e-placebo -0.110 [-0.295, +0.049], and e-oracle +0.374 [-0.399, +1.420]. On all rows only e-rule has an interval that excludes zero (+0.021 [+0.000, +0.042]). A series-origin may hold fewer than 672 forecast rows (14 days of half-hours), because the stored rows keep only half-hours with an observed target. These numbers are never pooled with the four screen months.

    The all arm's D8 outlier comes from one fold month and is consistent with a temporary excursion. D8's daily mean power was about 7.5 MW from 2026-01-19 to 2026-01-25 against about 4 MW before and after, with no mirror shift in its three neighbours. The all arm turned the excursion into an era. Leaving out fold 2026-01 changes the all contrast at D8 from +1.366 to -0.018. Section 26 has the details and the maintainer's open questions.

    Checks

    • Median minus mean forecast per site, and the error of the median forecast beside every CRPS contrast: not computed. The scorers print CRPS only, so the normalised mean absolute error of the median that the evaluation protocol requires is missing, and trigger 3 cannot be cleared.
    • Triggers that fired: trigger 1 (a known-answer test) required an audit of the bank table, because the bank is the known-answer test of the signal. A Sonnet agent that did not write the experiment re-derived the bank contrasts from the raw forecast files with its own code. All contrasts matched the table to three decimals, 0 events differed between arms, and no left-out site or fold flipped a sign of the era contrasts of soft and all, the switching contrast of all and hmm, or the D8 contrasts of hmm and stat. Trigger 2 applies to phase E, where stat changes sign against the screen months and e-oracle raises the D8 error. Trigger 4 found two fragile contrasts: the all contrast at D8 depends on fold 2026-01 alone, and hmm on eras 0 to 7 days changes sign when any of 6 sites or fold 2025-11 or 2026-04 is left out. The soft D8 contrast is driven by fold 2026-01 (-0.199), and the hmm all-rows contrast by fold 2025-11 (+0.216).
    • Scored Primary of a switching event: the table scores the donor. At the recipient, all changes the CRPS by -0.029, so the sign of its harm depends on which Primary is scored.
    • Controls: the placebo is within 0.06 points of none on injected eras and switching events, as a control that carries no information should be.
    • aug against none: the two agree to 3 decimals, because stage 3 always keeps its first epoch in phase B (section 23). Every phase B curriculum contrast is void.
    • Intervals: the real-row intervals resample 4 fold months, so they are wide, and an interval at 99.44% is wider than the 95% interval that the B2 rule and phase E use.
    • Not scored: C1b, C2, C4 to C7 except the switching-event reading above, the state slices (undetected, unknown, classified), the bank's classification scores, cold winter nights, avoidable-curtailment cost, and the four load-shift scenarios.

    Method

    • Architecture (all arms): the Candidate network of experiments 667 and 677, with frozen stage 1 and stage 2 weights from experiment 667 for seeds 0 and 1, and a stage 3 that sees the era signal as extra input columns.
    • Era signals: the oracle reads the true era dates and the injected switching events, rule and soft use fixed thresholds on shifts, hmm uses a hidden Markov model fitted before 2025-01-01, stat uses the statistical detector of phase A, and all turns every detected shift into an era. The placebo moves the oracle's change vector to other dates. Every signal at an origin uses data up to the day before the origin.
    • Training regime: stage 3 is fitted per fold, seed, and arm on the pre-fold history, with patience 8 and the best state restored. The curriculum injects eras and switching events into the training panel. Phase B2 reads the injected world with probability 0.5 per row and epoch.
    • Measurement: CRPS in points of effective capacity against the cleaned half-hourly power, at the contract's 12:00 UTC origin, on experiment 677's monthly-refit rows for 2025-11 to 2026-06 and on the phase E row set for 2025-04 to 2025-06. Differences are paired, with a bootstrap that resamples whole fold months. The injected bank is scored by a percentile bootstrap over passes, and each switching event is scored at its donor Primary only. The bank scorer maps an event's host panel index to the Primary's time series identifier through the site map. Its bootstrap resamples passes, and the 16 passes of a fold month share one trained network per seed, so the interval does not include the noise of fitting the network.
    • Changes in phase B2 and phase E: phase B2 changes the stage 3 rule to mix the curriculum into every batch and runs four arms; phase E runs five arms on three earlier months.

    Plan as registered

    The sections below are the plan as registered before the first fit, with the interim amendments of sections 22 to 27.

    This experiment asks whether the Candidate network (the modular network of the final models page) forecasts better after a permanent change at a Primary when it is told the Primary's era. The maintainer asked for it by name: "revisiting the idea that substations experience these eras" (2026-11-19). An era is a discrete, rare, sudden, and persistent regime, such as a large generator or solar farm connecting. A load shift is a temporary move of demand between neighbouring Primaries, and the NRA forecast must exclude it. The two look alike in recent data, so the plan includes arms that classify each shift.

    Phase E costs compute only. Phase E (three extra folds, 2025-04 to 2025-06, to score the network across D8's 2025-03-24 solar connection, and the conditional pre-training arm O-enc-pre) needs no purchase and no data fetch beyond what the workstation holds. It is excluded from the committed arms until the maintainer approves it through the manager.

    Earlier advice. The plan follows the advice that a step-capable offset cannot date a step (experiments 573, 575, and 577) by using an explicit detector and an explicit state input, and the advice to test the era signal with lags present (experiment 572) by building every arm on the full Candidate. It departs from experiment 565's advice to feed a drift embedding to the mixer only, because the design also tests an entry through the encoders (arm O-enc).

    Terms. NRA is the normal running arrangement: the electricity network's configuration without any load shift. The Candidate (class PermLagMdn, ml-experiments/00613-nra-roster-contrasts-with-intervals-five-seeds/e00613_nbmodel.py) outputs the NRA forecast. The prevailing output is a second forecast that keeps the conditions at the forecast origin: a survival head mixes the Candidate's quantiles with a shifted copy, weighted by the probability that a shift in progress persists (docs/ml-experiments/final-models.md). A switching event (a load shift) moves demand between electrical neighbours, usually for days to weeks. An era change is a permanent change in a substation's own net demand, such as a generator connecting. CRPS is the continuous ranked probability score in points of capacity, lower is better.

    1. Hypothesis and prediction

    Hypothesis: a discrete era signal, computed only from data before the forecast origin and fed only for shifts classed as eras, lowers the error of the NRA forecast in the weeks after a permanent change at a Primary, without carrying switching events into the NRA forecast and without raising the error where no shift happened.

    Prediction, written before any fit. The Candidate is trained to ignore a shifted lag, because a load shift is not part of the normal running arrangement: experiment 699 measured that the NRA output copies 0.075 to 0.127 of a lag-only shift. A new era is part of the normal running arrangement, so after an era switch the NRA output stays near the old level until refits learn the new level. C1a is therefore predicted large: 5 to 15 points for a step of 25% of capacity and 1 to 5 points for a step of 10%. C1b is predicted between 0.2 and 1.5 points, because the prevailing output already follows the conditions at the origin. C2 is predicted to keep 30% to 60% of C1b, limited by the days a shift spends undetected or unknown. C7 is predicted inside ±0.1 for L-rule and L-soft, and C8 predicted as a gain of 1 point or more, because L-all carries every switching event forward. C3 is predicted inside its noise for level steps and as a gain only for the solar-shaped kind. C4 and C5 are predicted inside ±0.03, and C6 under 0.3 points.

    2. Three states: the slow level, the era, and the switching event

    The slow level is the Candidate's per-Primary output offset, and the offset stays as built. The offset is piecewise-linear between monthly knots from 2019-09, is added to the three mixture means, and carries a penalty of 10 times the mean squared first difference of the knots (LagOffsetMdn.penalty, e00572_model.py). The offset models gradual change: demand growth, slow growth of rooftop solar, and efficiency. Experiments 573 and 575 found that an offset cannot date a step.

    An era is a discrete index per Primary that almost never changes. Each half-hour inherits the era of the half-hour before. A switch of era is sudden, large, and assumed permanent. Every learned arm encodes that prior: a constant hazard of one shift per 730 days in the change-point detector, a self-transition probability of 1 − 1/180 per day in the hidden-Markov model, and a minimum size below which a change is never an era.

    A switching event is a temporary shift that belongs to neither state. The event has an onset, a depth, and an end, and the NRA forecast must not contain the event.

    What the forecast does in each state of a shift:

    State at the origin NRA output Prevailing output Slow level and training rows
    Undetected Unchanged The existing survival head follows the shift from its own lagged inputs Unchanged
    unknown (alarm fired, class undecided) Unchanged: the shift is not carried, and the era encoder reads an unknown flag and the shift's age As above Rows since the estimated onset are held out of stage 3 until the class is known
    era Era vector on; era anchor added to the means; lags and gap signals from before the onset translated to the new level Follows through the Candidate's quantiles Penalty break at the onset; earlier-era rows weighted by 0.1
    switch The estimated depth is subtracted from lags and gap signals inside the event; no era vector and no anchor The existing survival head carries the event Rows whose valid time lies inside the event are dropped from stage 3; the era index and the offset are untouched

    A wrong era class is reversed. If the level returns to within tolerance of the earlier era inside 120 days, the shift is re-classed as a switching event, the era vector and anchor are withdrawn, and the rows are dropped from the next refit.

    The era anchor re-anchors the slow level without resetting the knots, and the anchor applies only where no stage-1 knot already carries the new level. In the Candidate, stage 1 trains every knot, and in stages 2 and 3 the older knots are a frozen buffer (knots_old) while only the newest knot (knot_new) trains (LagOffsetMdn, e00572_model.py). An anchor added to stage-3 rows whose stage-1 knots already hold the new level would count the step twice. The rule is therefore: for a stage-3 row, the anchor of an era is added only when the era's onset lies after the stage-1 cut, or when the row's valid time lies after the newest knot (where tau is clamped to the newest knot). For an era whose onset lies inside the stage-1 history, as at D8's two era onsets, the old knots after the onset already carry the level, the anchor is zero, and the era vector alone enters. A known-answer check injects an era into stage-1 rows, with its anchor, and asserts that the stage-3 output does not shift by twice the step. The first-difference penalty is unchanged: dropping the pair of the last old knot and the new knot would remove the only constraint on knot_new. A detector restart whose change is under the minimum size leaves the era index unchanged and is left to the offset.

    3. What separates an era from a switching event

    Both look like a sudden level shift in the recent data, and four kinds of evidence separate them.

    • The neighbours' mirror image. A switching event moves load between electrical neighbours, so a neighbour shows the opposite shift and the sum is conserved. An era changes the substation's own net demand, and no neighbour moves. The classifier reuses the causal mirror statistic of ml-experiments/00687-causal-detector-features-for-the-prevailing-forecast-heads/e00687_features.py, which reads no input later than 6 hours before the origin. Two limits apply: a neighbour may be unmetered, and experiment 456 found that a mirror test passed at shifted dates as often as at true dates.
    • Duration and reversal. The verified events of experiment 580 last 7 to 60 days and revert. The probability of era therefore rises with every day a shift persists, from a prior built on the labelled durations. Duration alone decides slowly, and experiment 101 found that 80.5% of logged half-hours lie in closures of more than 60 days.
    • The weather response and the daily shape. A new solar farm adds a term that follows radiation and is zero at night. A transferred load has the daily shape of demand and follows temperature. A gas engine follows neither. The classifier regresses the daily change in each hour band on the day's radiation and temperature. The weather is the ensemble mean of the freshest run published by the origin; ERA5 is used only for days at least 7 days before the origin, because ERA5 arrives about 5 days late.
    • Existing causal detectors of switching. The lag gate G4 of experiment 592 (fires a median of 1 day after onset, with 0.20 false triggers per Primary-year) and the step statistics of experiment 687 are inputs. The labels of experiment 41 and the verified set of experiment 580 are built with hindsight, so those labels score the classifier and never feed a forecast.

    L-rule sums the evidence as log-odds and holds three states. The class stays unknown until the summed log-odds pass a threshold in either direction. The weights and thresholds are set once on injected shifts in 2019-09 to 2024-12 at Primaries other than D8, and are then frozen.

    L-soft trains a small neural classifier on the same evidence plus the outputs of gate G4 and of the features of experiment 687. The classifier is a network with one hidden layer of 16 units, trained on injected eras and injected switching events in the same tuning period, and outputs a calibrated probability of era that is refreshed daily. The forecast uses the probability as a weight, so L-soft has no unknown state. For scoring, L-soft's shift counts as unclassified while the probability lies between 0.2 and 0.8.

    L-hmm uses duration and recurrence only. A state that the filter leaves within 28 days was a switching event, and a state held for 28 days becomes an era. L-hmm tests how far the cheapest evidence goes, at substations with no metered neighbour.

    5. The signal, row by row

    Every signal at a forecast row is a function of power readings up to the end of the UTC day before the origin day. The Candidate forecasts at 09:00 UTC on origin day D and relabels the origin to 12:00 UTC. The detector and classifier state used at origin D is the state after day D − 1, whose last reading ends 9 hours before 09:00. A training row with origin o carries the state after day o − 1, never a hindsight label, so one function builds training rows and forecast rows. Arm O is the one exception and is labelled non-causal.

    Detector input. For each Primary and UTC day, six numbers: the mean residual in four hour bands (night, morning, midday, evening), and the day's 5th and 95th percentile of the half-hourly residual. The residual is power over capacity minus a month-by-half-hour climatology (ClimatologyForecaster), trained on the 730 days that end 28 days before the start of the day's month. Each feature is scaled by a robust spread from the first 365 days.

    The detector is online Bayesian change-point detection (Adams and MacKay (2007)): an independent Normal mean and variance per feature within a segment, a constant hazard of 1/730 per day, and a run length truncated at 730 days. An alarm fires on the first day when the posterior mass on run lengths under 28 days has exceeded a threshold on 3 consecutive days and the change in segment mean is at least 3 scaled units in one feature and at least 5% of capacity. The threshold is tuned to at most 0.5 false alarms per Primary-year on clean history.

    The columns written for each (Primary, origin day): the class state (none, unknown, era, switch, or L-soft's probability), the shift's age in days (log-scaled, capped at 365), the six-number change vector (mean of the features since the estimated onset minus the previous segment's mean), the anchor, and the estimated depth of a switching event. The change vector and the anchor update daily for 28 days after an era class and are then frozen until the next era switch, so the era signal is piecewise-constant after a settling window.

    Two delays are printed for every known and injected shift: the detection delay (alarm day minus onset) and the classification delay (class day minus alarm day). The printed example has this form (the values show the layout only and are not measurements):

    primary=D8 onset=2026-02-01 truth=era kind=level size=-0.25

2026-02-04     0.93        no     -             none      -
2026-02-06     0.99        yes    +0.4          unknown   2026-02-07 09:00 UTC

    Arm L-hmm follows the stickiness idea of Fox et al. (2011): three Gaussian states per Primary over the six features, fitted by expectation-maximisation on days before the fold's training cut, with the self-transition probability fixed at 1 − 1/180. The signal is the forward-filtered state probabilities after day D − 1, with no smoothing.

    Arms O and P. O reads the true onset, the true class, and the true size (for D8, the hindsight mean over the era). P draws the same number of era switches per Primary at uniform random dates at least 28 days from any true shift, each carrying a permuted true change vector, with a fixed seed.

    A perturbation test proves the cut before any fit. The check changes every reading at or after 00:00 of day D, at the Primary and at its neighbours, and asserts that no signal value at origin D changes, for 200 random (Primary, origin) pairs per arm, in the style of check_state_cut in e00696_checks.py.

    7. How the signal enters the network, and the frozen encoders

    The mixer-entry arms add columns to mixer_ft at stage 3 only, with zero initial weights. A new class EraPermLagMdn(PermLagMdn) holds an era encoder (9 inputs: unknown flag, class weight, age, six changes; 16 hidden GELU units; 8 outputs) and widens mixer_ft[0] by 8 inputs with widen_first_layer from e00696_model.py. A zero era vector reproduces PermLagMdn, which a check asserts to 1e-6. The anchor is one more column read by index and added to the means, the pattern of OutputOffsetDrift.

    Within a fold the encoders are trained once, in stage 1, on rows from 2019-09, and are frozen in stages 2 and 3; the chain repeats stage 1 at each monthly fold (chain.sh of experiment 667). An era input learnt in stage 1 would need a label on every row back to 2019-09. The design handles the constraint in two steps.

    • O-enc avoids stage 1. The era vector enters the weather encoder and the date-time encoder through a separate zero-initialised nn.Linear(8, 64, bias=False) per encoder, whose output is added to that encoder's first-layer pre-activation. The two layers are registered on the network outside model.weather and model.datetime. (widen_first_layer replaces the first layer's weight with one nn.Parameter, and the frozen filter in train() of e00559_fit.py leaves every parameter of those two modules out of the optimiser, so widened columns would stay zero and O-enc would equal O.) A check asserts that the layers' weights move after one epoch of stage 3. Every pre-trained encoder weight stays frozen. Labels are needed only on stage 3 rows, and O-enc stays paired with A0. The lag-side calls of the two encoders pass a zero era vector, which is a stated limit.
    • A full pre-training arm (O-enc-pre) is conditional on C3. Its historic labels are causal by the as-of rule: the forward detector and classifier run once over the whole record, and a stage 1 row at time t reads the state after the day before t, which also lies before the fold's cut. O-enc-pre shares no weights with A0, so its contrast carries the unpaired noise of section 11.

    The curriculum teaches the two classes apart. The Candidate's recipe already injects lag-only shifts into stage 3: the lag moves and the target does not, which is a switching event with the NRA target. The curriculum adds persistent steps to half of the training passes: the target and the lags both move, with sizes uniform in ±5% to ±30% of capacity and a flat, midday-weighted, or evening-weighted band profile. The detector and classifier run on each injected series, so the learned arms train on signals with real delays, real unknown periods, and real misclassifications. A0-aug has the persistent steps and no signal.

    8. Arms that were dropped, and why

    • A piecewise-constant embedding trained end to end with a penalty on the number of switches. Experiments 573, 575, and 577 found that penalised step offsets date at most 23% to 32% of steps, and a knot learnt in training cannot switch after the training cut.
    • A soft latent state from recent residuals. The Candidate's lag encoder already reads 60 gap signals with half-lives of 1, 3, and 7 days, so A0 is that arm.
    • Era row weights as an arm of their own, and training on the current era only. The weights stay inside the era package. Stage 3's 90-day half-life already discounts old rows, and C6 says whether the package helps after a refit.
    • Penalised offline segmentation (PELT, Killick et al. (2012)). Run daily on an expanding window, PELT is the same kind of arm as the change-point detector. Stage 0 uses a penalised least-squares segmentation in hindsight as a cross-check.
    • L-R (reset only). Stage 0 found that L-rule classed 3 of 64 steps of 25% as era by day 28, so an arm that acts only on L-rule's era class would differ from A0 on almost no row. L-stat takes its place.
    • The hindsight switching labels as a forecast input. The labels are not available at the origin, so the labels score the classifier only.
    • The weather encoder and the date-time encoder as separate arms, and the gas-engine state. The split is worth a fit only if C3 shows a gain, and experiment 696 tested the engine state.

    9. Known-answer tests and the injected bank

    Stage 0 scores the detector and the classifiers on CPU, before any fit. The known eras are D8's two register connections and the other register rows of section 4. The known switching events are the experiment 41 labelled set and the experiment 580 verified set. The injected shifts have known dates, sizes, and classes. For each classifier the report prints the confusion table (era, switch, unknown, undetected) at 7, 14, 28, and 60 days after onset, the two delay distributions, and the share of switching events ever carried as an era with the days carried.

    Stage 0 gates, at no more than 0.5 false alarms per Primary-year:

    • Detection: median delay at most 7 days for era steps of 25%, and at most 21 days for steps of 10%; D8's 2023-08-15 change alarmed within 14 days. Earlier detectors alarmed at D8 65 and 113 days late (experiment 328).
    • Classification of eras: at least 70% of injected era steps of 25% classed era by day 28, and both D8 connections classed era within 28 days of the alarm.
    • Classification of switches: at most 10% of injected switching events, and at most 15% of the verified events of experiment 580, ever classed era.

    Two cautions on the real switching labels. The verified events were selected for a mirrored shift, so the mirror evidence is circular on that set and the score is optimistic. Experiment 565's erratum records that telemetry confirms few of the experiment 41 labels, so a miss on that set may be a wrong label. Experiment 295 labelled 42% of 14 to 60 day load shifts as states, which is the failure these gates guard against.

    The injected bank holds both classes. Each of 8 passes per fold injects shifts at 8 host Primaries on the first day of the scored month, so every event has 28 days before the next monthly refit. Four hosts get an era and four get a switching event.

    • Era kinds: a level step of −10% of capacity, a level step of −25%, a level step of +15% (a load connects), and a solar-shaped reduction with a peak of 25% of capacity that follows the reanalysis radiation at the valid time. The solar-shaped kind is absent from the curriculum.
    • Switching kinds: a transfer with the daily shape of the donor's demand, mirrored at an electrical neighbour so the pair's sum is conserved, reverting after a duration drawn from the durations of the verified events, built with the shared generator of experiment 660. One in four has its mirror hidden (the neighbour is treated as unmetered), and one in four lasts 60 to 120 days. Those two are the hard cases, reported apart.
    • Truth: an era event is scored against the injected power for both outputs. A switching event is scored against the power without the injected shift for the NRA output, and against the injected power for the prevailing output.

    The retrained worlds test days 28 to 90 and the refit's handling of a switching event. Two worlds each inject 4 eras and 4 switching events on one date (2025-12-01 and 2026-02-01), and the three folds after each date are refitted on the world's data.

    D8's real era switches are descriptive. Both register connections fall before the first scored month, 2025-11, so stage 0 tests them on the detector and classifier alone. Scoring the network across 2025-03-24 needs three extra folds (2025-04 to 2025-06) on at most 14 months of ensemble history, because the stored ensemble summaries start on 2024-04-01. Those folds are an optional last phase.

    11. Scoring, noise, and the decision rule

    Rows are sliced by the origin's distance from the onset, and by the state of the shift at the origin. The distance slices are: the 14 days before the onset with a valid time after the onset (reported, in no contrast); days 0 to 7; days 7 to 28; days 28 to 90 (retrained worlds); and stable rows, 90 days or more after any shift and outside events. The state slices are scored separately for every learned arm: undetected, unknown, classified correctly, and classified wrongly. Every table reports both outputs, CRPS, the normalised mean absolute error of the median, coverage and pinball loss at q01, q05, q95, and q99, the four load-shift scenarios of the ml-experiment skill, cold winter nights, and the avoidable-curtailment cost. The row set is experiment 677's monthly-refit rows, valid times 2025-11-01 to 2026-06-30.

    The minimum detectable effect comes from measured refit noise. Nine contrasts at a family level of 95% give a two-sided z of 2.77, and 80% power adds 0.84, so the detectable effect is 3.61 standard errors.

    Verdict.

    • Supported: C1b and C2 pass their thresholds with intervals that exclude zero, and every veto holds.
    • Partly supported: C1b passes and C2 does not (a perfect signal helps and detection or classification is the limit), or C2 passes and a veto on switching events fails.
    • Not supported: C1b's interval does not exclude zero in the gain direction, which means the prevailing output already does what an era signal would do.

    14. Scaling to 2,500 substations without labels

    No arm that would be ported needs a label, and the cost per substation is small. The detector reads only the substation's own power and a climatology: one day's update costs 730 run lengths times 6 features, so 2,500 substations take well under a second per day in NumPy. The classifiers add a regression on a few weeks of daily values and, where a neighbour is metered, the mirror statistic. The era encoder is shared, so L-rule and L-soft add no parameter per substation, and L-hmm adds 12. The bank's hidden-mirror cases measure what is lost at a substation with no metered neighbour. The curriculum needs no real labels. The oracle arm and the era table do not scale and are not meant to.

    17. Risks

    • The prevailing output may already capture the gain, in which case C1b fails and the result is a clean not supported.
    • A long or unmirrored switching event may be classed as an era. C7, the carried-depth veto, and the hard cases of the bank measure the harm, and the reversal rule limits the duration.
    • A real era may be classed as a switching event and erased from training rows. The state slices report the cost of that error.
    • The injected switching events come from the generator of experiment 660, and the curriculum's lag-only shifts from the Candidate's recipe, so a classifier may learn the generator. The real labelled sets in stage 0 are the check.
    • The curriculum may teach A0-aug to follow lags. A0 and A0-aug are both reported.
    • Injected steps are simpler than a real connection. A gas engine runs on a price-driven pattern that the four era kinds do not copy.
    • Experiment 565 found that an embedding added to the offset removed the offset's gain. The anchor rule is the answer, and C4 would show the conflict.
    • The noise figures come from experiment 677's step 5 network and from prevailing heads, not from the Candidate's stage 3, which is why the noise step comes first. A0 is refitted in the batch for every seed, because a control fitted in another batch scored 0.083 points apart in experiment 696.

    20. Further design decisions (these refine earlier sections)

    The sections above state the arms and the two largest decisions (the encoder entry in section 7 and the anchor rule in section 2). The list below states the remaining decisions.

    1. One cut everywhere. Recompute the mirror statistic of experiment 687 and gate G4 of experiment 592 at the end of day D − 1, so the perturbation test, which changes every reading from 00:00 of day D, holds for all inputs.
    2. Era encoder identity. The encoder output is multiplied by an indicator that the state is not none. The check asserts the identity after training, on rows whose state is none.
    3. C7 rows. The bank is inference-only on one fold's network, so C7 and the carried-depth veto use only switching events that end within 21 days of the injection. Longer events are scored in the retrained worlds.
    4. Duration bands. The gates and C7 are reported for events under 60, 60 to 120, and over 120 days. The bank gains transfers longer than 120 days.
    5. Verified set size. The verified set of experiment 580 holds 27 events, 4 with onsets in the scored window. State n = 27 and a binomial interval for the 15% gate. The duration prior, the injector's durations, and L-soft's training exclude those 4 events.
    6. Signed power at MVA-only Primaries. Injected steps and the solar shape are built in signed power and then passed through an absolute value at MVA hosts. The telemetry estimate of D8's solar size states how it handles reverse power flow (Monitor Direction.csv marks D8 "Yes").
    7. D15 and D18. Their state is none where the climatology and spread do not exist, and they stay out of the host pool.
    8. Winner's curse. The phase C bank uses fresh injection seeds and hosts, and C2 is also reported on the four folds the screen did not use.
    9. Decision rule. C1b or C2 with an interval that excludes zero below its threshold is small gain. A failed C7 or carried-depth veto gives "not supported for adoption", whatever C2 shows.
    10. Learning the injector. The bank, the curriculum, and the classifier's training use different generator settings and seeds, and C2 is also reported on the out-of-curriculum solar-shaped kind. The claim is scoped to "injected level steps" unless the solar-shaped kind also passes.
    11. Curriculum harm. Add L-best minus A0-aug and A0-aug minus A0 beside C4 and C7.
    12. D8 fade and network-level D8 test. D8 from 2026-02-15 to 2026-05-31 leaves the stable rows, and the fade is reported as a descriptive real case scored on the network. The network-level test across D8's 2025-03-24 connection stays in optional phase E, and the plan says so plainly: the stored ensemble summaries start on 2024-04-01, so three extra folds with 14 months of history are possible, and the maintainer decides whether to fund them.
    13. Bank hosts. An era host is never the mirror partner of, or a neighbour of, a switching pair in the same pass. A Primary with no metered neighbour hosts only the hidden-mirror kind.
    14. Stop-rule table corrections. Row 295: it scored a latest-regime offset after a step (2.2 points better), and a trailing 60-day mean residual did as well, so the trailing residual offset and experiment 706's look-back offset become no-fit reference rows on the bank.     21. Stage 0 results and the L-stat arm (interim)

    Stage 0 ran on 2026-11-19 and the numbers below are interim. The maintainer asked for arm L-stat: detect eras from 28-day summary statistics of raw power, find steps in those statistics, give each step an era id, and feed the id to the network. The design of L-stat is in this section and in the arm table. It replaces L-R, so the arm count and the compute budget stay level.

    The perturbation test found one real leak, which is fixed. Experiment 687's noise unit and the G4 threshold read past the origin for neighbours with an incomplete window. The rule and soft classifiers were refitted, and the cut test passes for 200 pairs and every arm.

    The daily-feature change-point detector detects few small steps. It needs a step of 3 noise units in one feature. At D8 one unit is 9% to 13% of capacity, so it detects 13 of 64 injected steps of 10% and 14 of 64 solar-shaped steps, and misses D8's 2025 solar connection. It detects D8's gas change 18 days after 2023-08-15 (gate: 14 days). Injected steps of 25% pass the detection gate (34 of 64 within 7 days).

    L-stat detects both D8 changes and rarely mistakes a load shift for an era.

    • D8 gas change: alarm 10 days after 2023-08-15, classed era 9 days later (gate passed).
    • D8 solar change: alarm 33 days after the register date (2025-03-24) and 21 days after the telemetry date fitted on the same statistic (2025-04-05). The gate passes on the telemetry date, which rests on the same statistic as the alarm, so the pass is labelled as such. The earlier telemetry estimate (2025-06-10) was 9 weeks later and is replaced.
    • Verified switching events (experiment 580, 15 transfers outside the scored window): 0 ever classed era (Wilson 95% interval 0.00 to 0.20). Experiment 41 labelled events: 2 of 93 (0.02).
    • Injected bank, era class by day 28: L-stat 79 of 190 era events, against 13 for L-rule. L-stat's era class on injected switching events is 5 of 211 at day 28.
    • False alarms: 0.31 per Primary-year on clean history at threshold 4.0 (limit 0.5).

    Expectation, recorded before any fit: L-stat keeps 10% to 30% of the ceiling on steps of 25% and under 10% on steps of 10%, because at most about 13 of the 28 scored days follow an era class (the state needs a dwell of 14 days). C7 is within +-0.1.

    Plan changes from the L-stat design.

    • L-stat is eligible as L-best. Two descriptive readings sit outside the nine contrasts: L-stat minus L-rule on injected era events (NRA output), and L-stat with its era-id table zeroed at inference, on stable rows and on rows after an era.
    • Era id 0 means "no era yet" and carries no vector. An id first confirmed after the training cut uses one shared "new era" vector.
    • Anchor rule for every learned arm: the anchor equals the level change minus the offset's own change between the knot before the onset and the newest knot, clipped between zero and the full change. The known-answer check gains a case with an era confirmed after a refit. Phases B and C never exercise that case, and phase D does.
    • Phase B gains 20 stage 3 fits (about 2 GPU job-hours) and about 1 hour of bank inference, and loses L-R. An irregular-engine era kind exists in stage 0 only.

    24. Phase B2 (proposed): mix the curriculum into every batch

    Phase B2 keeps phase B's selection rule and mixes the injected world into every batch, for four arms. A fixed number of epochs was rejected in the Opus review, because stage 3 fine-tunes the stage 2 weights at a low learning rate and every arm kept its first epoch. A fixed run of 4 epochs would give every arm, A0 included, a worse baseline than the production recipe, and the last update would be made on the injected world. The arms are A0-aug, the oracle, the placebo, and L-stat. A0 never reads the injected world, so A0 under B2 is phase B's A0, and B2 reuses those fits.

    Rule. In every epoch, each training row in a batch reads the curriculum tensors with probability 0.5, and its plain values otherwise. The coin comes from a separate CPU generator seeded with seed * 1000 + epoch, so the row order is phase B's. Selection is unchanged: patience 8, best state restored, loss on plain validation rows. The per-epoch validation loss is logged. The odd-epoch alternation of phase B is retired, and the curriculum acts from the first step, so selection cannot strip it out.

    Contrasts. Each B2 arm is paired against A0 from phase B on the same rows: A0-aug minus A0 (lag-only learning from injected eras), oracle minus A0, placebo minus A0, and L-stat minus A0. The write-up also reports CRPS on the touched Primaries separately, as a harm check. It says that the selection rule changed after an interim look and why, gives the realised number of eras per fold, and says that for the signal arms the mixing is partly a noise augmentation, because the injected rows get a second noise draw.

    B2 runs on phase B's cached curriculum worlds. The world held 2 eras in fold 2025-11, not the planned 8, because each pass touches up to 6 of 13 eligible Primaries and the passes must be disjoint. The cache is keyed by fold and seed, so B2 reuses the same worlds, and alt_eras is logged for every fit. A fold with fewer than 4 eras is reported as a weak test of the curriculum.

    Decision rule, fixed before the first B2 fit. A B2 arm helps when its difference from A0 on all rows is below 0 with the bootstrap interval (blocks of fold month and Primary, 95%) excluding zero, and the difference on the touched Primaries is not above +0.05 points. An arm whose interval contains zero is reported as not distinguishable from A0. Every B2 number is interim until all 4 folds and both seeds are scored.

    Cost. 4 arms on 4 folds and 2 seeds is 32 fits, about 9 to 10 minutes each at 2 concurrent jobs, so about 2.5 hours of wall-clock time after phase B finishes. Phase B's aug result is void.
