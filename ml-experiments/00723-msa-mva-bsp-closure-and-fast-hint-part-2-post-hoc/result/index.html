    Does the half-hourly output of solar farms under the same sky label the export stretches of a Primary that reports apparent power, in worlds where a farm is injected?

    We asked whether the output of solar farms under the same sky shows which way power flows through a substation that reports only a magnitude. Under the pre-registered rules the verdict is invalid, because a registered correctness check failed, and no test of the question ran. A Primary is a substation that steps the voltage down to 11 kilovolts (kV). A Primary that reports apparent power in megavolt-amperes (MVA) has no sign, so an exporting Primary shows a rise in its reading and not a negative value. A Primary that reports real power in megawatts (MW) has a signed series, and we call it a signed-MW Primary. A Primary exports when power flows out of it into the wider electricity network, and imports when power flows into it. A sign label states, for a stretch of readings, whether the Primary exports or imports. A farm-and-Primary pair is one metered solar farm together with one host Primary, and the pair qualifies for job W2 under the rule stated below. No sign label was produced.

    The experiment had three jobs. Job W0 ran checks that read no direction of flow. Job W2 would have added the recorded output of a real farm to a signed-MW Primary, hidden the sign, and scored how well the farms' output separates export from import. Job W3 would have run controls, and runs only if job W2 is supported.

    The registered verdict is invalid, and this is the pre-registered reading of the rules. The verdict rule says that the first row that matches gives the verdict. Row 1, invalid, matches when a correctness check fails, and the day count of erratum ruling 2 is one of those checks. Job W0 asserts that the later span has 413 days and calls any other count a code defect. The run counted 420 calendar days (later_span_ok is false), so row 1 matches before row 2 is reached. The committed code agrees: its correctness flag includes the day-count check, and its verdict function returns invalid when that flag is false. Job W0 ran once (attempt 1), took 5.1 core-seconds on one core, and read nothing on or after 2026-07-01. Job W3 did not run. Every bar, threshold, and ruling of the plan stays as written.

    POST HOC reading: the diagnosis finds that 413 was a flaw in the plan, and on that reading job W2 is inconclusive, row 2. The diagnosis found that 413 is experiment 722's count of days with a usable fit row, and not the length of the span, which the job counts correctly (finding 3). Job W2 would be void before run on a second ground that does not depend on the day count: fewer than 4 farm-and-Primary pairs qualify (finding 4), and row 2 of the verdict rule gives that case inconclusive. This reading was formed after the run. It does not override the registered reading, and the verdict of this page stays invalid.

    Independently of the day count, zero farm-and-Primary pairs qualify, so job W2 would be void before run. A pair qualifies when the farm and the host Primary are under the same sky (correlation of the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service (CAMS) series at least 0.5), the farm lies outside the host's service-area polygon, the host is one of the six signed-MW Primaries (D5, D9, D10, D11, D14, and D16), and the host's farm sensor keeps at least 2 farms once the injected farm is removed. The decision rule voids job W2 when fewer than 4 pairs qualify. The other findings below would each need a ruling before a rerun. The pair count was not recomputed with a repaired arc term, so the page does not say what a repair of finding 2 would do to the count.

    What job W0 found

    Finding 1: the spreadsheet of switching events could not be read

    The code could not read the workbook, which is a code defect, and no mask of switching events was built. The workbook lists switching events between Primaries, and the job uses it to mask the affected half-hours. The read stopped with a TypeError, which is the only fact w0_checks.json holds about the workbook. The private diagnosis, which opened the workbook to list its sheets and cell types and reproduced the error on a synthetic workbook, found 32 sheets: one Info sheet of text and 31 event sheets named by a pair of numbers or by a number and the word "unknown". The event sheets have no header row, so the first data row was taken as the column names, and a date cannot be a column name. The synthetic workbook in the tests had a text header row, so the tests passed. The structure on this page therefore comes from the diagnosis, and no W0 output stores it.

    Repairing the read would not be enough, because the workbook names each Primary by its sheet name, and the registered rule looks for a column. The ruling on switching-log times and the decision rule assume a substation column and a start and an end column in each row. Each real sheet has eight columns with no names, and two of them hold dates. A rule must say how a sheet name maps to Primaries, which date counts, and whether a window runs within one row or from an opening row to a later row. No such rule exists, so no mask was built, and every statistic would be labelled "transfers not masked". The file w0_switching_sheets.csv holds no sheet, because the read failed.

    Finding 2: the check on the sun's daily arc failed at every site

    The arc-term void flag fired at every site, because the clear-sky solar output used in the audit is clipped at the inverter limit. This is a flaw in how the audit was specified, not in the data. The arc term is the smooth rise and fall of a farm's output with the sun's height, which the fast change must remove. The audit counts the share of lit half-hours that end on the hour (the two ends of each hourly CAMS value) on which u_clear does not change. u_clear is the clear-sky output per megawatt (MW) of a generic fleet of photovoltaic (PV) panels in experiment 39. The plan expected 0%, and the run gave 16.8% to 18.1% across the 15 sites (the 9 Primaries and the 6 farms). The fleet's alternating-current (AC) capacity limits u_clear to 1.0 around midday, so both half-hours of those hours read 1.0. A synthetic reproduction in the diagnosis gave 18.2% with the clip and 0.0% with the clip removed. The 1% threshold voids the arc term when any one site exceeds it, and every site did. The arc-removal condition itself passes for all 6 farms, with correlations of size at most 0.032 against a bar of 0.1.

    The Opus audit before merge confirmed the diagnosis on stored data, and not only on the synthetic sky. On the stored frame of experiment 39 (26 sites, lit hour-ending pairs with the sun at least 10 degrees above the horizon at both ends, rows before 2026-07-01), the share of pairs with an unchanged u_clear runs from 16.8% to 18.2%. Every unchanged pair has both halves at exactly 1.0, the fleet's AC limit. On the same rows the CAMS clear-sky index is exactly unchanged on 56.1% to 58.2% and unchanged within 1e-12 on 100%. The check read no generator output column. The audit classes the first failure as a specification flaw and the second as a code defect, as above (section "Audit before merge").

    The second failed expectation is a second code defect. The report-only share of half-hours on which the CAMS clear-sky index does not change is 56.1% to 58.2%, against 100% expected. The audit tests exact equality of floating-point values, and the diagnosis found that a synthetic run gives 52% exactly equal and 100% equal within 1e-12. The shortfall is report-only and changes no verdict.

    Finding 3: the later span has 420 calendar days, not 413

    The later span has 420 calendar days, and the diagnosis calls the plan's 413 a specification flaw. The registered rules call any other count a code defect, and that is why the registered verdict is invalid. Experiment 722's write-up quotes 413 as the number of days on which its closure fit had at least one usable fit row (n_days_used). The plan copied 413 as the span's calendar length, and the job's assertion of 413 failed (later_span_ok is false). The seven extra days are, by the diagnosis's inference, days on which experiment 722's fit had no usable row. The diagnosis did not name the days, and a recount of experiment 722's days would confirm or reject the inference. The span starts on the same day in both experiments and ends the day before the holdout, and no row on or after 2026-07-01 was read.

    Finding 4: zero qualifying same-sky injection pairs

    After the coherence floor, only D9 keeps two farms in its sky sensor, and injecting one of them leaves one, so no signed host can host a pair. The zero is a data fact under the registered rules. The pair table holds 54 farm-and-Primary pairs on 9 Primaries (the six signed-MW Primaries, D3, D12, and D15). Of the 36 pairs at the six signed-MW Primaries, 21 are under the same sky, and 18 of those lie outside the host's polygon. Each of the six signed-MW Primaries has two or more same-sky farms outside its polygon: 2 at D5, 2 at D9, 3 at D10, 2 at D11, 6 at D14, and 3 at D16. The coherence floor of 0.5 then removes farms from each host's sensor. The audit holds 32 rows, one for each pair of a farm and a host sensor that the audit tested, over all 9 Primaries, and 6 of them reach 0.5 (the correlations run from 0.14 to 0.56). D9 keeps 2 farms, D14 keeps 1, and D5, D10, D11, and D16 keep none. D12 and D15 also keep farms but are not signed hosts. Injecting one of D9's two farms leaves a sensor of one farm. No pair qualifies and no host is powered, and the rule needs 4 pairs on 2 hosts.

    Job W3 could not have scored either control on the farm sensor, and the page's other findings do not show it. A host's farm sensor exists only when the host keeps at least 2 farms. D12 keeps 1 farm and D3 keeps 0, so D12's anchor-stretch control and D3's post-bounce control have no farm sensor under the stored farm table. The same fact is why D12's sky share is NaN: the function that computes the share returns NaN when D12 has fewer than 2 farms. Even a supported job W2 would therefore have led to a job W3 with no scorable control on the farm sensor.

    The reading of the coherence floor that decides the stop is not ruled, and the stop does not depend on it. The code forms each farm's comparison mean from every farm that entered the check, in one pass. No ruling of the plan states that reading, and the code marks it neither ruled nor provisional. POST HOC, the Opus audit recounted the pairs from the stored single-pass correlations with the floor lowered. At 0.5 the count is 0 pairs, as in the file. At 0.45 the count is 4 pairs and at 0.40 it is 6 pairs, both on D14 alone, so the rule of at least 2 hosts still voids job W2. A second host appears only near a floor of 0.30. A reading that drops the weakest farm and recomputes was not tested, because it needs private arrays.

    The amendment's 12 qualifying pairs is a different count. The 12 equals the same-sky farms outside the polygon at the three hosts that hold at least three of them, namely 3 at D10, 6 at D14, and 3 at D16, with no coherence floor (recomputed from w0_pairs.csv). The coherence floor, which the amendment registered, is what removes them.

    Counts at D10 and D15 that the run produced

    D10's export hours are few, as the plan predicted. An export hour is an hour-ending row in which D10's signed reading is negative. On the base of 10,078 hour-ending rows with a reading at both ends in the later span, D10 shows 59 export hours on 22 days. The plan quoted 77 hours on 36 days from a review, and the bases of the two counts differ. These counts, and the power table, come from the 420 calendar days of the later span and with no switching mask, because the workbook was unread. No later page may quote them as figures for a 413-day span or for masked transfers. The share of negative half-hours is 0.79% of all half-hours and 1.9% of daylight half-hours. The power table puts the predicted standard error of D10's fold ratio on export hours at 3.16 on 59 rows. Its detection limit is 8.85, so the slope cannot be read. D15's farm class from experiment 722's job 1 is unresolved, so the D15 dead-band bar does not bind, and the farm is not an add-back candidate at D15. D12's sky share (r²) is NaN, because D12 keeps 1 farm and has no farm sensor (finding 4).

    What the result does not show

    The result shows nothing about the injection-recovery bars, the closure slope, or the sign anchor. No world was built, so the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve, the placebo, the transfer check, the D10 recall, and the D12 and D3 controls were never scored. No slope was fitted. The run does not show that the within-day idea fails. It shows that, under the registered pair rule, the stored farm table gives no qualifying pair.

    Experiment 723 is the 20th experiment under the plan's stop rule, and it produced no sign label. The stop rule counts an experiment that holds at least one pre-registered test whose outcome could admit a sign label. Experiment 723 holds such tests, so it is the 20th, but it ran none of them. It admitted no hint, set no prior, and licensed no later design.

    The outcome table of the plan gives the same untangler result under both verdict words. Under invalid and under inconclusive alike, the fast gain a of the untangler (the weight of the within-day hint in the model that would write a series of past signed power for each Primary that reports apparent power) stays 0. Rung U3 and the build of the untangler proceed, and no third experiment on either idea starts without the maintainer's recorded approval. The two words differ in what may be rerun: the outcome table gives invalid a rerun as the same attempt under four conditions, and gives inconclusive a pause.

    Whether to rerun is a manager decision, and the manager has ruled that job W0 is not rerun, so nothing is rerun by this page. The registered rules say that inconclusive is never rerun. They also say that a rerun after invalid is the same attempt only when four conditions hold, and condition 2 is that the fix changes no constant, population, sensor, or bar. Correcting 413 changes a constant, and changing the base of the arc audit changes a condition of the decision rule. A rerun of job W0 with the three repairs would therefore be a new attempt, approved by the manager, with rule changes made post hoc, under either verdict word. The repairs would give a readable workbook summary, an audit that tests what it was meant to test, and a span check that passes. The pair count would need recomputing with the repaired arc term. A change to the coherence floor, the same-sky bar, or the sensor size, each of which is a pre-registered rule, or a different farm table, are the routes we considered that change the count.

    The plan, as registered

    Everything from here to the Method section is the plan as pre-registered, and the run changed none of it. The sections above this one, the verdict, headline, lessons, take-home message, and advice fields of the front matter, the first bullet of Method and its introduction, the "Files" introduction, and the data-file and diagnosis bullets of Files were written after the run. The rest is the plan, apart from the status field, which changed from planned to finished.

    POST HOC: we ask whether the half-hourly output of solar farms can show which way power flows through a Primary substation that reports only a magnitude, in a repeat of experiment 722 whose every test was written after 722's results were read. A Primary substation steps the voltage down to 11 kV. Eight of the 16 Primaries report apparent power in megavolt-amperes (MVA), which has no sign, so a Primary that exports solar output shows a rise in its reading and not a negative value. Where a Primary reports real power in megawatts (MW), its sign is known. Primaries are labelled D3, D8, D10, and so on. A bulk supply point (BSP) is the substation that feeds a group of Primaries, and its meter reports signed power. The two BSPs are BSP A and BSP B.

    Experiment 722 was inconclusive, and the diagnosis that followed it found four flaws in the plan and no defect in the code. Experiment 722 tested whether the BSP meter, minus the other Primaries, shows the sign of one Primary's flow. The test of the meter's closure slope, which is the slope of the hourly change of the meter's remainder on the hourly change of the left-out Primary, read about 2.0 at D15 where perfect cancellation gives 1, and the cloud-class audit failed. This experiment drops the meter route as a source of any label. Experiment 723 tests instead one use of the sky: within a stretch of daylight half-hours, does the reading of a Primary move against the sunshine (the Primary imports, so more sun lowers its reading) or with it (the Primary exports, so more sun raises its reading)?

    The terms below are used in every job.

    • Fold: an MVA reading is a magnitude, the square root of the squared signed flow plus the squared reactive floor. "Folded D10" is D10's signed series passed through the same formula, with the sign hidden from the method under test.
    • Sky sensor: a half-hourly series that moves with the sunshine over a Primary. The farm sensor is the mean output, as a share of capacity, of the metered solar farms under the same sky as the Primary and outside the Primary's own service-area polygon. The meter sensor is the negative of the BSP residual's change. The CAMS sensor is the hourly Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service estimate at the Primary's location, and serves as the reference.
    • Same-sky pair: a solar farm and a host Primary whose CAMS series correlate at 0.5 or more. Experiment 722's job 1 found 21 such pairs among 36 pairs of a farm and a host Primary. A pair qualifies for job W2 when the host's sensor keeps at least 2 farms after the injected farm is removed and the farm lies outside the host's polygon. The amendment counts 12 qualifying pairs, on three hosts (D10, D14, and D16).
    • Stretch and concordance C: a stretch is a run of daylight half-hours between two near-zero readings, inside which the sign cannot change. C is the share of usable half-hour pairs in a stretch on which the reading and the sky move the same way, minus the share on which the reading and the sky move opposite ways. C above +0.25 is export evidence, C below −0.25 is import evidence, and a stretch in between, or with fewer than 8 usable pairs, gives no evidence.
    • Closure slope and fold ratio: the closure slope is the slope of the hourly change of the residual that leaves one Primary out on that Primary's hourly change. The fold ratio is the same slope with the Primary's reading folded. The amendment shows that the fold ratio has the sign of the flow times the sign of one plus the co-movement of the rest of the residual with the Primary, and that the fold ratio's size identifies nothing.
    • Reactive floor and points: the reactive floor is the smallest reading that a magnitude meter shows when the real flow is 0. A point is 1% of the Primary's capacity, and the floor is given in points.
    • Post-bounce stretch and anchor stretch: a post-bounce stretch is a stretch that does not join the night, and an anchor stretch is a stretch that does, so night import fixes the sign of an anchor stretch.
    • Usable pair: a pair of consecutive daylight half-hours on which the sky index changes by at least 0.10 and the reading's fast change is at least 0.2 points.
    • BSP residual: the BSP meter minus every other child of the BSP.
    • Injection level: the multiple of the host's median absolute signed reading on sunny middays before 2025-07-01 at which a farm's output is added. A sunny midday is a half-hour that starts from 10:00 to 14:00 UTC, from April to September, with a clear-sky index of at least 0.7. The injected series is the farm's recorded output divided by the 99th percentile of the farm's own daylight output, with night set to zero, negative values set to zero, and a daylight dropout set to a gap, times the level, times that median (the definition of experiment 720).
    • Void: a test is void when its detection limit, 2.8 bootstrap standard errors, exceeds its bar, because a slope or area the size of the bar could not be told from zero. A void test is reported as void and never read as a pass.

    The experiment has three jobs, and no job reads a Primary's sign from the BSP meter. Job W0 decides which tests can be read and has no hypothesis. Job W2 scores the concordance in worlds where a metered solar farm's output is added to a host Primary that reports a signed series. Job W3 runs only if job W2 is supported, and checks the statistic against two controls: D12's anchor stretches, where night import fixes the sign, and D3's post-bounce stretches. D12 is the Primary whose sign is unknown. Every number on the page counts either half-hours, stretches, farm-Primary pairs, or calendar days, and each table says which.

    Which rules changed from experiment 722, and why

    Four flaws in the specification of experiment 722 changed the rules. Two bars were moved after their failure, and one group of bars was removed after its failure. The amendment says so plainly and so does this page. A rule changed after a result is seen is post hoc, so the page reports experiment 722's verdict as it stood, and any pass here is a pass of the new rule only.

    Rule in experiment 722 Rule in experiment 723 Reason, from the amendment
    D15 sign test and its placebo Dropped, with no replacement test at D10 A pooled slope reads only D15's known majority sign (D15 exports on 77% of half-hours). The sign score that deployment needs is per hour or per day, and an hour carries 0.016 nats and a day an area of 0.59. D10's export hours are too few (77 hours on 36 days) for a slope to be read
    Closure bar of 0.7 to 1.3 at D15 No size bar binds anywhere; dark-hour closure is a read-out The slope is the sign times one plus the co-movement times a positive factor, so the size identifies nothing. A bar of 0.3 would need about 935 days at D15

    What each verdict changes

    The stop rule: this is the 20th experiment that produces or opens a gate to a sign label, and it runs once. A test is a statistic with a bar or pattern stated in advance, of which at least one outcome admits: the outcome produces or opens a gate to a sign label, admits a hint or factor, sets a prior, or licenses a later design to cite the statistic as evidence for a sign. A read-out describes or withholds, and only an outcome that admits can add a false label to the record. Two counters use this definition: experiments that hold at least one test that produces or opens a gate to a sign label (the count of 18, 19th, and 20th below), and tests that use BSP closure for a sign (the "no fifth test" rule). A read-out that did not withhold may not later be cited as evidence for a hint. The design note for version 2 lists 18 earlier experiments by number: 79, 152, 358, 362, 365, 366, 370, 387, 408, 412, 417, 420, 424, 427, 506, 508, 543, and 674. Experiment 722 is the 19th. The stored BSP run is not counted. Experiment 722 was the fourth use of BSP closure for a sign, and this experiment makes no fifth test of BSP closure: its D15 fits and its pooled fold ratios are read-outs. [Decided by the manager] Experiment 723 is the 20th experiment and experiment 724 is the 21st, regardless of which runs first.

    Under every outcome, rung U3 and the build of the untangler proceed, the daylight BSP factor stays switched off, and no third experiment on either idea starts without the maintainer's recorded approval. Rung U3 is the hindsight switching model of experiment 720 with its known-answer rungs, and the untangler is the model that would write a probabilistic series of past signed power for each MVA Primary. The fast gain a is the gain between 0 and 1 on the fast PV term of the untangler's emission, and with a = 0 the untangler ignores half-hour cloud.

    Compute and pre-run gates

    The experiment needs CPU only, and its estimate is under 0.1 core-hours in total. The estimates scale from the 7.9 and 1.2 core-seconds of experiment 722's two jobs: 0.01 for job W0, 0.05 for job W2, and 0.01 for job W3. Each job has a cap of 1 core-hour. Each job runs inside one systemd-run --user --scope at a time, on one pinned core, with MemoryMax=12G, MemorySwapMax=0, OOMPolicy=continue, one thread for BLAS and Polars, CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES="", and a check for 25 GB of free memory before launch.

    The timing gate counts real units of work. Jobs W0 and W3 make one elapsed-time check each. Job W2 times the first world, scored with all four sensor variants (farm, meter, CAMS, and the sensor shifted by 28 days), and the first 10 bootstrap draws on the first world's stretches repeated to the expected size of the pooled population. It projects the whole job: seconds per world × 32 worlds + seconds per draw × 1,000, then five more pooled bootstraps in the read-outs, a second scoring of every world for the unmasked read-out, and 7 closure-fit bootstraps of 1,000 draws each. The job stops when the projection exceeds the cap, and checks the elapsed time again before the pooled bootstrap. The job writes the number of worlds it scored, the number it planned, and the name and reason of every world it skipped, and a test asserts that no world is scored twice. A projection over the cap before a job starts is reported to the manager, who may raise the cap, and writes no verdict. A stop after the job has started returns inconclusive.

    • Gate 1: a fresh Opus agent that did not write the code reviews it before any first run that produces a number the page quotes. The reporting fixes below go in with the new jobs.
    • Gate 2: the session sends the manager the message "fits starting" before each job, and waits for the reply.
    • Gate 3: the maintainer or the manager has ruled on decision 1 below.
    • Gate 4: both plan pages match erratum 1, and the must-fix items of the review of the erratum are applied.

    Three guards apply to every job.

    • Row 1 reads the known-answer tests: job W2 runs this folder's tests on synthetic arrays before it starts, and a failing test makes the verdict invalid. The run log holds the count of tests and the code hash.
    • A rerun keeps both sets of outputs: a job moves its earlier outputs into attempt_<job>_<n>/ before it writes new ones. Each job writes an attempt marker with its attempt number before it reads any input, and refuses to start when the marker exists (the earlier attempt crashed or ended with any verdict, invalid included), unless an environment variable names the job and the attempt that the manager approved.
    • A pair with no injection scale is counted, not dropped: a pair whose host has fewer than 200 reference rows with a finite injected series for the injection level gives no world. Job W0 counts such pairs, job W2 writes the name, the reason, and the count of reference rows of every skipped world to the verdict file, and the number of worlds scored is compared with the number planned.

    Three reporting fixes apply to every job.

    • R1, the row count of every statistic: each row of a results table reports n_rows from the mask that the statistic used, with the count of days. The verdict file holds the count at each filter stage: finite, daylight, outside a transfer window, usable pairs, scorable stretches, and stretches in each class.
    • R2, identities with their conditions: a docstring states Δm = s × c × ΔX only "within one sign", and the fold ratio s × (1 + κ) × g only under the linearity assumption of the amendment's section 2.1.
    • R3, an empty population shows its cause: the run log holds, for each sensor and host, the two thresholds of the usable-pair rule, the share of daylight pairs that pass each threshold alone and both together, the 99th percentile and the largest value of each change, and the count of scorable stretches.

    The experiment follows these earlier advice items: do not rerun a closure test with its old bars, check the size of a sign test before any run (job W0's power table, now a read-out), judge a placebo against a null distribution (the permutation rule), check the CAMS time resolution before defining a cloud class (the audit's first condition), and test closure on dark hours. It departs from none.

    Rulings before the run

    The design lead ruled on every reading below before any run, and none of the rulings changes a threshold, a bar, or a verdict word. The rulings answer the first and second Opus code reviews of experiment 723, which found that the plan page left each reading open. Each reading is marked RULED in the code. The readings that stay marked PROVISIONAL are the ones that the reviews judged unable to flip a verdict, apart from one low-risk case: the missing-value rule of the overcast days.

    • [Design lead] Void arc term: the arc term is void if any one site's share of hour-ending half-hours on which u_clear does not change exceeds 1%. A site whose share cannot be computed (NaN), for example a site with no lit hour-ending half-hour, also voids the arc term, and an empty set of sites is void. Job W0 writes the share of every site. When the arc term is void, job W2 builds no world and the verdict is inconclusive, row 2 (a failed sensor audit).
    • [Design lead] Placebo population: the placebo shifts only the fast change ΔZ̃ by 28 days and keeps the real sensor's index change. The placebo is read on exactly the stretches and pairs of the area, so it has no void state.
    • [Design lead] Injection level: the reference rows and the farm series are experiment 720's definition. A reference row is a half-hour that starts from 10:00 to 14:00 UTC, from April to September, before 2025-07-01, with a clear-sky index of at least 0.7, and a pair needs at least 200 such rows. The injected series is experiment 720's farm_norm: the farm's recorded output divided by the 99th percentile of the farm's own daylight output (a sun zenith under 75 degrees, before the holdout), zero at night, with negative values clipped to zero and a daylight dropout (an output of 0 or less with CAMS irradiance above 100 W/m²) set to a gap. The series is multiplied by the level and by the host's median absolute signed reading on the reference rows. The host's series is a gap wherever the farm's series is a gap, as in experiment 720; experiment 720 also masked the gaps of all three of its farms in every world of a site, and this experiment masks only the injected farm's own gaps, because the two levels of one pair share one farm. The code copies farm_norm from the folder of experiment 720 (see the Files section).
    • [Design lead] D15 dead band: the bar binds only when job 1 classes the farm inside D15's polygon outside every metered Primary and the farm is not an add-back candidate at D15. In every other case the bar is report-only. Under experiment 722's class definitions the bar is report-only in practice: a farm inside D15's polygon whose class is outside_or_added_back is also an add-back candidate at D15 whenever the farm is subtracted, so the binding condition holds only for a farm that is not subtracted. A reader should not take the D15 dead band for a live test.
    • [Design lead] Calibration bar: the bar is void by construction, because the plan page defines no likelihood ratio and nobody defines one after the fact. The code records the bar as void, and the page lists it beside the verdict.
    • [Design lead] Controls of job W3: D12's anchor stretches are scored on every day, and D3's post-bounce stretches are scored on days with exactly one post-bounce stretch.
    • [Design lead] Switching-log times: job W0 reads every cell of the workbook as text and parses each cell on its own, so one text cell such as "ongoing" in a column of dates becomes a missing time, is counted, and cannot make the sheet unreadable. A time with no time zone is read as Europe/London. A time that carries a zone is converted to UTC. The workbook program turns every date into a date and time at midnight, so two per-row rules read a date with no time. First, an end at exactly local 00:00 on a row whose end is not after a start on the same local date is local 24:00 of that date, so a same-day start never falls after its end. An end at 00:00 on a later date than the start stays at 00:00. Second, a start with no logged end covers the local calendar day of the start, from local 00:00 to local 24:00, whether the start is timed or not. A start or end in the spring-forward hour, a local time that does not exist, is shifted forward to the next valid instant (local 02:00) and counted, so its row still masks. A start or end that parses to a year outside 2000 to 2100 (a two-digit year such as "25" reads as year 25) is an error: it is counted as implausible and treated as a missing time. Windows whose end still precedes their start are counted and listed in the check file and are not masked, and the file also counts the ends that do not parse and the windows that lie wholly outside the grid.
    • [Design lead] False-export bar: the bar stays pooled over un-injected D5 and D10, as the single count in the decision rule reads, and the count of each host is reported beside it with no bar.
    • [Design lead] Pairs with no injection scale: a pair whose injection scale is not finite is logged and counted, and is never dropped without a message.
    • [Design lead] Farm that reads negative: job W0 writes, for each qualifying pair, whether the farm's median recorded output on the reference rows is 0 or less, and job W2 treats such a pair as non-qualifying and logs the count, because the injection would otherwise add import where the world is meant to gain generation.
    • [Design lead] Reruns: job W0, job W2, and job W3 each write an attempt marker and an attempt number before they read any input. A later start of the same job is refused, whether the earlier attempt crashed, ended invalid, or ended with any other verdict, unless the manager sets E723_RERUN_APPROVED_BY_MANAGER to the job and the number of the attempt to start, for example w0:2. Any other value refuses, so one approval permits one rerun, and the marker records the approval. A start that is refused or fails the launch check writes its run log to a separate file named run_log_<job>_refused_<time>.json, and never overwrites the run log of an earlier attempt. The folder of an archived attempt is named attempt_<job>_<n>, so each job's attempts are numbered on their own. The old marker is copied into that folder and then replaced, never moved, and a start whose archive folder already exists, or whose marker is unreadable, is refused, so a crash part-way through a start cannot reset the counter.
    • [Design lead] Days dropped by a farm's gaps: a gap or dropout in the injected farm's series on a lit half-hour drops the whole day from the injected world, because the world has no reading there. Job W2 writes, for each injected farm, the number of days of the later span that its gaps drop, beside the number of lit days, in the verdict file, so that a reader can tell a level 1.0 bar left unreadable by dropouts from one left unreadable by too few export stretches.
    • [Design lead] No qualifying pair: job W0 writes w0_checks.json and the pair table, with every column present, when no pair qualifies, so that job W2's row 2 records the outcome.
    • [Design lead] Timing gate: the projection covers the whole of job W2, including the second scoring for the unmasked read-out and the bootstraps of the read-outs.
    • [Design lead] Sensor on hour-ending rows: the CAMS sensor uses the hourly change of the clear-sky index and of the sky's fast change, and the reading's fast change is summed the same way, because the index of experiment 39's frame is held inside each hour. The CAMS sensor has about half as many candidate pairs per stretch as the farm sensor, so its area is read on its own, smaller set of scorable stretches, which is a different population from the farm sensor's. The prediction of 0.60 to 0.75 for the CAMS sensor refers to that population. The CAMS area enters no bar and no verdict.

    Method

    Only job W0 ran, so the bullets below describe the registered design, and the job that ran is the first bullet.

    • Job W0 ran on one core with one thread. It read the stored half-hourly series of the Primaries, the farms, and the bulk supply point (BSP) meters and the CAMS files, and it failed to read the switching-log workbook (finding 1). It fitted nothing and used no random draws.

    Architecture

    • Nothing is trained. Every job is a regression, a correlation, or a count on stored series, and no neural network or hidden-state model is fitted.
    • The inputs are the power table through studies.power.scan_power(), the frame and sun elevation of experiment 39, the CAMS files of experiment 412, the site map of experiment 521, and the outputs of experiment 722's job 1, with nothing on or after 2026-07-01. The experiment's own code imports experiment 722's loaders by PYTHONPATH and never copies them.
    • The remainder that leaves a Primary out is the BSP meter minus every other child, plus the generators that job 1 classed as outside every metered Primary. It never subtracts the Primary's own reading.
    • The farm sensor of Primary i is the mean output of the same-sky farms, less every farm inside i's polygon, as a share of capacity. Its index is k^Z = Z / (c_Z × u_clear), where c_Z is the 95th percentile of Z / u_clear over the daylight half-hours of the trailing 91 days, and its fast change removes the day's arc term.
    • Farms carry the labels F1 to F6 only, and any row with a date stays under PRIVATE/.

    Training regime

    • No training, so no seeds beyond the bootstrap seed, which is fixed. Each job runs once, on one core, with its own output file written atomically.
    • Each job's estimate is at most 0.05 core-hours and its cap is 1 core-hour, and the experiment's total estimate is under 0.1.
    • The switching-log workbook is read with engine="openpyxl", and a change of Primary i is dropped when either end lies inside a window of i or within 1 hour of a window's start or end. Every statistic carries the mask, and the same statistic without it is a read-out. When no window can be built, every statistic is labelled "transfers not masked".
    • No farm series is shown, and a page that publishes a posterior that used the farm sensor says that the posterior used farm output.

    Measurement

    • The scored rows are the post-bounce stretches that hold at least 8 usable pairs, on days with exactly one post-bounce stretch, scoring that stretch when it holds at least 8 usable pairs, in the later span (413 days in the plan; the span has 420 calendar days, finding 3).
    • The truth is the signed series of each host minus the injected farm's output, and the statistic is the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of C, pooled over pairs, levels, and hosts.
    • Every interval is the 2.5th to 97.5th percentile of 1,000 bootstrap draws that resample calendar days, with every pair, level, and host taking its stretches from the drawn days.
    • The experiment scores no forecast, so no climatology row, mean absolute error, or leaderboard row applies. It reads no load-shift event, and no standing practice that concerns fitted forecasts applies.
    • The verdicts are those of the decision rule, and the read-outs of the former job W1 can withhold and can never admit.
