    Can one neural network split D8's unsigned reading into the sign of the power, the output of unmetered solar panels in megawatts, and the steps of load transfers, without giving a worse probability of reverse flow than the sign-only neural network of the sign-detector experiment 730?

    We asked whether one neural network can take a day of D8's meter readings and give three answers at each half-hour: the probability that power flows out of D8 towards the grid, the output of the solar panels below D8, and whether a block of customers has been switched to or from another substation. D8 is a primary substation (a "Primary") whose meter reports apparent power, a magnitude with no sign. A gas engine and a solar farm connect below D8, so power sometimes flows in reverse, and the meter shows that export as a positive reading. No meter records the three answers on a real day, so we trained the neural network on synthetic days with a known answer: D8's real readings from before the engine was connected, plus a simulated engine, simulated solar output, and simulated switched blocks of customers (a "load transfer"), folded into a magnitude the way the meter does. We tested on synthetic days that training never saw, and ran checks of physical sense on real days.

    The neural network also read the meters of D8's three neighbouring Primaries (D3, D5, and D14) and the meter of the bulk supply point (BSP) above D8, because a load transfer shows as a mirror image at a neighbour. When customers move from a neighbouring Primary to D8, the demand at D8 steps up and the demand at the neighbour steps down by the same amount, and the simulated transfers change the neighbours' readings in the same way.

    The verdict rests on one question: does learning the extra answers make the probability of reverse flow worse? The sign-detector experiment 730 (a sign-only neural network against a neural network that also predicted D8's demand) found that a demand output made that probability slightly worse. This experiment compares the new neural network, called U*, with the best arm of the sign-detector experiment 730, the sign-only neural network S, on the same 94,080 test half-hours. The page gives the result, the disclosures, the synthetic world and the arms, the gallery of drawings that checks the simulators, the method, and the files.

    In short, the verdict is supported: the neural network with the extra tasks (U*) gives a probability of reverse flow no worse than the sign-only neural network (S) by more than a set margin. The extra tasks do not improve that probability, and the answers to the four questions are as follows.

    • (a) Do the extra tasks improve the sign? No. A sign-only neural network trained on the same windows as U* (S+) is slightly better than U*.
    • (b) Can it separate solar output from the reading? Partly, on synthetic days only. The neural network's solar output is closer to the truth than every rule we built (mean error 3.31 points of capacity against 6.93 for the best rule), but it misses 2 of 8 pre-registered pass thresholds and still outputs solar on real days before the solar farm existed.
    • (c) Can it detect switching events? Barely. It finds 7.7% of the simulated steps of 5 points or more, although its step probability beats a simple rule in Brier score.
    • (d) Does the bulk supply point input help the direction of flow? No detectable difference.

    The four questions in plain terms

    (a) The extra training tasks did not improve the probability of reverse flow, and the sign-only neural network does that one job at least as well. The measure is the Brier score of the probability of reverse flow (the mean squared difference between the probability and what happened, 0 or 1; lower is better) over the 94,080 test half-hours. U* scored 0.00787, and the sign-only neural network S of the sign-detector experiment 730 scored 0.00853. The difference (C1, the first of seven pre-registered contrasts, C1 to C7) is -0.00066, with an interval of -0.00223 to +0.00071, so there is no detectable difference.

    The sign-only neural network trained on the same windows is the fair comparison, and it scores better than U*. That arm is S+, trained on the same windows and inputs as U*. S+ scored 0.00721, and U* minus S+ (C6) is +0.00065 (+0.00004 to +0.00137), so the extra tasks made the probability slightly worse in the plan's bootstrap, by an amount whose interval just excludes zero. That interval holds the fitted neural networks fixed, and the reading depends on the bootstrap scheme: a two-way bootstrap of the independent audit gives C6 an interval of -0.00012 to +0.00156, a bootstrap over the 8 test months gives +0.00030 to +0.00106, and over the three seeds the nine seed-pair values run from -0.00019 to +0.00134 (t = 1.88, p = 0.14). The apparent gain over S is shared by S+, so it does not come from the extra tasks: S+ minus S (R4) is -0.00131 (-0.00299 to +0.00002), and R4 is strong over seeds (p = 0.001). S+ differs from S in fit code and seed as well as in training windows and inputs, so the page cannot say which of these produced the gain.

    (b) The neural network separates solar output from the reading on synthetic days to a useful degree, but it fails the pre-registered rule for the word "reasonable". A point is 1% of D8's capacity of 10.379 megavolt-amperes (MVA). Over all sun-up half-hours of the 94,080 test half-hours, its mean absolute error is 3.31 points, against 6.93 for the best rule built from the demand output of the sign-detector experiment 730 (the rule B-PV2, in contrast C4: -3.62, interval -5.58 to -2.14) and 16.80 for a rule that reads the sky alone. (C4 and the error of 4.89 points below read only the sun-up half-hours of worlds that hold solar output, so they sit on a different set of rows from the 3.31.)

    Six of the eight pre-registered solar bars pass, and two fail. A bar is a pass threshold fixed before any fit; the section "The solar output rises with an added solar output on synthetic days" lists all eight. The two that fail are the recovery of a 5-point injection (0.83 of the injected amount, against a bar of 0.9 to 1.1) and the output at night (a mean of 0.065 points against 0.05, and 1.02% of night half-hours above 0.5 points against 0.5%). The night share is twice its bar, and the 5-point recovery is 0.066 below the lower end of its bar. The solar word is therefore "not shown", because the rule withholds "partly" and "reasonable" when a recovery bar fails. On real days the sky check passes and the night check passes, but the two checks that ask for no solar output where no solar farm exists, and for no solar output that follows the engine, fail.

    (c) The neural network barely detects switching events, even on synthetic days with a known step at every transfer. At a threshold of 0.5 the neural network flagged 70 half-hours, 66% of them right, and found 7.7% of the 594 simulated transfer steps of 5 points or more (F1 0.139, against a bar of 0.6; F1 combines precision and recall). The neural network beats the logistic rule that reads the neighbours (B-TN) in Brier score (C5: -0.00059, interval -0.00091 to -0.00032), so the transfer word is "works only in Brier score". Steps of 8 noise units and more (about 27 points of capacity and above) are found 22% of the time, and steps under 4 noise units essentially never. The neighbours' readings made no detectable difference overall (C7: -0.00007, interval -0.00024 to +0.00009). The only one of the splits we scored with a gain is a transfer whose mirror at a neighbour the neural network reads exactly (R16: -0.00063, interval -0.00129 to -0.00015).

    (d) Adding the steps of the bulk supply point (BSP) meter did not help the direction of flow. The BSP is the point at which D8's grid connection joins the higher-voltage network. The Brier score of U* minus that of U-noBSP (the same neural network without the BSP input), on the two test months that the BSP meter covers (23,808 half-hours, 462 of them reverse flow), is -0.000003 (-0.00162 to +0.00210), so the BSP word is "no detectable difference". The interval is wide, so the result does not rule out a small effect. The BSP input did detectably worsen the step detection in two partner cases (R22).

    Against the sign-detector experiment 730, the harm that experiment found is smaller here and does not reach the verdict. In the sign-detector experiment 730 the arm with a demand output scored 0.00105 worse than S (interval -0.00011 to +0.00222) and failed the false-alarm check H1. In this experiment the extra tasks score 0.00065 worse than the same-window sign-only neural network S+ (C6, with the caveat above), a smaller difference in the same direction, and U* passes H1 pooled and at 3 MVA and above. In the band of 1 to 3 MVA, the mean probability of reverse flow on import half-hours is 0.0894 for U*, 0.0828 for S, and 0.0717 for S+, against a limit of 0.083. The verdict table labels this band "at the calibrated boundary", because the lower end of the harm reading HB (U* minus S in the mean probability of reverse flow on import half-hours of 1 to 3 MVA: +0.00657, interval -0.01052 to +0.02846) is not above zero. The verdict of the sign-detector experiment 730 was row 3 (not supported); the verdict here is row 4.

    The verdict is supported: the extra tasks leave the probability of reverse flow no worse than before

    C1's upper end (+0.00071) is below the margin m of 0.00122, so U* is non-inferior to S (verdict row 4). The margin is the margin of the sign-detector experiment 730: the larger of 0.02 times the share of reverse-flow half-hours (0.0364) and twice the standard deviation over seeds of S's validation Brier score. The verdict word "supported" means non-inferiority, and says nothing about an improvement. C1's interval includes zero, so the two neural networks cannot be told apart. U* passes the harm checks H1 pooled (a mean probability of 0.00473 on import half-hours of at least 1 MVA, against a limit of 0.0071) and at 3 MVA and above (0.00150 against 0.0042), and passes H2 on real held-out days before the engine was connected (a mean of 0.00012 and no half-hour above 0.5).

    The plan predicted row 5 (inconclusive) as the likeliest verdict and row 4 as the next; the result is row 4. The plan predicted C1 between -0.0003 and +0.0008, and C1 is -0.00066, a little better than the predicted range. It predicted that C6 and R4 would each have an interval that includes zero. R4's interval includes zero, only just (upper end +0.00002). C6's does not (lower end +0.00004), so that part of the prediction failed narrowly. The plan's other predictions fared as follows:

    • The solar word was predicted "reasonable"; it is "not shown". The recovery lies at 0.93 and 0.96 of the injected amount at 20 and 50 points, inside the predicted 0.93 to 1.03, and at 0.83 at 5 points, outside it. The recovery on reverse-flow half-hours is 0.997, inside the predicted 0.8 to 1.1, so the likeliest miss named by the plan did not occur.
    • The absolute-error, sky-alone, and demand-output bars passed as predicted. U*'s error on sun-up half-hours of solar worlds is 4.89 points against a bar of 15.5, it beats the sky-only rule B-PV0 (R23: -14.09), and C4 is a gain of 3.62 points against the predicted 2.
    • The transfer word was predicted "works"; it is "works only in Brier score". C5 is a gain, as predicted, but the F1 is 0.139 against a predicted 0.6 or more.
    • C7 was predicted to be a gain; it is not detectable. The plan also predicted that steps under about 4 noise units are undetectable, which holds, and that steps of 4 noise units and more are detectable, which does not hold: no size band reaches both a recall and a precision of 0.5.
    • The neighbours' effect on the sign on plain rows (R13) was predicted to be undetectable, and it is: -0.00088 (-0.00211 to +0.00012). Recall on solar-shaped transfers is 0.035 above recall on constant transfers, inside the predicted 0.1.
    • The BSP word was predicted "no detectable difference", and it is.

    The stop-gradient arm U-SG does not show that the extra heads hurt the sign.     Checks

    • The median forecast minus the mean forecast does not apply, because the neural networks output a probability of reverse flow and a solar output, not a quantile forecast.
    • The error of the median forecast beside the quantile-score contrasts: the solar output is a point output, and its mean absolute error (the normalised error of a median in percent of capacity) is the metric of C2 to C4, printed beside its root mean squared error in the solar table. The sign has no median, so no normalised error of the median is reported for it, and the plan's request for a normalised error of the median is not met.
    • Audit triggers 2 and 5 fired, and an independent audit has run. Trigger 5 fired because S+, a nested arm with fewer outputs, scores better than U* by a margin whose interval excludes zero (C6). Trigger 2 fired because C6 and the solar word differ from the prediction. The independent audit of the stored predictions reproduces the contrasts and finds that C6 is not robust to the bootstrap scheme, which is why the page does not claim that the extra heads hurt the sign. Trigger 3 did not fire: the Brier and log-loss points agree for C1, C6, and R4. Trigger 4 does not apply (the test bank is synthetic). Trigger 6 does not fire: C1's half-width is 0.00147, which is (0.00071 + 0.00223) / 2 and 1.26 times the half-width of 0.00117 of C1 in the sign-detector experiment 730, inside the limits of one half to two times. The independent audit by a fresh Opus agent found no leakage and no changed bar, and reproduced every point estimate.

    What the result means for later experiments

    A sign-only neural network is enough for the probability of reverse flow, and the extra heads did not improve it. C1 is non-inferior under every bootstrap scheme the audit tried, and the extra heads did not improve the sign under any of them. Of the arms fitted here, S+ has the lowest Brier score on all half-hours and on import half-hours, and the lowest H1 (the false-alarm check). U-noBSP has a lower Brier score on reverse-flow half-hours (0.1041 against 0.1102) and a higher detection (0.876 against 0.866). U* is the best arm that also gives a solar output.

    The solar output works on synthetic days, and the contrastive terms are what make it obey the physical rules, but the real-day checks do not yet support it. The next experiment on the solar output should keep the contrastive terms, raise the supervised solar weight (U-NCw's error is lower than U*'s), stop each fit on a score that includes the solar error, and read the real-day checks P2 and P4 as the targets, because the head outputs solar where no solar farm exists.

    The switching detector needs a different design. A transfer detector that finds 8% of simulated steps is not usable. The neighbours help only at an exact mirror, and the BSP input worsens detection, so the next experiment on transfers should drop the BSP input (U-noBSP found twice as many steps), and read the switching logs first, because the transfer generator has no realism gate.

    The synthetic world and the arms

    Terms

    • Signed power X is D8's real power, positive for import and negative for reverse flow.
    • The reading R is what D8's meter reports: R = sqrt(X² + Q²), where Q is the reactive floor, the reactive power that keeps the reading above zero when X crosses zero. Folding is the loss of the sign.
    • A point is 1% of D8's effective capacity of 10.379 MVA, the normaliser of data-processing. One point is 0.104 MW at power factor 1.
    • P(X<0) is the probability that a half-hour's signed power is negative. The solar output is the output of the simulated solar panels below D8 in a synthetic window, in points, and the solar head is the output of the neural network that estimates it.
    • A load transfer moves a block of customers between D8 and another Primary for a span of time, so D8's demand steps by a roughly constant amount at the start and steps back at the end. A transfer step is the half-hour that holds the start or the end.
    • The bulk supply point (BSP) is the grid connection point above a group of Primaries. The BSP above D8 is BSP A, which feeds D8 and eight other Primaries. Its meter reports signed MW, and the BSP steps are its half-hour changes. A transfer to a Primary under BSP A leaves the BSP meter unchanged, and a transfer to a Primary under another BSP moves it.
    • D8's electrical neighbours are D3, D5, and D14, listed as interconnected with D8 by experiment 159 (multi-day drops at D8) and experiment 709 (era signals). D3 is metered in MVA and D5 and D14 in signed MW.
    • A noise unit of a series is 1.4826 times the median absolute step statistic of the series over the training base days, where the step statistic is the mean over the next 2 hours minus the mean over the previous 2 hours, after the daily shape is removed. D8's noise unit is 3.42 points.
    • A base day is a UTC day of D8's real reading before the engine was connected. A segment is one calendar month of base days carrying one continuous injection draw. Import rows have X at or above 0, reverse-flow rows have X below 0, and decidable rows have a reading of at least 1 MVA. Sun-up half-hours have a cosine of the solar zenith angle above 0.1, and night half-hours have a cosine of the solar zenith angle at or below 0.

    The loss

    The loss of one training triple (the plain member, the window; its injected member, the window with an added solar output; and its raised-sky member, the window with a higher clear-sky index) is the sum of seven terms. The sign term is binary cross-entropy on X < 0 on the plain member (weight 1.0), the step term is binary cross-entropy on the step event (1.0), and five terms of weight 0.02 each are the Huber losses (scale 1 point) of the solar output and of the step size, the injection term (the rise of the solar output between the two members must equal δ), the night term (mean output at night), and the monotone term (a raised clear-sky index must not lower the output). U-NC drops the last three, and U-NCw drops them and gives the solar term a weight of 0.08. The injection term follows the contrastive term of experiment 624, at a smaller weight, with the same function for both members and a bounded Huber loss.

    The verdict table

    The first row that matches is the verdict. NI is non-inferiority: C1's upper end is at most +m (0.00122).

    Row Condition Verdict
    0 The line stops before C1 is read Stopped, no verdict
    1 A correctness check fails invalid
    2 A fit that a counted reading needs raised and was not rerun failed
    3 H1 pooled or at 3 MVA and above fails, H2 fails, or the band of 1 to 3 MVA is above its limit with the lower bound of HB above zero not supported
    4 NI supported
    5 C1's upper end above +m and its lower end at or below zero inconclusive
    6 Every other case not supported

    Four reading words sit beside the verdict and change no row. The solar word is "reasonable" when all eight solar bars pass, "partly" when the two recovery bars, the absolute bar, and the bar against the sky alone pass and another fails, and "not shown" otherwise. The transfer word is "works" when C5 is a gain and the F1 at 0.5 on steps of 5 points and more is at least 0.6, and "works only in Brier score" when C5 is a gain and the F1 bar fails. The neighbour word reads C7 and the BSP word reads R19.

    Method

    Architecture:

    • U* reads the sign-detector experiment 730's arm S inputs over a 48-hour window from 12:00 UTC on the day before the scored day to 12:00 UTC on the day after, and scores the middle 24 hours: the reading in points divided by 50 and its presence mask; the clear-sky index of the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service (CAMS) (clipped to 0 to 1.5), the clear-sky irradiance divided by 1,000, the cosine of the solar zenith angle, and a CAMS presence mask at D8; the half-hour change of the synthetic BSP meter divided by 20 and its presence mask; and the local half-hour, day of week, bank-holiday flag, and the 2 m temperature of ERA5 (the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts reanalysis), 10 m wind speed, and log precipitation at D8's grid cell. No input reads the month or the day of the year.
    • U* also reads 12 neighbour channels: for each of D3, D5, and D14, the scaled folded reading, its half-hour change, that change times the sign of D8's change of reading, and a presence mask.
    • The backbone is the U-net of experiment 729 (a convolutional neural network after Ronneberger et al. (2015)) with 4 levels of 32, 64, 128, and 256 channels, kernels of width 5, group normalisation, GELU activations, and skip connections, with 1.07 million weights. The bidirectional LSTM of U*-L has 0.55 million weights.
    • U* outputs, at each of the middle 24 hours' half-hours, a sign logit, a non-negative solar output in points (a softplus), a transfer-step logit, and a step size in points, each a linear layer on the backbone's last hidden layer.
    • The weather is the observed ERA5 and CAMS weather at the scored times, which is legitimate because this experiment reconstructs the past and makes no forecast. Any later use of these neural networks inside a forecast must read the weather issued at or before the forecast origin.
    • U-SG, U-NC, U-NCw, S+, U-N, U-noBSP, and U*-L differ from U* as the arms table says.

    Training regime:

    • The training windows come from the sign-detector experiment 730's training banks (synthetic D8 days built from D8's pre-engine base days, with a simulated engine and solar output from two sources, in the manner of Kelly and Knottenbelt (2015) and randomised in the spirit of Tobin et al. (2017)), with this experiment's load-transfer overlay drawn once per seed and an extra solar injection drawn for every window and epoch (log-uniform from 2 to 60 points).
    • The load transfers are synthetic: 0 to 6 per 30 days, 1 hour to 4 days long, 3 to 40 points in size, constant (60%), demand-shaped (20%), or solar-shaped (20%), and mirrored at D3, D5, or D14 in 60% of draws, with the mirror's size jittered by a factor of 0.7 to 1.3 in 30% of the mirrored draws. A transfer that gives load to D8 is mirrored by the partner's equal and opposite change.
    • The neighbours' readings are the real cleaned readings of D3, D5, and D14 on each base day, with D5 and D14 folded to a magnitude.
    • The loss is the sum of seven terms with the weights above, and the training uses AdamW with a learning rate of 1e-3 (halved after 10 epochs with no gain), weight decay of 1e-4, batch size 256, gradient clipping at 1.0, epochs of 8,000 plain windows, and early stopping after 25 epochs without a gain in the validation Brier score of P(X<0), capped at 300 epochs. Seeds 0 to 2 set the initial weights and the training bank. There is no refit schedule and no forecast.
    • The fits ran on one GPU process of at most 6 GB (peak 2.30 GB, U*-L) and 1 CPU thread each, 2.41 GPU-process-hours in total.

    Measurement:

    • The sign and the solar output are scored on the sign-detector experiment 730's 94,080 test half-hours (8 held-out calendar months of D8's pre-engine base days, 8 synthetic segment draws each, the middle 24 hours of a 48-hour window; the months are listed by rule in the known-answer experiment 729's private folder). The injection recovery and the monotonicity are scored on fixed test overlays of the same windows (injections of 5, 20, and 50 points, and a raised sky of 20%), the transfer steps on the transfer overlay, and the BSP contrast on the two test months that the BSP meter covers.
    • The baselines are the sign-detector experiment 730's stored S for the sign, the rules B-PV0, B-PV1, and B-PV2 for the solar output, and the logistic regressions B-TN and B-T for the transfer steps. P0 is the reference of the harm checks.
    • Seven contrasts are counted at 99.286%, from the known-answer experiment 729's two-way bootstrap with Kish widening and 20,000 resamples; the report-only readings, the harm reading HB, and the bar reading R23 are read at the same level outside the count.
    • The metrics are the Brier score and log loss of P(X<0), the harm means H1 and H2, the detection mean, the reliability term, the mean absolute error of the solar output in points of capacity, the recovery ratio, the Brier score, precision, recall, and F1 of the step event, and the mean absolute error of the step size.
    • The scored solar rows drop 243 half-hours of unreliable solar targets (see "Disclosures").
    • The standing practices that apply to a reconstruction are followed: cleaned data, saved neural networks, stored predictions as controls. Monthly refits, the cut of the noisy start of the short-history Primaries D15 and D18, the tail coverage, and the forecast plots do not apply, because no forecast is made.
