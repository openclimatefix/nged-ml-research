    Experiment 398: do the flagged zero actuals change any published demand score?

    Dropping 38 faulty zero readings changes none of the conclusions in four earlier demand- forecasting experiments. Experiments 379, 381, 391, and 395 each scored day-ahead to 10-day-ahead demand forecasts for 14 Primary substations (Primaries) against half-hourly power readings. Those readings still contained 38 half-hours that the cleaned power table flags as exact zeros from a faulty meter. We removed those half-hours from the stored per-row scores and recomputed each experiment's headline figures (experiment 381's weekly bundle was rescored from its stored forecasts). The continuous ranked probability score (CRPS, in percentage points of the Primary's effective capacity) falls by at most 0.0023 points at the 6 Primaries that report signed megawatts (the signed-MW six) and by at most 0.008 points at the 8 Primaries that report only apparent power in megavolt-amperes (MVA; the MVA-only eight). Within each score file, every forecasting variant (arm) falls by nearly the same amount, so the difference between any two arms agrees with the published difference. The verdict is "partly supported" because the hypothesis was that no figure changes at the printed precision, and the MVA-only CRPS values change in the third decimal place.

    • Coverage at q01, q05, q95, and q99 changes by at most 0.0043 (as a fraction of rows) in any group, for every arm of the four files.
    • Other slices (lead bands, winter, Economy 7 step, and the rest) change by at most 0.018 points in CRPS. Two slices change more. The load-shift event rows at the MVA-only eight fall from 5.197 to 4.952 (-0.245) for BG2, because 70 of 16,478 rows are dropped. The error of the median for the reference forecasts in experiment 391's sunny daylight slice falls by up to 0.080. The cold-night slice does not change at all.
    • The short-history group (D15 and D18) is report-only. In experiment 379's component file it falls by up to 0.185 points because that file holds 239 flagged keys at those two Primaries (D15 120, D18 119); no published headline uses it.

    Contrasts and rankings

    • No contrast interval changes sign or crosses zero. We recomputed 86 paired contrasts (5 had no rows, so 81 are compared) (99% interval, 5,000 resamples, month-resampled): experiment 379's 14 arms against BG2, experiment 391's five arms against the control (all rows, lag inside an event, lag outside), and experiment 395's seven monthly arms against the control (CRPS and error of the median), each at the signed-MW six and MVA-only eight. The largest change in a point estimate is 0.0008 points, and in an interval bound 0.0017 points.
    • The weekly bundle of experiment 381 was rescored from its stored forecasts. Bundle minus the GAM stack at the signed-MW six is +0.0749 old and +0.0749 new, at the MVA-only eight with D8 -0.3609 and -0.3612, and without D8 -0.1967 and -0.1966. The bundle's differences from the weekly baseline, BG2, the manual heuristic, and climatology each move by at most 0.0011 points. The largest single-Primary change for the bundle is -0.035 points at D21.
    • No arm ranking changes within experiments 379, 391, and 395 at the headline slice, because every arm of one file loses the same few rows and falls by nearly the same amount.

    Checks

    • Experiment 379's unit check cannot see the gap. The unit check compares the stored actual of experiment 87 with the cache actual on rows where both are finite, and a flagged actual is a finite 0.0 in both sources, so the maximum difference is zero. The rescore finds the gap because it joins to the production table's drop_reason, not to another copy of the same cache.
    • The median forecast minus the mean forecast and the error of the mean forecast are not recomputed: the experiment changes the scored rows, not the forecasts, and the stored losses already carry the quantile score.
    • Triggers fired: none. No control failed and no nested arm beats the arm that contains it. One prediction, P1 (every headline moves by less than 0.005), was missed: the MVA-only eight moved by up to 0.008. P2, P3 (D21 largest), and P4 held.
    • Compute used: the rescore reads and filters stored per-row scores and recomputes contrasts; no scorer was rerun except experiment 381's own weekly bundle.

    Caveats and what to do next

    • The 38 half-hours are not the whole cache. Experiment 388's scorer, which covers the full scored year for every Primary including D15 and D18, finds 622 unique flagged half-hours, 389 of them at D18 and 196 at D15. Experiments built on that scored set, and experiments 392, 395's Part F, and 396, report their own check columns and are not repeated here.
    • Interval resamples: the 99% intervals use 5,000 resamples, not the 20,000 of the headline protocol, to keep the job inside its time limit. All widths agree with the published figures to the printed precision.
    • A fix in src/studies/dp_features.py or experiment 103's builder (dropping flagged half-hours when the cache is built) would stop new experiments scoring against faulty zeros. We did not make it, because that fix changes the shared cache that open experiments read. The maintainer decides.
