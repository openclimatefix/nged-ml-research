    Which lagged-power columns improve the simple weekly XGBoost demand model at the 16 Primaries?

    Take-home message. None of the five column sets lowered the CRPS of the simple XGBoost demand model. Six weekly analogues made the forecast worse at 12 of the 16 Primaries, and the other column sets either changed nothing or were worse. The verdict is not supported.

    We asked whether more columns of past power help an XGBoost model forecast Primary substation demand 1 to 10 days ahead. We refitted the model with each of five column sets on the 1st of each month, scored the forecasts on a year of held-out data, and subtracted the scores of the current model (experiment 381's stored monthly control). A positive difference means the extra columns made the forecast worse. The pre-registered prediction for the weekly analogues (a change between -0.10 and +0.05 points) failed for arm A.

    Results

    • Arms and recipe. The baseline is the three origin lags (the newest usable reading, the reading at the latest earlier time of the same half-hour of day, and the reading at the latest earlier time of the same weekday and half-hour). A is six weekly analogues (five new columns: the one-week analogue equals the baseline's week lag up to about 6.9 days of lead). B is their mean and median. C is two anomaly columns. E is the baseline without the newest reading. F is E with the day lag replaced by the power exactly 24 hours before the valid time. All arms use experiment 87's n1h recipe with monthly refits (192 fits each), column subsampling fixed at 1.0. Arm D was not fitted, because B and C did not each help alone.
    • Row set and scoring. fold_a_daily_gap_v1, protocol demand-v1, 16 Primaries, valid times from 2025-07-01 to 2026-06-30, CRPS in points of effective capacity, headline band days 1 to 10. The scorer reads actuals from the shared, unfiltered cache of experiment 103. The check column (below) drops the flagged half-hours.
    • Baseline. Experiment 381's stored monthly control. Its absolute CRPS at days 1 to 10 is in work/score_monthly_final.log; the highest of the 14 scored Primaries is 7.65 at D8 and the lowest 1.75 at D3, and D15 (report only) is 15.60. Baseline minus reference, positive when the baseline is worse (signed-MW six; MVA-only eight): BG2 +0.096 (+0.040 to +0.169) and +0.042 (-0.011 to +0.120); manual_heuristic -0.494 (-0.677 to -0.335) and -0.588 (-0.797 to -0.381); climatology -0.720 (-0.965 to -0.509) and -1.029 (-1.348 to -0.727).

    Sweep results

    A prediction-only sweep adds a synthetic step of plus or minus 2%, 5%, and 10% of capacity to the lagged-power inputs, then forecasts eight runs, at four Primaries (D8, D4, D10, and D5), for the baseline and arms A, B, and C, to day 10. The runs are served by the refit of 2026-06-23, the latest weekly refit of the scored year, a rule fixed before any score was read. The sweep uses a separate fit of that date, with the trained XGBoost models saved.

    • The median follows only a fraction of the step. For a step of 5% of capacity, the baseline's median shifts by 0.04 of the step when only the newest reading is shifted, 0.20 when the two newest are, and 0.39 when every lag is shifted. Arm C follows more (0.12, 0.28, 0.47). When every lag is shifted, arm A follows 0.44 of the step, arm B 0.39, and the baseline 0.39.
    • No arm widens its band much. The width of the 1% to 99% band stays within 3% of its unshifted width in every arm and lead band up to a step of 5% (arm C reaches 1.029), and within 5% at a step of 10% (maximum 1.047, arm C).
    • The step level usually lies inside the bands. At a step of 5% the level lies inside the 1% to 99% band in 93% to 100% of rows, and q95 (q05 for a negative step) reaches it in 70% to 100% of rows. At a step of 2% the bands cover it in 96% to 100% of rows.
    • Shifting every lag by -5% worsens the CRPS. Against the observed power, the CRPS rises by up to about 1.0 point (0.91 for A, 0.96 for B, 0.97 for C). The full tables are in work/sweep_run.log.

    Checks

    • Pre-registered predictions. P1 (arms A and B move the score by -0.10 to +0.05) failed for A (+0.137) and held for B (+0.049, at the edge). P2 (largest gain at 72 to 168 h, near zero at 0 to 24 h) failed: A is worst at 0 to 24 hours (+0.182). P3 (arm C within 0.05) held (+0.008). P4 (no placebo moves unless D8 does) failed: D2 (+0.077) and D4 (+0.146) moved while D8 did not.
    • Median minus mean forecast, per site (points of capacity, baseline). This gap shows whether an arm shifts the whole forecast distribution while leaving its median alone. The gap is negative at 15 of 16 Primaries, between -3.04 (D15) and -0.14 (D18), with D22 at +0.03, so the forecasts are right-skewed. No arm moves the gap at any Primary by more than 0.3 points except arm B at D8 (baseline -0.58, B -0.99) and arm B at D15 (baseline -3.04, B -2.66); arm A is just inside the limit at D8 (-0.87). The full table is in work/checks.log.
    • Error of the median beside each contrast. The mean absolute error of the median is worse than the baseline's for arm A at 14 of 16 Primaries (better only at D8 and D15) and for arm F at 14 of 16 (better only at D16 and D18), the same direction as the CRPS.
    • Flagged half-hours (check column). The shared cache holds 622 scored-year half-hours that cleaned power lacks (all exact zeros: D18 389, D15 196, D21 13, D8 9, D22 5, D9 5, D12 4, D16 1). The headline uses the shared unfiltered cache; the check column drops those half-hours. The contrasts for arms A, B, C, E, and F are unchanged to within 0.001 (for example A: +0.137 at the signed-MW six, +0.047 at the MVA-only eight).
    • Reproduction gate (failed at one Primary). The weekly baseline refitted at 4 of 52 refits differs from experiment 87's stored weekly member by at most 0.0045 CRPS points at 15 Primaries and by 0.0195 at D22, against a gate of 0.01. No monthly baseline was refitted through the 388 script, so the monthly baseline is experiment 381's control (same recipe, same code path through experiment 87's functions). The 0.0195 gap at D22 suggests a noise floor of about 0.02 per Primary. That floor is small against arm A's pooled +0.137 at the signed-MW six, and large against its per-Primary effects at the MVA-only eight.
    • Audit trigger. The skill's second audit trigger fired: a result contradicts its own prediction and an earlier experiment. A fresh Sonnet audit found no bug and reproduced D10 (+0.1038 against +0.1031) and D4 (+0.1446 against +0.1455). We accepted and stated all four of its findings: the gate caveat above; analogue_1w duplicates the week lag; arm A scores lower at D15 and, with an interval that includes zero, at D8; and the mechanism of the loss is untested (one candidate, memorising the valid time from six weekly values, is a hypothesis).
    • Late steps. The fresh Sonnet code review ran after the fits had started, not before the first run. The review found one bug in the scorer's stored-baseline loader (duplicated July origins, which affected only the reference row), fixed before any score was read.

    Caveats and next steps

    • Monthly refits. The scored headline uses monthly refits. The weekly check is descriptive and covers arm A only.
    • Post hoc slices. The event-row and cold-night results carry no interval.
    • Sweep scope. The sweep covers four Primaries at one refit.
    • Mechanism. The cause of arm A's loss is untested.
    • One seed. Each arm has one fit per refit, and the gate gap suggests a noise floor of about 0.02 per Primary.
    • Not tested. Any arm that needs a second model or a blend; the 4-week anomaly window; a longer analogue window.
    • Possible next tests. Dropping the newest reading on event rows only (experiment 383's row set), and a hedge in the output (a wider band on rows whose lags lie in an event) rather than in more inputs.

    Pre-registered arms and design

    All arms refit weekly with experiment 87's n1h recipe (half-life 0.5 years, 500 rounds, eta 0.05, whole history), one seed. colsample_bytree and colsample_bynode stay at 1.0, because column subsampling would hand a wider arm a free win. A column is usable only when its meter reading is at least 3.5 hours older than the forecast origin; otherwise it is null.

    • Baseline. The three origin lags as e00087_common.origin_lags defines them: the newest usable reading, the reading at the latest earlier time with the valid time's half-hour of day, and the reading at the latest earlier time with the valid time's weekday and half-hour. The reproduction gate (experiment 383's: mean CRPS within 0.01 points at every Primary) refits the baseline at 4 of the 52 weekly refits and compares it with experiment 87's stored weekly n1h member. The gate failed at one Primary: D22 differed by 0.0195 points, and the other 15 Primaries differed by at most 0.0045 (the GPU fit is not bit-reproducible, and the largest median gap sat at the refit of 2025-12-30). Because a stored baseline would carry that noise into every contrast, all contrasts use a baseline refitted at all 52 refits with the same code and settings as the arms. The stored n1h member remains a reference row, with the gate's gaps reported.
    • A, weekly analogues. Baseline plus power at the valid time minus 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6 weeks (6 columns); the 1-week analogue is null beyond about 6.9 days of lead.
    • B, analogue summaries. Baseline plus the mean and median of whichever of those six are usable at the lead (2 columns; this is experiment 87's a7 pair).
    • C, lag differences. Baseline plus two columns. The origin anomaly is the newest usable reading minus the median reading at the same half-hour and weekday over the previous 4 weeks. The day-ago anomaly is the reading at the valid time minus 1 day minus the median reading at that half-hour and weekday over the previous 4 weeks, null when the one-day-earlier reading is not usable. The 4-week window is a choice, not a tested value.
    • E, drop the newest reading (maintainer, 2026-10-30). The baseline without lag_origin, which is one noisy value that is the same for every valid time of a forecast origin. It keeps the same-time latest-day and latest-week lags. Prediction that can fail: E beats the baseline beyond 24 hours and does not lose from 0 to 24 hours, so E is read by lead band before any pooled number.
    • F, strict 24-hour lag (maintainer, 2026-10-30). E with the day lag replaced by the power exactly 24 hours before the valid time, null where that reading is not yet usable (leads beyond about 20.5 hours). F is also read against E, which shows what the older latest-day values add at long leads.
    • D, B plus C. Fitted only if C2 and C3 each help alone (see the decision rule).

    Change of cadence to monthly refits (decided before any arm was scored)

    The maintainer asked for monthly refits in most research. The headline contrasts are therefore monthly: arms a, b, and c refitted on the 1st of each month from 2025-07-01 to 2026-06-01 (192 fits per arm), against experiment 381's stored monthly control, which has experiment 87's n1h recipe with no changes (the same columns, 500 rounds, eta 0.05, half-life 0.5 years, column subsampling 1.0). The weekly baseline and weekly arm a (832 fits each) were finished first and give a descriptive cadence check beside the monthly contrast. Weekly arm b is partial (384 of 832 fits) and weekly arm c was not fitted, so neither is scored. Arm d is fitted only if b and c each help alone at monthly refits. The decision rule above applies to the monthly contrasts.

    Sweep pre-registration (rule fixed before any score was read)

    After the main fits, the baseline and arms a, b, and c are refitted once more at the latest weekly refit of the scored year (2026-06-23), with the boosters saved, for a four-Primary screen to save compute (D8 the target, D4 a placebo, D10 and D5 signed-MW; 16 fits in all). A prediction-only sweep then adds a synthetic step of depth D (plus and minus 2%, 5%, and 10% of capacity) to the lagged-power inputs: first the most recent lag only (k = 1), then the two most recent (k = 2), then all of them, including the weekly-analogue columns that lie inside the step. Per arm and lead band it reports the shift of the median as a share of D, the band-width ratio against k = 0, and whether the quantiles span both levels. It also reports, per arm: the share of event-level readings inside the 1-99% and 5-95% bands, whether q95 and q99 reach the event level at each k, and the price of spanning (band widening, and CRPS outside events). NGED would accept a forecast whose median is the normal running arrangement while its tails always span the normal and the abnormal levels. The sweep uses at most 3 threads.
