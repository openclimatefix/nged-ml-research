    How does the candidate normal-running-arrangement forecast behave, plot by plot, on verified, labelled, and injected load-shifts and on windows with no load-shift?

    The plots show the candidate forecast (C2) covering the normal running arrangement (NRA) on more rows than the earlier v3+D5 forecast (C0) in the 7 days after a load-shift ends, and changing nothing on days with no load-shift. The NRA is the power a substation would have drawn without the load-shift. C2 does worse than C0 while the load-shift is under way and, at one real substation, before it starts.

    Verdict: partly supported. The pre-registered rule said the prediction fails if P1 or P2 fails, and P2 failed at one metering group, so the prediction as a whole failed. Outcomes:

    • P1 held. C2 equals C1 on all 4 event-free windows.
    • P2 held at the six MW substations (11 of 12 designs) and failed at the eight MVA-only substations (6 of 12). The run drew one substation per design from each group, not the same two substations for every design.
    • P3 did not hold. On the December pair, C2's band is narrower than C0's on the gate-on days before and during the event, and wider only after the end (verified table).

    Nothing here is adopted for production.

    How to read the plots. A load-shift moves part of one primary substation's demand onto another, so recent power readings no longer show the NRA. A primary substation (a Primary) is named D2, D3, and so on. MW (megawatts) is real power. MVA (megavolt-amperes) is apparent power, and the eight "MVA-only" Primaries report only apparent power; the six "signed-MW" Primaries report signed real power. Each figure shows day-ahead forecasts (issued the day before the valid day) for one Primary, with C0 in the top panel and C2 in the bottom panel, and a key under both. Both panels draw the forecast the same way: the orange band is the q05 to q95 range (the 90% band) and the dark orange line is the median. The black line is the observed power. The pale lilac shade is the load-shift. The sky-blue bar under the C2 panel marks origin days (days on which the forecast was issued) when the gate was on, or held for 7 days after it released. The gate is a rule that fires when the lagged power disagrees with a forecast made without it. No figure draws the NRA, because it is only an estimate (observed power minus the fitted load-shift); every coverage number on this page is scored against that estimate. Where a fold month is mentioned, it is one of the five test months.

    The arms. C0 is the forecast of experiment 572. C1 is the P5 recipe of experiment 584: the same network and training as C2, trained with synthetic load-shifts in its lag inputs, with no extra band. C2 is C1 with a wider band (the union of C1's band and the band of its lags-masked twin) while the gate is on and for 7 days after. The union band is that wider band.

    What the plots show

    • Event-free days are unchanged. C2's quantiles equal C1's on all 4 windows (event-free figure).
    • After the end, C2 beats C0 on both verified Primaries. D16 0.85 against 0.33 and D5 0.57 against 0.34 (verified table; descriptive, one transfer).
    • Before and during the event, C2 covers the NRA less often than C0. At D5 before the event 0.46 against 0.80, with the gate on for the whole window. At D16 from day 4 to the end 0.68 against 0.90 (verified table). experiment 584 reports the same.
    • On injected events C2 covers more than C0 after the end in 17 of 24 examples (12 of 15 distinct cases). The exceptions are negative-sign events at D8 and D12, and one near-tie at D11 (0.991 against 0.988; injected table).
    • For labelled events that the power readings did not confirm, the band does not follow the label's dates and sign. The gate was on for 18 origin days of event 147, 6 of event 149, and 42 of event 146, and for none of the others except event 55 (labelled figures).

    Checks and caveats

    • Row set: experiment_470_intersection_v1, five fold months (2025-10, 2025-12, 2026-01, 2026-03, 2026-06), July 2026 unused, D15 and D18 cut before 2025-03-01, cleaned data, seeds 0 and 1 averaged, coarse refits (days 0 and 14). Weather comes from the run issued at or before the forecast origin minus 9 h, and lags are at least 24 h old. Stored forecasts cover 0 to 120 h, so NGED's 1 to 10 day horizon is not shown.
    • Descriptive only: one verified pair, 11 labelled events, and 12 injected designs, each at one MVA-only and one MW Primary. No contrast is tested. Where experiment 584 quotes a contrast, its minimum detectable effect is on that page.
    • Unlogged events: NGED's logs may miss events, so the event-free windows may hold unlabelled events, and the real-event slices undercount.
    • Injected events make any rule keyed on the lags look better than it would on real events, because the gate reads the injected lags and not a change in the power (experiment 583).
    • Not computed: the avoidable-curtailment cost (the cost of curtailing a generator more than a perfect forecast would require; it needs node forecasts with generators), tail tables beyond those of experiment 584, and weekly refits.
    • Injected rows are cut by the March origin mask: rows before the start and late after the end are missing, so injected coverage here is not comparable with the 24 to 120 h bank tables of experiment 584.
    • Reviews: a fresh Sonnet agent reviewed the script before the numbers were quoted and found no leak and no generator data. A second reviewed the write-up against the tables.

    Scripts: ml-experiments/00588-nra-event-gallery-for-the-candidate-forecast/e00588_gallery.py, which wraps the code of experiments 582, 583, and 584 without editing it. Tables are in data/ml-experiments/00588-nra-event-gallery-for-the-candidate-forecast/ (private).
