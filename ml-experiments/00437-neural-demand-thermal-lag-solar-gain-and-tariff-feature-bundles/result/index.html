    Do simple input bundles that did not help XGBoost (thermal lag, solar gain, look-ahead, and calendar, tariff, and national signals) lower the error of the pooled mixture density network?

    This experiment tests, on four folds, whether windows of weather and a few calendar and national signals lower the error of the pooled neural network that experiments 397 to 425 built. The network predicts 11 quantiles of Primary substation demand from the ECMWF ensemble mean and spread at one valid time. Experiment 419 gave it a centred daily mean of shortwave and found -0.012 (-0.043 to +0.013), one sixth of its minimum detectable effect. Trailing and forward windows of temperature and shortwave were never given to the neural network, although the maintainer asked for them on 2026-10-31.

    Design

    • Reference. R and A of experiment 425: the pooled plain network with the 8-wide Primary embedding, 3 Gaussian components, setting g09, and the ensemble mean and spread inputs, trained on ensemble rows only (R) or pretrained on ERA5 and CAMS rows and fine-tuned at a learning rate of 2e-4 (A). Same rows, the D15 and D18 start cut at 2025-03-01, the flagged exact-zero drop, held-out weeks, row weights, 1.15 widening, and monthly refits. The stored seeds 0 to 2 of both arms on the four screen folds are the controls. A check (e00437_fit.py check) proves that a fold built here without extra columns equals experiment 425's fold, bit for bit.
    • Arms. Each arm appends columns to the 46 inputs and changes nothing else. Bundles:
      • T, thermal lag (8 columns). Trailing means of the ensemble-mean temperature over 3, 6, and 24 hours; the temperature change over 3 and 6 hours; the minimum and maximum over the local day of the valid time; and the days since the trailing 24-hour mean last exceeded 5 degrees C, capped at 10.
      • S, solar gain (6 columns). Trailing means of the ensemble-mean shortwave over 3, 6, and 24 hours; the 3-hour change in the clear-sky index (shortwave over Haurwitz clear-sky shortwave, held at its last daylight value overnight; the helper of experiment 396 arm W7); and the realised CAMS irradiance of the last complete UTC day before the origin (its mean in W/m2 and its ratio to CAMS clear-sky). CAMS publishes the previous day at midnight UTC, so production can read both.
      • F, look-ahead (6 columns). Forward means of ensemble-mean temperature and shortwave over the 6 and 24 hours after the valid time, taken from the same run only, and the share of each window the run covers (windows are truncated at the end of the 14-day horizon).
      • C, calendar, tariff, and national signals (14 columns). The Economy 7 step as a UTC half-hour indicator and that indicator times degrees below 15.5 C; five Lincolnshire school-term columns from experiment 11's calendar (term flag; signed days to the nearest term start and end; signed days from the year's first working day; a window flag); and seven signals of the local day before the origin: the mean, maximum, and 16:00 to 19:00 mean of the Elexon market index price, a Demand Flexibility Service flag, and the day's mean national demand, embedded solar, and embedded wind (NESO historic demand). All 16 Primaries lie at 52.9 to 53.3 degrees N, 0.2 degrees W to 0.3 degrees E, inside the box that experiment 11 found to be Lincolnshire, so one county's calendar applies to every Primary.
    • Pretrained form. T, S, and F are also run as T_pre, S_pre, and F_pre: pretrained on experiment 418's ERA5-and-CAMS pseudo-run rows with the same columns computed from the dense weather, then fine-tuned on ensemble rows at 2e-4. C has no pretraining form, because the pretraining rows carry no school or national columns. A pretrained network is fitted for each bundle, fold, and seed, and cannot be shared across folds: the pretraining rows end 3.5 hours before each fold's refit, so a network pretrained for a later fold has seen the earlier fold's test month.
    • Window construction. A trailing window ends at the valid time. For half-hours at or before the forecast origin (09:00 UTC) the series holds the ensemble mean of the previous run that covers them (leads of about 9 to 33 hours); after the origin it holds this run's own ensemble mean. ERA5 is not used, because it arrives about 5 days late and a column built from it could not be served. e00437_features.py check rebuilds sample runs with every later run deleted and requires identical columns. In the pretrained form the windows come from the dense ERA5-and-CAMS series, which is the same stand-in the pretraining rows use for the valid-time weather.
    • Known mismatch in the pretrained form. check-pretraining printed each column's standardised mean and spread in the pretraining rows and the ensemble rows for the 2026-01 fold. The weather windows agree to within 0.25 standard deviations in the mean and 0.07 in the spread, except csi_change_3h, whose spread is 2.6 in the pretraining rows against 0.9 in the ensemble rows. CAMS irradiance changes more over 3 hours than the mean of 51 ensemble members does, the same smoothing gap that affects the shortwave input itself. The write-up reports it as a caveat for S_pre.
    • Dismantling. A bundle that advances is split into halves and refitted in the same form: T_means/T_rest, S_means/S_rest, F_temperature/F_shortwave, and for C C_tariff/C_school/C_national. A combination of the winning bundles (T+S+F, with or without C) is fitted once at least two bundles advance. These are descriptive.
    • Fits and compute. 4 bundles by 4 folds by 3 seeds is 48 ensemble-only fits (about 75 seconds each including the forecast) and 36 pretrained fits (about 55 seconds of pretraining and 40 of fine-tuning each, plus a pretraining-row build of about 2 minutes per fold and bundle). That is about 1.0 plus 1.8 GPU-hours before dismantling, and up to 2 more GPU-hours for the halves and the combination. One GPU process under 8 GB, one feeder core, 8 GB of host memory, one BLAS thread.
    • Standing practices followed. Monthly refits; cleaned data (flagged exact-zero half-hours dropped, counts printed per fold); the D15 and D18 cut at 2025-03-01; every fitted network saved under work/models/; stored controls reused; tails, the avoidable-curtailment cost, and the example forecasts reported; four-fold screen with every number labelled interim.

    Caveats registered before the fits

    • Window seam at the origin. The ensemble-mean summaries hold no half-hour at or before the run's own origin, so half-hours between 00:00 and 09:00 on the origin day come from the previous run (leads of about 9 to 33 hours). Trailing windows therefore mix two runs at 09:00.
    • T is partly a look-ahead bundle. The daily minimum and maximum temperature use the later hours of the valid time's local day, which the run already forecasts. F isolates pure look-ahead.
    • Publication latency of the previous-day signals. The CAMS, Elexon price, and NESO demand columns use the whole local or UTC day before the origin date. The brief states that CAMS arrives at midnight UTC; the latency of the Elexon and NESO files at 09:00 UTC was not measured, and the training values are the final revised ones. The write-up states both as open points for serving.
    • Forward windows in the pretrained form come from ERA5 and CAMS, which do not degrade with lead, while the ensemble-mean forward windows do. The write-up reports this as a caveat for F_pre.
    • Economy 7 is on the UTC clock, as the brief and experiments 121, 137, and 281 define it. The protocol's economy_7_step slice is on local half-hours, so during British Summer Time the two differ by one hour. The write-up reports C on that slice and states the offset.
    • Bundle C has no pretrained form. The brief asks for the weather bundles in both forms; the pretraining rows hold no school or national columns, so C runs ensemble-only.
    • No summer fold. The four folds are autumn, Christmas, winter, and spring; 2026-06 is not included, so a solar-gain effect in summer cannot show.
    • Data searched for bundle C. On disk and used: Lincolnshire County Council term dates (experiment 11, 2021-22 to 2025-26), Elexon market index prices (p3-price-download), NESO historic demand and embedded generation (experiment 185), and Demand Flexibility Service requirements (experiment 177). A school calendar for another county is not needed, because all 16 Primaries lie in Lincolnshire. No download is made.
    • Reported beside CRPS: the 12-to-36-hour band, tail calibration per group and per Primary, the cold winter night slice, the avoidable-curtailment cost, and the dropped half-hours per fold. Load-shift plots and load-shift row sets do not apply. Experiment 396 arm W7 has no result yet; the write-up cites it if it lands.
    • Process, in order. Draft pull request; plan review (fresh Sonnet); code review (fresh Sonnet) before the first fit; fits; results review with the audit triggers; write-up; prose review.

    Results

    No bundle lowered the CRPS by the registered bar, so no dismantling or confirmation run is planned. The best arms lowered the CRPS at only one group, by less than 0.03 points, in 2 of 4 folds. The next step is to leave these bundles out of the pooled plain network and, if the question returns, to test the thermal-lag bundle T on more folds.

    Arm key. R is the ensemble-only pooled plain network, and A is its pretrained form; both are the controls. T adds thermal-lag columns, S solar-gain columns, F look-ahead columns, and C calendar, tariff, and national-signal columns (C_tariff adds only the 2 tariff columns of C). A _pre suffix marks the pretrained form of a bundle, whose control is A. "The six" and "the eight" are the 6 signed-MW Primaries and the 8 MVA-only Primaries.

    Predictions

    • P1 (solar gain S helps most on cold sunny days) is not supported. S was worse than R on cold sunny days (+0.308 at the six) and better on cold overcast days (-0.236), the opposite order.
    • P2 (thermal lag T helps at both groups, most on cold winter nights) is not supported. T was worse at the six (+0.016). The small gain of T at the eight came from two folds, 2025-12 (-0.071) and 2026-01 (-0.025); 7 of 8 Primaries improved.
    • P3 (look-ahead F helps at the eight) is not supported. F changed the CRPS at the eight by -0.000.
    • P4 (calendar, tariff, and national signals C help on the Economy 7 step) is not supported. C was worse on that slice at the six (2.985 against 2.805 for R).
    • P5 (the pretrained form gains at least as much over its control) is not supported. For each bundle, the gain over the control of the pretrained form beat that of the ensemble-only form at one group and lost at the other. The gain of S_pre over A beat the gain of S over R by 0.059 at the six and lost by 0.009 at the eight. For F_pre against F, the figures are 0.016 better at the six and 0.020 worse at the eight. For T_pre against T, they are 0.009 worse at the six and 0.007 better at the eight.

    Checks

    • Fold-by-fold differences, per-Primary CRPS, tails, and the cold-night, sunny, Economy 7, and cold sunny-versus-overcast slices are in the figures below; the slice intervals are not shown because few half-hours (769 and 911 on cold overcast days) make them unreliable.
    • A fresh reviewer re-derived the pooled CRPS of R, A, T, and S_pre from the raw forecast files and matched the scorer to three decimals. The median-error and CRPS contrasts agree in sign in all 16 arm-and-group pairs. Leaving out any one Primary flips no verdict.
    • The new columns are not degenerate: their standardised spread is near 1 and the first layer of the neural network uses them. The ensemble-only fold without extra columns equals experiment 425's fold bit for bit.
    • C_tariff (2 columns) beat C (which contains them) by 0.012 at the six. Both differences from the control are inside the noise.

    Caveats

    • The screen is too coarse to show "no effect". The minimum detectable effect is 0.064 points against R and 0.045 against A, which is 1.5 to 2 times the 0.03 bar, and fold-to-fold differences swing by more than the bar.
    • The four folds hold no summer month, so the second example is spring and S cannot be judged in summer.
    • The hyperparameters were tuned at 46 inputs and reused at 48 to 60. Extra inputs at fixed capacity may explain the worse scores of C and S, and a bundle might help with another setting.
    • The scorer does not compute P1 and P4 as single numbers, and it pairs by fold, not by fold and seed. The slice contrasts above were read by hand.
    • The pretraining mismatch for csi_change_3h registered above applies to S_pre.

    What to try next

    • Test bundle T on more folds, since it is the only bundle that lowered the CRPS at the eight with both controls (R and A).
