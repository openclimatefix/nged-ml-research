    Can the sign of an apparent-power (MVA) Primary's reading, and the split of that reading into demand, solar output, and a generator state, be recovered in hindsight on the CPU with no neural network?

    We asked whether the sign of an apparent-power reading can be recovered after the fact without training a neural network, and the part that ran on real data answers only a smaller question. Eight of the 16 Primaries (primary substations) report apparent power in megavolt-amperes (MVA), a magnitude, so an export of solar output shows as a rise in the reading and not as a negative value. The plan had three parts, all run on the CPU alone. First, count how often the Primaries that report real power in megawatts (MW) go negative, and find which MVA Primaries could export at all. Second, add a real solar farm's recorded output to six Primaries, fold the readings into magnitudes, and check whether a hand-built calculation recovers the sign. Third, fit a hidden Markov model that splits a reading into demand, solar output, and the number of units of a generator running, and score it against answers known by construction. The first part ran and matched its prediction. The second part never ran on real data. The third part ran on its first test and failed it, so the stop rule ended the rest.

    Verdict: inconclusive, as a judgement under the plan's last row, with the generator-state model failing its pre-registered bar. The plan's verdict table gives supported, partly supported, and not supported only through the real-data result of rung 0b (the sign read-out), and that result does not exist, because rung 0b was held and never run on real data. The known-answer checks of rung 0c did not fail, so invalid does not apply. The row that is left is inconclusive. Rung 0e has its own bars. Its first bar (V2, the paired contrast against experiment 694's tracker) failed, and the stop rule then closed the generator branch. Every verdict here assumes power factor 1 and is a hindsight ceiling, because the smoothed posterior reads half-hours after the one it labels.

    What the result means for the forecast: the hindsight model is not a better way to find a generator than the tracker that already exists, and the sign question is still open. The substation forecast needs the sign of a reading only where the Primary can export. The experiment does not change any forecast, and the model is not for production.

    Results

    Rung 0e: the hindsight model lost the pre-registered contrast

    The pre-registered test (V2) failed both of its bars. The test injected the schedules of a generator's units into 44 segments of readings that experiment 694 had stored, and scored "an engine unit is running" by F1. F1 is the harmonic mean of the share of running half-hours found and the share of flagged half-hours that were running. The model and experiment 694's M2 tracker were fitted to the same 396 runs (44 segments and 9 schedules), and the contrast is the model's F1 minus M2's. The bootstrap resamples 35 clusters of segments.

    The model's pooled F1 is 0.696 and M2's is 0.819 on the same segments. Both numbers pool the half-hours of all 396 runs. The interval excludes zero on the negative side, so M2 is better, and the pre-registered prediction that the contrast's lower end would sit above 0 is wrong.

    Across the eight hosts of the un-injected segments, the hindsight model flagged 21.6% to 59.2% of half-hours (31.5% on average) and 9.8 to 20.8 active days a month, and M2 flagged none. A host is one of the eight Primaries that supply the segments with no generator injected. An active day is a day with at least 4 flagged half-hours. The bar is 3% and 1 active day a month, so the model fails at every one of the eight hosts. The per-host values are stored in the private folder and are not shown here.

    The stop rule fired as written. The plan says that a failed paired bar, K1, or false-alarm bar stops the generator branch, so K1 and V3 were not fitted, the section 3 analyses (which refuse to run unless V2 and K1 both pass) did not run, and the hindsight model is not used on a real generator's history. Experiment 724 imports this experiment's hidden Markov code and its V2, K1, and V3 harness. The maintainer has closed the plan of experiment 724 as parked. The stop rule closed only the hand-built hindsight approach, not the neural untangler of experiment 729.

    One reading is a hypothesis, not a finding: the model's demand mean may absorb a demand swing as generator units. On experiment 694's segments no weather exists, so the model's demand mean uses the calendar only and is fitted on a 28-day pre-roll. A change of demand that a temperature input would explain could then read as units running, which fits the high false-alarm share on segments with no generator. A diagnostic that adds temperature would test the reading. The maintainer has parked that diagnostic, and this page does not plan it.

    Rungs 0a and 0d: the census and the gate table matched the prediction

    On real data, 2 of the 8 signed-MW Primaries have negative readings in the scored months, as predicted. Over the 8 scored months from 2025-11 to 2026-06, D10 has 88 negative half-hours of 11,614 (0.76%) and D15 has 7,967 of 11,511 (69.2%, against a prediction of 69.1%). D5, D9, D11, D14, D16, and D18 have none. The training windows of D18 each hold 48 negative half-hours (0.24% to 0.49% of the window, by fold), which the prediction did not cover. None of the 8 MVA-only Primaries has a negative reading, as expected of a meter that cannot show one.

    The export gate opens when the Spearman correlation of the reading with the clear-sky solar yield exceeds 0.3 or the 1st percentile of the reading is under 3 points of capacity. The statistic comes from the sunny summer-midday rows of each fold's training window. The gate is open in all 8 folds at D8 and D12, the two MVA-only Primaries the prediction named, and at D10 and D15, which have negative readings. The other 12 Primaries are shut in all 8 folds. D22 is the nearest shut MVA-only Primary, with a 1st percentile of 6.4 points against the 3-point line. D2 and D18 have strongly negative correlations (-0.58 to -0.56 and -0.55 to -0.53) and are shut, because the correlation test needs a value above +0.3. This page publishes no per-generator value for D8 and D12 beyond the open gate.

    Rungs 0b and 0c: not run on real data, and no stored output

    Rung 0b, the read-out of the sign, was not run on real data. The anchored read-out builds an estimate of gross demand from dull-sky rows and asks which half-hours of an injected farm's output are exports. The plan's stop rule for rung 0b and its references bar (the read-out must beat a reading-blind reference and an hour-of-day reference, neither of which looks at the meter) were therefore never judged. During code review, synthetic worlds suggested the anchored read-out would lose to the reading-blind reference, and the plan page proposed an alternative read-out as a second arm that needs its own pre-registration. The maintainer holds the choice between running rung 0b as written and adding that arm, and no number from those synthetic worlds is stored with this result.

    Rung 0c checks the folded loss against known answers, and this page has no stored output of it. The page for the plan recorded six checks passing on draws from a known mixture and on synthetic worlds. The check scripts are committed, and the outputs are not in the stored folder, so this page does not restate the checks' numbers.

    Method

    The experiment fits no neural network and no GPU was used. The bullets give the design of the arm that carries the headline (the hindsight model on V2) and then the differences.

    • Architecture: the hindsight model is a hidden Markov model for the readings of one Primary. The reading is sqrt(X² + Q²) with Student-t noise (4 degrees of freedom), where X is the signed power, D − c p − n g (demand D, the F7 estimate p of unmetered solar output scaled by its capacity c, and n units of size g running), and Q is a reactive floor (0.14 MVA in the V2 harness, and 2 points of capacity in rung 0b). The demand mean is a regression on the calendar, and on temperature where a series has weather. The residual is a first-order autoregression on a grid of residual values (21 values in the V2 harness, so 84 states), and the emission sits on the transition so that the averaging within a half-hour is exact. The output per half-hour is a two-lobe signed distribution, smoothed and filtered, stored as 11 signed quantiles and the probability that X is negative.
    • Architecture, trend: a slow trend term keeps a drift of demand from reading as units. It is the 0.8 quantile of the daily-mean residual over a 21-day centred window, smoothed over 7 days and limited to 0.03 MVA a day. After a first scan of the unit size, the trend is refitted from the half-hours with a smoothed probability of no unit running above 0.9, on days with at least 12 such half-hours, and the unit size is searched again. The unit size is never searched below 1 MVA. The rate limit, the probability threshold, and the day minimum were set on synthetic worlds after the first trend failed on them, before any real V2 run.
    • Reference (M2): experiment 694's tracker, rerun on the same segments from its code, and compared with its stored inject.pkl before any score is read.
    • Training regime: the parameters are fitted per segment by expectation-maximisation with 2 Baum-Welch rounds, after a coarse-then-fine profile of the unit size (19 to 20 passes per fit), and the number of units K is chosen by the Bayesian information criterion. The 396 fits took 5.9 core-hours in total on one core each, and the 396 reruns of M2 took 1.28 hours. The run was capped at 15 core-hours, with a cost gate that times the first run.
    • Training data for the injection: the segments, schedules, and un-injected hosts are experiment 694's stored assets (44 segments, 9 schedules, 8 hosts). Rungs 0a and 0d read the stored panel of readings read-only.
    • Measurement: F1 of "any unit running" pooled over the 396 runs, the paired difference from M2 with a 95% cluster bootstrap over 35 clusters of segments, and the flagged share and active days a month on un-injected half-hours, per host. The bars are the table in the section "Results". Rungs 0a and 0d count negative half-hours and compute the gate statistic from the 8 monthly folds 2025-11 to 2026-06 on cleaned data, with D15 and D18 cut before 2025-03-01, and no valid time on or after 2026-07-01 is read.
    • Standing practices followed: cleaned data and the short-history cut. Not applicable: monthly refit of a neural network, saved network states, and forecast plots, because nothing is forecast. No figure is published, because every chart of the generator-state results would show a host's flagged series and is kept in the private folder.
