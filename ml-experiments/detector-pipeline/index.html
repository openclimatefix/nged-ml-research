    How the history detectors fit together

    Four detectors read each Primary substation's historical power series in a fixed order, and their outputs serve two forecasting neural networks and the reports we send to National Grid Electricity Distribution (NGED). The detectors estimate the sign of the power, the capacity of the unmetered solar panels, the power moved by switching, and the level shifts and spikes that none of the first three estimates explain. Both forecasters predict a Primary's power for the coming days from its last 14 days of power, weather, and calendar. The MSA forecaster is the axial-attention neural network of experiments 713 to 733, and its name, MSA, comes from the multiple sequence alignment of AlphaFold 2 (experiment 713, axial-attention demand network). The Candidate is the demand neural network of experiment 677 (built up one component at a time) and experiment 699 (synthetic load shifts added to the training data). No detector reads a forecaster's output.

    This page records the design the maintainer approved, and every number on it is interim. The reverse-flow numbers come from D8 alone, the Primary where the sign work started. Two experiments the design depends on are not yet published: experiment 734 (an unsupervised Bayesian offset model of switching events) is running, and experiment 733 (the MSA forecaster reading D8's untangled history) is planned.

    What the meters hide

    At some Primaries the meter hides the direction of the power, and at every Primary the meter hides the output of the solar panels connected below it. Of the Primaries the MSA experiments score, six are metered in signed megawatts (MW), which show the direction of the power. Eight are metered in megavolt-amperes (MVA), which give the size of the apparent power and not its direction. Solar panels and other generators connected below a Primary can push power back up the electricity network. An MVA meter reads that reverse flow as a positive value, so at an MVA-metered Primary the sign of the power has to be estimated. D8 is one of the MVA-metered Primaries, and its power sometimes flows in reverse.

    Why we detect switching events

    Detecting switching events serves three uses: undoing lagged power, scoring the forecast with the events removed, and reporting the events to NGED. A switching event is a period when an operator moves part of one Primary's load onto a neighbour, so the Primary runs outside its normal running arrangement (NRA).

    • Undo lagged power. Lagged power is the past power readings the forecaster takes as inputs. Inside a switching event the 14-day history is distorted. The forecaster needs to know how much power was moved before it can forecast the NRA.
    • Score the NRA forecast with the events removed. A forecast of the NRA should not be marked wrong for missing a transfer it was never meant to predict.
    • Report events to NGED. NGED cannot easily extract switching instructions from its operational systems, so a table of detected events is useful to NGED in its own right.

    A level shift caused by the weather or the calendar is not a switching event. The forecaster's weather and date-time encoders model weather and calendar shifts. The detectors should therefore leave weather and calendar shifts in the series.

    The stages, in order

    Each Primary runs through five stages, and each stage reads only the outputs of earlier stages.

    1. Sign of the power (MVA-metered Primaries only). An MVA meter reports the size of the apparent power and not its direction. The sign comes from a stand-alone neural network: the sign-only U-net (a convolutional neural network with skip connections) of experiment 732 (one untangling neural network for D8). The sign network outputs the probability of reverse flow for each half-hour. A half-hour with a probability between 0.05 and 0.95 is flagged ambiguous and treated as missing by the later stages.
    2. Unmetered solar capacity. A Bayesian fit with a physical forward model turns irradiance from the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service (CAMS) and temperature and wind speed from the ERA5 reanalysis into solar output per unit of capacity. Capacity is held constant within each quarter. The prior (the belief the fit holds before it reads the data) favours growth from one quarter to the next. The DC:AC ratio (the panels' direct-current rating over the inverters' alternating-current rating) is a fixed prior, because experiment 73 (orientation mix and DC:AC ratio) could not pin the ratio down. Wind capacity may follow later.
    3. Switched offset. Experiment 734's offset model reads a residual: the signed power, minus a baseline built from weather and calendar inputs without any past power, minus the stage 2 solar estimate. The offset model outputs quantiles of the switched megawatts in each 6-hour block, and an event table with a probability, an evidence grade, and an 80% interval on each event's size.
    4. One feedback round. Stage 2 is refitted with the called events masked, and stage 3 runs again. The round stops there and reports how far each output moved.
    5. Residual report. Stage 3 grades each level change by two cues: a mirrored step at a neighbour, and a return to the old level. The report lists the permanent level jumps and gain changes that show neither cue, graded "neither". A half-hour layer looks for spikes of 1 to 6 hours in what remains, because the 6-hour blocks of stage 3 cannot see them. The spike layer is untested.

    The sign network has to run before the offset model wherever the power flows in reverse. A change from import to export reads on an MVA meter as an upward step, and the offset model would call that step a transfer.

    The sign network stays a separate, frozen neural network. In experiment 732, the neural network that also learned solar output and transfer steps scored a Brier score (the mean squared error of a probability) 0.00065 worse than a sign-only neural network trained on the same windows (interval +0.00004 to +0.00137). The extra tasks did not help the sign. A separate neural network also keeps the sign's own harm checks independent of changes to the other stages.

    Why a pipeline and not one joint model

    The four products work at different time scales and rest on different evidence, so a pipeline lets each stage be tested on its own known answers. The time scales are a half-hour for the sign, a quarter for solar capacity, a 6-hour block for the switched offset, and a half-hour again for spikes.

    Three test results would make a joint model of solar and switching the better design:

    • on synthetic series with simulated solar growth, the offset model calls more false switching events than on event-free time;
    • the capacity estimate moves outside its own 80% interval between the fits with and without the called events masked; or
    • the feedback round of stage 4 does not settle.

    What each stage hands on

    Every output carries its own uncertainty, and every "unexplained" label names the baseline it is measured against.

    Product Value Uncertainty
    Sign probability of reverse flow per half-hour calibrated probability; ambiguous flag
    Solar capacity per quarter; MW per half-hour 80% interval on each
    Switched offset switched MW per 6-hour block; event table seven quantiles, probability of zero, an "unknown" flag
    Residual report level jump, gain change, spike interval on size; grade "neither"

    Two versions of every product

    Each product comes in a whole-record version and a past-only version, because the whole-record version reads data after the half-hour it labels. The whole-record version suits the evaluation masks and the reports sent to NGED. The past-only version is recomputed with the record cut at each forecast origin, and only the past-only version may enter a forecaster's history inputs. Feeding the whole-record version into history inputs would leak the future into the forecast. A look-ahead of the same kind, weather issued after the forecast origin, voided every comparison above 24 h of lead in experiment 404 (lag-free mixture density network), as experiment 721 records.

    The past-only version is less accurate. In experiment 730 (D8 sign detector with a demand output), the past-only variant of the chosen arm scored a Brier score of 0.01049, against 0.00958 for the whole-record variant of the same arm. ERA5 and CAMS are published about 5 days late, so the past-only version can use ERA5 and CAMS only for history rows older than about 5 days.

    How the MSA forecaster uses the outputs

    In the design, the evaluation masks come first, and the history inputs wait until experiment 733 reports. Both uses are planned, and neither exists in a published MSA experiment yet.

    The design reports every score on three masks: confirmed switching, ambiguous, and clean. The masks use the whole-record version, so the NRA forecast is scored with the switching events removed. Reporting all three stops a detector's mistakes from silently flattering the score.

    The history inputs give the forecaster what it needs to undo lagged power inside a switching event. Each of the 14 history rows would carry the past-only probability of reverse flow, the solar estimate in MW, the switched-offset quantiles, and the probability of zero offset. With those inputs the forecaster can tell how far a lagged reading sits from the NRA, and forecast the NRA.

    The forecaster should ride through a switching event but follow a slow level shift that is not a switching event. The MSA forecaster already follows shifts in recent power more than the Candidate does: 7 to 28 days after an injected shift began, it followed 0.225 more of the shift's depth at the six signed-MW Primaries (interval +0.171 to +0.278; experiment 726, follow share on injected shifts). Recent power alone does not settle which kind of shift is under way. In experiment 714 (permanent change or temporary move), 6 summaries of a Primary's last 14 days separated a permanent level change from a temporary load move with an AUROC (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve) of only 0.63 at 7 days after the onset.

    The history inputs can improve the forecast by only a little, and a wrong mask can do much more harm. A perfect switching detector would lower the best guess's quantile score by 0.42% (-0.47% to 1.96%; experiment 44, switching share of the best guess's score). One wrongly masked interval of 60 days raised the tail error outside logged switching by 57% (0.4% to 134%; experiment 38, switching mask value). The gap between a gain of 0.42% and a harm of 57% is why, in the design, the forecaster follows a level change graded "neither" by default.

    Cleaning and scoring must not use the same detector. If one detector both cleans a forecaster's training targets and supplies the masks the forecaster is scored on, that detector's misses flatter the score.

    Feedback loops to guard against

    Seven loops could let one stage's error reinforce another's:

    • Solar growth read as a level shift or a transfer. Test: experiment 734's synthetic series with simulated solar growth, with and without the stage 2 solar term.
    • A transfer absorbed by the solar fit. Test: capacity with and without the called events masked (stage 4).
    • A sign error read as a step. Guard: the ambiguous flag.
    • A forecaster's error used as a detector baseline. A forecaster that follows a shift removes the shift from its own error, so the detector would miss the shift. Guard: the stage 3 baseline uses no past power.
    • Cleaning and scoring with the same detector. Guard: report all three masks.
    • Solar capacities setting the sign network's simulated solar while the sign feeds the solar fit. Guard: in each round, freeze either the sign network or the solar fit.
    • Weather read as an unexplained shift. A cold spell or a holiday shifts a baseline for weeks and then returns. Experiment 734's shared neighbourhood term targets regional weather. A single industrial Primary whose load does not follow the weather stays a risk.

    How solid each piece is today

    • Reverse flow: solid on synthetic days, unmeasured on real reverse flow, at D8 only. In experiment 730 the sign-only arm scored a Brier score of 0.00853 against 0.03643 for reading every half-hour as import, and was calibrated where the reading is 1 to 3 MVA (mean probability 0.2479 against an observed share of 0.2506). The sign-only arm of experiment 732 scored 0.00721. The real days on which the sign is known held no reverse-flow half-hours, so no real reverse-flow half-hour has been scored. Experiment 729 (D8 untangler on synthetic days) is invalid, because a bug silenced half of the simulated solar farms.
    • Switching: weak so far. The rule detector RO-NC found 0.357 of the moved power of 35 real transfers lasting over 1 day (experiment 101). The event-table method E1-J-sym of experiment 680 (symmetric search) found 0.515 of the injected megawatts of long, deep transfers, and paired both ends of a transfer for 0.379 of those megawatts. The transfer head of experiment 732 found 7.7% of the simulated steps of 5 points of capacity or more. Experiment 734 has not reported.
    • Solar: solid where the meter shows the sign, weak at MVA-metered Primaries. At the six signed-MW Primaries, a linear probe recovered 0.76 to 0.80 of an injected solar fleet's megawatts in experiment 656 (solar probe through the MVA fold), as experiment 662 (matched-level redesign) records. With the same Primaries' power turned into synthetic apparent power, the fold model of experiment 662 recovered 0.73 to 0.90. The fold model reads the solar term through the fold by which an MVA meter turns negative power into a positive reading. At real MVA-metered Primaries, 82 to 94% of the fold model's extra capacity disappeared when the fold model's reactive-power terms were fixed (experiment 671, fair denominator). Solar capacity at MVA-metered Primaries therefore depends on the sign stage. On real days before a solar farm existed, the solar output of experiment 732's neural network still stayed above zero. A solar estimate gave no detectable forecast gain in the MSA forecaster (experiment 716, history estimate; experiment 717, target-row forecast).
    • Spikes: untested. No experiment published here has scored the detection of a spike of a few hours.
    • Scale beyond D8: paused until NGED's new data arrive.

    The maintainer's current build order

    The maintainer's current plan builds the stages in this order, and the plan may change as results arrive:

    1. Finish experiment 734 (the Bayesian offset model).
    2. Export the sign network's probability of reverse flow for D8's history, in both versions.
    3. Fit the Bayesian solar capacity: first at the signed-MW Primaries, with a test that injects a known solar fleet, then at the MVA-metered Primaries on the signed series.
    4. Run the feedback round.
    5. Build the spike layer and the residual report.
    6. Build the evaluation masks, then the past-only history inputs once experiment 733 reports.
