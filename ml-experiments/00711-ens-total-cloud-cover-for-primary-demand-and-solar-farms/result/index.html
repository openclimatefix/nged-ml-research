    Does the ECMWF ensemble's forecast of total cloud cover improve the Primary demand forecast or the forecast of NGED's metered solar farms?

    Result: inconclusive. The experiment found no detectable gain from the ECMWF ensemble's forecast of total cloud cover, at the statistical precision these data allow. We asked whether a forecast of cloud cover helps two forecasts that already use the weather. The first forecast is the power that Primary substations draw, and the second is the output of six metered solar farms. The European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) runs 51 versions of each weather forecast (an ensemble, ENS) from slightly different starting conditions. We gave each neural network the mean and the spread of the 51 cloud-cover forecasts. We compared that neural network with a twin that received the cloud forecast of a different day, with exactly the same gaps. A gain over the twin is a gain from the cloud forecast itself.

    Primary demand reads inconclusive at both groups of Primaries, and the solar farms read inconclusive. The continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) is lower for a better forecast. Real cloud minus placebo cloud, in points of CRPS at 99.44%, is -0.029 (-0.112 to +0.056) at the six Primaries that report signed megawatts, -0.001 (-0.065 to +0.087) at the eight Primaries that report apparent power in megavolt-amperes only, and +0.022 (-0.056 to +0.103) at the six solar farms pooled. The intervals exclude gains larger than 0.056 to 0.11 points and cannot exclude a gain of 0.02 points, so the data neither show a gain nor show that cloud is useless.

    Every reading is final for the reduced design, which ran 282 of the 946 planned fits. The maintainer asked for the experiment by name on 2026-11-19. The reduced design is Stage A (132 fits) and Stage B1 (150 fits), both on seeds 0 to 2. The other 664 fits of Stage B were not run, among them seeds 3 to 5 of the replicate arms R1 and R1p. Section 13 of the appendix (differences between the plan and the code) records the amendments.

    The verdict wording is "no detectable gain at this resolution", never "cloud does not help". Here "resolution" means the statistical precision of the intervals, and has nothing to do with the spatial resolution of the weather forecast. A reading of not supported needs an interval that excludes a gain of 0.02 points, and none of the seven intervals does.

    Two further results qualify the figures.

    The flag that marks the cloud gap probably acts as a date proxy, and it makes the Primary demand forecast worse. The arm DF has the missing-value flag and no cloud values. Against stored D0 (the pooled neural network of experiment 707 without the flag), DF is 0.122 points worse at the six (95% interval +0.034 to +0.217) and 0.147 worse at the eight (+0.012 to +0.269). These readings are exploratory. The date-proxy explanation is a hypothesis that no arm tested. Stage B1 removed the flag and the gap in two further tests (C3 and G1), and both stayed inconclusive. The section "The missing-value flag raises the CRPS of Primary demand" gives the detail.

    The solar farms' gain over the stored PLP forecast is not a gain from cloud, and its cause is not separable without P0L. The arm P1 scores 0.163 points better than stored P0 (95% interval -0.349 to -0.019), but P1 is no better than its own placebo P1p (S1 above). The monthly refit, the longer training window, or both may explain the gain. The arm P0L would separate them, and P0L was not run.

    An exploratory split of the replicate by the ECMWF resolution change of 2023-06 detected no difference in cloud effect. The five Primaries scored +0.006 (95% interval -0.140 to +0.153) on the three folds before the change and -0.010 (-0.034 to +0.013) on the nine folds after it. The eight scored +0.004 (-0.082 to +0.092) before and +0.021 (-0.018 to +0.054) after. The fold 2023-06 straddles the change of 2023-06-27. The plan pre-registered the split as exploratory, so these four readings carry no verdict word and are not corrected for the many exploratory lines the scorer prints.

    What a future experiment needs

    A future experiment needs narrower intervals, which means more months of recent cloud and a base neural network without a date proxy. The recent cloud history holds 7 months (2025-12 to 2026-06), and the back-filled history ends in 2024-03, with a 19-month gap between them. Seven months give half-widths of 0.076 to 0.084 points, and the gap-free replicate with 12 months gives 0.038 to 0.046. The threshold is 0.02. The month-to-month spread sets the width, so more months would narrow the intervals, and adding seeds would barely do so. The experiment did not test whether more sites would help.

    Nothing from this experiment is ready to adopt in the production forecast. The result is inconclusive, so no change follows, and production_status is not-for-production.

    Method

    The experiment compares each real-cloud neural network with a placebo twin, and the plan in the appendix, with its amendments, is the Method. The architecture, training regime, and measurement are in sections 3 to 8 of the appendix, in the arms (section 5), the placebos (section 6), and the scoring (section 8). The bullets here summarise them.

    • Architecture: the Primary demand neural network is the pooled mixture density network of experiment 707 in its arm R_F (write-up), with up to three extra inputs: the mean and the spread of total_cloud_cover_atmosphere across the 51 ENS members, from the run issued at or before the forecast origin, and the missing-value flag. The arms of Stage B1 (R0, R1, R1p, D0g, D1g) leave out the flag. The solar neural network is the pooled network with a physics-lite power input (PLP) of experiments 659 and 666, with the same three inputs. Section 7 of the appendix describes how the forecast is kept from using weather after the origin.
    • Training regime: the Primary demand arms train as in experiment 707 on the six signed-MW and eight MVA-only Primaries, with seeds 0 to 2 and a refit at each monthly fold. The solar arms train on six metered solar farms, refit monthly. The placebo arm receives the cloud of a donor run from a different day, with the same missing-value pattern (sections 4 and 6). The 19-month gap from 2024-04-01 to 2025-11-20 is masked with a flag in the headline arms and removed in the replicate.
    • Measurement: CRPS at lead days 1 to 10 in points of effective capacity, on 7 cloud-scored monthly folds from 2025-12 to 2026-06 (7 solar folds) and 12 replicate folds from 2023-04 to 2024-03. The interval is the wider of a month-block bootstrap (50,000 resamples) and a t interval on the monthly differences, at 99.44% (1 - 0.05/9). The decision rule and the 0.02-point threshold are in the front matter and section 8 of the appendix.

    Stage A detail (scoring of 2026-11-20)

    Stage A placebo checks

    All three placebo checks passed, but each has little power. The numbers are in the Checks list above. In fold 2025-12 alone, the real cloud minus the placebo at the six is -0.118 points, and the audit attributes the six's C1 estimate to that fold.

    The missing-value flag raises the CRPS of Primary demand

    The flag alone makes the Primary demand forecast 0.12 to 0.15 points worse than stored R_F, so C1 is measured on a weaker base neural network than R_F. DF has one extra input column, the cloud_missing flag, and no other change from D0n. The flag-only arm DF minus stored D0 (experiment 707's R_F) is +0.122 points at the six and +0.147 at the eight. The audit found the difference positive in all 7 folds at the six and in 6 of 7 at the eight. D1 minus D0 is +0.094 at the six and +0.142 at the eight, so the masked-gap recipe as a whole is worse than R_F.

    The flag probably acts as a date proxy, but no arm tested the explanation. In training, the flag marks the 19-month gap, which carries 93% of the weight of the fine-tune window in fold 2025-12 and 45% in fold 2026-06. Every scored row has the flag set to 0, so the flag=0 mode that scoring uses rests mostly on older rows. The harm is largest in the fold with the most gap weight, but the correlation across 7 folds is only 0.41 at the six and 0.59 at the eight. The mean median forecast of DF minus D0 changes sign by fold at the six (-1.26 to +1.30 points of capacity), so the flag does not add a constant shift. The arm D0g, which drops the gap rows instead, is worse than D0 by about +0.30 and +0.33, so the gap rows carry information. A placebo-flag arm would separate a date proxy from an optimisation change. Experiment 568 found a similar effect.

    The refit code is not the cause. The fit gate holds D0n within 2 standard errors of the seed spread of stored D0, and the audit found D0n equal to D0 within -2e-6 and +3e-7 points per fold and seed on the two gate folds.

    C1 stays valid, and a null on this base is weaker evidence than a null on R_F. D1 and D1p hold the identical flag and the identical missing pattern, and each seed starts from the same initialisation in both arms, so C1 measures the information in the cloud values. The flag's level shift could absorb part of what cloud would otherwise add. A gain found on this base would need a re-test without the flag, and the D1g minus D0g pair of Stage B1 does that.

    Solar farms: the gain over stored PLP is not a gain from cloud

    P1 minus P0 is -0.163 points, and the gain does not belong to cloud, because P1 is no better than its placebo P1p (S1 above). The refit schedule, the training window, and the number of seeds differ between P1 and P0, and P0L (Stage B, not run) would separate the window from the refit. The audit output does not contain the over-forecast figures, so the page does not quote them.

    Review findings

    The review findings of the Stage A scorer output are in the Review points section above. Two extra facts: the scorer's solar example of section 7 carries no farm label and no cloud value, and the leakage and anonymisation checks passed.

    What Stage A does not show

    Stage A does not show that cloud is useless, only that its gain, if any, is smaller than the intervals above. It has no positive control, so a null cannot yet be told from a masked-gap recipe that cannot pass a known signal. The base neural network carries a flag penalty, and the cloud-scored folds cover December to June only. A reading of not supported needs an interval that excludes 0.02 points, which Stage A cannot give.

    Summary of arms and contrasts

    The cloud variable is total_cloud_cover_atmosphere, from 0% to 100%. The experiment adds it to two neural networks. The first is the pooled mixture density network of experiment 707 for Primary demand, in the arm that experiment calls R_F. The second is the solar network PLP of experiments 659 and 666 for the six metered solar farms. The deciding contrast subtracts a placebo arm that holds another day's cloud with the same missing-value pattern, so a gain cannot come from the missing-value flag acting as a date.

    The 19-month gap in the cloud history gets three answers, and each answer has its own arm. The cloud history has a gap from 2024-04-01 to 2025-11-20. The headline arms keep every training row and mask the gap with a flag. A same-row pair drops the gap rows from both the cloud arm and its reference. A gap-free replicate for Primary demand scores 2023-04 to 2024-03, when every training and scoring run carries cloud.

    The experiment is kind: other and adopts nothing. Neither base network is a logged research default. The Primary demand research default 3 is the GAM stack, and Metered solar farms research default 1 is a three-way blend. A supported result is a candidate for a later change experiment on the port target (the Candidate P7+D50 for Primary demand, and PLP for the solar farms).

    No new XGBoost model is fitted. The stored XGBoost-based forecasts BG2 and S0M appear as reference rows only, as the maintainer's decision of 2026-11-02 requires.

    1. Hypothesis, prediction, and the smallest gain worth porting

    The hypothesis and the prediction are in the front matter and are written before any fit. The prediction rests on three earlier results. Experiment 22 found that replacing every ENS weather input with ERA5 at serving gained only 0.10 points. Experiment 559's arm A11c found small positive gains with intervals that contain zero. For the solar farms, UKV irradiance and cloud, from a different weather model, gained 1.1% in experiment 248.

    The smallest gain worth porting is 0.02 points of CRPS for both targets. Both evaluation protocols set 0.02 points as the minimum gain for a new input. A threshold of zero would be too low, because the variable adds one more column to ingest, validate, and serve.

    The cloud-scored folds cannot be expected to resolve 0.02 points at the six signed-MW Primaries. The minimum detectable effect, estimated in section 8, is larger than 0.02 there. A reading at the six is therefore expected to come out inconclusive or small gain, which is why the gap-free replicate contrast C3 is planned.

    4. Cloud summaries and the handling of the gap

    Three summaries of the 51 members are built per row, all from the run named in section 7.

    • T1, mean and spread: the ensemble mean and the population standard deviation of total cloud cover, divided by 100 (two columns).
    • T2, clear and overcast shares: T1 plus the share of members at or below 20% cloud and the share at or above 80% (four columns). Cloud cover piles up near 0% and 100%, so a mean of 50% can describe 51 half-cloudy members or a split between clear and overcast members, and the two shares separate those cases.
    • T3, daylight mean (exploratory, Primary demand only): T1 plus the mean of the ensemble-mean cloud over the daylight half-hours of the row's local valid day (three columns). T3 is in no planned contrast.

    Headline handling: train on every row, and mask the gap. A row whose run falls in 2024-04-01 to 2025-11-20, or is the upstream gap of 2024-01-03, gets 0 in every standardised cloud column and 1 in one cloud_missing column. Standardisation uses the fold's training rows with cloud_missing = 0. The rule reads the run date, never the cloud value.

    The flag-only arm measures what the flag alone does. The arm holds cloud_missing and no cloud column. Flag-only minus the no-cloud reference is the date-proxy effect that experiment 568 warned about, and is reported with its interval.

    Same-row sensitivity: drop the gap from both arms. A cloud arm and its reference train only on rows whose run is outside 2024-04-01 to 2025-11-20, with no flag. Both arms lose 19 months of recent power history, so only the pair's difference is read, never its absolute scores.

    An era check runs before any fit. The back-filled runs and the recent runs come from two ingests, and ECMWF changed the ENS resolution on 2023-06-27. For each of the 16 Primaries and 6 farm cells, compute the monthly mean of ENS cloud minus ERA5 total_cloud_cover (times 100) at leads 12 to 36 hours, and the monthly ENS spread. The ERA5 source is the column total_cloud_cover of ml-experiments/00607-bakeoff-extend-cached-inputs-to-2026-10-31/work/era5_wind_components.parquet, a fraction for 22 series from 2019-09-01 to 2026-10-31. Compare the same calendar months across eras (December to March of 2021-22 to 2023-24 against December 2025 to March 2026), because cloud bias is seasonal. A step at 2023-06-27, or between those two groups of months, larger than 3 standard deviations of the adjacent 12 monthly values fails the check. On a failure, every arm gains an era indicator if the manager approves, and the page says so.

    6. Placebos and controls

    Both placebos copy the missing pattern exactly. A placebo row is missing if and only if the real row is missing, and the code asserts that the two cloud_missing columns are equal on every row before any fit. With a different pattern, the placebo and the real arm would differ in what the flag says about the date.

    • Shuffled-run placebo (in the planned contrasts). For each series, the cloud columns of run r, at every lead, are replaced by the cloud columns of a donor run at the same leads. The donor is drawn among the runs that carry cloud, lie in another calendar year, fall within 15 days of r's day of the year, and were published before the fold's cut. The donor map is seeded by series, fold, and fitting seed, and is stored under work/placebo/ so an audit can read it. Swapping a whole run keeps the daily cycle, the growth of spread with lead, and the season. The rule is experiment 707's donor rule applied to forecast runs. A recent run (2025-11-21 on) takes its donor from the back-filled years, and the page states that the donor then comes from the earlier ingest.
    • Time-shifted placebo (second control). The cloud columns of run r are replaced by those of the run issued 14 days earlier, at the same leads. Where that run does not exist (the first 14 runs of a segment, or the upstream gap of 2024-01-03), the run issued 21 days earlier is used, and then the run 7 days earlier. Every donor is issued before the row's own run.

    The checks use a common correction. Eight checks are read at 1 - 0.05/8 = 99.375%: D1p minus DF, D1t minus DF, and the gap-scored contrast, each at the six and at the eight, and P1p minus PF and P1t minus PF. The six and the eight are never pooled in a check, because pooling signed-MW and MVA-only Primaries would let a placebo effect at one group hide behind the other. A failing check triggers an audit. It changes no verdict, with one exception that Stage A needs: if D1p minus DF fails at a group (its interval does not contain zero, in either direction), the group's C1 verdict reads inconclusive until the audit reports, and if the check is incomplete the verdict is withheld. The same holds for S1 and P1p minus PF. The reason is that a shuffled-run donor that makes the placebo worse than no cloud at all would make C1 look like a gain. The scorer prints D1 minus DF (and P1 minus PF) beside C1 (and S1) as the bound, so that a reader can see how much of C1 the placebo's own shift explains.

    • Placebo carries nothing: D1p minus DF, D1t minus DF, P1p minus PF, and P1t minus PF each have an interval that contains zero. A failure fires audit trigger 5.
    • Gap-scored folds: D1 minus D1p on folds 2025-07 to 2025-10 has an interval that contains zero. The interval is a t interval over the four folds and the seeds, because four months give only 35 distinct bootstrap resamples. A failure means that the training-time difference between real and placebo cloud moves forecasts that receive no cloud, and fires audit trigger 5.
    • Positive control (Primary demand): temperature goes through the cloud's exact handling. K1 removes the mean and spread of 2 m temperature and of 2 m dew point from the base inputs and adds them back as four masked columns with the cloud's missing pattern and the flag. K1p is its shuffled-run placebo. The control passes if the 99.375% interval of K1 minus K1p is wholly below zero at both groups. A failure means the masked-gap handling cannot pass a known signal across the gap, and the Primary demand reading of C1 and C2 is then inconclusive whatever the intervals say. The control is a Stage B arm, so the Stage A verdict does not use it.
    • Solar farms have no positive control of this kind. Every strong PLP input feeds the physics-lite power at the centre of the mixture, so masking one changes the network. A solar null is read only through an interval that bounds the effect, and the same-row pair checks the gap handling.
    • Gate. D0n matches stored R_F on folds 2026-01 and 2026-06 within 2 standard errors of the seed-to-seed spread, and a prediction-only check reloads stored R_F states and reproduces the stored forecasts to 1e-4. P0L is compared with stored P0 descriptively, because the refit schedule and the window both differ.

    7. Weather at the forecast origin

    Every row takes all of its weather, cloud included, from one run: the 00 UTC run issued at or before the forecast origin and published by then. A run counts as published 9 hours after its initialisation. For Primary demand the origin is the run time plus 9 hours (09:00 UTC). For the solar farms the origin is 12:00 UTC on the run's day. No weather value, cloud included, comes from a run issued after the origin.

    The code asserts the rule on every row of every arm, training and scoring, real and placebo. The Primary demand code calls experiment 707's assert_origin_after_run (nwp_init_time + 9 h <= origin) on the cloud summaries as well as on the seven existing variables. The solar input builder has no row-level assertion, so the solar code adds one: init_time + 9 h <= origin, and init_time equal to 00 UTC of the origin's date. A failed assertion makes the experiment invalid.

    The leakage condition for a shuffled-run donor is its publication before the fold's cut. Every donor run satisfies donor_init + 9 h <= cut. The donor need not precede the row's own run, because a donor issued before the cut has no bearing on the row's origin. The time-shifted donor precedes the row's run by construction.

    The code prints one example row per target. For Primary demand: run 2026-01-14 00:00 UTC, origin 2026-01-14 09:00 UTC, target 2026-01-19 17:30 UTC (lead 128.5 hours), with cloud mean and spread from the run of 2026-01-14 and never from the run of 2026-01-19. For the solar farms: run 2026-04-10 00:00 UTC, origin 12:00 UTC, target 2026-04-13 11:30 UTC, with cloud from the run of 2026-04-10.

Experiment 404's head took the run nearest each valid time, which is weather issued after the origin. No arm, reference row, or comparison here reads a forecast or a score from that lineage.

    Metric. CRPS as twice the mean pinball loss over the 11 quantile levels, in points of effective capacity, with the normalised MAE of the median beside every CRPS number. Each farm's error is divided by its own capacity before any mean.

    Interval. Per-row losses of each seed go through experiment_store.losses_for_bootstrap and studies.bootstrap.bootstrap_difference_at_level, which resamples whole calendar months paired across arms and draws a fitting seed, with 50,000 resamples (about 140 draws per tail at 99.44%). Seed noise is therefore inside the interval. The scorer totals the per-row differences by seed and month and resamples those totals with the same random stream, which gives the same interval at a cost of one number per seed and month (a test checks the equality). The decisive interval is the wider of this interval and a t interval on the monthly differences, where wider means the larger upper minus lower, not the union of the two. A month bootstrap on 7 months covers less often than its nominal level, which is why the t interval is read beside it. The contrast on seed-averaged quantiles (experiment 707's convention) is printed beside both as a descriptive reading.

    Minimum detectable effect. The scorer prints it before any contrast is read. The minimum detectable effect is the half-width, at 99.44%, of a null contrast between two halves of the placebo arm's seeds (seeds 0 to 2 against 3 to 5 for Primary demand; 0 and 1 against 2 and 3 for solar). Because the bootstrap draws one fitting seed per resample, a half-split has the same noise structure as the real contrast. Experiment 707's intervals left seed noise out, and its caveat says the seed noise was about as large as its C1 at the six. The minimum detectable effect is therefore estimated (interim, not measured) at about 0.05 points at the six and 0.03 at the eight on the cloud-scored folds, and about 0.03 and 0.02 on the replicate. For the solar farms the only guide is experiment 292's half-width of 0.33% at 12 to 36 hours, about 0.02 points. C1 and C2 at the six are not expected to resolve a gain of 0.02 points.

    Verdict per target. The target's verdict is the better of its C1 and C2 readings (S1 and S2 for the solar farms), per group, ranked supported, small gain, inconclusive, not supported, refuted, with two modifiers for Primary demand. If C1 and C2 are inconclusive or not supported at a group and C3 is supported there, the group reads partly supported: cloud carries information that did not cross the gap. If C1 or C2 is supported and C3's lower limit is above 0, audit trigger 2 fires and the group reads inconclusive until the audit reports.

    Front-matter verdict. supported if the six, the eight, and the solar farms are all supported. partly supported if any is supported, partly supported, or small gain. refuted if any is refuted and none is supported. not supported if all three are not supported. Otherwise inconclusive. The first paragraph of the finished page states each target's verdict separately, because the question has two parts.

    Slices, all exploratory, each with its half-hour count and a 95% interval:

    • By lead time. Primary demand: day 1, days 2 to 3, days 4 to 6, days 7 to 10. Solar farms: the same bands plus days 11 to 14. ENS stamps are 6-hourly after 144 hours, so cloud past day 6 is a linear interpolation between 6-hourly values, and the page says so.
    • Sunny and overcast half-hours. Sunny is an index of 0.8 or more, overcast 0.4 or less (studies.era5_ladder.CLEAR_SKY_INDEX_THRESHOLDS), and the half-hours between are a third row.     • Overall, per Primary, per farm label (A to F), per fold, and against the number of months of recent cloud in the training window (1 to 7), which shows whether a gain grows as cloud history returns.

    • Real minus reference (D1 minus D0, P1 minus P0L) and flag-only minus reference are reported with intervals as the total effect and the date-proxy effect, at the six and at the eight separately.

    10. Compute

    The experiment runs on 2 concurrent fit streams. The manager's instruction is that the caps of concurrent systemd-run scopes must sum below the free memory, which is about 43 GB available now. Each stream's cap is set from a peak that one measuring fit has measured, and the two caps together stay below the free memory at launch. The GPU share is for the manager to give when the first fit is requested.

    No fit starts until the manager confirms that the ENS re-run is validated. Before the first fit (CPU only), the experiment runs the cloud builds (4 workers, 1 thread each), the era check, the placebo donor maps, the raw matrices, and a code review of every script.

    Launch rules. Before each launch, free -g shows memory available for the sum of the caps and uptime shows a load below 24. Every Python command runs with OMP_NUM_THREADS=1, MKL_NUM_THREADS=1, and OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1, under systemd-run --user --scope -p MemorySwapMax=0 -p OOMPolicy=continue with a MemoryMax set as above (12G for the measuring fit), and with uv run --no-sync after uv sync --group gpu. Each stream runs one (arm, fold, seed) job at a time, with a fixed seed, its own output file, and an atomic write, so a crashed job reruns without touching the others. The fourth core is for loading and scoring.

    11. Data hygiene, anonymisation, and standing practices

    Farms are labelled A to F only, and no farm name, identifier, coordinate, or megawatt series appears in a chart, a report, a commit message, a brief, or a pull-request body. The labels come from studies.anonymise.site_labels_for. The page publishes no start date, first-data date, or commissioning date for any label, because such a date would let a reader link a label to a public record. The solar example forecast uses a day-count axis with no calendar dates, states only the month and year in the text, and sets aria=False on the mark.

    Standing practices followed: monthly refits; cleaned data and the D15 and D18 cut; every fitted state saved under this experiment's data folder; stored predictions reused as references; tails and curtailment cost reported; example forecasts plotted; a draft pull request. Departed from: the four-month screen (section 2).

    Earlier advice followed: a placebo with the same missing pattern as the real arm (experiments 559 and 568), a cloud-missing flag tested on its own (experiment 568), and a paired contrast on the same rows with the seed noise inside the interval (experiment 707).

    • Bootstrap pooling. The pooled mean weights each Primary or farm by its row count, which the scorer prints beside every planned reading.

    Stage 1 code

    The stage 1 code departs from the plan above in six places, and each departure is deliberate. The code reviewer accepted the first four. The era screens await the maintainer's agreement before era-check runs, so that the definition is not chosen after its results are seen.

    • The cloud cache starts at the first back-filled run, 2020-10-15, not at 2021-07-20. Rows still read only runs from 2021-07-20 (985 back-filled runs). The 278 earlier runs serve as donors and lag sources, because the time-shifted donor of run 2021-07-20 is the run of 2021-07-06. The cache therefore holds 1,485 files (1,263 back-filled runs and 222 recent runs). The earlier runs also enter the shuffled-run donor pool, so a scored run can draw a donor from 2020-10 to 2021-07, which widens the era mix that section 12 discusses.
    • The time-shifted donor has a fourth fallback of 744 days. After 14, 21, and 7 days earlier, the rule takes the run issued 744 days earlier. The runs from 2025-11-21 to 2025-11-27 have no donor at 7, 14, or 21 days, because each of those days lies in the gap. The 744 days map 2025-11-21 to 2023-11-08 (two years and 14 days of the calendar, less 29 February 2024).
    • The origin assertion is a copy. The Primary demand code calls a copy of experiment 707's assert_origin_after_run, because e00707_prep.py imports torch and the tests run without it. The copy behaves identically, and assert_primary_rows adds two checks: the origin is exactly the run plus 9 hours, and the run is a 00:00 UTC run.
    • 7,243,785 is the number of rows of each run, not of all runs. Each of the 222 runs from 2025-11-21 to 2026-06-30 holds 7,243,785 rows at Delta version 2589, and the 222 runs together hold 1,608,120,270 rows. The table check tests the per-run figure.
    • The era screens use two scales, and a test fails if either reading exceeds 3. The literal reading divides the step by the standard deviation of the adjacent 12 monthly values, which includes the seasonal cycle. The deseasonalised reading removes the cycle. For the winters test (December to March of 2021-22, 2022-23, and 2023-24 against December 2025 to March 2026), the scale is the standard deviation of the earlier months about their calendar-month means. For the resolution-change test (each month from 2023-07 to 2024-03 against the same month a year earlier), the scale is the standard deviation of the year-over-year differences in the 12 months before 2023-06. The code also reports the step divided by its standard error.
    • The build refuses to run unless three checks have passed at the pinned Delta version. The checks are table-check, older-columns, and era-check, each of which records its result under work/checks/. Every cache file holds its Delta version, pin --force deletes or refuses to keep a file from another version, and the lineage note records the code commit, the package versions, and the SHA-256 of each script. The data check also fails on cloud stored as a fraction, on duplicate keys, on a stuck series, on two runs with identical cloud, and on a correlation below 0.95 with the cloud files of experiment 561. Delta-rs keeps old table versions for 30 days by default, so the cache can be rebuilt from the pinned version for a limited time.
