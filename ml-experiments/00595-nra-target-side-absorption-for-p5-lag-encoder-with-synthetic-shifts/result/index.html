    When a load-shift sits in the training power itself, how much of its depth does the fine-tuned demand neural network absorb into its median forecast if it reads power lags through the P5 recipe fine-tuned on synthetic lag-only shifts (C1), or through a shared lag encoder (A1)?

    The two release-candidate demand neural networks, C1 and A1, still learn a load-shift from the power readings they are refitted on, but less than plain P5 does. A load-shift moves load between neighbouring primary substations (Primaries) for days or weeks. Its depth is its size as a share of the Primary's capacity. As in experiment 587, we added 12 load-shifts of known depth to the readings of two connected Primaries, refitted each network on the first day and the fifteenth day of a month, and measured how far the median forecast moves toward the load-shift compared with a refit on readings without it. A value of 0 means the forecast did not move and 1 means it moved by the whole depth. C1 is P5 fine-tuned on synthetic shifts in its lag inputs (experiment 584). A1 shares one lag encoder across the lags and adds a lag-identity embedding, with the same synthetic-shift fine-tuning (experiment 591). P5 is the same network without the synthetic shifts. M0 has no offset and no lags, and D5 adds the slow output offset (both from experiment 587). Every share below is at the median forecast (q50) unless a tail quantile is named.

    Take-home message: C1 and A1 lower the leak a little but do not reach 0.10. Seven days after an injected load-shift ends, at the day-14 refit, the median forecast still sits 0.168 (95% interval 0.121 to 0.213) of the depth toward the shift for C1 and 0.160 (0.099 to 0.221) for A1. Plain P5 gives 0.219 (0.174 to 0.262). Both pre-registered predictions (at least 0.15) held. The hypothesis, that the two arms absorb no less than P5, failed for the total share and held for the refit share. The drop from P5 is 0.051 for C1 (Bonferroni interval -0.070 to -0.032; minimum detectable effect 0.019) and 0.059 for A1 (-0.114 to -0.010; minimum detectable effect 0.052). The refit alone moves the median by 0.089 (C1), 0.084 (A1), and 0.082 (P5) of the depth, a difference from P5 of at most 0.007 that we did not test. Total minus refit leaves a lag part of 0.137 (P5), 0.079 (C1), and 0.076 (A1), which matches the day-0 lag following. The proposed bar L4 (at most 0.10) is therefore a concern for both candidates. The next experiment should test a training change, such as dropping or down-weighting the newest training rows during a suspected load-shift.

C1 minus P5: -0.034 (2 designs), -0.067 (9 designs; minimum detectable effect 0.027), -0.051 (12; 0.019). A1 minus P5: -0.025, -0.073 (0.070), -0.059 (0.052).     • Design cells (total share pooled over day 4 to the end and the 7 days after). The 15-day designs that start on day 3 have the highest share (P5 0.357, C1 0.286, A1 0.296); the 15-day designs that start on day 12 give 0.122, 0.064, and 0.065.
    • Verified D5/D16 pair (descriptive, 2026-01 day-14 refit, 7 days after the end, total / refit). Primary D5: P5 0.172 / 0.038, C1 0.138 / 0.041, A1 0.141 / 0.051. Primary D16: P5 0.293 / 0.113, C1 0.240 / 0.104, A1 0.189 / 0.111.
    • Checks. The q50 forecast of each new arm's refit on the observed power matches the stored fit of experiments 584 and 591 to 2e-6 (mean 1e-8). Cells with fewer than 48 half-hours are dropped (6). The score is a share of q50 differences, not a quantile score, so there is no median-minus-mean or CRPS contrast to audit; the audit triggers for score contrasts do not apply.
    • Caveats. Injected shifts are not real ones; the real evidence is one pair. Two seeds. Only 2 designs fall in the first 4 days, so that phase has wide uncertainty. NGED says load-shift events may be missing from its logs, so the clean rows may hold unlabelled events and the shares here describe injected shifts only. Weather at lag times: none is used by the share measure; the forecasts use the ENS run issued at least 9 h before the forecast origin, as in 587. Nothing is adopted.
    • What to try next. (1) Test a training-side change that targets the refit share (0.08 to 0.09): an embargo of the newest rows once a gate fires, or down-weighting rows near a detected shift. (2) Repeat the measurement for the gated candidate C1 plus G4 once it is assembled.
    • Reviews. One fresh Sonnet code review before the first fit found no blocking issue and four minor ones (all fixed). A fresh Sonnet write-up review is recorded in the pull request.
    • Data and compute. Forecasts and fitted weights are in the private folder data/ml-experiments/00595-nra-target-side-absorption-for-p5-lag-encoder-with-synthetic-shifts/ (work/<arm>/<world>/work/{fits,models}; scores in scores/). Fits took about 0.4 GPU-h in total, one process at a time.
