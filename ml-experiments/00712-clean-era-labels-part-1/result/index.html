    Do labels for permanent changes in demand, made without hindsight, help the demand-forecasting neural network?

    We ask whether a forecast can use labels that say "this change is permanent" when those labels are made only from data available at the time. In experiment 709, an earlier experiment in this series, the demand-forecasting neural network (called the Candidate below) forecast demand at the 16 Primary substations ("Primaries") of one electricity network operator. Experiment 709 gave the Candidate the true dates of permanent changes, called eras, such as a solar farm connecting to a Primary. Because real eras are rare, experiment 709 also added synthetic eras and synthetic temporary moves of load between Primaries (switching events) to real histories. These synthetic events are called injected, and their true dates are known. With the true dates, the error in the weeks after an injected era fell by 7.4 points of capacity (the oracle arm of experiment 709). The five versions of the era signal that experiment 709 built from data available at the time (rule, soft, hmm, stat, and all) lowered that error by less, from 0.3 to 2.9 points. The versions that helped most also raised the error after an injected switching event (all by 3.9 points), because they treated the move as permanent.

    The experiment adds a language model between the detectors and the Candidate. A detector flags each sudden shift in a substation's load as a flagged shift. A language model reads the load around the flagged shift, as shares of the substation's capacity, and labels the shift a permanent change, a temporary load move, or noise. The Candidate then trains and forecasts with those labels, each delayed until the first forecast time after the label could have been made. This page is the plan. The only labelling run so far is an interim pilot on one Primary (see "D8 pilot of the labelling (interim)" below), and no neural network has been trained for this experiment. The figures taken from experiment 709 and from a small trial of the language-model calls (the cost trial, described under Cost, billing, and staging) are provisional, because experiment 667 stored only two random seeds.

    The plan runs in two stages, and stage 2 runs only if stage 1 passes its gate. Stage 1 labels the real record of the 16 Primaries, without the injected bank, then fits the arms that need no language-model call. Stage 2 labels the injected bank and fits the remaining arms. The injected bank is the 64 synthetic passes stored by experiment 709, where a pass is one injection run over the panel of 16 Primaries with two eras and two switching events. No labelling run starts until this plan's pull request is merged. The ML experiment manager, the session that allocates GPU slots to experiments, then names the go, and only after the maintainer has said so.

    What is tested

    The hypothesis is a gain of at least 1.5 points of capacity with no rise in error after a switching event. Labels made without hindsight lower the error of the normal-running-arrangement (NRA) forecast by at least 1.5 points of capacity in the 7 to 28 days after an injected era, and do not raise the error after an injected switching event. The error is the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS), in points of the Primary's effective capacity. The threshold of 1.5 points is 0.5 points for each of three components the pipeline would add to production: a daily detector job, a language-model labeller with an audit trail, and a register ingest. The 0.5 points is a stated convention. Dated changes are rare: the register dates about 0.04 connections of 1 MW or more per Primary-year. A gain of 1.5 points in the 28 days after an era is therefore about 0.005 points averaged over all rows. The maintainer decides whether the gain in those weeks justifies the pipeline.

    Five new arms are compared with eight arms stored by experiment 709. All use the stage 3 recipe of experiment 709 on four test months (2025-11, 2026-01, 2026-04, and 2026-06) with two seeds. The Embedded Capacity Register (ECR) is the distribution network operator's list of connected and pending generators.

    Arm Class of each flagged shift comes from Calls
    det none: every flagged shift is era from its alarm day 0
    llm the language model, reading the load only yes
    ecr none: the first alarm in a register watch window is era 0
    llm-ecr the language model, which also sees nearby ECR rows yes
    ceiling the true class, revealed on the alarm day; it trains on hindsight and is never a candidate for adoption 0
    none, oracle, placebo, all, soft, rule, stat, hmm (hidden Markov model) experiment 709's stored

    K6b below is not an arm. It re-scores ecr on the injected bank with each register row visible 30 days before the connection date, which needs bank inference jobs and no new fit.

    K1 is the gain, K2 and K4 are the vetoes, and the other nine contrasts explain the result. Twelve contrasts are planned, so each two-sided interval is 99.58%.

    Contrast Arms Rows Reads as
    K1 llm minus none injected eras, 7 to 28 days the gain; threshold -1.5
    K2, K2-ecr, K2-llm-ecr llm, ecr, llm-ecr minus none injected switching events, donor Primary veto: above +0.1, or upper end above +0.2
    K3 llm minus soft injected eras, 7 to 28 days llm loses at most 0.5 points of capacity relative to soft (upper end)
    K4 llm minus none all real rows of the four test months veto: at most +0.03, and an upper end no higher than the stored placebo's
    K5 llm-ecr minus llm eras with a simulated register row what the register adds
    K6, K6b ecr minus none; ecr with the row visible 30 days early minus none the same eras the effect of the register alone; K6b is simulated
    K7 llm minus det switching events, donor the harm the language model removes
    K8, K9 llm NRA output minus A0-prev eras; switching events at the donor whether the labelled NRA forecast beats the prevailing forecast

    A0-prev is the Candidate's prevailing output. It is the NRA forecast mixed with a shifted copy of itself by a survival probability, reused from the heads of experiment 667. Computing A0-prev needs no language-model call and no GPU, and the manager may defer it. If A0-prev is deferred, a supported verdict means only that llm beats the neural network with no era signal.

    Gates before any score is read

    Stage 1 ends at a gate on the detectors, before any bank call. The gate reads det minus none on injected eras at 7 to 28 days, with ceiling beside it. The two detectors are a change-point detector and a year-on-year detector, both from experiment 709. If det is above -1.5 points, the two detectors alarm too late or too rarely for llm to pass K1, because llm can only relabel detections the detectors have already made. The experiment then stops as not supported after the stage 1 labelling. Stage 2 starts with a pilot of two passes per fold month (about 60 calls). The pilot stops the run if the language model labels three or more of the detected switching events in the pilot as era with confidence 0.8 or more, or labels fewer than 40% of the detected eras in the pilot as era within 7 days of the alarm.

    Two reproduction gates stop the run before any score is read. The new script must reproduce experiment 709's none forecast to within 1e-5 on the median for one fold and seed. A fixed probe of 10 calls asks the language model to name the year and month from real prompts. The prompt is pinned (fixed as the prompt used for every later call) only if at most 3 of the 10 years are right.

    The label pipeline

    Each label is built from data up to the end of the day before the forecast origin. A forecast origin is the time at which a forecast is issued, here 09:00 coordinated universal time (UTC). A candidate is one flagged shift. The language model sees the substation's load around the candidate, as shares of capacity and as offsets from the check day, with no name, date, weekday flag, or megawatt value. Checks run on the alarm day, 7 days later, and 21 days later, and on the day a closure becomes known. A label from check day d reaches the neural network at the origin of day d + 1. A perturbation test changes every reading from 00:00 of day D onward. The test then asserts that every prompt table for a check before day D has an unchanged 256-bit Secure Hash Algorithm (SHA-256) fingerprint. Labels are made once and replayed from the call store, because the language model's sampling cannot be fixed.

    Example: the 2025 solar connection at Primary D8 (register date 2025-03-24, 8.91 MW). The stored stat arm of experiment 709 entered the era state 32 days after the register date, and then withdrew the class when the level fell back. The columns are the day the state was written, the state, the era identifier, the estimated onset, and the first forecast origin that reads the state:

    entry day    stat state  era_id  onset_est    first origin reading it
2025-04-25   era         4       2024-12-21   2025-04-26 09:00 UTC
2025-04-26   era         6       2025-03-29   2025-04-27 09:00 UTC
2025-06-08   switch      4       2025-03-29   2025-06-09 09:00 UTC  (withdrawn=1)
2025-06-09   era         4       2024-12-21   2025-06-10 09:00 UTC

    In the new pipeline the language model's answer, not the detector's alarm, ends an era: the state changes only if a closure check answers switch or noise at confidence 0.8 or more.

    Cost, billing, and staging

    Labelling bills to the maintainer's shared plan quota, so the dollar figures are prices, not charges. The cost trial's init event showed apiKeySource = none, which means the calls ran on the subscription login. Every US$ figure below is the price the command-line tool reports, which is the application programming interface (API) equivalent price. The rate-limit events do carry an account-wide seven-day utilisation (unifiedWindows.seven_day.utilization = 0.32, constant across all 22 calls, shared by every session), so the trial's own share is below the resolution of that field and cannot be attributed. The labelling script counts calls and tokens, and the plan treats the quota as the limit.

    The cost trial on a single Primary measured cost and nothing else. It sent 22 calls (one probe and 21 check calls on 5 candidates) with 167,708 input tokens and 10,071 output tokens in 198.9 s, at a reported US\(0.743. The 21 check calls are 20 scheduled checks (4 per candidate) and 1 retry after a reply failed the schema. That gives 4.2 check calls per candidate with no early stop, or 4.0 without the retry. The mean price is US\)0.0353 per check call and US$0.0338 per call over all 22 including the probe. The median is 7,800 input tokens per check call. The labels from the trial were never read and are not results.

Stage 2 covers the injected bank (182 candidates), which uses 2 checks per candidate (the alarm day and 7 days later), not the trial's 4.2. Arm llm therefore takes at most 364 calls, and arm llm-ecr adds about 104 calls for about 52 candidates. Prices use US$0.0353 per call and 7,957 input tokens per call.

    Staging and limits. At most two calls run in parallel. The run stops on any rate-limit event and reports to the manager. The labelling script prints running totals of calls, tokens, and reported price every 25 calls. At 0.8 candidates per substation-year and about 4.2 calls each, 2,500 substations with the same 7.2 years of record as the 16 Primaries would need about 480 million input tokens once to label the history and about 67 million a year after that.

    D8 pilot of the labelling (interim)

    A pilot on the 19 candidates of Primary D8 ran the labelling script before the 16-Primary run. Every figure in this section is interim. The pilot is not the write-up and sets no verdict. D8 is a substation, so its Embedded Capacity Register (ECR) dates are public facts. The pilot used the prompt as pinned after the year probe. No cell below holds more than 3 candidates, so we give no intervals.

    The year probe passed. The year probe sent the language model 10 prompts, built from 5 candidates of Primary D10, and asked it for the calendar year of each. The 10 replies are not independent, because several prompts come from the same candidate. The language model named the exact year for 2 of the 10 prompts, and a year within 1 year of the true year for 3 of the 10 (including the 2 exact years). The pin rule under "Gates before any score is read" allows at most 3 exact years of 10, so the prompt passes. The year probe made 11 calls: the 10 prompts and 1 billing probe, which shows which key source bills the calls. The calls used 61,140 input tokens and 8,697 output tokens, at a reported US$0.33 (price as reported by the command-line tool, the application-programming-interface (API) equivalent).

    The labelling made 87 calls on D8, of which 65 were distinct checks. A check is one request to label one candidate on one check day. The 19 candidates took 65 checks. A strict reply parser rejected the first reply to 20 of the checks, and the script retried each of those once, so the 65 checks took 85 label calls. The other 2 calls were billing probes. The first run stopped at its 80-call cap, and a second run resumed it. Together the 87 calls used 551,253 input tokens and 44,666 output tokens, at a reported US$2.179 (API-equivalent). The summed call time was 830 s. The elapsed time was about 440 s, with 2 calls in parallel. The init event showed apiKeySource = none, so the calls ran on the subscription login. No call was limited by a rate-limit event. The account-wide seven-day utilisation was 0.36 before and after the run, so the pilot's own share of the quota cannot be attributed.

    The strict reply parser left 10 of 65 checks without a valid reply, and a looser parser accepts all 65 on the first reply. Of the 85 label replies, 55 were valid under the strict parser and 30 were invalid. Of the 65 checks, 45 had a valid first reply, 10 more had a valid retry, and 10 never had a valid reply. Of the 30 invalid replies, 16 lacked the register_row key (the field that names the ECR row the language model cites, if any) and 14 had a reason (the language model's free-text justification) longer than 60 words. The looser parser makes register_row optional and cuts a longer reason to 60 words. The prompt is unchanged. We re-parsed the stored first replies offline with the --reparse option, with no new call, and all 65 checks are valid on the first reply. The 20 retries would not be needed. The looser parser is the parser to use for any later run, and the prompt pin stays.

    D8 attempt 2 of the labelling (interim)

    The maintainer approved one more attempt on D8 alone. The labeller again recovered none of D8's known eras or switching events. Every figure in this section is interim. The attempt is not the write-up and sets no verdict. No run was made on the 16 Primaries, and no label was injected into the bank. The attempt 1 numbers in the section above are unchanged, and attempt 1 is closed to spending. In this section, a known class counts as recovered when a check labels the candidate with that class at confidence 0.8 or more, as in the pilot section. A parser is the code that reads a reply and checks its format. The strict parser is attempt 1's original parser, and the looser parser is the parser described in the pilot section, which makes register_row optional and cuts a longer reason to 60 words.

    Attempt 2 changed the reply format only. The worker who designed the change says it was decided without reading D8's label content or its agreement with the known classes. The page holds no evidence for that claim, and the null result of attempt 1 on D8 was known when the change was designed. The attempt 2 prompt tells the language model to reply with raw JSON and nothing else. The llm arm has no register_row key. The reason (the language model's free-text justification) is limited to 40 words. When a reply fails to parse, the script retries the check once and counts the retry as a call. The prompt has a new hash and a new pin file. The hash is a fingerprint of the prompt text, the schema text, and the prompt-builder code, and the pin file records it, so a changed prompt cannot reuse an earlier approval. A go token is a single-use approval string that the manager writes before each paid step, and the script now refuses a token it has used. The scoring rule is the pre-registered rule, unchanged.

    A code review of the changed scripts found 3 blockers and 4 should-fix items, and the fixes are recorded in review_triage.md. The review was a fresh review by a larger language model (Opus), and the findings were verified before they were applied. A blocker is a finding that must be fixed before any call, and a should-fix item is a finding that is worth fixing but does not stop a run. The blockers were that the attempt 2 run could overwrite attempt 1's signal files, that a go token was not truly single-use, and that nothing stopped the run from continuing past D8 once the reported price reached about US$4. The should-fix items included two findings: the probe scorer could record the wrong prompt hash, and the script did not count reply formats (for example, how many replies were raw JSON) without reading the labels.

    The year probe passed again. The year probe tests whether the language model can recover the calendar year from the prompt. A pass means it cannot do so beyond chance: the pin rule allows at most 3 exact years of 10. The probe used the same 10 prompts as attempt 1's year probe, rebuilt with the attempt 2 prompt builder. The language model named the exact year for 2 of the 10 prompts, and a year within 1 year for 4 of the 10 (including the 2 exact years). The 10 answers are not independent, because they come from 5 candidates. The probe made 11 calls (10 prompts and 1 billing probe) and used 61,384 input tokens and 10,432 output tokens, at a reported US$0.350 (the price the command-line tool reports, which is the price the same calls would cost through the pay-per-token application-programming-interface, API).

The calls used 438,551 input tokens (a mean of 6,449 per call) and 32,732 output tokens, at a reported US$2.033 (API-equivalent). The summed call time was 584 s, and the elapsed time was 301 s with 2 calls in parallel. The init event showed apiKeySource = none, so the calls ran on the subscription login. No call was limited by a rate-limit event. The account-wide seven-day utilisation was 0.39 before and after the run, so the run's own share cannot be attributed.

    Under the looser parser, all 65 checks reached a valid reply, and the format change did not measurably improve on that. Of the 65 checks, 63 were valid at the first reply, 2 were valid after the retry, and none lacked a valid reply. The looser parser alone had already accepted all 65 of attempt 1's first replies. Attempt 1 and attempt 2 are separate sets of calls, not the same replies. Attempt 1's strict parser accepted 55 of its 65 checks: 45 at the first reply, 10 only after a retry, and 10 never. Read by the structure of the 67 label replies of attempt 2 (65 first replies and 2 retries), the 40-word limit was ignored in 30 replies, and 1 reply still carried register_row. The looser parser cuts a longer reason to 60 words, and the attempt 2 prompt asks for 40.

    Under the 0.8 rule, the labeller again recovered none of D8's known eras or switching events. The table states each check as unknown at the alarm, 7 days after it (+7 d), 21 days after it (+21 d), and at closure (the last check of the candidate). The +21 d check exists for only 1 of the 2 switching-event candidates. The cells for the known eras, the verified switching events, and the unresolved candidate each hold at most 3 candidates, so we give no intervals.

    Compute

    The fits run on the manager's GPU slot, and none starts until the manager names a share. A fit takes about 3.5 minutes (50 s to build the fold's rows and 158 s to train) and peaks at 1.45 GiB of graphics processing unit (GPU) memory and 6.3 GiB of host memory. A bank inference job takes about 2.5 minutes. The main set is 71 fits: 1 for the reproduction gate, 40 for the screen (5 new arms, 4 test months, 2 seeds), and 30 for the 2025 connection at D8 (5 new arms, 3 months, 2 seeds). The fits alone take about 4 job-hours. Adding 70 forecast jobs at about 1 minute and 64 bank inference jobs (40 for the screen and 24 for the register sensitivities) gives about 8 GPU job-hours, or about 4 hours at two jobs. The A0-prev runs use at most 4 CPU cores for about 4 to 7 job-hours. The experiment follows the standing practices of experiment 709's recipe: the flagged zero half-hours dropped and counted, rows of Primaries D15 and D18 before 2025-03-01 dropped, monthly refits, stored predictions reused, and fitted networks saved.

    Method

    • Architecture: the Candidate neural network of experiment 709 with its stage 1 and 2 weights from experiment 667; the era signal enters through 709's four states (unknown, era, switch, and none), where era turns on the era vector and anchor and switch subtracts its depth from the lags.
    • Candidates and labels: the candidates are the detections of either of experiment 709's two detectors. They number about 92 on the real record and 182 on the injected bank. The language model is claude-sonnet-5-5 with all tools off and no fallback to another model. Its output is one JSON object with label, confidence, and an onset estimate.
    • Training regime: 709's stage 3 recipe with early stopping, 2 seeds, monthly refits, and the training epoch that early stopping kept, logged for every new fit.
    • Measurement: the 4 test months of experiment 677's row set, the 64 cached passes of the injected bank, and phase E, which scores the months around the 2025 connection at D8 (2025-04, 2025-05, and 2025-06). Every number is interim because experiment 667 stores seeds 0 and 1 only.
    • Register: the ECR is one snapshot with no history, so visibility uses an assumed publication lag of 14 days (0 and 60 days as sensitivities), and register rows on the bank are simulated: half the eras get a true row and a quarter of the switching events get a decoy.

    Files

    • ml-experiments/00712-clean-era-labels-part-1/e00712_trial.py: the cost trial. The script sends the billing probe and the 20 scheduled checks for the one Primary tried, plus any retry, and writes the call log. The script produces no label that is used. The --summary option reads the seven-day utilisation of the shared quota from unifiedWindows.<type>.utilization and from rate_limit_info.utilization. The option also prints that the figure is account-wide.
    • ml-experiments/00712-clean-era-labels-part-1/e00712_prompt.py: lists the candidates and the checks and builds the prompt. The prompt carries no calendar key (a field that reveals the date; see calendar_keys.md), scales capacity using readings up to the check day only, and includes the register table for arm llm-ecr. The module makes no call.
    • ml-experiments/00712-clean-era-labels-part-1/e00712_asof.py: builds the check schedule and, for every arm, a table of the label known on each day (the as-of table), in the format of experiment 709's signals. The module makes no call.
    • ml-experiments/00712-clean-era-labels-part-1/e00712_label.py: the stage 1 labelling script. The script runs as a dry run unless told otherwise. A call needs the manager's go token, a billing probe that confirms which credential pays for the calls, the prompt pin, and explicit limits on calls, tokens, and reported price. The script also builds the arms that need no call. The --reparse option re-parses the stored replies with the looser parser (see the D8 pilot section) and makes no call. The --agreement option compares the call log with the known classes of the candidates and makes no call. The script ran the labelling of attempt 1 and of attempt 2.
    • ml-experiments/00712-clean-era-labels-part-1/e00712_prompt_v2.py: the attempt 2 prompt builder. The module replaces the reply-format instructions of e00712_prompt.py with an instruction to reply with raw JSON and nothing else, no register_row key for arm llm, and a 40-word reason. The module reuses the rest of e00712_prompt.py and makes no call. The attempt 2 outputs sit beside the attempt 1 outputs in the private folder:
      • stage1_v2/: the call log and stored replies of the attempt 2 D8 run.
      • signals_v2/: the label signals the run built.
      • prompt_pin_v2.json: the hash of the approved attempt 2 prompt.
      • probe/prompts_v2/ and probe/probe_result_v2.json: the year probe's prompts and result.
      • stage1_go_token_v2.txt: the go token, which a run renames to <name>.used.<timestamp> when the run claims it.
    • ml-experiments/00712-clean-era-labels-part-1/e00712_probe.py: the year-guessing probe, whose result decides whether the prompt is pinned. A probe reply is never used as a label.
    • ml-experiments/00712-clean-era-labels-part-1/e00712_score.py: the scorer for the planned contrasts, vetoes, gates, and checks. It has been self-tested only, and no score has been read.
    • ml-experiments/00712-clean-era-labels-part-1/test_e00712_label.py and test_e00712_probe.py: tests of the labelling script and the probe. No test makes a call, because each test replaces the command-line tool that makes the calls with a stand-in.
